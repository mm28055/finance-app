# BLS International

## Verdict for the portfolio
Not now, though it is the closest miss in this group. It is the best business and the cheapest price of the five: 13 times profit, net cash of ₹1,100–1,600cr, and a visa arm that earns 40% margins and turns more than all its profit into cash. But profit is growing about 12–13% a year, not the 20%+ it grew before. Even with a modest re-rating to 15x, that gives about 19% a year. Two flags argue for patience. Spain's National Court probe of visa fraud at the Algiers consulate was reported in August to have been widened to BLS; Spain is about 18–20% of its visa business. And in September its subsidiary Atyati sold a unit to a director-linked company for ₹59 lakh.

## Clean profit base
- Screener (consolidated) net profit: FY26 ₹724cr, last four quarters ₹744cr. Both include the minority share of BLS E-Services (about 52% owned). Owners' profit: FY26 about ₹687cr (EPS ₹16.68 × 41.17cr shares), last four quarters about ₹706cr (EPS ₹17.15).
- One-offs: Q4 FY26 had a ₹5–6cr currency gain (note). I remove ₹5cr.
- Treasury income: other income is ₹93cr a year (about 11% of pre-tax profit), mostly interest on the cash pile. I keep it in profit because I give no separate credit for the cash. Without it, and without the cash, the business trades at about 12x.
- Clean trailing owners' profit: about **₹700cr**. P/E check: site 13.2, Screener 13.3, mine 13.4 on ₹9,373cr. These agree.

## Profit path FY27–FY30

|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | 2,998 | 28% | 687 | Visa 40% margin, digital 7–8% |

- **Volume × price:** visa applications were flat at 11.3 lakh in Q1 FY27. Revenue per application rose 11% to ₹3,521, but management says it has now "stabilised". The CFO frames visa growth as travel (7–8%) plus about 5% from price and mix, so 12–15%, or 10–15% after expiring contracts. I use 10–12% for visas.
- **Margin:** the visa margin holds at 38–40% (stated range). The group margin drifts down from 28% to about 26.5% as digital (8%), Aadhaar (10–15%) and Atyati (about 7%) grow faster.
- **Depreciation, interest and tax:** depreciation rose to ₹32cr a quarter with Aadhaar and rises a little more, to about ₹150cr by FY30. Interest is about ₹30cr. Tax rises from 9% (FY26) to about 12% in FY27 (CFO) and about 13% by FY30, as Dubai's new 9% tax and global minimum-tax rules bite. Each point of tax costs about 1% of profit.
- **Acquisitions:** management plans about ₹2,000cr over 4–5 years at a claimed 17–20% return. I include only half, at 15% pre-tax, net of the deposit interest lost.
- **Track record:** Q1 FY27 beat (revenue +25%, operating profit +24%), but profit grew only 12%. Management cut its five-year growth target from 20–25% to 15–20% while saying it was unchanged. At a 28 Sep conference the CFO talked of about 20% revenue and 20–25% operating-profit growth for FY27–FY28 from the existing business, then said "we do not provide future guidance". I haircut accordingly.
- **Result:** FY30 owners' profit about ₹1,100cr, about **13% a year** from ₹700cr (the note's own estimate is 13%).

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway in March 2030:** outsourced visa processing (outside the US) is about $2.2bn growing to $3.2bn by 2029, about 8–9% a year, with the outsourced share rising from 38% to 50%. It is effectively a duopoly with VFS (over 60%) and BLS (about 17%). TLS is selective. Add consular services, citizenship-by-investment and the digital arm. The market will expect 10–13% profit growth for FY31–FY33.
- **Anchors:** year-end P/E was 34 (FY23), 41 (FY24) and 32 (FY25) per Value Research, but it is about 13 today after the MEA debarment (later quashed), the Spain probe report and slower growth. There is no listed peer (VFS and TLS are private).
- **Exit P/E: 15**, in the lower half of the 14–20 band for 8–12% growth. Net cash, a 30% return on equity and 116% cash conversion would justify the upper half. Governance noise holds it down: the Atyati related-party sale, the Spain probe, goodwill at half of equity, and related-party sales to Kuwait. The 15 is a little above today's 13.4. I allow it because the business carries ₹1,100–1,600cr of net cash (12–17% of market value) that the trailing P/E ignores. No further re-rating is assumed.

## Returns
- **Normal:** ₹1,100cr × 15 = ₹16,500cr in March 2030 against ₹9,373cr today. No dilution; dividend yield 1.1% (payout about 15%). ((16,500 ÷ 9,373)^(1/3.45) − 1) + 1.1% = **18.9% a year**.
- **Bad case:** visa volumes stay flat, re-tenders reprice lower (one Cyprus tender was decided by lots at €1.90 a visa), tax goes to 15%, and acquisitions earn less. FY30 owners' profit about ₹850cr. Exit P/E 11 (one band down). ₹9,350cr, about equal to today, plus the dividend = **1.0% a year**.
- **Tail risk (separate):** in August Spanish media reported that the National Court had widened its Algiers visa-fraud probe to BLS. The company denies any role and says its Spanish lawyer found BLS named in no public court document. A finding against it could cost the Spain contract (about 18–20% of visa revenue by the CFO's estimate; Algeria is one of its largest source markets) and taint other European tenders.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 5.** Net cash is ₹1,103cr on Screener (March) and ₹1,617cr by the company (June), with ₹431cr of borrowings held alongside. Contingent liabilities are about ₹24cr.
- **Earnings quality: 4.** Operating cash was ₹2,528cr against ₹2,180cr of operating profit over FY22–FY26 (116%), and working capital is negative because applicants pay up front. Held back from 5 because goodwill is ₹1,213cr (half of equity), debtor days doubled from 10 to 21, and 11% of pre-tax profit is deposit interest.
- **Management: 2.** It cut its growth target while saying it hadn't, and could not quantify the value its deals created. The September sale of Atyati's ₹100.6cr-turnover lending unit to a director-linked company for ₹59 lakh came as Atyati's auditor resigned. The chairman was made executive at up to ₹30 lakh a month. It also had an MEA debarment in 2025 (quashed) and a Spanish probe in 2026 (unproven).
- **Moat and runway: 4.** It holds exclusive 5–10-year government contracts in a two-player market, with entry barriers built into tenders (one million applications a year, two Schengen clients). Contracts are re-tendered on price, and the easy price gains from moving to self-run centres are done.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Spain's court probe of Algiers visa fraud reportedly widened to BLS (24 Aug); shares fell 13% that day.** This is the tail risk. It is why management scores 2, and a breaks-it trigger. The note does not mention it and should.
2. **Atyati sold a ₹100.6cr-turnover lending unit for ₹59 lakh to a company sharing a director; Atyati's auditor resigned (28–29 Sep).** It counts in the management score. I would not buy before an explanation.
3. **Visa applications flat at 11.3 lakh in Q1 FY27; revenue per application now "stabilised".** My visa growth is 10–12%, below the CFO's 12–15%.
4. **Growth target cut from 20–25% to 15–20%, presented as unchanged.** The profit path is about 13%, not management's 15–20%.
5. **Goodwill ₹1,213cr, about half of equity, after ₹1,100cr+ of deals; management could not say what value they created.** I credit only half the planned deals, at 15%. The bad case lets them earn less.
6. **Group margin diluting as low-margin arms grow (digital 8%, Aadhaar 10–15%).** The margin path drifts from 28% to 26.5%.
7. **Tax rising from 9% towards 12–15%.** Included at 13%. The bad case uses 15%.
8. **MEA barred it from Indian-mission tenders in October 2025 (quashed in December).** It shows government-contract risk. It is part of why the exit P/E stays at 15, not 18–20.
9. **Related-party sales to a Kuwait affiliate rose to about ₹88cr from ₹33cr; rent paid to promoter family.** These are small, but they count in the management score.
10. **Foreign funds cut their stake from 10.9% to 4.1% in 18 months; the share price is down 30% in a year.** The market already reflects the flags. No re-rating beyond 15x is assumed.
