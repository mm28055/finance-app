# Avalon Tech

## Verdict for the portfolio
Avalon is the best-run electronics maker in the group: profit should roughly triple to about ₹337cr by FY30. But at ₹13,546cr (about 101 times trailing profit), a buyer today needs the market to still pay about 87 times profit in March 2030 to earn 25% a year. On a realistic exit P/E of about 30, the base case loses about 8% a year. **Not now.** It would clear 25% at a market value near ₹4,700cr, about ₹700 a share against ₹2,025.

## Clean profit base
- **Reported:** profit after tax for the last four quarters (Jul-25 to Jun-26) is ₹134cr (Screener; ₹113cr in FY26). There are no minority holders.
- **Removed:** other income was ₹30cr over the year, about 16% of pre-tax profit. Management said it rose on currency gains, since most sales are exported. I removed an estimated ₹12cr of currency gains before tax (about ₹9cr after tax) and kept interest on the ₹171cr of cash. **Clean base: about ₹125cr.**
- **P/E check:** the site shows 102.9 and Screener 101 (₹13,546cr ÷ ₹134cr), so the two agree. On the clean base the P/E is about 108.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue | Operating margin | Profit after tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | ₹1,603cr | 10.8% | ₹113cr |
| FY27 | ₹2,100cr | 12.3% | ₹167cr |
| FY28 | ₹2,646cr | 12.8% | ₹218cr |
| FY29 | ₹3,254cr | 13.2% | ₹275cr |
| FY30 | ₹3,905cr | 13.5% | ₹337cr |

What drives the numbers:
- **Revenue:** the August call raised FY27 growth guidance from 24–27% to 26–30% after Q1 grew 50%. The FY29 target is about ₹3,200cr, double FY26. I use 31% for FY27, then 26%, 23% and 20%, which lands FY29 at about the company's target.
  - **Track record:** it beat its FY26 guide (46% growth against 18–20% at the start of the year) and doubled FY24 revenue a year early. But FY24 itself was a bad year: revenue fell 8% and profit halved (Screener). So I do not go above the target.
- **Order cover:** ₹2,208cr is due within about 14 months and ₹1,256cr over 15–36 months. A further ₹3,465cr of 3–15-year contracts is not counted in the order book.
- **Margin:** Indian plants earn 16.7% at the operating level and make 72% of revenue. The US plant, 28% of revenue, still loses about ₹4cr a quarter, with break-even targeted by March 2027. About half the costs below gross margin are fixed. With US break-even and scale, the margin rises from 12% in Q1 to about 13.5%. Gross margin stays in the guided 33–35% band.
- **Below the operating line:** capex runs at ₹50–60cr a year plus a modular plant on the new 25-acre Chennai site, so depreciation rises from ₹34cr to about ₹70cr. Interest stays near ₹15–20cr, other income about ₹18cr, and tax 26%.
- **Growth rate:** about 30% a year from the clean base, or 31% from FY26. That matches the site's ~30% estimate.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**What the market will see in March 2030:**
- **For growth:**
  - Programmes run 10–15 years and top customers stay about nine years.
  - Box-build (the whole unit) keeps rising: about 60% of sales, up from 45% in FY22.
  - Newer lines are just starting: chip-making equipment, HVDC data-centre power, the Kavach rail anti-collision system, Europe and Southeast Asia, and a JV with Zollner.
  - Indian electronics makers are taking work moved out of China, which should run well past FY30.
- **Against:**
  - Avalon builds to customers' designs and owns no product ("we do not do ODM").
  - 59% of revenue comes from US customers.
  - Clean-energy storage, 29% of Q1 revenue, depends on US incentives. On the call, management would not explain the storage outlook once solar credits end in December 2027 ("happy to take this offline").
  - Chip-equipment demand is cyclical.

**My call:** the market will expect about 18% profit growth for FY31–FY33. That band (16–20%) pays 24–34 times profit. I use **30**, in the middle:
- **Up:** net debt is only ₹24cr and return on capital is 23%.
- **Down:** only 18% of five years' operating profit arrived as operating cash.

**Peers today:** Kaynes 63, Cyient DLM 82 and Syrma 85 (Screener). Avalon's own P/E has ranged from roughly 45 to over 130 since its 2023 listing (my estimate from Screener prices and EPS). Those multiples price today's 30–50% growth, not 18%. Even the top of the next band up (42 times, for 20–25% growth) gives only about +1% a year.

## Returns
Market value ₹13,546cr (Screener, 16 Oct). No new shares expected and no dividend.
- **Normal:**
  - FY30 profit ₹337cr × P/E 30 = ₹10,110cr.
  - (10,110 ÷ 13,546)^(1/3.45) − 1 = **−8.1% a year**.
- **Bad:** US storage demand fades after the credit changes and chip-equipment orders slow.
  - Revenue grows 22%, 20%, 19% and 18% (FY30 ₹3,295cr) at a 12% margin, so FY30 profit is ₹241cr.
  - P/E falls one band to 22: ₹5,302cr.
  - (5,302 ÷ 13,546)^(1/3.45) − 1 = **−23.8% a year**.
- **What it would take to earn 25% a year:** a market value of about ₹4,700cr today, or a P/E of about 87 in 2030.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 4.** Net debt was ₹24cr in June 2026 (debt ₹196cr, cash ₹171cr), under 0.1 times operating profit. The audit opinion is clean, but the size and cost of the 25-acre Chennai site are not yet disclosed.
- **Earnings quality: 2.** Over FY22–FY26, operating cash was ₹102cr against ₹562cr of operating profit (18%) and ₹323cr of net profit (32%). Inventory runs at 161 days. FY26 improved to 33% of operating profit (₹57cr), and free cash was only ₹4cr.
- **Management: 3.** It has beaten its own growth guidance for eight quarters and raised it in August. But FY24 revenue fell 8%. The former co-promoter has sold 11.7% since June 2025 and left the board. The group CFO left for a rival in October 2025.
- **Moat and runway: 4.** Long, approved programmes and whole-box capability make it slow to replace, and the runway is long. It owns no designs, though, and 59% of sales go to US customers.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Weak cash conversion:** only 18% of five years' operating profit became operating cash. I set the exit P/E at the middle of its band, not the top.
2. **The former co-promoter keeps selling:** 5.71% in June 2025 and 6.0% (about ₹914cr) on 1 October 2026, leaving his family 9.4%, which could also be sold. Promoters now hold about 38%, down from 51%. This lowers my management score and adds selling pressure. It does not change the profit path.
3. **FY24 downturn:** revenue fell 8% and profit fell from ₹52cr to ₹28cr. The bad case allows for a slowdown, and my base path stays at management's FY29 target, not above it.
4. **US exposure:** clean-energy storage (29% of Q1) depends on US incentives, and 59% of revenue is from US customers. The bad case cuts growth on exactly this.
5. **US plant still losing money:** about ₹4cr a quarter, with break-even only "by end of FY27". The margin path assumes break-even, so if it slips, FY27–FY28 margins come in 0.5–1 point lower.
6. **No product ownership:** build-to-print only. It is part of why the exit P/E is held at 30.
7. **Spending not yet sized:** the 25-acre, 99-year Chennai lease has no disclosed cost. I assume capex rises (depreciation doubles) but stays funded from cash and debt, with no new shares.
8. **Price:** at about 101 times trailing profit, even the best case barely breaks even. This is the deciding fact.
