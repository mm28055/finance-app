# Caplin Point Labs

## Verdict for the portfolio
A very good business at a full price. **The 3.5-year view, plainly:** buy at ₹2,302 today and hold to March 2030, and the likely result is about **11% a year**. Profit grows about 12% a year to ₹1,030cr. The P/E eases from 26 to about 24, its own five-year middle. Even if the P/E stays at 26, it is about 14%, and even at the note's faster 16% growth it is about 17%. The site moved it from follow to own on 9 Oct because the price had fallen to a level that "fits" a quality compounder on the site's own yardstick (about a 12% yearly return). It does not reach the portfolio's 25%. The note also missed a fact: the US injectables plant received **10 FDA observations in August 2026**. **Not now** for this portfolio.

## Clean profit base
- Screener (consolidated): FY26 net profit ₹650cr, of which **₹641cr** is shareholders' (EPS ₹84.36 × 7.60cr shares). The last four quarters are ₹678cr, of which **₹665cr** is shareholders' (EPS ₹87.53); the rest belongs to minority holders in the US subsidiary. There are no exceptional items and tax is a steady 19–20%.
- Other income is **₹126cr** over the last four quarters, about 15% of pre-tax profit. It is interest on ₹1,502cr of cash plus currency gains (FY26: ₹20–21cr realised and ₹40–50cr unrealised, sitting in receivables). I keep it, because the cash is part of the market value. Taking the cash out of the price and its interest out of profit, the P/E is about 27–28 rather than 26.
- P/E check: site ₹17,496cr ÷ ₹665cr = **26.3** (site 26.27; Screener 26.5). They agree.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
**Drivers:**
- **Latin America** (about 79% of revenue): built on its own distribution, with six months of fast-moving stock held next to customers and 41% of shelf space in the independent pharmacies it surveyed. It grew in single digits for several quarters before the El Salvador tender (₹50–55cr in Q4 FY26, most of it supplied by Q1), which took Q1 to +18%. Follow-on tender orders are only 10–15% of the original. Newer markets are Chile (35 products worth about US$10m over 18 months), Mexico (25 registrations, about US$4m in two quarters, land bought for a plant) and Brazil later. I take +12% in FY27, then 10–11%.
- **US injectables (Caplin Steriles)**: ₹470cr in FY26 at a 32% margin. 60 approvals, about 39 launched, 40+ in development, and its own label about ₹100cr going towards ₹200cr. The plant is "booked out until almost February". Line 7 comes in about 6–7 months, five more lines in Phase 3 "by this time next year", and 3–4 after 2029. Each product moved to a new line takes 9–10 months before it can be sold, so the big step-up arrives in FY29–FY30. The guide is +25–30% a year, and Q1 delivered +26%. I take 22–28%.
- **Margin**: 34–35% for 12 quarters. The floors management sets are 55% gross and 25% net, and Q1 ran at 59.8% gross. Latin America's segment margin slipped from 37.5% to about 33%, and new plants add cost before revenue. I hold 34–34.5%.
- **Below operating profit**: ₹510cr of the ₹1,000cr-plus programme is still to spend (plus the Mexico plant), all from cash, so depreciation rises from ₹78cr to about ₹125cr. Other income grows slowly as cash is spent. Tax 20%.
- **Track record**: no formal guidance, but the US and margin promises were kept (US +26% against 25–30%). The 5-year profit CAGR is 21%; FY26 revenue grew only 13%.

FY30 profit **₹1,030cr**, about **12% a year** from ₹665cr. The note expects about 16%. My figure is lower for three reasons: the margin is already at a peak, depreciation from ₹500cr+ of new plants rises, and Latin America's underlying growth has been single-digit outside tenders.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway**: in March 2030 the market will see 13 injectable lines filling, 40+ US products waiting, and Mexico, Brazil and Chile private markets opening. The chairman wants a top-three Indian injectables company (the largest has about 30 lines). Growth of 13–15% a year after FY30 is reasonable. The band is 18–26, and Caplin earns the upper half: net cash, about 24% return on capital, a long runway.
- **Against that**: the US arm is small against Hikma and Fresenius. Its plant has just had 10 FDA observations. US tariffs on generics are exempt only until a review around April 2027. Return on capital has fallen every year (from 68% to 24%) as cash piles up and plants are built.
- **Anchors**: Caplin's own five-year median P/E is about 23 (MoneyWorks4Me 22.7; Trendlyne five-year average 23.8). Its range ran from 6.8 (March 2020) to 40 (December 2024). Today it is 26.3, and 32 when the note was written. The site's own pharma note calls the sector "fully priced", and Marksans trades at 27.
- **Exit P/E: 24.** That is the upper half of the band and in line with its own five-year middle, a little below today's 26. Nothing makes today's P/E unusually low, so no re-rating is assumed.

## Returns
Market value today ₹17,496cr (site, 15 Oct; Screener ₹17,606cr). Dilution 0: no equity raised, all capex from cash. Dividend yield 0.35%.
- **Normal case**: (₹1,030cr × 24 = ₹24,720cr) ÷ ₹17,496cr = 1.41 times. Over 3.45 years that is 10.5% a year, plus 0.35% = **10.9% a year**. If the P/E stays at 26.3, it is 13.9%. On the note's 16% growth (about ₹1,160cr) at 26, it is 17.4%.
- **Bad case**: Latin America grows only about 8% once the tender fades, the US grows 18% as product transfers to new lines slip, and the margin settles at 33%. FY30 profit is about ₹850cr. The P/E steps down to 19. (₹850cr × 19 = ₹16,150cr) ÷ ₹17,496cr = 0.92 times, which is **−1.9% a year**.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 5.** ₹1,502cr of cash (June 2026), no bank loans, and capex all from cash. Screener's ₹221cr of "borrowings" has been flat since FY21 and is not bank debt.
- **Earnings quality: 4.** Five-year operating cash was ₹1,881cr against ₹2,805cr of operating profit (67%), and FY26's ₹523cr was 80% of profit. But debtor days rose from 91 to 138, inventory days are 181, Q1 FY27 operating cash was only ₹95cr (after ₹76cr more warehouse stock and ₹46cr of government receivables), and part of FY26 profit was unrealised currency gains.
- **Management: 4.** It kept its US and margin promises, takes no debt, and promoters hold 70.6% with no pledge or selling in three years. Against that: no formal guidance, other auditors cover subsidiaries with over 90% of revenue, audit trails were incomplete, and three senior managers left in April–July 2025. The August 2026 FDA inspection gave 10 observations after a clean (zero) inspection in August 2024.
- **Moat and runway: 4.** The Latin American last mile (stock next to the customer, about 4,000 registrations) has produced 34–35% margins for 12 quarters through freight and cost shocks. US injectables give runway but no moat yet.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Form 483 with 10 observations at the Caplin Steriles plant (13–21 Aug 2026).** The company says all are procedural, none involve data integrity and none repeat; the previous inspection in 2024 had none. The note does not mention it. The tail risk is a warning letter, which would stop new US approvals from the plant. The FDA's final classification usually comes within about 90 days, so around November–December 2026.
2. **Debtor days up from 91 to 138; Q1 operating cash only ₹95cr.** Earnings quality scores 4, not 5. Management says the El Salvador money arrives by Q3. Debtor days are a next check.
3. **Other auditors audit over 90% of revenue; audit trails incomplete.** Management scores 4, and it is the reason the exit P/E is not above 24.
4. **Return on capital down from 68% to 24%; ₹1,500cr earns treasury rates.** The P/E is applied to profit that includes about ₹100cr a year of interest, which is why the exit is below today's.
5. **Latin America's segment margin down from 37.5% to about 33%; underlying growth single-digit before the tender.** The path takes 10–12% for Latin America, and the bad case 8%.
6. **US tariffs on generics after the April 2027 review ("cross the bridge when we reach it").** It is in the bad case through slower US growth. A full tariff is a tail risk.
7. **Currency gains in FY26 profit (₹20–21cr realised, ₹40–50cr unrealised).** The margin is held flat. A stronger rupee would hurt.
8. **The price fell about 18% since the note was written (₹21,268cr to ₹17,496cr), with two exchange queries on volume spikes (1 Sep, 25 Sep).** The company says there is no undisclosed news. The returns above already use today's lower price.

The company says they are procedural, with no data-integrity or repeat items. The shares fell about 2.4% to about ₹2,497 that day.
