# Acutaas Chemicals

## Verdict for the portfolio
Acutaas has the best growth engines in Indian intermediates: a 10-year contract on Bayer's fast-growing prostate-cancer drug, four more contract products waiting for customer approvals, a sold-out battery-additive plant and a new Korean chip-chemical plant. But its margin has already doubled to 36%. From here profit can only grow about as fast as sales, roughly 20% a year. At 66 times profit, that leaves about −3% a year on a fair exit P/E. Follow it, and buy only if the price falls toward ₹1,300 (₹3,132 today).

## Clean profit base
- FY26 reported profit: ₹356cr. Trailing four quarters to June 2026: ₹387cr (Screener).
- One-off removed: currency gains in other income. The CFO said Q4 FY26 had "10 crores" of positive exchange gain against 10 lakh in Q1 FY27. Removing ₹10cr before tax (₹7.5cr after) gives **clean trailing profit of about ₹380cr**. FY26 other income of ₹42cr was also mostly currency and interest, so clean FY26 is about ₹335–345cr.
- Minority interests: 25% of the Korean joint venture IndiChem and 10% of the electrolyte subsidiary go to partners. Both are small today and are deducted in the path.
- P/E check: site 66.4 (₹25,646cr ÷ ₹387cr), Screener 67.1. They match. On clean profit it is about 67.5.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue (₹cr) | Operating margin | Profit (₹cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 actual | 1,339 | 35.9% | 356 |
| FY27 | 1,675 | 35.5% | 415 |
| FY28 | 2,077 | 35% | 501 |
| FY29 | 2,534 | 35% | 602 |
| FY30 | 3,041 | 35% | 719 |

What drives these numbers:
- **FY27 revenue grows 25%, the company's guide**, which it calls "our history for more than a decade". Sales grew from ₹160cr in FY17 to ₹1,339cr in FY26, about 27% a year. Q1 FY27 was up 59% to ₹330cr, but Q1 is always the weakest quarter and H1 is about 40% of the year. To hit 25%, the rest of the year needs only about 19% growth, so FY27 looks safe.
- **FY28 grows 24%.** Contract revenue is targeted at ₹1,000cr by FY28, and management says it is "very confident to not only achieve this target but to beat it". It does not split contract revenue out. Four validated contract products are worth ₹50–100cr each at peak, mostly from FY28. The 4,000-tonne battery-additive plant (2,000 t vinylene carbonate, VC, and 2,000 t fluoroethylene carbonate, FEC) is contracted for three years and should be full within three. The Korean plant starts selling in FY28 and should reach about ₹200cr in 3–4 years.
- **FY29 grows 22% and FY30 20%.** Ankleshwar fills by FY28 and Sachin is at 83%, so new land and an R&D centre with 10 times today's capacity are planned but not yet costed. Growth slows as the base grows.
- **The margin is held at about 35%, not raised.** Management guides FY27 "similar" to FY26's 35.9%. Contract work earns more (the pharma segment earned 44% in Q4 FY26), but battery additives earn less, and specialty chemicals run at about 24–29%. **This is the key difference from the past:** profit doubled in FY26 because the margin went from 23% to 36%. That cannot repeat.
- **Battery prices are a swing factor.** VC prices rose from about 46,000 yuan a tonne at the 2025 low to 200,000–220,000 yuan in July 2026, and FEC was about 54,000 yuan in November 2025. At full use the plant could earn roughly ₹240cr a year at 2025-low prices or roughly ₹650cr at July 2026 prices (my rough conversion). Contract terms are not disclosed, so I assume about ₹300cr by FY30.
- **Costs below operating profit.** Depreciation rises from ₹36cr to about ₹95cr (the Korean plant, battery phases, R&D centre and land). Interest is negligible. Tax is 25.5–26%. Minority share reaches about ₹14cr by FY30.
- **Result: FY30 profit of about ₹719cr**, 19% a year from reported FY26 and about 21% from clean FY26. The note's 25% needs margins to keep rising, which management itself does not guide.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **The runway after FY30 is decent.** Nubeqa (darolutamide) sales rose about 57% in 2025 to roughly €2.4bn, and Bayer targets more than €3bn at peak. Its main US compound patent runs to about March 2033, with later patents to 2036–2042 (Pharsight estimates), so the anchor molecule should keep growing into FY31–FY33. 30–40 new molecules enter its development pipeline every year. Battery and chip chemicals add new legs. Against that, one molecule still dominates contract revenue, battery contracts come up for renewal around FY30 against Chinese supply at whatever price the cycle then sets, and generic darolutamide arrives after 2033. **I expect the market to price about 18% growth for FY31–FY33.**
- **Band and position.** That is the 16–20% band, 24–34 times. Points for the upper half: net cash and 32% return on capital. Points against: one-molecule dependence, battery prices that swing, and five years of negative free cash. Net, upper-middle.
- **Anchors.** Today's P/E is 66 against a pharma sector average of about 38 (Univest).
- **Exit P/E: 32.**

## Returns
Market value today: ₹25,646cr (site, 15 Oct close). New shares: none (shareholders rejected the subsidiary share-option scheme). Dividend yield: 0.08%.
- **Normal:** ₹719cr × 32 = ₹23,008cr. (23,008 ÷ 25,646)^(1/3.45) − 1 = −3.1%, plus 0.08% = **−3.0% a year**.
- **Bad:** darolutamide orders plateau or customers destock (Blue Jet's similar molecule fell from ₹196cr to ₹2.4cr in a quarter), battery prices fall back and the new contract products slip a year. Revenue grows 14% a year to ₹2,260cr at a 31% margin, for a profit of ₹456cr. P/E drops one band to 24: ₹10,944cr. (10,944 ÷ 25,646)^(1/3.45) − 1 + 0.08% = **−21.8% a year**.
- **Cross-check.** A strong case of ₹900cr of profit at 40 times gives only 10.4% a year.
- **Price that would work:** a 25% annual return needs about ₹1,730cr of FY30 profit at 32 times, 2.4 times my path. On my path, it needs a share price near ₹1,300.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 5:** ₹314cr of net cash at June 2026 (company; ₹189cr at March on Screener). Borrowings are ₹36cr and contingent liabilities ₹17cr.
- **Earnings quality 3:** 55% of five years' operating profit came in as cash (₹589cr of ₹1,068cr, FY22–FY26), and 84% in FY26. Free cash has been negative every year from FY21 to FY26, mostly because of capex. The cash cycle rose from 121 to 149 days in FY26, and ₹75.3cr was paid to small suppliers after the legal due date.
- **Management 3:** Delivery is strong: Q1 +59% against a 25% guide, battery supply started as promised, and the Korean plant opened early, though one battery product and the pilot plant slipped a quarter. Governance has flags: contract revenue is never disclosed, promoter holding fell from 39.9% (Dec 2023) to 32.7% as both founders sold, ₹48cr of goodwill was booked when 25% of the Korean venture was given to a partner, and shareholders rejected the subsidiary share-option scheme in September 2026.
- **Moat and runway 4:** It is the primary supplier of a darolutamide intermediate under a 10-year contract, and it is hard to switch while the drug is patented. It claims a 50–90% world share in key intermediates and has about 600 customers. The weakness is concentration.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **Free cash negative every year from FY21 to FY26 (−₹36cr in FY26); 55% five-year conversion.** Earnings quality is 3, and the exit P/E stays inside the band.
- **Contract growth rests on one molecule, and contract revenue is never disclosed.** This is the bad case (destocking) and the reason the P/E is not at the top.
- **The cash cycle rose to 149 days; working capital went from 91 to 99 days in Q1 FY27.** It is a next check.
- **₹75.3cr paid to small suppliers late in FY26.** It is in the earnings and management scores.
- **The margin swung from 25% to 42% and back to 34% in five quarters.** The path holds 35% flat with no further rise.
- **Promoter holding fell from 39.9% to 32.7%, and the founders each sold about 13.5 lakh shares in FY26.** In the management score.
- **Shareholders rejected the subsidiary share-option scheme (72.6% for, 75% needed).** In the management score. No dilution assumed.
- **₹48cr of goodwill was booked for a partner's 25% of IndiChem, and 10% of the electrolyte arm went to a business partner.** Minority interests are deducted in the path.
- **Battery-additive prices are cyclical (VC rose about fourfold to a 2026 spike), and contracts reprice in about three years.** I assume about ₹300cr of battery revenue, not spike pricing, and the bad case includes a fall.
- **P/E 66 against a sector average of about 38.** This is the main reason for the −3% result.
- **Generic darolutamide is likely after the main US patent ends around 2033.** This is beyond FY30, and it caps the exit P/E.
