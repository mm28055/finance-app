# MTAR Technologies

## Verdict for the portfolio
MTAR is in a genuine boom: Bloom Energy's fuel-cell orders could lift profit about five-fold, to roughly ₹580cr by FY30. But Bloom is 73% of revenue, and the price (158 times trailing profit, ₹21,532cr) needs the market to still pay about 80 times profit in 2030. On an exit P/E of 26 the base case loses about 10% a year. **Not now.** It would clear 25% at a market value near ₹7,000cr, about ₹2,275 a share against ₹7,000.

## Clean profit base
- **Reported:** profit after tax for the year to June 2026 was ₹133cr (Screener; FY26 ₹94cr). There are no minority holders.
- **Removed:** management said FY26 other income of about ₹25cr was "mainly from foreign exchange gains", and the year to June carries ₹27cr. I removed about ₹20cr before tax (₹15cr after tax) of currency gains. **Clean base: about ₹118cr.**
- **Caveat:** this base understates the run-rate. Q1 FY27 alone earned ₹50cr, so about ₹200cr a year if Q1 repeats.
- **P/E check:** the site shows 165 and Screener 158, so the two agree. On the clean base it is about 182, and on Q1 annualised about 108.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue | Operating margin | Profit after tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | ₹876cr | 19.5% | ₹94cr |
| FY27 | ₹1,621cr | 24.0% | ₹220cr |
| FY28 | ₹2,601cr | 24.0% | ₹378cr |
| FY29 | ₹3,303cr | 23.5% | ₹481cr |
| FY30 | ₹3,997cr | 23.0% | ₹581cr |

What drives the numbers:
- **Guidance (July call):** 80% (±5%) revenue growth in FY27 at a 24% (±1) margin, "confident in doing better".
- **Order book:** ₹5,143cr at June, of which ₹3,431cr is clean energy, plus about ₹800cr received on the call day for next year's delivery. Around ₹5,900cr in all.
- **Capacity:**
  - Hot-box Phase 2 is due by October 2026, and a "multi-fold" Phase 3 by March 2027.
  - About ₹500cr of capex over FY27 and H1 FY28, part debt-funded.
- **Bloom, the main customer:**
  - Bloom guides 2026 revenue of $3.9–4.2bn, about double 2025's $2.02bn.
  - It is expanding its Fremont plant from 1 to 2 GW by end-2026, with room for 5 GW (Bloom 10-K; Motley Fool summary).
  - MTAR says it holds "the majority" of Bloom's hot-box supply.
- **Other businesses:**
  - Nuclear: ₹684cr book, rising to about ₹815cr, executed over 1–3.5 years.
  - Aerospace: management expects it to double in FY27.
  - Products: a division that "may comfortably cross ₹1,000cr" in 3–4 years.
  - Oil and gas: a plant worth ₹400–500cr of revenue over 3–4 years.
  - Data-centre assemblies: ₹400–500cr potential.
- **My revenue path:** 85% for FY27, 60% for FY28 (Phase 3 plus the orders already in hand for next year), then 27% and 21%.
  - FY30 lands at about ₹4,000cr, against management's ₹5,000cr ambition.
  - The haircut is deliberate. MTAR missed its FY26 margin guide (19.5% against 21%) and its order-book guide (₹2,592cr against ₹2,800cr). Its revenue was flat in FY24 (₹574cr to ₹581cr) and profit roughly halved from ₹103cr to ₹56cr.
- **Margin:** 24% in FY27–FY28, easing to 23%. Gross margin has fallen from 54.2% (Q1 FY26) to 45.6% (Q1 FY27) as volume hot-box work grows, and a dominant customer will press on price.
- **Below the operating line:** depreciation rises from ₹36cr to about ₹110cr, interest stays at ₹45–55cr (Q1 was ₹16cr), and tax is 25%.
- **Growth rate:** about 53% a year from the clean base, or 58% from FY26.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**What the market will see in March 2030:**
- **For growth:**
  - India targets 100 GW of nuclear power by 2047, with Kaiga 5–6, Mahi Banswara's four reactors and refurbishments ahead. MTAR is qualified across reactor types.
  - Aerospace is moving to volume: customers now order 10–15 times their first-article quantities.
  - Oil and gas, and data-centre assemblies for a second customer.
- **Against:**
  - Clean energy will still be the bulk of revenue, and it rests on one customer's fuel cells and on AI data-centre spending.
  - One published forecast has Bloom's revenue growth slowing to about 26% a year to 2029. By 2030 Bloom will have every reason to add a second hot-box supplier.
  - MTAR builds to Bloom's design and does not own the product.

**My call:** I expect the market to want about 16% profit growth for FY31–FY33. That band (16–20%) pays 24–34 times profit, and I use **26**, the lower half, because one customer is about 70% of revenue.

**Anchors:**
- MTAR has always traded at a premium: roughly 55 to 160 times since FY23 (my estimate from Screener prices and EPS).
- Data Patterns, a design-owning defence firm, trades at 84 (Screener).
- Those multiples price the present surge. A one-customer business at 16% growth does not hold them.

## Returns
Market value ₹21,532cr (Screener, 16 Oct). No dividend. No new shares are expected: capex is funded from profits and debt, at a debt-to-equity target of about 0.5.
- **Normal:**
  - FY30 profit ₹581cr × P/E 26 = ₹15,106cr.
  - (15,106 ÷ 21,532)^(1/3.45) − 1 = **−9.8% a year**.
- **Bad:** Bloom adds a second hot-box supplier for Phase 3-era volumes, or AI data-centre spending pauses.
  - Revenue grows 75% in FY27, 40% in FY28, then about 5–10% a year (FY30 ₹2,479cr) at a 20% margin, so FY30 profit is ₹263cr.
  - P/E falls one band to 20: ₹5,260cr.
  - Return: **−33.5% a year**.
- **What it would take to earn 25% a year:** a market value of about ₹7,000cr today, or a P/E of about 80 in 2030.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 3.**
  - Borrowings were ₹424cr at June 2026 against ₹379cr of investments at end-July, so the company says net debt is ₹20–30cr. Screener shows ₹356cr of net debt at March before ₹215cr of investments.
  - About ₹500cr of capex is coming, part debt-funded. The rating was upgraded to ICRA A+.
- **Earnings quality: 3.**
  - Operating cash was ₹332cr against ₹653cr of operating profit over FY22–FY26 (51%), improving to ₹197cr in FY26 and ₹248cr in Q1.
  - That improvement rests on customer advances and better terms. Screener still shows 399 days of inventory, and the company targets about 100 days of working capital, against 59 days now.
  - FY26 other income was mostly currency gains.
- **Management: 2.**
  - It missed its FY26 margin and order-book guides, and FY24 revenue was flat.
  - Q1 FY27 beat, at 130% growth.
  - Promoters have sold down from 39.1% (September 2023) to 29.2% (September 2026), with open-market sales again on 8 and 18 September 2026 and small pledges.
- **Moat and runway: 3.** Very hard qualifications protect it in nuclear, space and with Bloom (12–15 years as a supplier). But 73% of revenue depends on one customer's technology and its willingness to keep MTAR as the main source.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **One customer:** Bloom was 73% of FY26 standalone revenue and about two-thirds of the order book. This sets the exit P/E in the lower half (26). A second supplier at Bloom is the bad case.
2. **Falling gross margin:** 54.2% in Q1 FY26 to 45.6% in Q1 FY27. The margin path does not rise above the 24% guide and eases to 23%.
3. **Promoters keep selling:** from 39.1% to 29.2% in three years, still selling in September 2026, with small pledges. This drives the management score of 2. It is a signal that insiders think the price is full.
4. **Missed guidance before:** FY26 margin (19.5% against 21%), the FY26 order book, and flat FY24 revenue with halved profit. I cut FY30 revenue to ₹4,000cr against the ₹5,000cr ambition.
5. **Cash rests on customer advances:** Screener inventory is 399 days, and working capital is guided to rise back toward 100 days. The earnings-quality score is held at 3.
6. **Debt and interest rising:** borrowings of ₹424cr and ₹16cr of interest in Q1, with ₹500cr of capex. The path carries ₹45–55cr of interest a year.
7. **Nuclear slow to convert:** only ₹3cr of nuclear revenue in Q1 against a ₹684cr book. The path does not depend on nuclear beyond a modest ramp.
8. **Currency gains in profit:** removed from the base.
9. **Price of 158 times trailing profit:** the deciding fact. Even five-fold profit growth does not rescue the return.
