# Capillary Technologies

## Verdict for the portfolio
Capillary is the leader in a sticky niche: loyalty-programme software for 465 big brands, with churn of about 5% a year. But today's clean, normally taxed profit is only about ₹38cr. The plan to multiply it rests on moving bought customers onto its own platform. Meanwhile its controls failed (a ₹33.4cr wire fraud) and its private-equity-backed promoter has sold about 5% of the company since June. My FY30 profit of about ₹185cr, at 28 times, gives about 5% a year. **Not now.**

## Clean profit base
- **Filed figures (Screener, consolidated).**
  - FY26: revenue ₹735cr, operating profit ₹93cr (13%), other income ₹39cr, depreciation and amortisation ₹75cr, pre-tax ₹51cr, net ₹52cr.
  - Last four quarters: revenue ₹811cr, operating profit ₹115cr, pre-tax ₹42cr, net ₹42cr.
- **One-offs removed.** Two large ones run in opposite directions:
  - **+₹25cr** of "churn indemnity" income from the Kognitiv sellers in Q4 FY26.
  - **−₹33.4cr** lost to wire fraud at the newly bought Czech subsidiary in Q1 FY27 (only ₹4.7cr frozen; an insurance claim has been filed, with no amount or timing known).
- **Clean TTM pre-tax profit:** ₹42cr − ₹25cr + ₹33.4cr = **₹50cr**.
- **Tax.** It is near nil today because of past losses. At a normal 25% the clean TTM profit is **about ₹38cr**. The company's own "normalised" profit is about ₹53cr, at near-zero tax.
- **Run-rate.** Q1 FY27 alone, ex-fraud, was ₹24.8cr pre-tax: about ₹74cr a year after a normal tax.
- **The P/E.** The site shows 83 (₹4,259cr ÷ about ₹51cr). Screener shows 84.3. On reported TTM profit it is about 101, on clean taxed profit about **112**, and on the Q1 run-rate about 57.
- **Cash.** About ₹483cr at March, including ₹323cr of unspent IPO money; Screener counts ₹441cr net. That is **about 10–11% of today's value**.

## Profit path FY27–FY30

| Year | Revenue (₹cr) | Operating margin (filed) | Profit at 25% tax (₹cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | 735 | 13% | ~20 clean (reported 52, incl. ₹25cr indemnity) |
| FY27 | 1,090 | 16.5% | ~83 (before the ₹33.4cr fraud loss) |
| FY28 | 1,190 | 19% | ~117 |
| FY29 | 1,310 | 20.5% | ~148 |
| FY30 | 1,450 | 22% | **~185** |

**Drivers.**
- **FY27.**
  - Management guided revenue above ₹1,065cr and adjusted EBITDA above ₹172cr, and says it will "definitely beat" both.
  - SessionM (about $32m of yearly recurring revenue, bought for a net ₹17cr) arrived on 1 May and is already at break-even.
  - Annual recurring revenue is ₹1,026.6cr.
- **FY28–FY30 revenue growth of only 9–11% a year.**
  - The organic book (₹673cr in FY27) grows about 15%: net retention of 111–116% plus new contracts of ₹70–90cr a year.
  - The acquired book (about ₹390cr) shrinks as customers move over, because Capillary historically keeps only about 70% of acquired revenue after migration.
  - I add only small new acquisitions.
- **Margin.**
  - The organic business earns over 20% EBITDA and management sees 25–30% at steady state.
  - Migrating Kognitiv and SessionM could add ₹100–130cr of EBITDA "over time". I take about half of that by FY30.
- **Below the margin.** Depreciation and amortisation stay about ₹90cr (acquisition intangibles plus ₹36.6cr a year of capitalised development). Finance income is about ₹25cr.
- **Growth rate.** From ₹38cr to ₹185cr is 52% a year, which looks dramatic only because the base is tiny. From the Q1 run-rate (₹74cr) it is about 28% a year, the same as the note's estimate.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**The runway.**
- The loyalty-management market is about $12.9bn (2025) heading to $20.4bn (2030), about 9.6% a year (MarketsandMarkets).
- Capillary's roughly $107m of recurring revenue is under 1% of it. Software firms get under 10% of loyalty spending, and about 90 sub-scale rivals ($10–100m revenue) are acquisition targets.
- So in 2030 the market can expect about 18–20% growth (organic 15% plus roll-ups). That puts it in the 24–34 band.

**Why the lower half.**
- The top five clients are about 34% of revenue.
- Controls failed, and the KPMG forensic review is still open.
- The private-equity-backed promoter is selling.
- Reported return on capital is only 3%.

**Anchors and choice.** Indian SaaS peers trade at 37.5 times (RateGain) and 20.6 times (Newgen) (Screener, 16 Oct). Capillary listed in November 2025 and has traded at about 68–125 times reported profit (₹438–₹799). **I use 28.**

## Returns
Market value ₹4,259cr (site, 15 Oct). Dilution 2% (stock options; ESOP cost ₹12–15cr a year). No dividend.
- **Normal case:** ₹185cr × 28 = ₹5,180cr. (5,180 ÷ (4,259 × 1.02))^(1/3.45) − 1 = **+5.2% a year**.
- **Bad case:** migrations stall, as Persuade's took five years, and organic growth drops to about 11% once currency (about 6 points of today's 17%) is gone. The margin stays near 18%. FY30 profit is about ₹120cr and the exit P/E is 20. ₹2,400cr gives **−15.8% a year**.
- **Bullish check:** ₹1,600cr of revenue at a 25% margin is about ₹250cr of profit. At 30 times that gives about 17% a year, still under 25%.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 4/5:** net cash of about ₹441cr and borrowings of ₹45–54cr. But acquisition intangibles are large, and the fraud recovery is uncertain.
- **Earnings quality 2/5:**
  - Customers pay upfront, so FY26 operating cash (₹150cr) beat operating profit (₹93cr). But the five-year cash ratio of 606% is arithmetic on a tiny base.
  - Large one-offs run in both directions: indemnity income, fraud loss and deferred-tax swings.
  - It capitalised ₹36.6cr of development, a key audit matter.
  - The auditor could not confirm a database audit trail at a third-party accounting provider.
  - Debtor days are 90.
- **Management 2/5:**
  - It beat its own margin guidance (17.2% adjusted against about 16%).
  - But an executive wired ₹33.4cr to fraudsters impersonating the CEO, seven weeks after that subsidiary was bought.
  - The head of finance (June) and head of corporate development (July) resigned.
  - The promoter entity cut its stake from 52.29% at listing to 47.23% (Sep 2026).
- **Moat and runway 4/5:** a Forrester leader with about nine integration points per client, churn of about 5% and net retention of 111–116%, in a fragmented market with years of runway.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **₹33.4cr fraud loss; Q1 FY27 filed loss of ₹9.6cr; KPMG review pending.** The loss is excluded from the base as a one-off. A second lapse is a break trigger, and management is scored 2.
- **The promoter (Capillary Technologies International, backed by private equity) sold 4.14% in August at ₹520.86 (₹171.5cr) and 0.74% in June; it now holds 47.23%.** This supply caps the price. No uplift is assumed.
- **FY26 profit included ₹25cr of indemnity income.** Removed.
- **Organic growth is about 17% including about 6 points of currency; net retention fell to 111% as one large US healthcare client stalled.** Organic growth is modelled at about 15%, and the bad case at 11%.
- **The top five clients are about 34% of revenue.** This keeps the exit P/E in the lower half of the band.
- **The acquired book (about ₹390cr) earns near break-even, and about 30% of it is usually lost in migration.** Revenue growth is held to 9–11% a year from FY28.
- **Capitalised development and audit-trail gaps.** Earnings quality is scored 2.
- **Tax is near nil on past losses.** Everything is re-taxed at 25%.
- **Return on capital is 3%.** It should rise as margins grow. Until then it is a reason for the lower-half P/E.

## Since the note was written
- **Promoter stake.** The September 2026 shareholding shows promoters at 47.23%, against 51.45% in June. The note, written 27 Sep, still showed 51.45% and missed the August block sale of 4.14% at ₹520.86.
- **Institutions.** Domestic funds rose to 21.87%. Kotak Mahindra AMC crossed 5% (filing recorded 24 Sep).
- **Price.** The stock rose 7.5% on 6 Oct with no news. At ₹535 it is about 11% below its value when the note was written (₹4,771cr then, ₹4,259cr now).
- **Results.** No Q2 FY27 results yet. The checkpoint is quarterly revenue above ₹270cr with a filed operating margin of at least 16%, plus any update on the forensic review and insurance claim.
