# Krishna Defence & Allied Industries

## Verdict for the portfolio
Krishna is the highest-quality small business in this group: about 31% return on capital, net cash, and approvals for naval steels India used to import from Russia. A warship-building wave is coming. But orders have been thin, revenue fell 15% in Q1, and after a 20% jump on 5 October the stock trades at 42 times profit. Even if profit doubles to about ₹89cr by FY30, a fair exit P/E of about 24 gives only about 5% a year. **Not now.** I would buy below a market value of about ₹1,000cr, roughly ₹650 a share against ₹1,213.

## Clean profit base
- **Reported:** profit after tax for the year to June 2026 was ₹43cr (Screener; FY26 ₹41cr). This includes about ₹3cr of profit share from associates Conceptia (20%) and Waveoptix (40%).
- **Kept:** other income of ₹4cr is interest on about ₹77cr of fixed deposits. It is small (7% of pre-tax profit), so I kept it. There are no tax credits or exceptional items. **Clean base: ₹43cr.**
- **P/E check:** the site shows 41.5 and Screener 41.9 (₹1,813cr ÷ ₹43cr), so the two agree.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue | Operating margin | Profit after tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | ₹245cr | 21.3% (company) | ₹41cr |
| FY27 | ₹270cr | 24% | ₹51cr |
| FY28 | ₹340cr | 24% | ₹63cr |
| FY29 | ₹414cr | 24% | ₹76cr |
| FY30 | ₹489cr | 24% | ₹89cr |

What drives the numbers:
- **Guidance (May call, the latest; there was no Q1 call):** "30% plus" growth a year for three years, all from existing products.
  - Bulb bars are about 60% of sales, welding wire 15% and armour profiles 15%.
  - The Halol plant can support ₹400–500cr of revenue, and adjoining land has been bought.
- **What the orders show:**
  - The order book was ₹103cr in March and ₹117cr in June, after only about ₹30cr of orders in H2 FY26 against ₹100–150cr promised.
  - Orders since then: ₹45.64cr from the Ministry of Defence in June and about ₹20cr in August (19-month delivery).
  - Q1 FY27 revenue fell 15% to ₹58cr.
- **My revenue path:** FY27 at only +10%, then 26% in FY28 as warship programmes order steel, then 22% and 18% as the plant nears ₹500cr.
  - The next-generation corvettes are an eight-ship, ₹33,000cr programme. GRSE's contract signing was expected in Q2 FY27 and is not yet confirmed. Bulb-bar orders follow the shipyard contract.
  - Antique's initiation (5 October) forecasts 31% revenue growth a year to FY29. I am well below that because the company has just missed its order promise.
- **Margin:** Q1 was 26%, FY26 21.3%, on a richer mix of welding wire and armour. Management warns "we cannot expect margins to increase by 5% every single year." I hold 24%.
- **Below the operating line:** depreciation rises to about ₹10cr with automation and the new plot, interest is near zero, other income about ₹5cr, tax 25%, and associates add ₹3–4.5cr.
- **Growth rate:** about 21% a year, close to the site's ~22% estimate.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**What the market will see in March 2030:**
- **For growth:**
  - Antique counts a warship pipeline of about ₹4.59 lakh crore over 10–15 years, plus ₹2.2 lakh crore of commercial ships.
  - Krishna is one of only two approved bulb-bar vendors, and it now holds IRS approval for commercial ships.
  - Options: an autonomous underwater vehicle with the Navy and DRDO, smart naval ammunition (47% stake), composite hatches (51% JV) and aircraft arresting gear. All are 2–3 years from revenue.
- **Against:**
  - At about ₹500cr of revenue it will be close to the size of its niche.
  - A new plant is needed beyond FY30.
  - It wins orders by lowest bid, and one shipyard is 35–40% of sales.
  - Its naval steels cannot be exported.

**My call:** I expect the market to want about 15% profit growth for FY31–FY33. That band (12–16%) pays 18–26 times profit, and I use **24**, the upper half, for net cash, 31% return on capital and a visible naval runway.

**Peers:** MIDHANI trades at 59 and Data Patterns at 84 (Screener). Defence names carry a premium today, but it is a premium for growth that, at Krishna's size, will be slowing by 2030. Even at 30 times (the next band up), the return is only about 12% a year.

## Returns
Market value ₹1,813cr (Screener, 16 Oct). Dividend yield 0.1%. Dilution 1%: employee stock options were granted in FY26, and promoters went from 73.4% to 59.9% through earlier share issues.
- **Normal:**
  - FY30 profit ₹89cr × P/E 24 = ₹2,136cr.
  - (2,136 ÷ (1,813 × 1.01))^(1/3.45) − 1 + 0.1% = **+4.7% a year**.
- **Bad:** shipyards keep ordering in small batches and the corvette steel arrives late.
  - Revenue is flat in FY27 and then grows 12–15% a year (FY30 ₹363cr), with the margin back to 20%. FY30 profit is ₹55cr.
  - P/E falls one band to 17: ₹935cr.
  - Return: **−17.6% a year**.
- **What it would take:** a market value of about ₹980cr (about ₹655 a share) to clear 25% a year, or about ₹1,130cr (about ₹755) to clear 20%. Alternatively, an exit P/E of about 44 on ₹89cr of profit.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 5.** Borrowings are ₹2cr against more than ₹100cr of cash and deposits, including ₹76.9cr in long-term deposits. The only guarantee is ₹5cr for Waveoptix's bank loans. CRISIL rates it BBB/Positive.
- **Earnings quality: 3.**
  - Operating cash was ₹65cr against ₹109cr of operating profit over FY23–FY26 (60%). But three of those four years were negative.
  - FY26's ₹86cr was flattered by a year-end stock dip: ₹16.3cr against a normal ~₹40cr.
  - Late-delivery charges nearly doubled to ₹9.97cr.
- **Management: 2.**
  - It missed its H2 FY26 order promise (₹30cr against ₹100–150cr), and Q1 revenue fell 15% against a 30%-plus guide.
  - It bought two flats from the MD during FY26.
  - The CFO resigned in April 2026 and the company secretary in August 2026, after a whole-time director died in March.
  - Promoters were diluted from 73.4% to 59.9%.
- **Moat and runway: 3.** Naval, shipyard and classification approvals (DNV, ABS, IRS) and buy-Indian rules keep imports out, and it is one of two approved vendors. But the niche is small, orders come by lowest-bid tender, and a large steel maker could qualify.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Thin order book:** ₹117cr in June, under half a year of sales, after ₹30cr of H2 FY26 orders against ₹100–150cr promised. My FY27 is cut to +10% against the 30%-plus guide, and the bad case keeps FY27 flat.
2. **Q1 FY27 revenue fell 15%:** this is built into the low FY27.
3. **One shipyard is 35–40% of sales, and tenders are won on lowest bid:** this sets growth after FY30 at 15%, not higher, and the moat score at 3.
4. **Late-delivery charges up from ₹5.25cr to ₹9.97cr (about 4% of contract value):** this keeps the margin at 24%, not above the Q1 26%.
5. **FY26 operating cash flattered by the stock dip:** the earnings-quality score is held at 3.
6. **Related-party flat purchases from the MD, CFO and company-secretary resignations, promoter dilution:** the management score is 2. Not a bar on its own, but a reason not to pay up.
7. **Plant ceiling near ₹500cr:** a new plant is needed after FY30, part of why the exit P/E is not higher.
8. **Price jumped 20% on 5 October on a broker initiation, with no new order:** at 42 times profit, the buyer pays for growth the order book does not yet show.
