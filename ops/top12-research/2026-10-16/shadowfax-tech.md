# Shadowfax Tech.

## Verdict for the portfolio
Shadowfax is executing very well: revenue is up 65%, margins are rising and it raised its guidance. But the price already assumes profit grows about 4.5 times by FY30. At ₹16,779cr a buyer earns roughly nothing a year even if that happens, because the multiple will fall as growth slows. One customer gives about half its revenue and runs its own delivery network. **Not now.** This is a follow, not a buy, until either the price or the customer concentration falls a long way.

## Clean profit base
- **Filed figures (Screener, consolidated):**
  - FY26: revenue ₹4,202cr (+69%), operating profit ₹221cr (5%), other income ₹36cr, pre-tax profit ₹111cr, net profit ₹112cr.
  - Last four quarters (Sep-25 to Jun-26): revenue ₹4,737cr, operating profit ₹278cr, other income ₹49cr, pre-tax ₹168cr, net ₹169cr.
- **What I removed.**
  - **Tax shelter.** The tax rate has been 0% every quarter, because past losses shelter profit. That ends, so I tax at 25%. Clean TTM profit is ₹168cr × 0.75 = **about ₹126cr**. FY26 on the same basis is about ₹83cr.
  - **Treasury income.** Other income has jumped to ₹21cr a quarter, earned on ₹1,537cr of IPO cash and investments. That is 29% of TTM pre-tax profit. I keep it in profit but flag it. Without it, the delivery business earns about ₹89cr after a normal tax.
- **The P/E.** The site's 98.9 matches Screener's 99.8: ₹16,779cr ÷ ₹169cr of untaxed profit. On clean, taxed profit the P/E is **about 133**. Cash is **about 9% of today's value**. Without it, the business costs about 171 times its taxed core profit.

## Profit path FY27–FY30

| Year | Revenue (₹cr) | After-rent margin (company basis) | Profit at 25% tax (₹cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | 4,202 | 3.8% | ~83 (reported 112, untaxed) |
| FY27 | 5,840 | 5.0% | ~210 |
| FY28 | 7,420 | 6.0% | ~305 |
| FY29 | 9,050 | 7.0% | ~430 |
| FY30 | 10,680 | 7.8% | **~560** |

**Drivers.**
- **Revenue.**
  - FY27 is at the raised guide of +38–40%. Q1 was +65% on 25 crore orders (+83%), and Q2 to Q4 must slow sharply to land at 39%.
  - After that I assume +27%, +22% and +18%. In May management called 28–30% the "maximum" longer-term growth.
  - The sources are share gains: 28–30% of outsourced express parcels, against 17–18% a year earlier. Add D2C brands (Prime +2.7 times), Amazon Now in quick commerce and Prime Large.
- **Margin.**
  - Management guides the after-rent margin up 1–1.2 points a year to FY28, then 2–2.5 points a year towards "early double-digit". That implies about 10.5% in FY30.
  - It beat the guide in Q1 (+2 points). But it has said it will hand extra margin to customers or reinvest it, and its rate cards cut prices as volume grows. So I take FY30 at 7.8%, not 10.5%.
- **Below the margin.**
  - Pre-tax profit sits about 1.7 points below the after-rent margin. That gap is owned-asset depreciation on ₹180–190cr a year of capex, plus interest.
  - Other income stays about ₹85–100cr a year.
  - Tax is 25% once losses are used. Reported profit will be higher than this table while the shelter lasts.
- **Growth rate:** about 49% a year from the clean taxed base (₹126cr to ₹560cr over 3.75 years). That is the outcome of a margin going from 3.8% to 7.8% on revenue that grows 2.5 times.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**The runway.**
- **For more growth:** in March 2030 the market will expect revenue growth of perhaps 15–18%. Online retail is about $80bn and growing about 21%, and quick-commerce outsourcing could go from 12–15% to 20–25% of volume. Margin can still rise towards 10%. Together that is 20–25% profit growth, in the 30–42 band.
- **Against:**
  - Its biggest customers decide how much they outsource and on what terms.
  - The margin is so thin that a 1% price cut removes about a fifth of operating profit.
  - The labour codes add a gig-worker levy of 1–2% of turnover.
  - Delhivery's share price shows how the market treats parcel carriers' P/Es once growth slows: 257 times on ₹93cr of TTM profit (Screener, 16 Oct), because profit never arrived.
- **I use 30**, the bottom of the band, because one customer is about half of revenue. The stock listed at ₹124 in January 2026 (about 1,000 times FY25 profit) and is now ₹286, so it has no stable P/E history to anchor on.

## Returns
Market value ₹16,779cr (site, 15 Oct). Dilution 2% (employee options). No dividend.
- **Normal case:** ₹560cr × 30 = ₹16,800cr. (16,800 ÷ (16,779 × 1.02))^(1/3.45) − 1 = **−0.5% a year**.
- **Bad case:** the largest customer moves more volume to its own network, or forces a price cut at renewal. Revenue growth slows to about 20% in FY28 and 12% after, and the after-rent margin stalls near 5.5%. FY30 profit is about ₹300cr and the exit P/E is 24 (one band down). ₹7,200cr gives **−22.2% a year**.
- **Even management's full path** (10.5% margin, about ₹780cr FY30 profit, 35 times) gives only about **14.5% a year**.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 5/5:** ₹1,537cr of cash and investments from the January IPO, no bank debt (₹247cr of borrowings are mostly leases) and only ₹2.15cr of contingent liabilities.
- **Earnings quality 3/5:**
  - FY26 operating cash was ₹350cr on ₹221cr of operating profit. But Q1 FY27 turned only ₹50cr of profit into operating cash, as working capital took ₹46cr.
  - Debtor days are 45 and rising with large platform customers.
  - Profit is untaxed, and 29% of pre-tax profit is interest on IPO cash.
  - The auditor made manually calculated revenue its key audit matter and could not confirm database audit trails for Jan–Mar 2026.
- **Management 3/5:** beat every Q4 promise in Q1: revenue +65%, margin +2 points, heavy-goods pin-code target met early. But it has been listed only nine months, the audit-trail gap is open, and the venture backers are selling heavily.
- **Moat and runway 3/5:** one of only two national third-party parcel networks, with 16,372 pin codes and a crowdsourced rider base. But the customers are giants that can and do build their own fleets, and Shadowfax has no pricing power beyond passing on fuel.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **Two customers gave ₹2,709cr, 64% of FY26 revenue.** The prospectus showed the largest alone at about 48–49% (FY25 and H1 FY26), and press reports name Meesho and Flipkart as the biggest clients. Meesho runs its own Valmo network, and its shift of volume away from Ecom Express led to that firm's distressed sale. This is named as the tail risk, kept out of the bad case, and it holds the exit P/E at the bottom of its band.
- **Profit is untaxed.** It is re-taxed at 25%, which lifts the clean P/E from 99 to about 133.
- **29% of pre-tax profit is treasury income.** It is kept in profit, valued as cash, and flagged.
- **The auditor flagged manual revenue calculation and missing audit trails.** Earnings quality is scored 3. A repeat in the FY27 audit would be a break.
- **Early holders keep selling:**
  - In July, Flipkart and two other investors offered up to 9.08% at a ₹197 floor.
  - Qualcomm sold ₹184cr in August and exited in October.
  - TPG and NewQuest sold ₹301cr and then about ₹114–115cr twice (30 Sep and 6 Oct).
  - This supply caps the price but says nothing about the business. No return uplift is assumed from it ending.
- **Rate cards cut prices as volume grows, revenue per express order fell from ₹52.2 to ₹50.4, and Shadowfax absorbed five days of diesel.** This is why the base margin is 7.8%, not management's 10.5%.
- **Minimum-wage rises cost about ₹2–2.5cr a month, and the gig-worker levy is 1–2% of turnover.** These are inside the slower margin path.
- **Q1 operating cash was only ₹50cr; the cash cycle lengthened from 36 to 45–48 days.** This is part of the earnings-quality score of 3.
- **It listed in January 2026, so the record is short.** Management is scored 3 despite beating its guidance.
