# Jeena Sikho Lifecare

## Verdict for the portfolio
Jeena Sikho is the cheapest of the five at 20 times profit, with a 41% margin, net cash and very high returns on capital. If the founder's promise of 30% a year comes true, the shares could return over 30% a year. But he has already missed on beds, a profit figure and timing. Q1 operating profit grew only 17%, and the annual report carries a GST order alleging hidden turnover, an auditor who resigned and months of revenue records with no audit trail. On my haircut path (about 16% profit growth), the return is about 17% a year: below the 25% target, with a governance flag that argues for staying out.

## Clean profit base
- FY26 reported profit: ₹222cr. Trailing four quarters to June 2026: ₹236cr (Screener).
- One-off removed: in Q1 FY27, ₹7–7.5cr of other income was one-time (₹5cr revaluation and ₹2.5cr capital gain, per the CFO on the Q1 call). That is about ₹5.5cr after tax. **Clean trailing profit is about ₹230cr.**
- Not added back: Q4 FY26's ₹19–21cr of costs that management called one-off. Advertising and set-up costs keep recurring as the chain grows, so I treat them as normal.
- P/E check: site 20.4 (₹4,832cr ÷ ₹237cr), Screener 20.4. They match. On clean profit it is about 21. The machine transcript of the Q1 call says operating profit grew 43%. The filed figure is 17% (₹79cr to ₹92cr), and I use the filing.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue (₹cr) | Operating margin | Profit (₹cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 actual | 801 | 43.8% | 222 |
| FY27 | 990 | 40.5% | 256 |
| FY28 | 1,180 | 39.5% | 294 |
| FY29 | 1,390 | 39% | 344 |
| FY30 | 1,620 | 38.5% | 400 |

What drives these numbers:
- **Revenue grows about 19% a year (24% in FY27, easing to 16–17%)**, against the founder's "approximately 30% year-on-year" and ₹3,000cr of revenue "in approximately 3 years and, at the latest, within 5 years". Q1 FY27 revenue rose 29% to ₹224cr, but only 4% on Q4's ₹216cr. Services grew only 11% as government business was cut from ₹15cr to ₹5cr a quarter and prices were discounted.
- **Beds are the main driver, and the record is poor.** There were 2,400 operating beds at June (59% full in Q1; 61% per the MD on 29 September). In June the promise was 2,800–3,000 "in three to four months"; only 100 were added in Q1. The promise is now 3,000–3,500 by March 2027 and 7,000–10,000 in 3–5 years. A new model of taking over Ayurveda college hospitals costs only ₹1–1.5 lakh a bed (three colleges, 400 beds), but 20 more are on hold until Ayushman cover or insurance grows. My path assumes about 3,000 beds by March 2027 and roughly 4,500 by FY30.
- **Price per bed falls.** Revenue per occupied bed was ₹8,300 a day (29 September call). From October, 500 empty beds are offered at ₹3,300–5,100 a day ahead of Ayushman Bharat cover, which slipped from "one to two months" in June to "two to six months". Insured patients were 26% of revenue.
- **Medicines (53% of Q1 revenue, ₹118cr, +47%)** grow faster, mostly through its own website (264,000 orders in Q1). The chemist deal with Entero has been played down because the channel takes 60–65% of the price. Manufacturing moves from seven outside makers to a dedicated plant in Baddi from about November.
- **Margin eases from 41% to 38.5%** as discounted beds, heavier TV advertising (up 25% in Q1) and new centres dilute it. Management promises "EBITDA of more than 40%" and 27–30% net margin. Depreciation and lease interest rise with beds (₹48cr to ₹92cr; ₹14cr to ₹18cr). Tax is 25%.
- **Result: FY30 profit of about ₹400cr, 16% a year.** FY27 at ₹256cr is well below the "minimum ₹300 crores" promised in June, which management did not repeat in August. The note's estimate is 20% and management's is 30%. I haircut because of the missed promises and the 17% Q1 operating-profit growth.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **The runway looks long:** organised Ayurvedic hospital care is small next to thousands of independent clinics, insurers must cover Ayurveda, Ayushman inclusion could fill beds, and beds are cheap to add. But the brand rests on one founder, insurers and the government will set prices, rivals can copy a clinic cheaply, and disease-reversal claims draw regulators' attention. **I expect the market to price about 13% growth for FY31–FY33.**
- **Band and position.** That is the 12–16% band, which pays 18–26 times. The governance flags put it in the lower half, despite net cash and a 64% return on capital.
- **Anchors.** Today's P/E is 20.4. The stock fell 47% in a year from a high of ₹840. The market has already cut the multiple for the same doubts.
- **Exit P/E: 20**, about today's level. I see no reason to expect a rerating until the governance questions clear.

## Returns
Market value today: ₹4,832cr (site, 15 Oct close). New shares: none ("We do not need any dilution", 29 September). Dividend yield: 1.18%.
- **Normal:** ₹400cr × 20 = ₹8,000cr. (8,000 ÷ 4,832)^(1/3.45) − 1 = 15.7%, plus 1.18% = **16.9% a year**.
- **Bad:** beds keep slipping, discounting and Ayushman rates cut the price per bed, and advertising claims draw scrutiny. Revenue grows 10% a year to ₹1,173cr at a 35% margin, for a profit of ₹243cr. P/E drops one band to 15: ₹3,645cr. (3,645 ÷ 4,832)^(1/3.45) − 1 + 1.18% = **−6.7% a year**.
- **If management delivers 30% a year** (about ₹634cr of profit in FY30) at 20 times, the return is about 33% a year. At an exit P/E of 22 on my path it is 20.2%. **The whole case is a bet on believing this founder.**
- **Price that would work:** a 25% annual return on my path needs a share price near ₹308 (₹389 today), or FY30 profit of about ₹522cr, 24% a year.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 4:** ₹92cr of net cash on Screener (about ₹108cr of cash and bank at March, with ₹110cr of investments against ₹127cr of borrowings including leases). The hospitals are leased, so rent is a fixed commitment, and ₹31.6cr is still due on warrants in Chandan Healthcare.
- **Earnings quality 3:** 66% of operating profit came in as cash over the four years Screener shows (₹369cr of ₹556cr), and 72% in FY26 (₹254cr of ₹351cr). Debtor days fell from 66 to 32 after it dropped slow-paying government work. Against that: a GST order alleging suppressed turnover, no edit log in the revenue software from April 2025 to January 2026, and one-off income in Q1.
- **Management 2:** Promises keep missing: beds (2,800–3,000 promised, 2,400 delivered), FY27 profit of ₹300cr dropped, Manali delayed from July to Diwali, Ayushman timing moved. The previous auditor resigned in August 2025. ₹69cr was paid to Oregano Life for five centres (₹58cr of it goodwill), and Oregano now owns 5.7%. The MD's pay may rise from ₹2.4cr to ₹6cr. His wife and daughter are whole-time directors, and the company secretary resigned in July 2026. On the Q1 call the MD said he does "not understand these percentages well".
- **Moat and runway 3:** A known brand and a loop between medicines and hospital stays: ₹66cr of advertising brought in ₹801cr of revenue in FY26, and gross margins are 85–91%. But clinics are cheap to copy, the brand is the founder, and insurers are becoming the price-setters.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **GST order (₹5.08cr including penalty) alleging suppressed turnover, under appeal.** This is in the tail risk and the earnings-quality score. The amount is small; the allegation is not.
- **The previous auditor, KRA & Co, resigned in August 2025, and the revenue software kept no edit log from April 2025 to January 2026.** Tail risk, management 2, and the lower-half exit P/E.
- **₹69cr paid to Oregano Life (₹58cr goodwill), which now owns 5.7%.** In the management score.
- **Promises missed on beds, FY27 profit, Manali and Ayushman timing.** Growth is haircut from the promised 30% to 16% profit growth.
- **Q1 operating profit +17% against revenue +29%; services +11% while in-patients rose 33% (discounting).** The margin path falls from 41% to 38.5%.
- **Q1 profit included ₹7.5cr of one-off income; Q4 FY26 margin was 36% with ₹19–21cr of "one-off" costs.** One-off income removed; the costs are not added back.
- **Beds discounted to ₹3,300–5,100 a day against ₹8,300 of revenue per bed.** Part of the margin path and the bad case.
- **The MD's pay may rise 2.5 times to ₹6cr; it is a family-run board; the brand is the founder.** In management 2 and moat 3.
- **Advertising claims about reversing chronic disease invite regulatory scrutiny.** Tail risk and the bad case.
- **The chemist plan was announced, then played down; OTC strategy keeps changing.** No chemist revenue is assumed.
- **The stock fell 47% in a year.** The exit P/E is held at today's 20, with no rebound assumed.

  - medicines were all made by outside makers until now, with a dedicated Baddi plant starting about a month later;

- Ayushman Bharat cover for Ayurveda is still not live. Web searches found no finalised AYUSH package rates.

- Market value fell from ₹6,518cr at writing to ₹4,832cr (−26%). Screener shows ₹388 on 16 October.
