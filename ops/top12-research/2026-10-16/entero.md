# Entero

## Verdict for the portfolio
Not now. Entero is executing well (5% margin reached in Q1 FY27, organic growth 17.8%). But at 53 times trailing profit the price already assumes almost everything management promises. Profit might roughly treble by FY30, yet the multiple should halve for a thin-margin wholesaler whose five years of profit have not turned into cash. That leaves about 9% a year. It would need FY30 owners' profit near ₹460cr and a 30x exit to reach about 20%.

## Clean profit base
- Screener's "Net Profit" includes the share belonging to minority partners in part-owned subsidiaries. That share was ₹14cr in Q1 FY27 (about 27% of profit) and ₹17cr in Q4 FY26. Profit attributable to Entero's own shareholders is what matters: FY26 about ₹115cr (EPS ₹26.44 × 4.35cr shares); last four quarters about ₹125cr (EPS ₹28.82).
- One-offs: Q4 FY26 carried about ₹11.5cr of one-off costs from closing low-margin businesses (Q1 call). After tax and the minority share that adds back about ₹6cr. Other income is small (₹8cr a year) and stays in.
- Clean trailing owners' profit: about **₹130cr**.
- P/E check: site 53.3, Screener 53.0, mine 52.9 on ₹6,881cr. These agree. (Screener's 40x on profit including minorities would understate it.)
- Hidden claims on the equity: ₹343cr owed to buy out minority partners (₹309cr within a year) on top of ₹515cr of net debt at March. Together they are 2.7 times trailing operating profit.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue (₹cr) | Operating margin | Owners' profit (₹cr) | Drivers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | 6,591 | 4.0% | 115 (clean ~121) | Acquisitions plus organic growth of about 1.5 times the medicines market |
| FY27 | 8,100 (+23%) | 5.0% | ~165 | Guide 23% (11–12% from last year's deals counted for a full year, 11–12% organic); Q1 at 5.0%; interest ~₹20cr a quarter; tax 22.5%; minority ~25% |
| FY28 | 9,550 (+18%) | 5.2% | ~220 | Organic ~18%; MedTech and selling-for-makers deals; minority falls as partners are bought out |
| FY29 | 11,180 (+17%) | 5.4% | ~290 | Operating leverage plus buying scale |
| FY30 | 12,970 (+16%) | 5.6% | **~360** | Management says 20% growth and 6–6.5% by FY30 (29 Sep). I haircut both |

- **Volume × price:** the medicines market grew about 14% in Q1 but 7–10% in most years, mostly on price. Entero has grown 1.4–1.8 times the market, so 16–18% is a reasonable organic path. MedTech is about 15% of FY27 revenue and grows about 20%.
- **Margin:** gross margin was 11.4% in Q1. Management targets 6–6.5% operating margin by FY30 at 20% growth ("directionally" agreeing with an analyst's ₹14,000cr revenue and ₹850–900cr operating profit). I use 5.6% on ₹12,970cr, which gives ₹726cr of operating profit, about 15% below management's figure.
- **Interest, depreciation and tax:** working capital of 60–65 days on ₹6,400cr of extra revenue absorbs about ₹1,100cr, and the partner buy-outs another ₹343cr. Operating cash after tax and interest covers most of it, so net debt drifts up to about ₹650–700cr. I take interest at ₹80–95cr a year and depreciation at ₹50–80cr (reagent-rental machines). Tax is 22.5% in FY27, then 24%.
- **Minority share:** about 25% of profit in FY27 (CFO), falling to about 15% by FY30 as call options are exercised over 2–5 years.
- **Track record:** the new CFO (since April 2025) met every FY26 promise and hit the full-year margin in Q1. He also admitted "we have been guilty of this in the past, and we have been severely punished". Hence the haircut.
- **Result:** FY30 owners' profit of about ₹360cr, about **31% a year** from ₹130cr. Most of that comes from the margin rising from 4% to 5.6%, not from revenue.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway in March 2030:** about 90% of drug distribution is still with small firms, and Entero serves about 1 in 10 of India's pharmacies, so share gains can continue. But by FY30 most of the margin step-up will be done. Profit growth after that tracks revenue: the market (8–10%) times 1.5, so 13–17%.
- **Anchors:** there is no listed Indian peer of the same kind. Entero buys private distributors at 5–7 times operating profit (note). Since listing in February 2024 it has traded at 40–60 times profit (Screener P/E of 53 now). A broker (Arihant, Q1 FY27) has a DCF target of ₹1,789. Its FY28 profit estimate (₹339cr) appears to ignore the minority share, so I do not rely on it.
- **Exit P/E: 26**, at the bottom of the 24–34 band for 16–20% growth (top of the 18–26 band for 12–16%). It sits in the lower half because of weak cash conversion, ₹749cr of goodwill, a 5–6% margin that customers can arbitrage, and subsidiary audit flags. It is far below today's 53, and nothing suggests the market will keep paying 50x for a wholesaler growing 15%.

## Returns
- **Normal:** ₹360cr × 26 = ₹9,360cr in March 2030 against ₹6,881cr today. No dilution and no dividend. ((9,360 ÷ 6,881)^(1/3.45) − 1) = **9.3% a year**.
- **Bad case:** the medicines market falls back to 7–9%, organic growth is 15%, the margin stalls near 5%, and partner buy-outs are paid with debt. FY30 revenue about ₹12,170cr, owners' profit about ₹280cr. Exit P/E 20 (one band down). ₹5,600cr against ₹6,881cr = **−5.8% a year**.
- What it would take to clear 20%: management's own FY30 (about ₹875cr operating profit, owners' profit ~₹460cr) plus a 30x exit gives about 22%. That needs everything right and a still-generous multiple.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 2.** Net debt is ₹515cr (March) plus ₹343cr owed for partner buy-outs (₹309cr within a year). That is about 2.7 times trailing operating profit of ₹313cr. India Ratings rates it A−.
- **Earnings quality: 1.** Over FY22–FY26 operating cash totalled −₹98cr against ₹638cr of operating profit. FY26 was the first positive year (₹96cr). Debtor days rose from 54 to 67, and subsidiary audits flag stock statements to banks that do not match the books.
- **Management: 3.** It delivered every FY26 promise and the 5% margin in Q1, and won't raise guidance until it is sure. Against that: past misses (admitted), a CFO change in April 2025, the institutional-business president's exit (March 2026), and about 15 subsidiaries with cash losses. No pledges.
- **Moat and runway: 3.** It has scale in a fragmented trade (83,000+ products, 138 warehouses, 3,000 makers) and a long runway. But margins are 10–11 paise gross per rupee and chemists split orders across wholesalers daily.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Five-year operating cash −₹98cr against ₹253cr of profit (FY22–FY26).** Earnings quality scores 1, and the exit P/E sits at the bottom of its band.
2. **₹343cr owed to buy out partners, ₹309cr within a year, against FY26 operating cash of ₹96cr.** The profit path funds these with debt, and the extra interest is included. The bad case adds more.
3. **Goodwill ₹749cr; return on all capital about 10% (21% excluding goodwill).** That is why I don't pay a premium multiple. Growth was bought (51 acquisitions).
4. **Subsidiary audits: stock statements to banks don't match the books in most of ~30 subsidiaries; ~15 had cash losses.** Named as the tail risk.
5. **About 25–27% of group profit belongs to minority partners.** The case uses owners' profit only and lets the minority share fall only gradually.
6. **Thin margin (4–5%); a 10% cost rise not passed on would wipe it out.** The bad case holds the margin at 5%.
7. **The medicines market's growth is cyclical (7–16%) and mostly price.** The bad case uses 7–9% market growth.
8. **OrbiMed (a healthcare fund) holds 38% and foreign funds cut from 23% to 4%.** A future fund exit is a supply overhang. It is part of why I don't assume the 53x multiple holds.
9. **AI-generated copies of its calls misstate guidance (25% vs the official 23%).** I use 23%, confirmed by the CFO on 29 Sep.
