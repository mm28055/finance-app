# EPL

## Verdict for the portfolio
Not now for a 25% target, though it is a good business at a fair price. The Indovida merger is the swing factor. It adds about 58% more shares, but Indovida earns about as much profit (₹410cr in 2025) as EPL itself. So today's EPL shareholders end up owning 63.5% of a company earning about twice as much: roughly 28% more profit per share at completion. Built from both businesses, profit per share grows about 12–13% a year to FY30. The market should value the merged company at about 16 times, because a third or more of its profit will come from bottle preforms for Coca-Cola and Pepsi, a plainer business than tubes, controlled by a resin seller. That gives about 19% a year.

## Clean profit base
- Screener (consolidated): FY26 profit ₹394cr; last four quarters ₹393cr.
- One-offs: Q4 FY26 carried merger costs. Management said that quarter's profit "excluding exceptional items" grew 1%, against a reported 11% fall. The tax rate was 18% in FY26 against a 20–22% steady state. Adding back about ₹10–15cr of merger costs and normalising tax to 21% gives a clean trailing EPL profit of about **₹400cr**.
- P/E check: site 18.5, Screener 18.4, mine 18.9 on ₹7,572cr (EPL alone). These agree.
- **What Indovida brings** (from the 30 Mar 2026 merger call):
  - 2025 revenue about ₹3,800cr, operating margin 21.3% (about ₹810cr), profit ₹410cr (10.6% of revenue), return on capital 23.7%.
  - Net cash. Combined debt falls to 0.25 times operating profit from 0.65.
  - 19 plants in nine countries, 90% of revenue in emerging markets.
  - Preforms are 75% of sales, bottles and caps 25%. Customers include Coca-Cola, Pepsi, Nestlé, ThaiBev and Unilever.
  - 5-year volume growth of 8% a year including acquisitions, but 2025 was weaker than 2024 (Thai baht sales and volumes fell, from 0.33 to 0.30 million tonnes).
  - Q2 2026 operating profit was THB 1.3bn (about ₹383cr), up 62%. It looks inflated by the resin-price spike, so I don't extrapolate it.
- **Pro-forma merged base:** about ₹810cr of profit (₹400cr + ₹410cr) on about 50.5cr shares. The market today values that at about ₹11,960cr (₹7,572cr ÷ 63.5%), or about 14.8 times. EPS rises from about ₹12.5 to about ₹16.0.
- **Dilution:** 18.5cr new shares on 32.0cr existing, so d = 0.58. (The swap is 286 EPL shares for 10,000 Indovida India shares; Indorama ends at 51.8%, Blackstone at 16.6%.)

- **EPL alone:** revenue at 11–12% a year after FY27, against a long-term guide of 11–13% (beauty tubes 20%+, oral care high single digits, Thailand ramping). The operating margin stays at 20%. Depreciation rises from ₹405cr to about ₹540cr because capex (₹482cr in FY26) runs above depreciation. Interest is about ₹90–115cr and tax 21%. That gives EPL FY30 profit of about ₹670cr, about 13% a year. Its 5-year history is 10% and 3-year 22% from a low base.
- **Indovida:** 8% a year from the 2025 base (₹410cr), in line with its 5-year volume record and management's "high single digits" steady state, giving about ₹560cr by FY30.
- **Synergies:** management claims $35–50m a year (₹330–480cr of operating profit), mostly from entering each other's countries and formats, which takes years. I include about ₹60cr of profit by FY30, roughly a sixth.
- **Track record:** EPL beat its revenue guide in Q1 FY27 (25% against 11–13%) and fully recovered cost inflation. Profit fell 1.4% on a higher tax rate, and Europe earned 4.8% at the EBIT level against a mid-teens target. The post-COVID episode took two years of margin repair. A new MD took over in January 2026.
- **Result:** merged FY30 profit of about ₹1,290cr, about **13% a year** from the pro-forma ₹810cr. Per share that is about ₹25.5 in FY30 against about ₹12.5 for EPL alone today.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway in March 2030:** tubes grow 11–13% (beauty share 8%, with an aim of 16%). Preforms grow high single digits in emerging Asia and Africa (Tanzania entered, Morocco and Algeria planned). There are also cross-selling and new formats. The market will expect about 10–12% profit growth for FY31–FY33.
- **Anchors:** EPL's year-end P/E was 28.5 (FY22), 22.7 (FY23), 26.6 (FY24), 18.0 (FY25) and 16.9 (FY26) (stockanalysis), with a 5-year median of about 23. It is 18.5 today on EPL alone. In the deal Indovida was valued at 8.1 times operating profit (about 16 times profit) and EPL at 12.5 times. The merged company trades at about 15 times pro-forma profit today.
- **Exit P/E: 16**, in the lower half of the 14–20 band for 8–12% growth. For: an 18% return on capital, AA+ ratings and very low debt. Against: preforms are a plainer product than qualified tubes; a resin maker (Indorama) controls the company and supplies it; customers are concentrated (a few toothpaste giants, Coca-Cola and Pepsi); and Europe is weak. 16 is below EPL's 18.5 today and its 23 median. It is a little above the merged company's implied 14.8, which I allow only because the merger overhang (approval, control change, dividend freeze) clears by Q4 FY27.

## Returns
- **Normal:** ₹1,290cr × 16 = ₹20,640cr in March 2030 against ₹7,572cr × 1.58 = ₹11,964cr today (all shares after the merger). The dividend is suspended until the merger completes, then about 40–60% payout. I average 1.5% a year over the hold. ((20,640 ÷ 11,964)^(1/3.45) − 1) + 1.5% = **18.6% a year**. Per share: about ₹408 in March 2030 against ₹236 today.
- **Bad case:** Indovida's profit stays near the weak 2025 level (about ₹430cr) as beverage volumes in Southeast Asia and Africa stay soft. EPL's Europe doesn't recover and the margin slips to 18.5% (about ₹560cr). No synergies. FY30 merged profit about ₹990cr. Exit P/E 13 (one band down). ₹12,870cr against ₹11,964cr, plus the dividend = **3.6% a year**.
- **Tail risk (separate):** the merger needs tribunal and shareholder approval and could slip or fail. Once completed, Indorama, the world's largest PET resin maker, controls the company and supplies its raw material. Related-party terms that favour the controller would move value away from minority holders.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 4.** Net debt was ₹581cr at June, 0.58 times operating profit, and falls to about 0.25 times with Indovida's net cash. CARE rates it AA+ on watch and India Ratings AA+ on watch with developing implications. Contingent tax disputes are about ₹64cr.
- **Earnings quality: 4.** Operating cash was ₹3,018cr against ₹3,661cr of operating profit over FY22–FY26 (82%). But inventory has risen every year since FY21, to 177 days, the tax rate swings between 14% and 22%, and Indovida converts only 60–65% of operating profit into cash.
- **Management: 3.** It beat its revenue guide and recovered all cost inflation in Q1 FY27. Against that: profit fell 1.4%, Europe is at 4.8%, the MD is new (January 2026, from Unilever, new to packaging), the dividend is suspended, control is passing to the resin supplier, and Blackstone is exiting.
- **Moat and runway: 4.** It is the world's number one in laminated tubes (about 20% globally, a third of oral care), and qualification on customers' lines makes switching slow. The beauty runway is long. Indovida is number one or two in each of its markets but sells a less differentiated product.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **The Indovida merger adds about 58% more shares and hands 51.8% control to Indorama, a resin maker that will also supply it.** Dilution is in the formula (d = 0.58) and Indovida's profit is added. Related-party risk is the tail risk and caps the exit P/E at 16.
2. **Indovida's 2025 sales and volumes fell (0.33 to 0.30 million tonnes); its Q2 2026 jump (+62%) looks crisis-driven.** I grow Indovida from the weak 2025 base at 8%. The bad case keeps it near ₹430cr.
3. **Q1 FY27 profit fell 1.4% on a 22% tax rate; tax swings 14–22%.** I use 21% throughout.
4. **Stock days up every year since FY21 to 177; about ₹180cr more went into stock in Q1.** Earnings quality is 4, not 5. I watch for stock days easing once polymer prices settle.
5. **Europe earned only 4.8% at the EBIT level in Q1 against a mid-teens target.** The base holds the group margin at 20% without a Europe recovery bonus. The bad case takes it to 18.5%.
6. **Depreciation (₹405cr) growing faster than operating profit; capex above depreciation.** Depreciation rises to about ₹540cr by FY30 in the path.
7. **No dividend until the merger completes; the timetable is "not in our hands".** The average yield is cut to 1.5% from a historical 40–60% payout.
8. **Synergy claims of $35–50m a year are unproven.** I include only about ₹60cr of profit.
9. **A new MD since January 2026 who is new to packaging (and a new AMESA head since August 2025).** Part of the management score of 3.
