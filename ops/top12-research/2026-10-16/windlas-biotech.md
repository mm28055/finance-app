# Windlas Biotech

## Verdict for the portfolio
Windlas is the cleanest business of the five: 14 straight record quarters, net cash, and 90% of profit turned into cash. Reported profit should double by FY30 as sales grow about 16% a year and share-option costs fall away. But the margin is a thin 12–14% contract fee, so the market will not pay much more than 22 times. That gives about 15% a year, a good steady holding but short of the 25% target. It would clear 25% only near ₹680 a share (₹924 today).

## Clean profit base
- FY26 reported profit: ₹66cr. Trailing four quarters to June 2026: also ₹66cr, because Q1 FY27 (₹18cr) matched Q1 FY26 (₹18cr). Screener has no June 2026 column yet; the Q1 figure is from the company.
- Nothing removed. Share-option costs (₹16cr in FY26, ₹22cr guided for FY27, ₹12cr for FY28, ₹6cr for FY29) are non-cash but are real pay, so I keep them. On the company's "adjusted" basis, which leaves them out, FY26 profit was ₹83cr.
- Kept in: about ₹18cr a year of treasury income on ₹250cr of net liquidity, about 20% of pre-tax profit. The cash is part of how the company runs (it funded a ₹47cr buyback), so I keep the income and add no separate value for the cash.
- P/E check: site 28.6 (₹1,906cr ÷ ₹66.5cr), Screener 28.4. They match. On adjusted profit it is about 23.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue (₹cr) | Operating margin (after option costs) | Profit (₹cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 actual | 904 | 11.6% | 66 |
| FY27 | 1,060 | 11.4% | 71 |
| FY28 | 1,240 | 12.6% | 93 |
| FY29 | 1,440 | 13.3% | 117 |
| FY30 | 1,670 | 13.5% | 139 |

What drives these numbers:
- **Revenue grows 16–17% a year**, close to its record (16% a year over five years, 21% over three; FY26 +19%; Q1 FY27 +18%). Contract manufacturing grew 29% in Q1 while Indian medicine volumes grew only 3.4%, because brand owners are outsourcing more and the new Schedule M quality rules push work away from small plants. Management gives no guidance.
- **Capacity sets the pace.** Plant 6 opens at the end of Q2 FY27 and takes capacity to about ₹1,100cr of revenue (₹1,000cr in tablets and liquids plus ₹100cr in injectables). Debottlenecking can add 10–15%, and management sees ₹100–150cr more at Plant 6. That covers FY27–FY28. FY29–FY30 needs the next block, which management decides when use passes about 60% and builds in about a year. I assume about ₹100cr of capex in FY28–FY29.
- **Margin before option costs stays at 13.5–13.8%.** It was 13.4% in FY26 and 13.7% in Q1. Contract pricing is cost-plus, so there is no big expansion. Exports at ₹11cr a quarter (+79%) earn more but are small. The codeine ban removed a trade-generics product worth about ₹50–60cr a year, and trade generics fell 32% in Q1.
- **Reported margin rises from 11.4% to 13.5%** only because option costs fall from ₹22cr (FY27) to about ₹5cr. Against FY26 (₹16cr of option costs), **about ₹8cr of the ₹73cr profit increase to FY30 is that fade**, not business growth.
- **Costs below operating profit.** Plant 6 adds about ₹3cr of depreciation a quarter (Q1 call), so depreciation goes from ₹31cr to about ₹58cr. Interest is about ₹5cr, other income ₹16–20cr, and tax 24%.
- **Result: FY30 profit of about ₹139cr**, 20% a year on reported profit and about 15% a year on adjusted profit (₹83cr to about ₹143cr).

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **In March 2030 the market will see a steady contract maker**, with Indian outsourcing still rising (IMARC: Indian formulation contract manufacturing grows 7.4% a year to 2034), exports growing from a small base, injectables filling and step-by-step capacity additions. It also has a thin margin, cost-plus pricing, customers' own plants as a ceiling on fees, and no technology edge. **I expect the market to price about 14% growth for FY31–FY33.**
- **Band and position.** That is the 12–16% band, which pays 18–26 times. Points for the upper half: net cash and 90% cash conversion. Points against: return on equity of only about 12% and a commodity-like fee. Net, mid-band.
- **Anchors.** Today's P/E is 28.6 on reported profit and about 23 on adjusted. Motilal Oswal's peer table showed Windlas at about 27 (6 August). Simply Wall St puts Innova Captab, which grows faster, at about 37.5.
- **Exit P/E: 22.** That is below today's reported P/E, because today's is inflated by temporarily high option costs.

## Returns
Market value today: ₹1,906cr (site, 15 Oct close). New shares: about 1% from the 2025 share-option scheme. Dividend yield: 0.68%.
- **Normal:** ₹139cr × 22 = ₹3,058cr. (3,058 ÷ (1,906 × 1.01))^(1/3.45) − 1 = 14.4%, plus 0.68% = **15.0% a year**.
- **Bad:** trade generics do not recover from codeine, a lower-fee rival takes volume and the next capacity block slips. Revenue grows 11% a year to ₹1,370cr at a 12.5% margin before option costs, for a profit of ₹94cr. P/E drops one band to 16: ₹1,504cr. (1,504 ÷ 1,925)^(1/3.45) − 1 + 0.68% = **−6.2% a year**.
- **Cross-check.** At an exit P/E of 26 (top of the band) the normal case gives 20.5%. Even the top of the band does not reach 25%.
- **Price that would work:** a 25% annual return needs about ₹189cr of FY30 profit at 22 times. That is 27% a year growth, which the model has never shown. On my path, it needs a share price near ₹680.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 5:** ₹235cr of net cash at March 2026 on Screener (₹251cr of net liquidity per the company) even after the ₹47cr buyback. Borrowings are ₹35cr. ICRA rates it A+. Maintenance capex is only ₹12–15cr a year.
- **Earnings quality 4:** 90% of five years' operating profit came in as cash (₹352cr of ₹389cr, FY22–FY26), with no write-offs. But part of that came from stretching suppliers: payable days rose from 76 to 156, funding ₹189cr over five years. That can reverse. The auditor also noted gaps in accounting-software edit logs.
- **Management 3:** It has delivered steadily, with 14 record quarters and a buyback in which promoters did not take part. But one person has been both CEO and CFO since February 2023, despite a promise to hire a CFO, and 55% of the 2025 option grant went to that executive. The independent chairman resigned on 12 October 2026 citing personal reasons. There is no guidance.
- **Moat and runway 3:** Its largest customer is only 6.5% and the top 10 are 32%. It handles 70–80 customer audits a year, and moving a product to another plant needs paperwork, so relationships stick. But the fee is cost-plus and the dosage forms are standard. Runway is steady 15–18% growth, not more.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **Operating margin of 10–13% for eight years (11.6% reported in FY26).** No margin expansion is assumed beyond the option-cost fade, and the exit P/E is mid-band.
- **Option costs of ₹22cr in FY27 (about a fifth of pre-tax profit before them), with 55% of the latest grant to one executive.** Kept as a real cost, and in the management score.
- **One person is both CEO and CFO, and the promised separate CFO has not been named.** In the management score.
- **The independent chairman resigned on 12 October 2026.** In the management score. Watch for any later explanation.
- **Trade generics fell 32% in Q1 after the codeine ban, which removed about ₹50–60cr a year of sales.** Part of the bad case. The base assumes slow refilling.
- **The negative cash cycle rests on 156 payable days.** This keeps earnings quality at 4, not 5.
- **Plant 6 capacity (about ₹1,100–1,250cr) is full by about FY29.** About ₹100cr of capex and its depreciation are in the path.
- **Return on equity is about 12%.** This caps the exit P/E.
- **Uttarakhand raised the minimum wage backdated, and staff costs rose 17%.** It is in the flat margin.
- **The auditor noted gaps in accounting-software edit logs.** Noted; this is common across the sector.
