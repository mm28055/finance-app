# Atlanta Electricals

## Verdict for the portfolio
A well-run, fast-growing transformer maker. But the share price already pays for growth that runs well past 2030. Even if profit grows about 27% a year to ₹533cr in FY30, the market is unlikely to pay today's 61 times profit in a business whose industry capacity is doubling. At an exit P/E of 27, a buyer today earns about 2% a year. **Not now.** It would start to fit below about ₹1,000 a share (20% a year) and ₹870 (25%).

**Where it sits among the five power-capex names (Atlanta, TD Power, Transrail, Dynamic, Pitti):** it has the fastest profit growth I expect (27–28% a year), the best returns on capital (45%) and a net-cash balance sheet. It is also the second most expensive at 61 times profit, behind only TD Power at 87. Its base return of about 2% a year ranks fourth of the five, ahead of TD Power (−9%) and behind Dynamic (17%), Transrail (15%) and Pitti (10.5%).

## Clean profit base
- **Reported:** FY26 profit ₹202cr. Profit over the last four quarters (to June 2026) is ₹217cr (Screener, consolidated).
- **What I removed:** about ₹9cr in all. That is about ₹5cr of the ₹14cr FY26 other income (mostly interest on IPO cash that has since been spent), plus about ₹5cr of Q4 FY26 profit from an unusually low 22% tax rate (normal is 25–27%). **Clean base: ₹208cr, last four quarters.**
- **Not removed but noted:** interest was ₹57cr in FY26. The ₹340cr of term loans behind it were repaid by March 2026, and Q1 FY27 interest was only ₹6cr. That lift to future profit belongs in the forecast, not the base.
- **P/E check:** the site shows 60.3 (15 Oct). Screener shows 60.8 (₹13,269cr ÷ ₹217cr). On the clean base it is 63.8. They agree.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue | Operating margin | Profit after tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | ₹1,852cr | 18.6% | ₹202cr |
| FY27 | ₹2,600cr | 17.5% | ₹293cr |
| FY28 | ₹3,400cr | 17.5% | ₹380cr |
| FY29 | ₹4,100cr | 17.0% | ₹449cr |
| FY30 | ₹4,800cr | 17.0% | ₹533cr |

**Drivers.**
- **FY27, revenue +40% as guided.** Q1 was ₹466cr. Management expects about ₹2,400cr of the ₹3,117cr order book to be executed in the rest of the year (Q1 FY27 call). That gives ₹2,870cr of headroom against a ₹2,600cr guide, so I take the guide.
- **FY28.** Bloomberg consensus (via PL, 24 Aug) has ₹3,369cr of revenue and ₹419cr of profit. Management's "40% a year for three years" from the IPO implies about ₹3,400cr. I take ₹3,400cr of revenue but a lower profit than consensus (₹380cr against ₹419cr). Consensus assumes a 19.8% margin; management guides only 17–18%.
- **Capacity is not the limit before FY30.** Installed capacity is 63,060 MVA. FY26 output was 22,943 MVA, and Vadod Unit 4 (30,000 MVA) ran at only 39% in its first seven months. Management targets 65% at Vadod in FY27 and 100% in FY28. Ankhi (about 16,000 MVA, built for up to 765 kV) and the 5,000 MVA solar-transformer plant (production from Q4 FY27) add more.
- **Volume growth slows from FY29.** The 400 kV and 765 kV units sell for about ₹5–5.5 lakh per MVA, against ₹7–8 lakh at 220 kV (PL). So revenue grows more slowly than MVA once the mix shifts up.
- **Margin.** FY27–FY28 sit at the guided 17.5%. FY29–FY30 drop to 17%. Industry capacity roughly doubles from about 375 GVA to 690 GVA by FY28, and Chinese makers can now bid in public tenders. The 400/765 kV mix (management says about 2 points richer) is partly eaten by a 2–4% royalty on 765 kV.
- **Below the operating line.** Interest is ₹30–45cr: working capital rises to 80–90 days of sales, and about ₹130cr of debt funds the tank plant. Depreciation goes from ₹42cr to ₹65cr (the tank plant, the solar-transformer plant and upkeep). Tax is 25.5%.
- **Track record.** FY26 revenue grew 49% against a 40% plan, and term debt was repaid as promised. But the 400 kV test and the 765 kV deal have each slipped a quarter, and the margin guide eased. So I take the revenue guide as given and haircut the margin below consensus.
- **Result:** FY30 profit is ₹533cr, about 28% a year from the clean base (27.5% a year from FY26).

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway in March 2030.**
  - Transmission spending stays heavy to 2032: ₹9.15 lakh crore under the National Electricity Plan, and 765 kV substation capacity roughly triples by FY32.
  - Atlanta should then be a 400 kV supplier with a first 765 kV record.
  - Exports are targeted at about 15% of sales by FY28.
  - Battery-storage and solar transformers add demand.
- **Against that.**
  - By 2030 the industry will have about 690 GVA of capacity, against CEA's FY27 need of 158,339 MVA (220 kV and above).
  - The cycle will be four years older.
  - Atlanta's own record shows profit fell from ₹87cr (FY23) to ₹64cr (FY24) in a softer year.
  - So I expect the market to price **about 16% a year growth after FY30**.
- **Exit P/E: 27, inside the 24–34 band for 16–20% growth.** Lower half because only 48% of five years' operating profit turned into cash, it depends on government order cycles, and there are promoter-linked dealings. Not the bottom of the band, because returns on capital are 45% and there is net cash.
- **Peers today (Screener, 16 Oct):**
  - Indian transformer makers: TARIL 33, Voltamp 34, Shilchar 31, Supreme Power 35.
  - Multinational premium names: Hitachi Energy India 108, GE Vernova T&D 75.
  - Atlanta's own P/E since its September 2025 listing: 38–96, median 62. That is a one-year boom history, not a cycle.
- I am not assuming the P/E stays near 60. That would require the market in 2030 to expect more than 25% growth for years after FY30, with capacity doubling. That is not a base case.

## Returns
Market value used: **₹13,269cr (Screener, 16 Oct)**. No dividend. New shares: none assumed in the base. See the public-float issue under worst facts: promoters must cut their 87.3% holding to 75%. I assume they do it by selling their own shares, not by issuing new ones.

- **Normal case:** ₹533cr × 27 = ₹14,391cr in March 2030. ₹14,391 ÷ ₹13,269 = 1.085. 1.085^(1/3.45) − 1 = **2.4% a year**.
  - If the 75% cap were met by issuing new shares instead (about 16% more shares, with the cash cutting interest), the return falls to about −0.6% a year.
  - Even at an exit P/E of 35 it is only 10.4% a year.
- **Bad case** (capacity glut hits 220 kV pricing as Chinese and new Indian capacity bids):
  - FY30 revenue ₹4,000cr at a 14.5% margin. Profit before tax ₹485cr, profit ₹361cr.
  - Exit P/E one band down: 21 (18–26 band).
  - ₹361cr × 21 = ₹7,581cr. ₹7,581 ÷ ₹13,269 = 0.571. 0.571^(1/3.45) − 1 = **−15.0% a year**.
- **Tail risk (not in the bad case):** the stock-statement mismatch with banks or the Amod Stampings dealings turning into a governance finding (a lender, auditor or SEBI action).
- **Price that works:** ₹7,672cr market value (about ₹998 a share) for 20% a year; ₹6,664cr (about ₹867) for 25% a year.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 4/5.** Net cash of ₹49cr at March 2026. All ₹340cr of term loans repaid. CRISIL A/Stable. Not a 5: the ₹170–180cr tank plant is likely debt-funded (CRISIL), working capital is heading to 80–90 days, and ₹1,320cr of bank limits rest on a promoter LLP guarantee.
- **Earnings quality 3/5.** Five-year operating cash of ₹408cr against ₹845cr of operating profit (48%). Debtor days improved from 145 to 84, but inventory days rose from 89 to 102 and supplier credit shrank from 192 days to 112. No one-offs of size.
- **Management 3/5.**
  - Delivered: FY26 revenue +49% against a 40% plan, and debt repaid early.
  - Slipped: the 400 kV short-circuit test was "June–July" in May, became "this quarter end" in July, and was still not announced by 16 Oct. The 765 kV tie-up was "next couple of months" in May and "close in Q2" in July, and is still not signed as far as I could find.
  - On the Q1 call the CFO said growth had "no material changes in pricing", yet revenue per MVA rose about 22%.
  - Governance: ₹114.6cr bought from promoter-influenced Amod Stampings in FY26, and bank stock statements ₹44.5–68.1cr above the books each quarter.
- **Moat and runway 3/5.** Power Grid's 400 kV approval and price-variation clauses on about 75% of the book are real. But the 765 kV design is licensed (a $3–5m fee plus a 2–4% royalty for 3–4 years), the whole industry is adding capacity at 132–220 kV, and Chinese makers can now bid in public tenders.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
| Worst fact | How the case deals with it |
|---|---|
| Only 48% of five years' operating profit turned into cash | Exit P/E in the lower half of its band; working-capital interest rises to ₹45cr in the forecast |
| ₹114.6cr of purchases from promoter-influenced Amod Stampings (plus ₹39.5cr of sales to it) | Management scored 3; named as a tail risk; a "breaks it" trigger if it grows |
| Bank stock statements ₹44.5–68.1cr above the books each quarter | Tail risk; management 3 |
| 400 kV test and 765 kV tie-up each a quarter late, neither announced by 16 Oct | No 765 kV revenue before FY29 in the forecast; margin held at 17%, not lifted |
| Margin guide eased to 17–18% after an 18.6% year; Q1 gross margin fell from 29.7% to 27.3% in a quarter | I use 17–17.5%, below consensus at 19–20% |
| About half of Q1 growth was price per MVA (copper pass-through), not volume | Revenue path assumes slower growth from FY29 as price effects fade |
| Promoters own 87.3% and must reach 25% public holding within three years of listing (about Sep 2028, by my reading of the rule) | Base assumes a promoter share sale (an overhang on the price); if done with new shares, the return falls to about −0.6% |
| Industry capacity roughly doubles by FY28; Chinese makers now allowed into public tenders | Margin falls to 17% in FY29–FY30; bad case 14.5% |
| 765 kV technology rented, with a royalty | Margin not lifted for the mix |
| Profit fell 26% in FY24: a cyclical history | Exit P/E on a non-peak 17% margin, not on FY26's 18.6% |
| No exports and no data-centre orders yet | No export revenue before FY28 in the forecast |
| P/E of 61 against 31–35 for listed Indian peers | The main reason for "Not now" |
