# South West Pinnacle

## Verdict for the portfolio
A small exploration driller with a record order book and a habit of beating its own guidance. But profit turns into cash poorly, two customers drive most of the work, and the promoters sold shares in September. At 16 times trailing profit the base case returns about 17% a year. The coal block is the swing factor and is unfunded and years away. **Not now**: keep following until operating cash catches up with profit.

## Clean profit base
- **Profit is clean.** FY26 profit was ₹33cr; the four quarters to June 2026 total ₹40cr (₹8.4cr + ₹9.2cr + ₹13.1cr + ₹9.3cr). Screener shows no exceptional items, tax is a normal 22–27%, and other income is small (₹5–6cr a year, partly the Oman joint-venture share of about ₹1.3cr a quarter). Nothing removed.
- **Use Screener's market value.** Screener puts it at **₹656cr** (₹201 × about 3.26cr shares after the warrants were converted in July). The site's ₹592cr uses the older 2.98cr share count. True P/E is **16.4** (₹656cr ÷ ₹40cr), not the site's 14.8.

## Profit path FY27–FY30

| Year | Revenue | Operating margin | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 | ₹243cr | 24% | ₹33cr |
| FY27 | ₹315cr | 24.5% | ₹46cr |
| FY28 | ₹365cr | 24% | ₹52cr |
| FY29 | ₹430cr | 24.5% | ₹60cr |
| FY30 | ₹530cr (incl. ~₹40cr first coal) | 25% | ₹70cr |

**Order book and the two big contracts.** The order book is ₹761cr at June 2026, 3.1 times FY26 revenue. About 60% of it is two contracts:
- **Hindustan Zinc:** ₹307cr including tax over four years, so about ₹65cr a year of revenue. It started in June and takes about three months to reach full speed.
- **Reliance coal-bed methane extension:** ₹166.8cr over an estimated 15 months, so about ₹130cr a year. Management expects Reliance to be 35–40% of FY27 revenue. The extension ends around late FY28 unless Reliance takes up the further extension the contract allows.

**Guidance and track record.** Management guides about 20% revenue growth and calls it conservative. It grew 35% in FY26 and 53% in Q1 FY27, so it has beaten guidance for two years. The monsoon makes H1 weak.

**My path:**
- **FY27:** revenue +30%. Q1 was ₹62cr; H2 is normally the strongest half.
- **FY28:** slows to +16% as the Reliance extension rolls off mid-year.
- **FY29–FY30:** about 15–18% a year as mining auctions, about 200 coal blocks and 500 mineral blocks, keep exploration busy for the "three to five years" management sees.

**Margin.** 24–25% (FY26 24%, Q1 24.2%) is inside management's 22–25% band. The chairman partly attributes it to "a huge shortage of resources", which is cyclical, so I do not push it higher.

**Costs below operating profit.** Depreciation rises as rigs are added (four were on order in May). Interest rises from FY29 as coal-block debt arrives. Tax is 24%.

**Coal block (Jharkhand).** Exploration is done and the geological report is being prepared. Mining plan, environmental and forest clearances still lie ahead, and first revenue is "FY 2028-29". Phase 1 costs about ₹200cr, part of it bank guarantees rather than cash. Management says full output gives about ₹300cr of revenue a year at about 46% EBITDA, before a 24.25% revenue share to the government. The CMD himself says Indian coal blocks take "five to six years, seven years on average" to start. I include only about ₹40cr of FY30 revenue and a small profit.

**Growth rate.** FY30 profit is **₹70cr: about 21% a year from FY26 (₹33cr), or about 16% a year from the trailing ₹40cr**.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **What March 2030 will look at:** the services business growing 10–12% with the exploration cycle, plus the coal block if it is producing.
- **Coal block at full output:** about ₹300cr of revenue could add roughly ₹50–60cr of profit after interest and depreciation, near-doubling profit by FY32–33. But it is unfunded today: ₹200–400cr against about ₹230cr of equity including warrant money. It has also not yet cleared the slow approval steps, and the company has never run a mine.
- **Base expectation for growth after FY30: about 12%**, with coal as upside.
- **Anchors:**
  - Over the last year the price ranged from ₹122 to ₹288 (Screener). On today's trailing earnings of ₹13.4 a share, that is 9–21 times.
  - It is at 16.4 today.
  - The site's oil note judged it "fits" at about 11% a year needed. I take no peer multiple I could not verify.
- **Exit P/E: 16**, the middle of the 14–20 band for 8–12% growth. It sits in the lower part of what a 12–16% grower could earn because of:
  - weak cash: 44% over five years;
  - three customers making up 72% of revenue;
  - promoter selling;
  - its small size.
  It is not above today's 16.4 because the reason for a re-rating, a producing coal mine, will probably not be proven by March 2030.

## Returns
- **Normal case:** ₹70cr × 16 = ₹1,120cr against ₹656cr today (Screener 16 Oct). No dilution assumed: warrants are already converted, and management says the coal block will use internal cash, bank debt and buyer advances. Dividend 0%.
  ((70 × 16) ÷ 656)^(1/3.45) − 1 = **16.8% a year**.
- **Bad case:** Reliance does not renew after the 15-month extension, government tenders are won on price and the margin slips back to the 19–20% of FY24–FY25, and the coal block is delayed past FY30 and needs fresh shares. FY30 profit ₹45cr, exit P/E 11. ((45 × 11) ÷ 656)^(1/3.45) − 1 = **−7.8% a year**.
- **Tail risk:** a large equity raise or guarantee call to fund a coal block that slips, on a balance sheet of about ₹230cr.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 3.** Net debt was about ₹68cr at March (₹80cr of borrowings), about one year of operating profit, and lower after ₹28cr of warrant money. The CFO's "around ₹15cr" of debt does not fit the 0.39 debt-to-equity he also quoted. The ₹200–400cr coal block hangs over it, and CRISIL called its funding "not finalised".
- **Earnings quality: 2.** Only 44% of five-year operating profit came in as cash (₹72cr against ₹163cr). FY26 was ₹12cr against ₹33cr of profit, debtor days are 175, and revenue is booked before billing, which the auditor made a key audit matter.
- **Management: 3.** It beat its ~20% growth guide two years running and won its largest-ever orders. But the promoters sold 2.5% (₹18.5cr at about ₹225) in September, weeks after saying they were "still thinking" about dilution, and the chairman sits personally on the board of JV partner Alara, into whose rights issue the company is putting money.
- **Moat and runway: 2.** Rigs are expensive and private clients value its record, but anyone with capital can buy rigs. Government work is won on lowest price, and the top three customers were 72% of FY26 revenue (48% in FY25).

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Operating cash ₹12cr against ₹33cr of FY26 profit; 44% over five years.** Exit P/E held to 16; earnings-quality score 2; first check in the next results.
2. **Top three customers 72% of FY26 revenue; Hindustan Zinc and Reliance about 60% of the order book.** The FY28 slowdown in the path covers Reliance's extension ending.
3. **Coal block ₹200cr (phase 1) to ₹400cr against about ₹230cr of equity; funding not finalised (CRISIL).** Only a token contribution by FY30; bad case adds dilution.
4. **24.25% of coal revenue goes to the government, and coal is priced off a ₹3,100 a tonne index.** Limits the coal upside.
5. **Promoters sold 2.5% at about ₹225 in September; promoter holding down from 74.7% (Dec 2023) to 61.7% (Sep 2026).** Management score 3; a reason not to pay up.
6. **Chairman is a director of, and personally holds shares in, Alara Resources; the company is putting up to A$1.5m into Alara's rights issue.** Watched as a related party.
7. **CFO's debt figure (₹15cr) does not match the 0.39 debt-to-equity he gave.** Balance-sheet score held at 3.
8. **Revenue booked by stage of completion before billing (key audit matter); inventory about 500 days on Screener.** Earnings-quality score 2.
9. **Small subsidiary with a going-concern doubt (auditor).** Minor; noted.
10. **The margin rise is partly a cyclical "shortage of resources".** Margin not pushed above 25%.
11. **Oman JV cash not yet sent back to India; the company has lent to the JV.** No JV cash assumed.
