# Syrma SGS Tech.

## Verdict for the portfolio
Syrma is a good, broad electronics maker that should grow profit about 27% a year to roughly ₹770cr by FY30. Its circuit-board plant adds a second leg from FY28. But at ₹31,595cr (85 times trailing profit), the price already assumes the market will still pay about 89 times profit in 2030. On an exit P/E of 28 the base case loses about 10% a year. **Not now.** It would clear 25% at a market value near ₹10,000cr.

## Clean profit base
- **Reported:** profit after tax for the year to June 2026 was ₹402cr including minority holders, or about **₹367cr to Syrma's shareholders**. That is trailing EPS ₹19.10 × 19.3cr shares. Elcome (60% owned), the medical-device arm and the Elemaster, Kaga and circuit-board JVs all have outside partners.
- **Removed:**
  - The electronics production incentive (PLI) ended in March 2026. The net incentive was ₹38cr in FY26, of which about ₹28cr falls in this window. Removing it takes about ₹21cr off after tax.
  - I kept treasury income (₹41cr of other income on about ₹800cr of cash). It will shrink as cash goes into the circuit-board plant, and the path reflects that.
- **Clean base: about ₹347cr. FY26 on the same basis: about ₹290cr.**
- **P/E check:** the site shows 87.6 and Screener 85.1. Screener divides by attributable EPS, so the two agree. On the clean base it is about 91.

## Profit path FY27–FY30

|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | ₹4,819cr | 11.3% (about 10.5% without PLI) | ~₹317cr (₹290cr ex-PLI) |
| FY27 | ₹6,554cr | 10.6% | ₹406cr |

| FY29 | ₹10,402cr | 11.0% | ₹622cr |
| FY30 | ₹12,483cr | 11.2% | ₹768cr |

What drives the numbers:
- **Revenue guidance (July call):** 35%-plus growth in FY27, "confident of exceeding", and 30–35% a year for two to three years.
  - The order book is ₹6,770cr, of which ₹5,400cr is due within 12 months, and Q1 order intake was ₹1,970cr.
  - 18 new clients joined in Q1. The 32 added in FY26 have potential of ₹1,000cr-plus a year.
- **My revenue path:** 36% for FY27, then 28% (including about ₹250cr from the circuit-board plant in its first year), 24% and 20%. That is below management's range after FY28 because the base gets large.
- **Margin:**
  - Guidance is 10.5–11%, and Q1 came in at 10.2%. The FY26 figure of 11.3% included the incentive.
  - Mix helps: own designs are 17% of sales (target 25%) and exports are 24%. The circuit-board plant earns under 10% in year one but 15–18% at full use.
  - Consumer work (30–34% of sales) and pass-through of dearer parts hold the margin down. I let it rise slowly to 11.2%.
- **Capital spending:**
  - Circuit boards: phase 1 is ₹400cr and phases 2–3 another ₹400cr, with a 50% capex subsidy claimable.
  - Flexible boards and laminate: ₹800cr over FY28–FY30.
  - Organic: ₹100–150cr a year.
  - Effect: depreciation rises from ₹86cr to about ₹225cr and interest from ₹47cr to about ₹100cr. Tax is 24%, and minority holders take about 8% of profit.
- **Track record:**
  - FY26 beat its targets: operating profit ₹545cr against ₹400cr (₹490cr without Elcome), and exports ₹1,200cr against ₹1,100cr.
  - Q1 FY27 missed two May promises: margin below the band, and working capital up from 63 to 71 days instead of down.
  - FY24 margin collapsed to 6% and operating cash was negative for three years to FY24.
  - Net: mixed, so I do not take the top of their range.
- **Growth rate:** about 27–28% a year from FY26 (ex-incentive) to FY30, in line with the site's ~28%.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**What the market will see in March 2030:**
- **For growth:**
  - India's electronics manufacturing should still be growing fast.
  - Syrma will own circuit-board and laminate plants with subsidy support, and own designs aiming at 25% of sales.
  - Exports will be larger: it holds only 1–2% of the spend at global customers, aiming for 4–6%.
- **Against:**
  - The company will be a ₹12,000cr-plus business.
  - About 30% of sales is low-margin consumer work re-bid often.
  - Bigger rivals are arriving: Dixon, Tata, L&T, Foxconn and Jabil.
  - It still needs heavy capex and supplier credit (it pays in about 199 days).

**My call:** I expect the market to want about 17% profit growth for FY31–FY33. That band (16–20%) pays 24–34 times profit. I use **28**:
- **Up:** little net debt and many customers.
- **Down:** only 20% of FY22–FY26 operating profit became operating cash, and consumer work is price-driven.

**Peers:** Cyient DLM 82, Kaynes 63 and Avalon 101 today (Screener). Syrma's own P/E has run from roughly 50 to 100 since its 2022 listing (my estimate from Screener prices and EPS). These multiples price 30%-plus growth that will be fading by 2030.

## Returns
Market value ₹31,595cr (Screener, 16 Oct). Dividend yield 0.09%. I assume no new shares: the ₹1,000cr share-sale approval is renewed every year "with no immediate need". If it were used at today's price, it would add about 3% more shares.
- **Normal:**
  - FY30 profit ₹768cr × P/E 28 = ₹21,504cr.
  - (21,504 ÷ 31,595)^(1/3.45) − 1 + 0.09% = **−10.5% a year**.
- **Bad:** the margin stays near 10% (consumer mix, parts inflation) and growth slows to 30%, 20%, 18% and 16%.
  - FY30 profit is ₹524cr.
  - P/E falls one band to 22: ₹11,528cr.
  - Return: **−25.3% a year**.
- **What it would take to earn 25% a year:** a market value of about ₹10,000cr today, or a P/E of about 89 in 2030.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 4.** The company reported net cash of ₹122cr in June 2026: debt of ₹686cr against ₹808cr of cash and investments. It borrowed ₹340cr in Q1 to stock parts. Contingent claims are about ₹5cr, goodwill is ₹455cr, and the rating is AA.
- **Earnings quality: 2.** Operating cash was ₹274cr against ₹1,393cr of operating profit over FY22–FY26 (20%), with three negative years to FY24. FY26 improved to ₹290cr (53%). Suppliers fund much of the working capital (199 payable days), and FY26 profit included a ₹38cr incentive that has now ended.
- **Management: 3.**
  - FY26 beat on operating profit, exports and cash.
  - Q1 FY27 missed on margin and working capital.
  - The CEO changed in June 2026.
  - Promoters fell from 47% to 42%, through the FY26 share sale (QIP).
  - The executive chairman's pay is ₹3cr plus 1% of pre-tax profit.
  - The audit is clean.
- **Moat and runway: 3.** It has many customers (largest 13%) and a growing design business. Moving a product to it takes 12–30 months. But consumer and much contract work are price-driven, and scale protects share more than margin.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Weak cash history:** 20% five-year conversion, and three negative operating-cash years. This sets the exit P/E in the lower-middle of its band (28) and the earnings-quality score of 2.
2. **Q1 FY27 missed margin and working-capital promises:** margin 10.2% against 10.5–11%, and working capital 71 days against a promised fall from 63. The base path holds the margin at only 10.6% for FY27.
3. **Borrowed ₹340cr in one quarter to stock parts:** net cash fell from ₹467cr to ₹122cr. This is covered by interest rising to about ₹100cr by FY30. If parts prices fall, the stock could need writing down, which is part of the bad case.
4. **Supplier credit of about 199 days:** if suppliers of scarce parts demand faster payment, more debt or equity is needed. I flag a possible use of the ₹1,000cr share-sale approval, which would cost about 0.9 points a year of return.
5. **Incentive income gone:** I removed about ₹21cr after tax from the base.
6. **Heavy new capex at low early returns:** the circuit-board plant turns its cost over only about 1.5 times a year and earns under 10% in year one. Depreciation and interest in the path rise accordingly.
7. **Promoter dilution and pay:** promoters went from 47% to 42%, and the chairman takes 1% of pre-tax profit. Both are in the management score.
8. **About 30% of sales is low-margin consumer work:** the reason the margin path stays near 11%.
9. **Price of 85 times trailing profit:** the deciding fact.
