# Rubicon Research

## Verdict for the portfolio
Rubicon is the best product-picker in Indian generics. Even if profit triples by FY30, about 32% a year, the price leaves almost nothing: at 85 times profit, the shares need the P/E to stay near today's level for the owner to earn anything. On a fair exit P/E of 30 the normal case returns about −3% a year. The bad case, with US tariffs or price cuts, loses about 22% a year. It would only make sense near ₹616 a share against ₹1,477.

## Clean profit base
- FY26 reported profit: ₹247cr. Trailing four quarters to June 2026: ₹288cr (Screener).
- One-off removed: an insurance claim for goods lost in transit, booked in Q1 FY27 other income (CFO, Q1 call). Other income was ₹6cr against ₹1–4cr in earlier quarters. I remove about ₹3cr before tax, or ₹2cr after. **Clean trailing profit is about ₹286cr.**
- No treasury income worth adjusting: other income is only ₹13cr a year.
- P/E check: site 84.8 (₹24,451cr ÷ ₹288cr), Screener 87.5 on the 16 October price. They match. On clean profit it is about 85.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue (₹cr) | Operating margin | Profit (₹cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 actual | 1,754 | 22.8% | 247 |
| FY27 | 2,300 | 23% | 334 |
| FY28 | 2,920 | 24% | 451 |
| FY29 | 3,620 | 24.5% | 583 |
| FY30 | 4,420 | 25% | 741 |

What drives these numbers:
- **Revenue grows 26% a year, slowing from 31% in FY27 to 22% in FY30.** Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹534cr, up 52%. That includes ₹12cr from the newly bought Indian brands business Arinna and a weaker rupee. In dollars, US sales were $55m, up 32%, and management says Q2 "is tracking strongly". Research spend sets the pace: ₹500cr over the nine quarters to Q1 FY28. Management measures about 5–6 times research spend in extra revenue three years later. There were 24 filings under FDA review at March, and 88% of approvals turn into launches.
- **FY27 matches the run-rate.** Q1 profit was ₹85cr. Four times that is ₹340cr, and I use ₹334cr because of new share-option costs, Arinna start-up costs and pre-opening costs at the two new plants. Management folded all of these into its "at least 23%" margin guide.
- **Margin rises from 23% to 25%.** Pithampur starts in Q1 CY2027 and New Jersey later in 2027. Both replace outside contract makers, which management calls "far more efficient" without giving a number. Specialty products gave 36% of gross profit in Q1. The rise is held back by research at 10–11% of revenue and by Arinna earning about 5% on its price.
- **Costs below operating profit.** Depreciation rises from ₹45cr to about ₹110cr, with ₹300cr of capex over two years plus the new plants. Interest stays at ₹30–40cr, because some debt and receivable factoring remain. Tax is about 24%.
- **Track record is excellent.** It beat its margin guide (24.2% against 22–23%) and raised it. It cleared FDA inspections at every plant it bought on the first try. I do not haircut growth further. The risk is in the price, not the plan.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **In March 2030 the business will be about 2.5 times today's size.** Growth should still be strong: research is kept at 10–11% of a bigger revenue base, there is room to build at Pithampur (30 acres, 5–6 in use), US government contracts open up through the New Jersey plant, and the Indian brain-and-nerve brands business is small today. Against that, a bigger base, US generic buyers who set prices, product-by-product moats that end when rivals arrive, and 99% US exposure. **I expect the market to price about 18% growth for FY31–FY33.**
- **Band and position.** That is the 16–20% band, which pays 24–34 times. Points against the upper half: weak cash conversion (31% over five years), five customers buying about 72%, and one-country and tariff risk. Points for it: high return on capital (28% on Screener, 36% company-stated) and a visible research pipeline.
- **Anchors.** The pharma sector average P/E is about 38 (Univest's sector figure). Rubicon has only traded since October 2025, always at a large premium: it listed at about ₹600 and trades at ₹1,477, so it has no five-year P/E history to lean on.
- **Exit P/E: 30**, the middle of the band. That is already generous for a US generics maker. Today's 85 assumes 30%+ growth lasts well beyond FY30, and I cannot justify that.

## Returns
Market value today: ₹24,451cr (site, 15 Oct close). New shares: about 1% from the new share-option scheme approved in 2026. Dividend yield: 0.1%.
- **Normal:** ₹741cr × 30 = ₹22,230cr. (22,230 ÷ (24,451 × 1.01))^(1/3.45) − 1 = −3.0%, plus 0.1% = **−2.9% a year**.
- **Bad:** The US puts tariffs on generics after the review due around April 2027, or prices fall, so revenue grows 18% a year to ₹3,400cr at a 22% margin. Profit is ₹473cr. P/E drops one band to 22: ₹10,406cr. (10,406 ÷ 24,695)^(1/3.45) − 1 + 0.1% = **−22.1% a year**.
- **Cross-check.** Even at an exit P/E of 42 (top of the 20–25% band), the normal case gives only 7.0%.
- **Price that would work:** a 25% annual return needs about ₹1,780cr of FY30 profit at 30 times, 2.4 times my path. On my path, it needs a share price near ₹616.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 4:** Close to zero net debt: about ₹86cr at June 2026 after paying about ₹173cr for Arinna, against ₹35cr of net cash at March on Screener. Borrowings are ₹311cr against about ₹520cr of yearly operating profit. About a third of receivables that can be factored are sold to banks.
- **Earnings quality 2:** Only 31% of five years' operating profit came in as operating cash (₹247cr of ₹798cr, FY22–FY26), and 47% over the last three years. Q1 FY27 operating cash was ₹28.5cr on ₹129cr of operating profit, blamed on late GST refunds. Inventory is 550 days. Factoring flatters the cash figures.
- **Management 3:** Delivery is strong: guidance beaten and raised, inspections cleared, and plants and New Jersey bought cheaply. But the private-equity promoter General Atlantic sold 8.4% (₹2,300cr at ₹1,660) in August and still holds 27.45%. Disclosure was cut: the filings-under-review count is now given only yearly and products are not discussed. The CFO moved to a commercial role in August. An analyst's question on material costs against sales growth went unanswered.
- **Moat and runway 4:** It turns 88% of approvals into launches. It is first in US volume in metoprolol tartrate (48%) and three other molecules, and four of its six showcase products gained rank and share. Its moats are real but narrow, product by product, and it depends on one country.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **31% five-year cash conversion; ₹28.5cr of operating cash in Q1 FY27.** Earnings quality is 2, the exit P/E is held mid-band, and H1 cash flow is the first check.
- **550 inventory days and 125–130 working-capital days guided; a third of receivables factored.** As above. Growth keeps absorbing cash.
- **99% US revenue, and five customers buy about 72%.** This sets the lower half of the case. The bad case is built on US prices or tariffs.
- **The US tariff exemption for generics is reviewed around April 2027.** This is the bad-case trigger.
- **General Atlantic sold 8.4% in August 2026 and still owns 27.45%, so more selling is likely.** This is in the management score. A further sale could keep the price capped.
- **P/E of 85.** This is the main reason the return is near zero even on 32% profit growth.
- **It relies on outside contract makers until Pithampur and New Jersey start in 2027, and gross margin dipped in late FY26.** The path needs only 25% margin by FY30.
- **The FDA review count is now given once a year, with no product detail.** In the management score.
- **Arinna cost about ₹173cr, partly borrowed, for about 5% return on price.** It is small, and it holds the margin back in the path.
- **The new share-option scheme adds cost and about 1% dilution.** Included (d = 0.01).
- **FDA observations: 2 at Pithampur (July), 6 at New Jersey before purchase (now cleared), 1 at Satara (October).** Each was procedural. A warning letter is the tail risk.
