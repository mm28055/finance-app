# Clean Max Env. Energ

## Verdict for the portfolio
CleanMax (Clean Max Enviro Energy Solutions) is the best business in this group. It sells wind and solar power to about 600 companies on 23-year contracts, and its growth is contracted and being delivered. I value it in March 2030 from capacity, operating profit (EBITDA) and debt:
- **EBITDA:** about ₹5,000cr in FY30, against ₹1,295cr in FY26.
- **Net debt:** about ₹29,000cr by then.
- **Valuation:** 13 times EBITDA, which is no richer than today.

That gives a base case of about **23% a year**. The equity is a thin slice on top of a lot of debt, so each one-point change in that multiple moves the return by about 5 points a year. **Medium**: own a small position, and add on dips toward ₹1,200 or when FY28's ₹3,000cr EBITDA floor is clearly in hand.

## Clean profit base
- **Profit is small next to operating profit.** In the last four quarters operating profit was ₹1,287cr (Screener; company "EBITDA", which includes other income, was about ₹1,486cr). Profit after tax was only ₹157cr in total.
- **Shareholders' share is slightly higher:** EPS ₹14.05 × 11.76cr shares = **₹165cr**. Young plant companies make losses, and client co-owners absorb part of those losses.
- **No one-offs removed.** FY26 profit was ₹86cr. Two caveats:
  - Other income was ₹199cr over the last year against ₹255cr of pre-tax profit. Part of it is interest on IPO and bond cash, which will be spent on plants.
  - Tax runs at an odd 40% because loss-making plant companies cannot offset profitable ones.
- **P/E:** ₹14,758cr ÷ ₹165cr = **89** (site 94 on ₹157cr; Screener 96). The P/E is a poor guide here: interest and depreciation on new plants swamp a profit that is still small. So the case is built on EBITDA and net debt.

**Contracted capacity and EBITDA.**
- About 6 GW is contracted: 3.5 GW running at June 2026 and 2.5 GW under construction at about ₹4.00 a unit.
- **Management's rule of thumb:** a year's EBITDA is about 1.0–1.1 times the "run-rate" EBITDA of plants running at the start of that year. Run-rate was ₹1,870cr at March 2026, about ₹0.60cr per MW.
- **FY27:** ₹1,870cr run-rate × 1.0–1.1 = ₹1,870–2,060cr, less about ₹170cr lost to curtailment at the Bikaner plant, where the grid cannot take about 70% of output this year. That gives about ₹1,900cr. Q1 delivered ₹462cr.
- **FY28:** management's "minimum ₹3,000cr" floor rests on at least 1.5 GW added in FY27. That build is 403 MW done in Q1, with a light monsoon quarter and a back-ended H2, including a 543 MW wind project at Koppal whose second grid bay is due only in March 2027. I take the floor as the base, not a beat.

**FY29–FY30 build pace.** I assume about 1.6–1.7 GW a year, near the 1.74 GW a year achieved in the 12 months to June 2026. The CEO says the firm can sign as much as the 4 GW it signed in the last two years, but not triple it. That gives 7.9 GW at the start of FY30 and about 9.6 GW by April 2030. At ₹0.60–0.62cr of EBITDA per MW plus plant-building income, FY30 EBITDA is about ₹5,000cr, with Bikaner's curtailment assumed fixed by FY29.

**From EBITDA to profit.**
- **Interest:** about 41% of EBITDA by FY30 (₹2,070cr). Average net debt is about ₹27,000cr at 8.3%. The project-debt cost is 8.45% today and the green bonds were priced at 8.25–8.76%.
- **Depreciation:** about ₹1,250cr on roughly ₹37,000cr of average gross block (3.25%, as in Q1).
- **Tax and co-owners:** 30% tax, and client co-owners take about 8%.

**Growth rate.** Shareholders' profit grows from ₹165cr (trailing) to about **₹1,100cr in FY30, about 66% a year**. That is a small base becoming normal as plants age; EBITDA grows about 40% a year.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **What the market will expect in March 2030:**
  - Another 1.5–2 GW a year of contracted plants.
  - Data centres: each 1 GW of load needs about 6 GW of wind and solar. CleanMax has about 35% of the hyperscaler deals in India, and 42% of its contracted capacity is data and AI.
  - Only 7–8% of industrial power is bought green, and customers save 22–25% on bills.
  - Battery storage adds ₹3–4 a unit.
  - EBITDA growth of about 18–20% a year after FY30, with profit faster as plants age.
- **What limits it:**
  - State rules on direct power sales (Karnataka and Gujarat are 63% of run-rate EBITDA; Maharashtra already cut banking hours from 17 to 8).
  - Grid curtailment.
  - Adani, Reliance and NTPC entering this market.
  - The need for ever more debt, and probably equity.

**Equity value in March 2030 (base):**
1. **Enterprise value:** 13 × FY30 EBITDA of ₹5,000cr = **₹65,000cr**.
   - Today the market pays about 14 times this year's EBITDA (₹26,567cr of market value plus net debt ÷ about ₹1,900cr), and about 10 times FY28's floor once the debt to build it is added. So 13 times is no re-rating.
   - Peers: Adani Green at about 23–26 times (TipRanks, Value Research); Kotak valued NTPC Green at about 16 times FY28 estimates.
2. **Less net debt of about ₹29,000cr at March 2030.** Roll-forward from June 2026:
   - start: ₹11,809cr;
   - plus about ₹27,600cr of plant capex (about 5.75 GW-equivalent at ₹4.8cr per MW blended: solar ₹3.5cr, wind ₹7.8cr);
   - less about ₹6,700cr of cash after interest and tax;
   - less about ₹1,800cr of client co-investment;
   - less an assumed ₹1,900cr share issue.

   This is about 5.8 times FY30 EBITDA but about 4.9 times run-rate, close to management's ₹16,000cr-for-₹3,000cr steady-state ratio.
3. **Less client co-owners' share of plant companies:** about ₹2,500cr.
4. **Equity value ≈ ₹33,500cr**, which is about **30.5 times FY30 profit of ₹1,100cr**. That sits at the bottom of the 30–42 band for 20–25% expected growth, as it should with this much debt.

## Returns
- **Normal case:** equity ₹33,500cr (₹1,100cr × 30.5) against ₹14,758cr today (site, 15 Oct; Screener ₹15,075cr at ₹1,282). Dilution of 10%: an expected raise of about ₹1,900cr, as capex of ₹7,500–9,000cr a year outruns internal cash. No dividend.
  ((1,100 × 30.5) ÷ (14,758 × 1.10))^(1/3.45) − 1 = **23.4% a year**.
- **Sensitivity:** at 12 times EBITDA the return is 17.7% a year; at 14 times, 28.4%. At the ₹1,053 IPO price the base case would give about 30%. At about ₹1,200 it gives 25%.
- **Bad case:** the build slows to about 1.2 GW a year, and Bikaner-type curtailment spreads to new central-grid plants. A state tightens direct-sale or banking rules in Karnataka or Gujarat, and rates rise. FY30 EBITDA ₹4,200cr at 11.5 times = ₹48,300cr; less ₹27,500cr net debt and ₹2,000cr co-owners gives equity ₹18,800cr. That is about 25 times FY30 profit of ₹750cr. ((750 × 25.1) ÷ (14,758 × 1.10))^(1/3.45) − 1 = **4.4% a year**.
- **Tail risk:** a sharp rise in interest rates while net debt passes ₹25,000cr, or a state reversing group-captive rules. Separately, Brookfield's 21% leaves lock-in in February 2027, and the founder has pledged shares; both could weigh on the price.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 1.** Net debt is ₹11,809cr at June, 9 times trailing operating profit, and interest covered operating profit only 1.7 times in FY26. Net debt heads to about ₹29,000cr. It is mitigated by 23-year contracts against 18–19-year loans, an AA- to AA rating, and 40% fixed-rate debt.
- **Earnings quality: 3.** Operating cash was 134% of operating profit over five years (₹4,583cr against ₹3,409cr) and clients pay in about 22–25 days. But free cash was −₹4,039cr in FY26 and other income is most of pre-tax profit. The auditor also found the accounts' audit trail switched off until 27 March 2026, and about ₹97cr of advance tax was paid 6–9 months late.
- **Management: 3.** It did what it promised in May: 403 MW in Q1, a first battery project, lower borrowing costs, and plants built within budget for 3–4 years. But the founder group had pledged about 1.16cr shares (about a tenth of the company) for its own LLP's loans, it would not disclose Q1 capex, and it has the audit-trail and late-tax lapses.
- **Moat and runway: 4.** It has 23-year contracts with 18-year lock-ins, about 600 customers, 80% repeat business, client equity in about 100 plant companies, and 42% of capacity with data centres. But the model rests on state open-access rules, and bigger groups are entering.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Net debt ₹11,809cr (9 times trailing operating profit), rising toward about ₹29,000cr; interest cover 1.7 times.** Valued on EV minus debt; balance-sheet score 1; 10% dilution assumed.
2. **Bikaner plant losing about 70% of output to curtailment (about ₹170cr of FY27 EBITDA).** FY27 cut; bad case assumes curtailment spreads.
3. **Karnataka and Gujarat are 63% of run-rate EBITDA; Maharashtra cut banking hours from 17 to 8.** Bad case includes a state rule change.
4. **Founder and his LLP pledged about 1.16cr shares (about 10% of the company) to 360 ONE Prime.** Management score 3; named in tail risk.
5. **Brookfield's 21% stake leaves lock-in in February 2027.** Possible price overhang; a reason to buy on dips.
6. **Audit trail switched off at database level for the holding company and 32 subsidiaries until 27 March 2026; ~₹97cr of advance tax paid 6–9 months late.** Earnings-quality score 3.
7. **Free cash flow −₹4,039cr in FY26; return on capital 6%, return on equity 1.8%.** Expected for a builder of 23-year assets; valued on EBITDA, not P/E.
8. **Trailing profit of ₹157cr leans on ₹199cr of other income.** Base uses EBITDA including other income, as management does, but values the business on run-rate assets.
9. **₹400cr holding-company bullet loan due June 2027; about ₹1,200cr of foreign-currency project debt to renew in September 2028 (CARE).** Covered by the ₹2,500cr green bonds raised 28 September.
10. **Would not give Q1 capex on the call.** Capex estimated from cost per MW.
11. **New grid-deviation penalty rules (DSM) in court; Adani, Reliance and NTPC entering.** In the bad case and the moat score.
12. **Likely future equity raises.** 10% dilution built in.

## Since the note was written
- **28 Sep:** raised ₹2,500cr of secured green bonds at 8.25–8.76% for 2–10 years. About ₹1,100cr refinances loans and about ₹1,400cr funds plant equity (CRISIL).
- **21–22 Sep:** sold 26% stakes in three new plant companies to their power buyers (Strata Geosystems, Goodluck India, Nuvoco Vistas) for ₹26,000 each. This is the normal client co-ownership structure, not a value event. A new Head of Projects was also appointed.
- **Price:** ₹1,255 (site, 15 Oct) against about ₹1,372 when the note was written, about 8% lower.
- **Q2 FY27:** no results or commissioning figures yet. Management had said the July–September quarter would be light on solar commissioning, so the 1.5 GW target is back-ended.
