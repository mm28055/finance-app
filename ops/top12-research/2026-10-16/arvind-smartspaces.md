# Arvind SmartSpaces

## Verdict for the portfolio
A well-run, fast-growing small developer at a fair price, but not a 25% stock on sober numbers. Bookings are compounding (₹1,550cr in FY26, guided ₹2,100–2,200cr in FY27) and the price roughly equals the cash from projects already in hand, so the downside is cushioned; but reported profit lags bookings by 2–4 years, filed operating cash has been negative for three of five years, and a fair exit multiple gives about 18% a year. **Not now** for a 25% target; it stays a follow, and turns interesting if FY27 bookings land at the top of the guide and filed operating cash turns positive.

## Clean profit base
- Screener (consolidated): FY26 net profit ₹103cr, of which **₹97cr belongs to shareholders** (EPS ₹21.03 × 4.59cr shares). Last four quarters (Jul-25 to Jun-26): net profit ₹189cr, **₹184cr to shareholders** (EPS ₹40.18).
- No exceptional items, tax credits or land gains in either period. Other income (₹20cr) is small treasury interest and is left in.
- The trailing figure is flattered: Q1 FY27 profit of ₹97cr came from handing over Orchards Phase 1 in Bengaluru at a 49% operating margin. Management told investors to "factor in a 25% EBITDA margin as the company average". At 25% on the last four quarters' ₹780cr of revenue, trailing profit would be about ₹120cr, not ₹184cr.
- P/E check: site ₹2,807cr ÷ ₹184cr = 15.2 (site shows 15.24); Screener 15.1. They agree. On the normalised ₹120cr it is about 23; on FY26's ₹97cr about 29.
- For a developer the profit line mainly says which buildings received completion certificates. FY26 revenue fell 21% to ₹564cr while bookings rose 22%, because certificates slipped into FY27. So the case is built from bookings, collections and the backlog of sold-but-unbooked homes (₹3,825cr at June 2026).

## Profit path FY27–FY30
How the path is built: revenue is booked when a project is completed, on average 2–3 years after the homes are sold. Sold-but-unbooked revenue was ₹3,700cr at March 2026, about 2.4 years of FY26 bookings. I assume bookings of ₹2,100cr (FY27, bottom of the guide), ₹2,600cr, ₹3,100cr and ₹3,600cr (FY30): about 23% a year against management's 25–30% four-to-five-year guide, a haircut because Mumbai has no delivery record and Bengaluru has a supply glut (Anarock: Bengaluru unsold stock up 34% in a year). With about 92% of bookings reaching consolidated revenue (joint-development partners' shares are excluded), and the backlog rising to about 2.4 years of bookings, FY27–FY30 revenue totals about ₹5,600cr.

Below operating profit: other income about ₹20cr; interest charged to profit rising from ₹45cr (last four quarters) to about ₹75cr as net debt rises towards India Ratings' ₹800–850cr; depreciation about ₹12cr; tax 25%; about ₹10cr to minority partners (HDFC Capital platform, joint ventures). FY30 profit **₹280cr**, about **30% a year from FY26's depressed ₹97cr** (about 24% a year from the normalised ₹120cr).

Cross-checks: ICICI Securities (10 Aug 2026) forecasts ₹152cr for FY27 and ₹178cr for FY28, a little below my path. Management guides operating cash (its own measure, before land) of ₹400–500cr in FY27 and puts the cash still to come from existing projects at ₹5,119cr over 4–5 years. Track record: FY26 bookings growth of 22% was as guided; FY26 own-basis operating cash was ₹417cr; FY26 revenue missed because certificates slipped. Management does not guide revenue.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway:** management's plan is 25–30% bookings growth for 4–5 years and a national-scale business in ten. The three markets absorb ₹3–4 lakh crore of homes a year, so at ₹3,600cr of FY30 bookings Arvind would hold about 1%. Share is no limit. The limits are execution (a new team, Mumbai approvals) and the cycle: management itself says "cycle-driven demand is stabilizing" and price rises "should now moderate".
- **What the market will expect in March 2030:** FY31–FY33 profit follows FY28–FY30 bookings (about 20% a year in my path), but by 2030 the housing upcycle that began in 2021 will be nine years old, and a developer's profit is lumpy. I expect the market to price in about 12–16% a year of profit growth after FY30.
- **Anchors:** Brigade trades at about 26–29 times trailing profit, Mahindra Lifespace about 22, Sobha 54–57 (on depressed profit), and the realty sector average about 35 (Value Research and Motilal Oswal pages as reported in search results, Aug–Oct 2026). Arvind's own P/E has mostly been in the 20s–30s on low accounting profit (FY26 EPS ₹21 against a 52-week range of ₹487–708 is 23–34). Today it is 15 on flattered trailing profit, 17 on my FY27 figure and 23 on normalised trailing profit.
- **Exit P/E: 18.** That is the lower half of the 12–16% band (18–26), because filed operating cash is weak, it is a cyclical developer and it is still small. It is roughly today's multiple on normalised profit, not an expansion. Cross-check: ₹280cr × 18 = ₹5,040cr, which is 1.4 times FY30 bookings, against 1.3 times FY27 bookings today. It is also about what is left of the ₹5,119cr project cash after tax, plus value for later land.

## Returns
Today's market value is ₹2,807cr (site, 15 Oct). Dilution: 2%, for the ESOP 2025 scheme (the CEO alone holds 8.5 lakh options, 1.9% of 4.59cr shares); HDFC Capital's money is project-level debentures, not parent shares. Dividend yield: 0.4% (₹2.25 on ₹608).

- **Normal case:** (₹280cr × 18 = ₹5,040cr) ÷ (₹2,807cr × 1.02 = ₹2,863cr) = 1.76×. Over 3.45 years that is 17.8% a year, plus 0.4% dividend = **18.2% a year**.
- **Bad case:** Bengaluru's oversupply and slow Mumbai approvals hold bookings to about 10% growth after FY27 (₹2,530cr in FY30) and joint-development mix plus cost inflation hold margins at 23%. FY30 profit is about ₹195cr and the exit P/E drops one band to 14. (₹195cr × 14 = ₹2,730cr) ÷ ₹2,863cr = 0.95×, which is **−1.0% a year** after the 0.4% dividend.
- For reference only, the 25%-clearing case: bookings growth at the full 25–30% guide, margins at 27% and a P/E of 22 give ₹330cr × 22, about 31% a year. Every lever has to go right at once.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 3.** Company net debt was ₹215cr at June (0.29 times equity), but Screener shows ₹409cr at March including leases. Gross debt doubled to ₹577cr in FY26, and India Ratings expects net debt of ₹800–850cr, about 2.5 times FY30 operating profit at peak, with a stated ceiling of 1:1 debt to equity.
- **Earnings quality: 3.** On the company's own measure (collections less construction and overheads, before land), operating cash was ₹417cr in FY26 and ₹81cr in Q1 FY27, 36–37% of collections. On Screener's filed figures, five-year operating cash was −₹164cr against ₹326cr of profit, because ₹600cr+ a year of land buying counts as operating spending. Q1's margin is a one-project spike.
- **Management: 4.** The team delivered the FY26 bookings guide (+22%) and the own-basis cash guide. It walked away from Sohna on legal concerns, is backed by the Lalbhai group, has no pledges and the promoter raised its stake to 53.8%. Against that, about half the CEO's direct reports joined in the past year, two CFOs left in two years, and the Bengaluru head and legal head left in mid-2026.
- **Moat and runway: 3.** It leads in plotted land in Ahmedabad and launches sell 50–70% within days (Spire 53% in a day, Greenfields 72%, Sylva ₹500cr in 30 days). But real estate has no lasting moat, it is a newcomer in Mumbai, and Bengaluru's Forest Trails is still more than half unsold. The runway is long, but cyclical.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Filed operating cash −₹164cr against ₹326cr profit (FY22–FY26), −₹170cr in FY26.** Handled by scoring earnings quality 3 and keeping the exit P/E in the lower half of its band. Ownership waits for filed cash to turn positive.
2. **Debt rising on purpose: gross ₹577cr, net heading to ₹800–850cr (India Ratings).** Interest charged to profit is raised to ₹75cr in FY30 and the balance-sheet score is 3.
3. **Q1 FY27's 49% margin is one project (Orchards).** Excluded from the base. The path uses the 25% guide by FY30.
4. **FY26 revenue fell 21% on delayed completion certificates.** The path assumes the backlog stays at 2.4 years of bookings, so further delays are absorbed rather than assumed away.
5. **Mumbai has no delivery record; Goregaon is a 4-year, 35-floor MHADA redevelopment with a 44% profit share.** Bookings growth is haircut to about 23% from the 25–30% guide, and the bad case assumes slow Mumbai approvals.
6. **Bengaluru (31% of FY26 bookings) faces oversupply: unsold stock +34%, launches +41% against sales +1% (Anarock).** This drives the bad case.
7. **Forest Trails villas more than 50% unsold, with cancellations in FY26.** A small drag; it is one of the next checks.
8. **Thin, new team: about 50% of the CEO's direct reports joined within a year; two CFO exits in two years; the CBO South and Head Legal left in May–June 2026.** Reflected in the haircut to guidance. The statutory auditor change (S R B C to Walker Chandiok, 7 Aug 2026) was mandatory 10-year rotation, not a dispute.
9. **Management says price growth will moderate and the cycle is "stabilizing".** Margins are held at 25%, with no price-led expansion.
