# Apollo Tyres

## Verdict for the portfolio
A well-financed market leader at a fair price, not a cheap one: the reported P/E of ~12 drops to about **15.6 once a ₹570cr tax gain and ₹1,034cr of plant-closure charges are cleaned out**. On mid-cycle margins (14%) FY30 profit is ~₹2,400cr and the return is about **11% a year**. Rubber hit a record ₹284.50/kg on 13 October, the CFO has left with no successor, and a capacity wave lands in FY28–FY30, so the bad case loses money. **Not now** for a 25% target.

## Clean profit base
- **FY26 reported ₹1,372cr.** It contains two big one-offs:
  - ₹1,034cr of exceptional charges to close the Dutch (Enschede) plant, which Screener nets into "other income" (−₹900cr for the year).
  - A ₹570cr deferred-tax gain in Q4 from moving to the lower 25% tax rate.
  Cleaned: profit before tax ₹1,309cr + ₹1,034cr = ₹2,343cr, taxed at ~31% (the old 34% Indian rate blended with ~20% in Europe), gives **~₹1,600cr**.
- **Last four quarters (Q2 FY26–Q1 FY27) reported ₹1,708cr.** Adding back ~₹630cr of exceptional charges in Q2 and Q4 FY26 gives profit before tax of ~₹2,370cr; removing the ₹570cr tax gain and taxing at ~29% gives **~₹1,680cr**.
- **P/E check.** The site shows 12.36 and Screener 12.2, both on the reported ₹1,708cr. On clean profit the P/E is **15.6** (₹26,287cr ÷ ₹1,680cr). That is a material difference: the stock is about 25% dearer than the headline suggests. Market value: site ₹26,287cr at ₹413.9 (Screener ₹25,938cr at ₹408).
- FY27 itself will be weaker. Q1 profit was ₹349cr at an 11.7% margin, and I expect FY27 near ₹1,400cr.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue | Operating margin | Profit after tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual, clean) | ₹28,471cr | 14.6% | ~₹1,600cr |
| FY27 | ~₹32,000cr (+12%) | 11.8% | ~₹1,390cr |
| FY28 | ~₹34,200cr (+7%) | 14.0% | ~₹2,000cr |
| FY29 | ~₹36,800cr (+7.5%) | 14.0% | ~₹2,170cr |
| FY30 | ~₹39,400cr (+7%) | 14.0% (mid-cycle) | **~₹2,400cr** |

Drivers:
- **Volume and price.** India (69% of revenue) grew 15.6% in Q1 FY27, about 12 points from volume (replacement +13%, vehicle makers +10%, exports +15%). Plants are 91% full in India and 94% in Europe. Price rises of 9% in truck tyres and 11% in other tyres were in place by August against the 15–16% needed, so FY27 revenue rises on price. From FY28 I assume 5–7% volume (ICRA 5–7%, CRISIL 4–5% for the industry) plus a little price and mix, about 7% a year in rupees.
- **Margin.** Q1 FY27 was 11.7% (India 12.0%, Europe 8.9%). Raw materials rose 17% in Q1 and about 8% more in Q2. Management expected rubber to cool from Q3, but Kerala RSS-4 rubber hit a record ₹284.50/kg on 13 October (international ₹282.16), so I take FY27 at 11.8%, not 13%. For FY28–FY30 I use **14%**, the 12-year average (FY15–FY26: 11–18%). That is not the FY24 peak of 18%; it includes Europe moving toward its 16%+ "normal" after the Dutch move, offset by price pressure as new industry capacity lands.
- **Capex.** ₹3,500cr in FY27 (₹650cr spent in Q1), ~₹3,000cr in India, including the ₹5,810cr Andhra Pradesh expansion (3.7m car and 1.3m truck tyres a year by FY29; first lines at the end of FY27) and Hungary going from 17,000 to 21,000 car tyres a day from H2 FY27. Depreciation rises from ₹1,543cr to ~₹2,100cr by FY30. Interest stays ~₹380–430cr as net debt peaks around FY28 ("we will be a net borrower").
- **Tax.** 25% in India from FY27 (was 34%) and ~20% in Europe, about 24% blended. This lift is real and alone adds ~12% to profit.
- **Growth:** clean profit **10.7% a year** from FY26, but much of it is FY28's recovery from a trough plus the tax cut, not new demand.

**Track record:** the company met its Q1 cost forecast (17% against "mid to high teens"), took the price rises it announced, and closed the Dutch plant on schedule in June 2026. Its earlier promise of 16%+ European margins slid for three years before the closure. No revenue or margin guidance is given. Its rubber-cooling call is already being tested.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**Runway:** tyre replacement grows with the number of vehicles on the road, steadily but slowly: 5–7% volume plus price and mix, so 8–10% profit growth in a normal year. Andhra Pradesh will still be filling in FY30 (headroom), and EU anti-dumping duties of 4.3–45.3% on Chinese car tyres (from July 2026, for five years) help its Apollo brand in Europe. Against that, the six big makers are spending ~₹18,000cr over FY27–FY28 (CRISIL), nearly double the prior two years, when demand growth is mid-single-digit; more than 70% of truck tyres are retreaded; and margins swing with rubber.

**Exit P/E:** expected growth of 8–10% puts it in the 14–20 band; commodity exposure and the capacity wave put it in the lower half. Peers in August 2026: MRF ~24, CEAT ~25, JK Tyre ~15.5. Apollo has long traded at a discount to MRF and CEAT because of Europe and governance. Today's clean P/E is ~15.6. I use **15** on mid-cycle FY30 profit: no re-rating, because the reasons for the discount (pay and related-party dealings, Europe's history, the rubber cycle) will not clear by 2030.

## Returns
Formula: ((FY30 profit × exit P/E) ÷ (market value × (1 + d)))^(1/3.45) − 1 + dividend yield. Market value ₹26,287cr (site, 15 Oct). Dividend ~₹6 a share, a 1.45% yield. No new shares (d = 0).

- **Normal case:** ₹2,400cr × 15 = ₹36,000cr. (36,000 ÷ 26,287)^(1/3.45) − 1 = 9.5%, plus 1.45% = **11.0% a year**.
- **Bad case:** rubber stays near ₹280/kg into FY28 and the capacity wave sets off price cuts, so the margin settles at 12% on revenue of ~₹37,000cr. FY30 profit ~₹1,510cr, exit P/E 11 (one band down). (16,610 ÷ 26,287)^(1/3.45) − 1 = −12.5%, plus 1.45% = **−11.0% a year**.
- **Tail risk:** losing the 2018 Competition Commission case (₹425.5cr penalty for truck-tyre price collusion, not provided for, before the Supreme Court), or an industry price war as ₹13,000cr+ of new capacity at Apollo, JK and CEAT starts in FY28–FY30.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 4.** Net debt about ₹1,700cr (company figure; ₹2,631cr with leases), 0.4–0.6 times operating profit, down from 3 times in 2020. It will rise in FY27–FY28 with ₹3,500cr of capex, and ₹425.5cr (Competition Commission) plus ₹515cr (sales tax) of claims are not provided for.
- **Earnings quality: 3.** 73% of five years' operating profit turned into operating cash (FY22–FY26: ₹13,221cr of ₹18,161cr), but exceptional charges recur (₹1,034cr in FY26), stock is 128 days and the cash cycle stretched from 54 to 93 days.
- **Management: 2.** It forecasts costs accurately and closed the Dutch plant on time. But the CFO of 22 years left on 6 October with no successor, the managing director's pay nearly doubled to ₹78.4cr (541 times the median employee) and he missed the Q1 call, and it bought ₹685cr from promoter-group firms (₹473cr a year earlier).
- **Moat and runway: 3.** It has over 30% of truck-radial and 21% of car-tyre replacement (company estimates) and about 6,500 dealers, but cannot pass on a rubber spike for two or three quarters, and the industry is adding capacity faster than demand.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **Margin fell from 14.6% (Q4 FY26) to 11.7% (Q1 FY27); materials rose 17% in Q1 and ~8% more in Q2.** FY27 is set at 11.8% and profit ~₹1,390cr.
- **Rubber hit a record ₹284.50/kg on 13 October, against management's call that it would cool from Q3.** This drives the bad case (12% margin); a 14% margin is only assumed from FY28.
- **The CFO left on 6 October with no successor named; the managing director missed the Q1 call.** Management scores 2; naming a credible CFO is a next check.
- **Managing director's pay ₹78.4cr (+96%), plus pay from a UK subsidiary; ₹685cr bought from promoter-group firms; a plant leased from promoter-group PTL.** This is why the exit P/E stays at the low end of the band, below MRF and CEAT.
- **FY26 profit includes a ₹570cr deferred-tax gain and ₹1,034cr of closure charges.** Both are cleaned; the true P/E is 15.6, not 12.
- **₹425.5cr Competition Commission penalty unprovided; sales-tax claims up from ₹189cr to ₹515cr.** Named as tail risk (about 3.5% of market value combined).
- **₹466cr of goodwill and brand for Reifencom, which earns 2–3%.** A write-down would be non-cash; it is ignored in profit but would cut book value.
- **₹5,810cr Andhra Pradesh plant pays back in 8–10 years on rough sums; CRISIL sees return on capital below 10% in FY27.** Depreciation rises to ~₹2,100cr in the path.
- **Industry capacity of ~₹18,000cr over FY27–FY28 against 4–7% demand growth.** This is in the bad case and the tail risk.
- **Europe margin was 8.9% in Q1; 20–25% of high-end farm tyres are now bought in; farm tyres were ~12% of European revenue.** Europe is not assumed to reach "high teens"; the group stays at 14%.
- **Ten-year sales growth 9% and profit growth 7% a year; ten-year return on equity 9%.** Exit P/E stays at 15.
- **Foreign holding fell from 22.1% to 10.1% in three years.** Noted; no re-rating assumed.
- **Cash cycle stretched from 54 to 93 days.** Earnings quality scores 3.
