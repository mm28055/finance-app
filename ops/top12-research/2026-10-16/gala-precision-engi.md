# Gala Precision Engi.

## Verdict for the portfolio
Gala holds hard-won vendor codes at Vestas, Siemens, ABB and Schaeffler and can grow sales about 20% a year. But profit grows more slowly than sales: depreciation rises, the unusually low tax rate should drift up, and the business eats cash. Free cash was negative in FY25 and FY26. On an exit P/E of about 21, profit of about ₹66cr in FY30 gives roughly a 0% return a year from ₹1,374cr. **Not now.** It would clear 25% at a market value near ₹640cr, about ₹495 a share against ₹1,072.

## Clean profit base
- **Reported:** profit after tax for the year to June 2026 was ₹37cr (Screener; FY26 ₹35cr). The effective tax rate was about 17%, against 25% normally.
  - Management credits deductions for new-product development, staff share options and faster tax depreciation, plus a solar project from Q3.
  - Those deductions are partly lasting, so I normalised tax to 21%, not 25%.
- **Kept:** other income of ₹4cr (interest on unspent IPO money and currency) is small, so I kept it.
- **Clean base: about ₹35cr** (pre-tax ₹45cr × 0.79).
- **P/E check:** the site shows 36.0 and Screener 36.1, so the two agree. On the clean base it is about 39.

What drives the numbers:
- **Guidance (29 September, the latest call):** 20–25% revenue growth "over the next 2–3 years", with an operating margin of 17–19%.
  - Fasteners (₹106cr in FY26) should grow 35–40% a year. Coil springs grow about 15–17%. Disc springs grew 31% in Q1.
  - The firm order book was ₹110cr on 1 July, up from ₹80–85cr a year earlier, and Q1 orders rose about 40%.
- **Capacity:**
  - The two plants can make about ₹450–460cr a year: Chennai ₹120cr after phase 2, Wada ₹325cr. They should be full by mid-to-late FY28.
  - The new Wada plot (₹40–45cr of capex, mostly FY28, still at the MoU and legal-check stage) adds about ₹110cr at the guided 2.5–2.75 times asset turns.
  - FY30 at ₹646cr needs one more round of capex, which I fund with debt.
- **Track record:** FY26 revenue grew 32%, beating the guide. But Q1 FY27 grew only 19.5% (₹75.4cr) at a 16.3% margin, under the 17–19% guide. Chennai phase 2 slipped from June–July to Q3–Q4, and the Wada land is still at MoU.
- **My path:**
  - Revenue: 21%, 21%, 19% and 18% a year.
  - Margin: 17–17.5%. It has been 17% for two years, after 19% in FY24.
  - Below the operating line: depreciation rises from ₹11cr to ₹24cr, interest from ₹3cr to ₹7cr (more working capital), and tax is 21%.
- **Growth rate:** about 18% a year in profit, under the site's ~22%. The gap is depreciation and the tax rate, not sales.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**What the market will see in March 2030:**
- **For growth:**
  - Its share at its largest customer is under 10% of a ₹500cr spend.
  - Fasteners open a $2.5bn addressable market (aiming at $5bn), and disc springs plus wedge-lock washers about ₹3,000cr.
  - Newer areas: electrolysers, gas turbines, rail and seat-belt springs.
  - Entering a customer takes 12–24 months, and Gala then grows from 5–10% to 50–75% of that customer's spend.
- **Against:**
  - Wind fasteners are about a quarter of sales. Management itself says the US renewable market "is declining".
  - Every rupee of growth needs about half a rupee of working capital (about 175 days by its count, 294 by Screener's), plus capex.
  - It is still small, at about ₹650cr of sales in FY30.

**My call:** I expect the market to want about 15% profit growth for FY31–FY33. That band (12–16%) pays 18–26 times profit, and I use **21**, the lower half, for weak cash conversion (32% of five years' operating profit), the tax search and the patent suit.

**Peers:** Pitti Engineering 30.9 and Sundram Fasteners 37.5 (Screener). Gala's own P/E has ranged from roughly 27 to 45 since its September 2024 listing (my estimate from Screener prices and EPS). Even at 26 (the top of its band) the return is about 6% a year.

## Returns
Market value ₹1,374cr (Screener, 16 Oct). No dividend. Dilution 1% for employee share options.
- **Normal:**
  - FY30 profit ₹66cr × P/E 21 = ₹1,386cr.
  - (1,386 ÷ (1,374 × 1.01))^(1/3.45) − 1 = **0.0% a year**.
- **Bad:** wind orders slow (US renewables falling) and working capital stays near 175 days.
  - Growth runs at 13–15% a year (FY30 revenue ₹526cr) with a 16% margin, so FY30 profit is ₹44cr.
  - P/E falls one band to 17: ₹748cr.
  - Return: **−16.4% a year**.
- **What it would take:** a market value of about ₹635cr (about ₹495 a share) to clear 25% a year, or about ₹730cr (about ₹570) to clear 20%. Alternatively, an exit P/E of about 45.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 4.** Net cash was ₹12cr at March 2026: borrowings ₹38cr against cash and about ₹28cr of unspent IPO money. Crisil rates it BBB+/Positive. The Nord-Lock claim is about ₹2cr so far, and any demand from the tax search is unknown.
- **Earnings quality: 2.**
  - Operating cash was ₹57cr against ₹180cr of operating profit over FY22–FY26 (32%), and only ₹3cr and ₹10cr in FY25 and FY26.
  - Free cash was −₹31cr and −₹28cr in those two years. Inventory runs at 257 days.
  - The tax rate is low, at 17–18%.
- **Management: 3.**
  - It beat FY26 growth (32% against 20–25%).
  - It let three dated milestones slip in one quarter and held back a ₹3cr dispatch for non-payment.
  - The income-tax department searched its office and the promoter's home on 18 August. The search was declared concluded on 24 August with no demand disclosed.
  - Promoters are steady at about 54%.
- **Moat and runway: 4.** Safety-critical parts take 12–24 months to qualify, and it says it has never lost a customer on quality or delivery in 25 years. It holds small shares of large spends, which gives it a long runway. The weak spots are wind cyclicality and slow-switching European buyers.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Cash does not follow profit:** 32% five-year conversion and negative free cash in FY25 and FY26. This sets the exit P/E in the lower half (21). The path adds interest for debt-funded working capital. The KPMG plan targets a 15–20% cut in working-capital days over 9–12 months; if it works, the exit P/E could move up about 2–3 points (worth about 3–4 points a year of return).
2. **Working capital about 175 days (294 on Screener):** the reason growth needs debt or, eventually, equity. I allow only 1% dilution, a risk if growth outruns cash.
3. **Low tax rate:** normalised from about 17% to 21%. This cuts the base by about ₹2cr and holds FY30 profit growth under sales growth.
4. **Milestones slipping:** Chennai phase 2 late, Wada land still at MoU, Q1 margin 16.3% against 17–19%. The margin path is set at 17–17.5%, the bottom of the guide.
5. **Income-tax search (18 August) on its office and the promoter's home:** concluded, no demand disclosed. This is named as a tail risk, not in the base.
6. **Nord-Lock patent suit over Gallock washers:** a tail risk too. Gallock is part of the disc-spring segment (54% of Q1).
7. **Wind dependence:** about a quarter of sales, with US renewables now falling (management). The bad case.
8. **A ₹3cr dispatch held for non-payment in Q1:** a sign of customer payment stress. Watched in receivables.
