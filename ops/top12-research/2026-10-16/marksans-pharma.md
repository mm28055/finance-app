# Marksans Pharma

## Verdict for the portfolio
A well-run exporter at the top of its own valuation range. It has net cash of ₹1,031cr, its own front-end companies, and beat its guidance in Q1. But today's 27 times profit is the highest the stock has carried in five years (8.7 at end-2021, 26.5 at end-2025). The trailing profit includes a 25% margin built on cheap stock, which management itself expects to fall back to 21–22%. On a path where profit reaches about ₹795cr in FY30 (12% a year from today's inflated base) and the P/E settles at 22, the return is **about 7% a year**. At 24 times it is about 10%, and even on management's own revenue-doubling path at 26 times it is about 16%. **Not now** for a 25% goal.

## Clean profit base
- Screener (consolidated): FY26 net profit ₹420cr, of which **₹418cr** is shareholders' (EPS ₹9.22 × 45.3cr shares). The last four quarters are ₹521cr, of which **₹517cr** is shareholders' (EPS ₹11.41); the rest belongs to the 40% partners in Australia. There are no exceptional items.
- **Two things flatter it, and I keep both but do not grow them.** First, Q1 FY27's 25.3% margin used raw material bought before prices rose 20–30%. Management guides 55–56% gross and 21–22% operating margins for the year. At 21.5% the four quarters would have earned about ₹32cr less operating profit, about ₹24cr after tax. Second, other income of ₹102cr is mostly interest on about ₹1,000cr of cash plus currency gains.
- P/E check: site ₹13,954cr ÷ ₹517cr = **27.0** (site 27.01; Screener 27.1). On the margin-normalised ₹493cr it is 28.3.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
**Drivers:**
- **US** (₹1,533cr in FY26, +24%; 45% of Q1): mostly store-brand over-the-counter medicines with large retail chains. 112 products launched in FY26 and about 50 more in the pipeline. The target is US$300m (about ₹2,600cr), then US$400m within about five years. Prescription prices are falling at single digits.
- **UK and Europe** (₹1,015cr in FY26; Q1 ₹356cr, +75%, or +53% without QliniQ): QliniQ (Netherlands, bought for €7.5m) is expected to bring ₹150–175cr in FY27, and ABCnow (Germany) is consolidated from Q2. The aim is about ₹1,000cr of European revenue within five years, "most of that... through acquisitions". The UK is growing over 40%.
- **Australia and NZ** (₹303cr in FY26): the target is A$100m within three years. Marksans owns 60%.
- **Capacity**: the group runs at about 50–55% of 26bn units, and the ex-Teva Goa plant at about 60–65% of its ₹80cr target. Another Indian plant "may" be needed in 1–2 years.
- **Guidance**: FY27 revenue +15–20% (kept "because of the volatility"), margin 21–22%, ₹4,000cr of revenue "within the next 2 years" (FY28), and revenue doubled in 3–5 years.
- **Track record**: Q1 beat on growth (35.6%) and margin (25.3%). FY26 revenue grew 12.5%, and the margin dipped to 16% in Q1 FY26. I assume no further large acquisition and only modest use of the cash.
- **Below operating profit**: other income eases from ₹102cr to about ₹80cr as cash goes into deals. Depreciation goes from ₹100cr to about ₹135cr (new dosage-form lines, a possible new plant). Interest about ₹25–30cr. Tax 25%.

FY30 profit **₹795cr**. That is about **12% a year** from the trailing ₹517cr, or 17% from FY26's weak ₹418cr; the note expects 17%. Management's "double revenue in 3–5 years" (about ₹6,000cr) at 21% would give about ₹900cr.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway**: more store-brand products with existing US retailers, the Canada filings, a European prescription business built mostly by acquisition, Australian branded generics, and a "top global consumer healthcare company by 2030" aim. In March 2030, 12–14% growth a year is a fair expectation. That puts it in the 18–26 band.
- **Against that**: the products are off-patent and anyone can make them (Perrigo, Strides and Granules compete). Two customers buy 23% of sales. US tariffs on generics come up for review around April 2027. Europe growth depends on buying companies at sensible prices.
- **Anchors**: Marksans' year-end P/E was 8.7 (2021), 10.1 (2022), 12.0 (2023), 21.9 (2024) and 26.5 (2025), and 20.5 on 19 March 2026 (Value Research). Today it is 27. On a forward basis Marksans is 21.4x against Strides at 16.3x (Simply Wall St).
- **Exit P/E: 22.** That is the middle of the 18–26 band. It is well above the stock's five-year average of about 16, because the business is bigger, debt-free and diversified now. It is below today's 27 because nothing makes today's price cheap: the P/E rests on a record quarter with cheap stock.

## Returns
Market value today ₹13,954cr (site, 15 Oct; Screener ₹14,007cr). Dilution 0: no equity raised, and acquisitions are from cash. Dividend yield 0.29%.
- **Normal case**: (₹795cr × 22 = ₹17,490cr) ÷ ₹13,954cr = 1.25 times. Over 3.45 years that is 6.8% a year, plus 0.29% = **7.1% a year**. At a P/E of 24 it is 9.8%. On management's path (about ₹900cr) at 26 it is 16.5%.
- **Bad case**: raw-material and freight costs stay high, US store-brand pricing tightens, and growth slows to 10%. The margin returns to 19%, about where it was in FY25–FY26 (16–23% by quarter). FY30 profit is about ₹590cr. The P/E steps down to 17. (₹590cr × 17 = ₹10,030cr) ÷ ₹13,954cr = 0.72 times, which is **−8.8% a year**.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 5.** Net cash of ₹1,031cr at June 2026 (cash ₹1,058cr; the only bank debt is a ₹29.6cr US line), rated CARE AA-/Stable (upgraded October 2025). It is debt-free even after paying for QliniQ.
- **Earnings quality: 3.** Five-year operating cash was ₹1,231cr against ₹2,192cr of operating profit (56%). FY26 improved to 76% and Q1 FY27 to 87%. But inventory runs at 278 days (5–6 months of stock held on purpose), the trailing margin includes cheap stock, and other auditors cover about 95% of revenue.
- **Management: 4.** It beat the Q1 guide on both growth and margin, brought Europe in early, paid about 7–8 times profit for QliniQ, and has kept the balance sheet clean. Against that: the founder drew about ₹1.3cr from the US subsidiary on top of his listed-company pay, he says he would "not put much weight" on the tariff threat, and it is a founder-led company where promoters own 43.9%.
- **Moat and runway: 3.** Owning the front-end companies and licences (175+ UK marketing authorisations, 100+ in Australia) makes it a preferred store-brand supplier. But there is no patent protection, two customers buy 23%, and Europe has to be bought.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Q1's 25.3% margin includes cheap stock; management guides 21–22% and 55–56% gross.** The path uses 21.5% from FY28, and the exit P/E falls from 27 to 22.
2. **Today's P/E (27) is the stock's highest in five years (8.7 at end-2021).** No re-rating; the base assumes some de-rating.
3. **Two customers buy about 23% of sales.** Losing one is the tail risk. Moat scores 3.
4. **Other auditors audit about 95% of revenue.** Earnings quality scores 3.
5. **Inventory 278 days; five-year cash conversion 56%.** Earnings quality 3. Management says the stock is deliberate, after the tariff scare.
6. **US tariffs on generics after the April 2027 review (talk of 200%).** The Farmingdale US plant is a partial hedge. It is in the bad case through pricing, and a full tariff is a tail risk.
7. **Europe growth depends on buying companies with the ₹1,000cr of cash ("200, 300, or 400 crores could go").** No large deal is assumed. A pricey one is a break trigger.
8. **The founder drew ₹1.3cr from the US subsidiary.** It is small, but it counts against the management score.
