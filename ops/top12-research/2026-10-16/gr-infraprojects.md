# GR Infraprojects

## Verdict for the portfolio
Cheap for a reason that will not clear by March 2030. At about 7.7 times clean profit and 0.77 times book value, the price already counts the road projects at a discount. But profit keeps going into project equity instead of arriving as cash: ₹3,346cr is still to invest, and management wants to put more into toll (BOT) roads. Order wins were negligible in Q1 against a ₹20,000cr guide, and the legal list is long. On a haircut path, profit grows about 7–8% a year to ₹1,250cr in FY30. At an unchanged P/E of 8, that gives **about 9% a year**. Even if the market valued every part in full, with no discount, it would be about 19%. **Not now.**

## Clean profit base
- Screener (consolidated): FY26 net profit **₹903cr**; last four quarters **₹1,016cr**. Q1 FY27 includes a **₹46cr exceptional gain**, booked when GR's stake in its own Indus Infra Trust was diluted from 43.56% to 31.58%. I remove it, which leaves **₹970cr** for the last four quarters.
- FY26's ₹253cr gain on selling four HAM projects to the trust shows in the standalone accounts (₹182cr after tax). Screener's consolidated Q4 FY26 (pre-tax profit ₹320cr, other income ₹110cr) shows no gain of that size, so I make no further adjustment.
- Other income of about ₹365cr over the last four quarters (₹411cr less the ₹46cr gain) is trust interest and dividends plus deposit interest. Management says the Q1 run rate holds. The trust pays GR ₹200–250cr a year in cash, part of it as capital repayment that never reaches profit. I keep this income in.
- P/E check: site ₹7,503cr ÷ ₹970cr = **7.7** clean. The site shows 7.99 and Screener 8.0 on reported profit. They agree.
- About half of consolidated operating profit (₹1,622cr in FY26 against about ₹840cr for the building arm alone) comes from the HAM project companies, mostly finance income, not building work. The building arm alone earned an 11.0% margin in Q1 against a consolidated 16.8%.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
**Drivers:**
- **Order book**: ₹25,300cr at 1 July, about 3 years of revenue, plus ₹32,000cr of bids still unopened (₹28,000cr transport, ₹4,000cr hydro and tunnels). FY26 wins were ₹10,700cr. The FY27 guide is about ₹20,000cr (roads ₹14,000cr), but Q1 brought almost nothing. I assume ₹12,000–14,000cr a year.
- **Start dates**: Agra–Gwalior (₹4,263cr toll road, 910 days to build) got its start date on 7 September. Two HAM roads are expected around December. BharatNet (about ₹400cr in FY27) waits on rights of way. Oil and gas brought ₹270cr in Q1 against a ₹1,000cr+ FY27 target. Power lines brought ₹110cr.
- **Guidance (Aug call)**: FY27 revenue +15–20% (25% possible), FY28 about +20% (₹11,000–12,000cr). Building margin 10–11%. Capex ₹300cr in FY27 and ₹200–250cr in FY28.
- **Track record**: mixed. Revenue is still below FY23's ₹9,482cr. Q4 FY26 came in at ₹2,521cr against about ₹3,000cr guided. In May the CFO said execution "is not going to increase", yet Q1 rose 40%. I haircut the guide by about 4–8 points a year.
- **Below operating profit**: interest rises from ₹494cr to about ₹620cr as Agra–Gwalior and the new HAMs borrow, partly offset by 3–4 HAM sales a year to the trust. Depreciation goes from ₹193cr to about ₹245cr. Tax is 25–26%.
- **Margin**: the consolidated margin drifts from 18–19% to about 17%. The building share grows and sold HAMs take their finance income with them, while new HAMs and the Agra–Gwalior tolls (from about FY29–FY30) hold it up.

FY30 profit **₹1,250cr**: about **7% a year** from the clean ₹970cr, or 8.5% from FY26's ₹903cr. That matches the note's 8% base. Management's path (20% growth, same margins) would give about ₹1,500cr.

## The road-project portfolio, valued on its own
This is material: the project stakes are worth about two-thirds of the company's market value.
- **Trust units**: GR held 19.29cr units of Indus Infra Trust. At about ₹125 a unit (June 2026 quote), that is about **₹2,400cr**, in line with the company's "₹2,000–2,400cr".
- **Equity and loans in its own HAM and toll companies**: **₹2,445cr** at June 2026, with ₹3,346cr more committed over about 3 years (₹900–1,000cr in FY27). Past sales to the trust fetched about 1.2 times book; management quotes a 1.25–2.25 range.
- **Building business**: about ₹550cr a year of profit, not counting trust income. At the 9–11 times peers trade at (Motilal Oswal, 6 Aug 2026: KNR 8.9, H.G. Infra 10.9, Patel 10.5), it is worth about ₹5,000cr.
- **Parts today**: ₹5,000cr + ₹2,400cr + ₹2,445cr ≈ **₹9,800cr**, against a market value of ₹7,503cr. That is a 23% discount, which the market applies because the cash keeps going back into projects.
- **Parts in March 2030**: building profit of about ₹735cr × 9 ≈ ₹6,600cr, plus project stakes of about ₹7,000–8,000cr at book (after the ₹3,346cr goes in and some is recycled through the trust) at 0.9 times ≈ ₹6,800cr. Total about **₹13,400cr**, or about 1.0 times FY30 book of about ₹13,700cr.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway**: India's transport pipeline is about ₹3.85 lakh crore. The government is reviving toll roads (a new toll-plus-annuity model, more than 10,000 km of BOT), and transmission needs ₹5–6 lakh crore of spending between FY27 and FY32. Tighter net-worth rules favour strong balance sheets like GR's. In March 2030 the market will expect 8–10% growth, if orders recover.
- **Against that**: plain highway building has no moat left (bids come in 30–40% below the authority's estimate). GR is new to power lines, pipelines and BharatNet (fixed prices, no escalation). The award rate is the bottleneck, and management "has been watching reality for the last 2 years". Most important, it says it will keep deploying its cash into BOT projects. Profit then keeps turning into project equity, and return on equity stays near 9–10%.
- **Anchors**: peers trade at 8.9–10.9 times trailing profit and 0.64–1.10 times book (Motilal Oswal, 6 Aug 2026). GR's P/E was 10.2 in June and 7.8 in mid-September, with P/B of 1.0 and then 0.8 (Value Research). Its forward P/E is 9.9 against PNC's 10.7 (Simply Wall St).
- **Exit P/E: 8**, the same as today. The table would say 10–15 for under-8% growth, but builders with negative operating cash and legal overhangs trade below it, and GR does today. The discount comes from four things: profit not becoming cash, a 9% return on equity, no orders, and the CBI, tax and GST matters. I do not expect these to clear by March 2030. The equity programme ends around FY29, but management has said new BOT money replaces it. ₹1,250cr × 8 = ₹10,000cr, about 0.73 times FY30 book. That is the same discount to book as today, and close to the parts value after a 25% holding discount.

## Returns
Market value today ₹7,503cr (site, 15 Oct; Screener ₹7,512cr). Dilution is 0: no equity raised, and the projects are funded from cash and trust sales. Dividend yield is 0.3%.
- **Normal case**: (₹1,250cr × 8 = ₹10,000cr) ÷ ₹7,503cr = 1.33 times. Over 3.45 years that is 8.7% a year, plus 0.3% dividend = **9.0% a year**. At a P/E of 9 it is 12.8%. If the market paid full parts value (about ₹13,400cr, no discount), it would be 18.6%. Even that is below 25%.
- **Bad case**: orders stay near ₹10,000cr a year, revenue grows about 8% a year, the margin slips to about 16% on fixed-price power lines and BharatNet, and the NTPC and GST disputes cost money. FY30 profit is about ₹1,000cr. The P/E falls a step to 6.5 (₹6,500cr, 0.5 times book). (₹1,000cr × 6.5) ÷ ₹7,503cr = 0.87 times, which is **−3.8% a year** with dividends.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 3.** The building arm borrows only ₹239cr and CARE rates it AA+. But consolidated borrowings are ₹5,286cr (Screener net debt ₹3,660cr, about 2.2 times operating profit, mostly project debt repaid by government annuities). On top of that sit ₹3,346cr of equity still to invest, about ₹2,449cr of bank guarantees, and NTPC's ₹90.87cr guarantee and surety claim.
- **Earnings quality: 2.** Five-year operating cash was −₹6,640cr against ₹5,527cr of profit, and −₹2,811cr in FY26. Most of that is HAM building spend counted as operating cash, which is really an investment that returns over 15 years or on sale at about 1.2 times book. But working capital rose from 117 to 148 days, ₹1,784cr is owed by GR's own HAM companies, and oil-and-gas bills are paid only on completion (May 2027).
- **Management: 2.** Q1 revenue beat (+33% standalone against about 15% guided), but Q4 FY26 missed (₹2,521cr against about ₹3,000cr) and FY27 orders are nowhere near the ₹20,000cr guide. Governance flags: an auditor emphasis on a 2022 CBI case (employees arrested with highway officials; stayed by the Gauhati High Court), an October 2025 tax search of offices and promoters' and the CFO's homes, a ₹321.6cr GST notice (writ filed 25 Sep), and the walk-away from NTPC's battery contract. Projects are sold to a trust GR sponsors. The board is family-heavy.
- **Moat and runway: 2.** The balance sheet is the only widening moat: it lets GR bid for HAM and toll work fewer rivals can fund. Highway building itself is commoditised (margin down from about 14% to 11%). Power lines, pipelines and fibre are new and fixed-price, and road awards have disappointed for three years.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Operating cash −₹6,640cr against ₹5,527cr of profit (FY22–FY26), and ₹3,346cr still to invest.** Earnings quality scores 2, and the exit P/E stays at today's 8 with no re-rating, because management plans more BOT investment.
2. **Q1 orders negligible against a ₹20,000cr FY27 guide; orders have missed for three years.** The path assumes ₹12,000–14,000cr a year and revenue growth fading from 15% to 8%. The bad case assumes ₹10,000cr.
3. **CBI case (auditor emphasis), Oct 2025 tax search, ₹321.6cr GST notice.** Management scores 2. A debarment by the highway authority (as happened to PNC) is the tail risk.
4. **NTPC invoked ₹90.87cr of guarantees after GR walked away from a battery contract.** It is in the bad case. It is under 1% of revenue.
5. **Standalone margin down from 12.65% to 11.0%; bids 30–40% below estimates; no escalation on power lines or BharatNet.** The path holds the building margin at 10–11%, not higher.
6. **Working capital up from 117 to 148 days; ₹1,784cr owed by its own HAM companies.** Earnings quality 2. No release of cash is assumed.
7. **Missed the Q4 FY26 revenue guide; the CFO's "execution won't increase" became a 15–20% guide a quarter later.** The guide is haircut by 4–8 points a year.
8. **Projects are sold to a trust GR sponsors (at about 1.2 times book).** The parts value takes project stakes at 0.9 times book, not at sale prices.
9. **Return on equity fell from 20% (10-year) to 9%; the shares are down 37% in a year, near the 52-week low of ₹770.** It is why P/B stays about 0.75 in the base case.
