# Time Technoplast

## Verdict for the portfolio
Not now. It is a steady compounder at a fair price: profit grew from ₹316cr in FY24 to ₹477cr in FY26, resin costs are passed on monthly, and net debt is nearly gone. But profit should grow about 16% a year to FY30, and the market is unlikely to pay more than about 17 times for a resin-linked packaging maker that turns only half its profit into cash. That gives about 17% a year. It is a reasonable holding for a 15% target but does not reach 25%.

## Clean profit base
- Screener (consolidated): FY26 profit ₹477cr; last four quarters ₹498cr (EPS ₹10.12 × 49.4cr shares; the minority share is small).
- One-offs: none of size. Interest on about ₹340cr of unused share-sale money sits in fixed deposits and is netted inside the interest line. It is temporary, a few crore a quarter. I trim about ₹3cr.
- Clean trailing profit: about **₹495cr**. P/E check: site 18.0, Screener 18.2, mine 17.7 on ₹8,775cr. These agree.
- Dilution: on 29 Sep the board approved merging its 74.86% subsidiary TPL Plastech, issuing about 79 lakh new shares (1.6%). I count the dilution and ignore the small extra profit it brings back.

- **Volume × price:** management guides 15% volume growth. It delivered 13.5% in FY26 and 11% in Q1 FY27, and CRISIL expects 8–10% revenue growth. I use about 11–12% volume plus a little price. Plants run at 80% in India and 85% abroad, so growth needs the planned ₹350cr of FY27 capex and ₹200–250cr a year after.
- **Margin:** 13–15% for a decade. Composites earn 17–18% against 12–13% for legacy packaging. I allow a drift to 15%, below management's 14–15.5%.
- **Interest and depreciation:** interest fell from ₹105cr (FY23) to ₹75cr (trailing) and floors at ₹35–40cr of guarantee and documentation charges (CFO). Most of that gain is in FY27–FY28. After that, profit grows with operating profit, about 11–13%. Depreciation rises from ₹190cr to about ₹250cr. Tax is 25%.
- **Track record:** it met its profit promises (FY26 +21% against at least 21%; Q1 +22%) but has missed on volume twice (13.5% and 11% against 15%). I haircut volume, not profit, for FY27.
- **Result:** FY30 profit about ₹870cr, about **16% a year** from ₹495cr. That is below management's 23–24% (true for FY27 only) and close to the note's 18%.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway in March 2030:** industrial packaging grows with chemicals and FMCG output (10–12%). It already holds about 55% of India's market and plants in 11 countries. Composites have a large but slow optional market: India has 32.6 crore LPG connections and 50 crore cylinders in use, but the oil companies have paused the 14.2-kg composite cylinder. There are also CNG cascades, hydrogen and 800,000 fire extinguishers a year for refineries. Management aims for "$1 billion of business in five years". The market will expect about 12% profit growth for FY31–FY33.
- **Anchors:** its own P/E was about 8 (2022), 18 (2023), 24 (2024), a peak of 31 (Dec 2024), 21 (July 2026) and 18 today. Global packaging peers (Greif, Mauser) are unlisted or trade at low teens. Indian plastics makers trade at 25–40 but earn better cash returns.
- **Exit P/E: 17**, in the middle of the 14–20 band for 8–12% growth and the bottom of the 18–26 band for 12–16%. It sits low because of resin exposure, 50% cash conversion and half the group audited by other firms. That is close to today's 18. No re-rating is assumed.

## Returns
- **Normal:** ₹870cr × 17 = ₹14,790cr in March 2030 against ₹8,775cr × 1.016 (TPL Plastech shares) = ₹8,915cr. Dividend yield 0.8%. ((14,790 ÷ 8,915)^(1/3.45) − 1) + 0.8% = **16.6% a year**.
- **Bad case:** volume grows 8–10% as CRISIL expects, the oil companies keep composite cylinders on hold, polymer swings squeeze the margin to 13–14%, and working capital stays at 110+ days. FY30 profit about ₹720cr. Exit P/E 13 (one band down). ₹9,360cr against ₹8,915cr, plus the dividend = **2.2% a year**.
- **Tail risk (separate):** the group auditor did not audit nine subsidiaries with ₹3,092cr of revenue and ₹259cr of profit, about half the group. The MD is also the CFO.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 4.** Net debt was ₹153cr at March (Screener), down from ₹812cr in FY22, and fell another ₹90cr in Q1. That is about 0.1 times operating profit. Against that, ₹400–500cr of bank guarantees are in use for government composite orders.
- **Earnings quality: 2.** Operating cash was ₹1,731cr against ₹3,451cr of operating profit over FY22–FY26 (50%). In FY26 it was ₹233cr, or 26%, and free cash was −₹137cr. Debtor days are 87, inventory 111 days and working capital 127 days. Q1 FY27 brought ₹155cr of operating cash.
- **Management: 3.** It meets profit guidance and raised the FY27 guide. Against that: repeated volume shortfalls, the MD doubling as CFO, half the group audited by others, a Systoverse deal size given as ₹25cr in the slides but ₹125–150cr by the MD, ₹340cr of share-sale money unused, and two independent directors leaving in September.
- **Moat and runway: 3.** It leads India's industrial packaging (about 55%), passes resin through monthly, and holds composite approvals from PESO and the oil companies. But drums and jerry cans are commodities, and composite demand runs on the state oil companies' timetable.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
1. **Only 26% of FY26 operating profit became operating cash; 50% over five years; free cash −₹137cr in FY26.** Earnings quality scores 2, and the exit P/E stays at 17. Management targets working capital of 100 days by March 2027 and 90 later. It is a check, not an assumption.
2. **Half the group (₹3,092cr revenue) audited by other firms; the MD is also CFO.** This is the tail risk and counts in the management score.
3. **Volume missed guidance: 13.5% in FY26 and 11% in Q1 FY27 against 15%.** The path uses about 11%. The bad case uses CRISIL's 8–10%.
4. **Composites depend on state oil companies, which paused the 14.2-kg cylinder launch.** No big LPG tender is in the base case. HPCL's 1.4 lakh cylinders (₹38cr) are small.
5. **Systoverse acquisition put at ₹25cr in the slides but ₹125–150cr by the MD; Elan/Ebullient deal on hold; ₹340cr of share-sale money idle.** Counted in the management score. No profit from these deals is in the base case.
6. **PE pipe sales depend on government contractors who wouldn't accept price rises (Q1 weak).** Pipes are a small slice, and the path does not rely on the 22–25% pipe growth guided.
7. **Promoter stake down from 51.6% to 47.5% (share sale); TPL Plastech merger adds 1.6% more shares.** Dilution is counted (d = 1.6%).
8. **Resin-linked: a 10% unrecovered cost rise would cut the margin by about 7 points.** Monthly re-pricing has held the margin at 13–15% through two shocks. The bad case takes it to 13–14%.
