# Gland Pharma

## Verdict for the portfolio
Gland is a good business with a believable plan: earnings can grow about 22% a year to FY30 as contract work grows, margins climb from 25% to about 30% and the French arm Cenexi recovers. But at 44 times profit, far above its own five-year median of about 29, the price already assumes most of that. A buyer today gets about 10% a year in the normal case. It does not meet the 25% target. It would be a buy at about ₹1,950 a share, against ₹3,066 today.

## Clean profit base
- FY26 reported profit: ₹1,027cr (Screener). Trailing four quarters to June 2026: ₹1,129cr.
- One-offs removed: currency gains booked in other income, about ₹51cr in Q4 FY26, less a ₹3.6cr currency loss in Q1 FY27 (CFO, Q1 FY27 call). That is about ₹47cr before tax and ₹34cr after. **Clean trailing profit is about ₹1,095cr.** On the same basis FY26 is about ₹985cr.
- Kept in: about ₹240cr a year of interest on ₹3,300–3,500cr of cash. This is about 15% of pre-tax profit. The cash is real and will partly fund the ₹2,000cr capex plan, so I keep the interest in profit and apply no extra value for the cash.
- Share-option costs (₹17cr in Q1) are kept as a real cost. The company's "adjusted" figures leave them out.
- P/E check: site 44.1 (₹50,597cr ÷ ₹1,147cr), Screener 43.6. They match. On clean profit the P/E is about 46.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue (₹cr) | Operating margin | Profit (₹cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 actual | 6,431 | 25.3% | 1,027 (clean ~985) |
| FY27 | 7,400 | 27% | 1,294 |
| FY28 | 8,500 | 28% | 1,555 |
| FY29 | 9,900 | 29% | 1,895 |
| FY30 | 11,600 | 30% | 2,316 |

What drives these numbers:
- **Revenue grows about 16% a year, below management's "around 20% over the next 4 years" (Q1 FY27 call).** The FY27 guide is 15% at constant currency, so I assume no more currency help after Q1. In May management guided 12–13% for FY27, 15% for FY28 and 19–20% for FY29, and 15% a year over three years.
- **The 20% guide came after one contract.** The 55-product transfer is worth $90–100m a year, or about ₹800–850cr, once every product has launched around CY2030. Revenue only starts in CY2029. On its own it lifts four-year growth from 15% to about 17%. Management said the rest depends on "a few other contracts that we are discussing". I give FY30 only about half a year of this contract.
- **Margin rises from 25% to 30%.** The Indian base business already earns 38% (41% in Q4 FY26). Cenexi earns 4% on about €190m of revenue and targets a high-single-digit margin in FY27 and mid-teens in the medium term. More contract work helps too. Management's goal is about 30% "in the near term" and 35% in three to four years. I stop at 30% because Cenexi has been "turning around" since 2023.
- **Costs below operating profit.** Depreciation rises from ₹424cr to about ₹620cr as the ₹2,000cr capex plan is spent: ₹550cr in FY27, including a ₹165cr oncology isolator line. Interest stays at about ₹30cr, because the only debt is at Cenexi (₹243cr). Other income is ₹250–300cr of interest. The tax rate eases from 27% to 26% as Cenexi turns profitable, which management also said.
- **Result: FY30 profit of about ₹2,316cr.** That is 22.5% a year from reported FY26 and 24% from clean FY26. Most of the growth comes from the margin, not volume.
- **Track record cuts both ways.** Q1 FY27 beat the guide: revenue +20% (15% at constant currency) against 12–13%, and adjusted margin 28% against 25–26%. But profit fell from ₹1,212cr in FY22 to ₹699cr in FY25, and five-year profit growth is 1% a year. That is why I haircut the 20%.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **In March 2030 the market can see three more years of growth.** These are the full year of the 55-product contract in FY31, GLP-1 cartridge volume once US patents expire in 2031–32 ("major volume ... FY30 and FY31"), microsphere and other complex generics that management says are "post-29", and an in-licensed liposomal product from FY30. Against that, US generic prices keep eroding (the market grows 3–4% a year, per management) and Cenexi's tender and contract business grows slowly. **I expect the market to price about 16% growth for FY31–FY33.**
- **Band and position.** That falls in the 16–20% band, which pays 24–34 times. Points for the upper half: net cash and a long, visible runway. Points against: return on capital of about 15% (not high), a Chinese promoter that trades with the company, and generic price-taking.
- **Anchors.** The stock's five-year median P/E is about 29 (MoneyWorks4Me). It trades at 44 today. The pharma sector average P/E is about 38 (Univest's sector figure).
- **Exit P/E: 30**, in the middle of the band and near its own median. Today's 44 is a rerating that earlier contract wins already earned. I see no reason the market keeps paying it once growth slows to the mid-teens.

## Returns
Market value today: ₹50,597cr (site, 15 Oct close). New shares: none. Dividend yield: 0.65%.
- **Normal:** ₹2,316cr × 30 = ₹69,480cr in March 2030. (69,480 ÷ 50,597)^(1/3.45) − 1 = 9.6%, plus 0.65% dividend = **10.3% a year**.
- **Bad:** Cenexi stalls at a low-single-digit margin, US price erosion speeds up and the 55-product launches slip into FY31, so revenue grows 11% a year to ₹9,760cr at a 27% margin. Profit is ₹1,680cr. P/E drops one band to 22: ₹36,960cr. (36,960 ÷ 50,597)^(1/3.45) − 1 + 0.65% = **−8.1% a year**.
- **Two cross-checks.** At an exit P/E of 34 (top of the band) the normal case gives 14.3%. On management's own plan (20% revenue growth, about 33% margin, about ₹3,050cr profit) at 34 times, it gives 23.8%. **Even the company's own plan does not clear 25%.**
- **Price that would work:** a 25% annual return needs about ₹3,640cr of FY30 profit at 30 times. On my profit path, it needs a share price near ₹1,950.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 5:** ₹3,294cr of net cash at June 2026 (company; ₹3,075cr at March on Screener). The only debt is ₹243cr at Cenexi. A Belgian Cenexi dispute is not quantified but is small next to the cash.
- **Earnings quality 3:** 61% of five years' operating profit came in as operating cash (₹4,098cr of ₹6,767cr, FY22–FY26). Inventory is 284 days and debtors 107 days. Interest on cash is about 15–20% of pre-tax profit. Currency gains swing profit (₹51cr in Q4 FY26).
- **Management 3:** It beat and raised in Q1 FY27, and Cenexi went from losses to a 4% margin. But profit fell three years running (FY23–FY25), the group had no CEO from May to 16 November 2026 (Deepak Sapra joins then) and the COO retired. The 15% to 20% guide jump rests on contracts not yet signed, and Fosun is both supplier and customer, and now JV partner too.
- **Moat and runway 4:** Each sterile plant is named in the product's US filing, and moving a product takes up to two years. Management says Western sites cost 5–6 times more. It has 342 US approvals, and its largest customer is only 13.5% of revenue. The weak spot is plain generic vials, where it takes the market price.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **Profit fell from ₹1,212cr (FY22) to ₹699cr (FY25), and five-year profit growth is 1% a year.** I haircut the 20% guide to 16% revenue growth and cap the margin at 30%.
- **Return on equity is only about 11%, because ₹3,300cr of cash earns about 7%.** This is why the exit P/E sits mid-band rather than at the top.
- **Cenexi earns 4% on about €190m and has been "turning around" since 2023.** My path needs Cenexi to reach only a high-single-digit margin. The bad case keeps it low.
- **61% cash conversion and 284 inventory days.** This puts earnings quality at 3 and argues against a top-of-band exit.
- **No CEO from May to November 2026, and the COO retired in March.** This is in the management score. The new CEO's first call (Q3 FY27) is a check.
- **The Chinese promoter Fosun (51.8%) buys from and sells to Gland. On 1 October 2026 Gland put $21m into a 60/40 US joint venture with a Fosun company.** This is a governance discount: mid-band P/E, management 3.
- **About 5 points of Q1's 20% growth was the weaker rupee, and an analyst questioned how constant-currency growth was worked out.** I assume no further currency help after Q1.
- **The 55-product contract pays only from CY2029, and "around 20%" depends on unsigned deals.** I count only half a year of the contract in FY30.
- **P/E 44 against a five-year median of about 29.** This is the main reason for the 10% answer.
- **The Belgian Cenexi dispute is not quantified.** It is noted and covered by net cash.
- **Fosun cut its holding from 57.9% to 51.8% in 2024.** It is noted. There has been no further selling since.

## Since the note was written
- The note was written on 28 September. On **1 October 2026** Gland invested **$21m (about ₹175cr) for 60% of Novelstar Pharma Inc.**, a new US company. A Fosun subsidiary owns the other 40% (NSE filing on the site's tracker). It is small, but it is another related-party tie to the promoter.
- Q2 FY27 results are not out. Last year's September-quarter board meeting was on 3 November 2025, so early November is likely. No Sep 2026 column is on Screener yet.
- The price moved from about ₹2,943 (₹48,565cr at writing) to ₹3,066 (₹50,597cr), and Screener shows ₹3,022 on 16 October.
