# State Bank of India

## Verdict for the portfolio
A sound, cheap, steady bank, but not a 25% stock. At 10 times profit and 1.4 times book it should earn about 12–13% a year to March 2030 (about 10% profit growth plus a 1.8% dividend), because the reasons for its low P/E (state control, a long history of bad-loan cycles, a thinner margin and capital cushion than private banks) will still be there in 2030. Keep the note's "own" as a steady holding; for the 25% target it is **Not now**.

## Clean profit base
I use **consolidated profit attributable to SBI's shareholders**, because the price includes the subsidiaries (SBI Life, SBI Card, SBI Funds Management, SBI General, SBI Caps).

| | Standalone bank | Consolidated, attributable |
|---|---|---|
| FY26 | ₹80,032cr | ₹85,168cr |
| Q1 FY27 | ₹21,121cr | ₹24,113cr |
| TTM to June 2026 | ~₹81,990cr | ~₹87,770cr |

- **One-offs in FY26.** Q2 FY26 included a ₹4,593cr gain on selling 13.18% of Yes Bank (adjusted Q2 profit was only ~₹16,700cr, down 9%). Q4 FY26 carried a ₹4,520cr mark-to-market loss on bonds. The two roughly cancel, so I treat FY26 ₹85,168cr as clean. Interest on tax refunds (₹1,002cr in Q4 FY26, ₹220cr in Q1 FY27) recurs at some level and is kept.
- **One-off coming in Q2 FY27.** SBI sold up to 12.83 crore shares (6.3%) of SBI Funds Management in its July 2026 IPO at a ₹545–574 band, about ₹7,000cr of proceeds at the top of the band and almost all gain. I strip it out of FY27.
- **P/E check.** Market value ₹886,154cr (site, 15 Oct; Screener ₹886,338cr) ÷ ₹87,770cr = **10.1**. The site shows 10.52 and Screener 10.5 (both closer to the FY26 base). No big gap. Price to book: ₹960 ÷ ₹674 consolidated book value = **1.42**.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
A lender's "revenue" is mainly interest less interest paid. I model loans, pre-provision operating profit (income less staff and other costs, before bad-loan provisions) and credit cost (loan-loss provisions ÷ loans).

| Year | Loans (₹ lakh crore, year end) | Operating profit before provisions (standalone) | Credit cost | Profit, consolidated attributable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | ~49.0 | ₹123,015cr | ~0.3% | ₹85,168cr |
| FY27 | 56.1 (+14.5%) | ~₹135,500cr | 0.35% | ~₹91,500cr (excl. ~₹6,000cr SBI MF gain) |
| FY28 | 63.4 (+13%) | ~₹152,500cr | 0.45% (ECL starts) | ~₹99,600cr |
| FY29 | 71.3 (+12.5%) | ~₹171,800cr | 0.45% | ~₹112,200cr |
| FY30 | 79.9 (+12%) | ~₹192,800cr | 0.45% | **~₹125,800cr** |

Drivers, from the latest call (7 Aug 2026):
- **Loans.** Guidance raised to 14–15% for FY27 (nominal GDP 12–12.5% plus 2–3 points). The 18.6% in Q1 was flattered by a weak base; the chairman called 18% "a little difficult". I use 14.5% then fade to 12%, roughly nominal GDP plus 1–2 points.
- **Margin.** Domestic margin guided at about 3% for FY27 (3.00% in Q1, 2.93% in Q4 FY26). Gold loans at 8.5–8.9% (₹3.1 lakh crore, growing ~100%) and cheap corporate loans pull it down; moving corporate loans to MCLR and lower deposit costs pull it up. Operating profit grew only 9.77% in Q1 against 18% loan growth, so I let operating profit ÷ average loans slip from 2.70% (FY26) to 2.55% and stay there. This also absorbs the next bank wage settlement (due from November 2027) and slowly falling pension costs.
- **Credit cost.** 0.27% in Q1 FY27, against the 0.5% the bank has guided for three years. Expected-loss provisioning (ECL, which makes banks provide for losses before loans turn bad) starts 1 April 2027; the bank will phase the one-time hit to net worth and says the yearly cost "will not have any major impact". I lift credit cost to 0.45% from FY28, short of a downturn.
- **Tax** 25.5%. **Subsidiaries** add ~₹5,100cr in FY26 (after removing their dividends to SBI), growing ~15% a year to ~₹9,000cr; SBI now owns 55.5% of SBI MF instead of ~62%.
- **Result:** FY30 profit ~₹125,800cr, **10.2% a year** from FY26. Return on assets ~1.03% (management promises "1% through the cycles"); return on equity falls from ~15% to ~14%.

**Track record:** the bank has met its recent guidance (domestic margin ~3% for FY26, credit cost well under 0.5%, return on assets above 1%) but missed its own ECL disclosure date twice. I take its numbers as given, but not the Q1 loan-growth pace.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**Runway:** strong and long. India's credit should grow at nominal GDP plus a little for years. SBI wants one extra point of market share in every district a year (it has about 20–22.5% now, aiming for 25%) and has a ₹9 lakh crore corporate pipeline of unused limits and new projects, plus new lending to data centres, batteries and transmission. In March 2030 the market will expect profit growth of about 11–12% for FY31–33.

**Exit P/E:** the spec's band for 8–12% growth (14–20) is for mid-sized private companies; the market has never paid that for a state bank. SBI's own year-end P/E over FY22–FY26 was 13.9, 9.3, 11.0, 9.7 and 11.2 (median 11, standalone), and the consolidated P/E today is 10.1. **Why it is cheap:**
1. The government owns 55.45% and can push lending, pay and pension decisions.
2. In the last bad-loan cycle return on equity fell to −2% (FY18) and 1% (FY19).
3. Its return on assets of ~1% is about half the big private banks'.
4. Assets are 14 times net worth, about double the private banks.

None of these is likely to clear by March 2030: the government will still control it, and ECL will be only three years old, untested by a downturn. So I set the **exit P/E at 10**, the same as today, with no re-rating. Cross-check: ₹1,258,000cr in 2030 is ~1.33 times my FY30 book of ~₹945,000cr, for a ~14% return on equity, a little below today's 1.42 times for ~15%.

## Returns
Formula: ((FY30 profit × exit P/E) ÷ (market value today × (1 + d)))^(1/3.45) − 1 + dividend yield. Market value ₹886,154cr (site, 15 Oct).

- **Normal case:** ₹125,800cr × 10 = ₹1,258,000cr; no new shares (profits and the SBI MF sale fund growth, and the chairman says capital covers ₹12 lakh crore of extra loans); dividend yield 1.8% (₹17.35 on ₹960). (1,258,000 ÷ 886,154)^(1/3.45) − 1 = 10.7%, plus 1.8% = **12.5% a year**.
- **Bad case:** farm, small-business and personal loans turn worse than the seasonal story (Q1 slippages were ₹7,046cr, early-overdue large loans ₹1,687cr). Under ECL credit cost rises to 0.8%, the domestic margin settles near 2.8% and loans grow 11%. FY30 profit ~₹94,000cr, exit P/E 8 (where state banks trade when bad loans rise), and a 3% share sale to rebuild capital. (752,000 ÷ 912,739)^(1/3.45) − 1 = −5.4%, plus 1.8% = **−3.7% a year**.
- **Tail risk** (not in the bad case): a repeat of the FY16–FY19 bad-loan cycle (return on equity near zero for three years), or the state using SBI for a rescue merger, loan waivers or directed lending.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 3.** Capital adequacy 15.67% (12.89% core) and bad loans at a two-decade low (1.47% gross, 0.38% net), but assets are 14 times net worth and the pension and gratuity funds are short by ₹42,073cr.
- **Earnings quality: 3.** For a bank, operating cash flow means little; I use clean-profit share and loss recognition. Provision cover is 74%, but reported profits lean on one-offs (Yes Bank ₹4,593cr, SBI MF ~₹7,000cr, bond swings of ±₹4,500cr) and on a 0.27% credit cost that is near the cycle low.
- **Management: 3.** Career bankers who met the margin, credit-cost and return-on-assets guidance and raised ₹25,000cr at ₹817 without fuss, but the ECL number slipped twice and the state picks the chairman and can direct the bank.
- **Moat and runway: 4.** India's largest deposit base (₹19.26 lakh crore in savings accounts, growing 10%) and a branch network no one can rebuild, with decades of credit growth ahead; but it has little pricing power (it loses corporate borrowers when it raises rates).

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **Deposits grew 9.7% against loans of 18.6% in Q1 FY27 (deposits +0.5% in the quarter); borrowings +43%.** The case grows loans only 12–14.5% and keeps margin pressure; ₹3–4 lakh crore of surplus government bonds and ~$10bn of NRI deposits fund the gap.
- **The ECL number was promised for Q4, then Q1, and slipped to Q2.** Credit cost is raised to 0.45% from FY28 and book value cut by ~₹25,000cr for the phase-in. A big Q2 number breaks the case.
- **Early-overdue large loans rose from ₹932cr to ₹1,687cr; slippages ₹7,046cr in Q1.** This is in the bad case; ₹1,400cr was recovered by August.
- **Credit cost of 0.27% is near the bottom of the cycle.** It is not extrapolated; I use 0.45% (0.8% in the bad case).
- **Domestic margin dipped to 2.93% in Q4 FY26, the thinnest of the three big banks; core operating profit fell 7% in Q2 FY26.** Operating profit ÷ loans is cut from 2.70% to 2.55%.
- **Return on equity was −2% to 1% in FY18–FY19.** This is why the exit P/E stays at 10 and is the named tail risk.
- **Pension and gratuity shortfall of ₹42,073cr (₹37,040cr a year earlier).** This, plus the next wage settlement, is in the cost line. Management says pension costs fall meaningfully only after 2035.
- **Assets are 14 times net worth; ₹25,000cr of shares were sold in July 2025 (~3.4% more shares).** No new shares in the base case, 3% in the bad case.
- **Some corporate borrowers left after the MCLR repricing (corporate loans −0.3% in Q1).** Loan growth fades to 12%.
- **Fee income was lifted by an accounting switch (about half of a ₹500cr rise in government fees).** Fees are not extrapolated separately.
- **The government owns 55.45%.** No re-rating is assumed.
- **The stock is down ~22% from its ₹1,235 high** after the Q4 margin miss and the ECL worries. The base case is built on today's price; the fall does not change the profit path.
