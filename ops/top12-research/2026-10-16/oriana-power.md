# Oriana Power

## Verdict for the portfolio
On the face of it this is cheap fast growth: 11 times profit, revenue up 84%, a large new battery award. Underneath, about 60% of FY26 revenue was plants sold to project companies it controls, it guarantees ₹1,385cr of their debt off its books, about ₹1,020cr of short-term bills are not counted as debt, and it missed almost every FY26 promise. Even letting profit grow ~12% a year to ~₹400cr in FY30, the return is about **12% a year**, and the bad case loses about a fifth a year. **Not now.**

## Clean profit base
- **Reporting.** Oriana is SME-listed and reports only half-yearly, so the latest full period is FY26 (to March 2026). H1 FY27 results are due by mid-November.
- **FY26 profit ₹252cr** (Screener; the company says ~₹250cr): H1 ₹122cr + H2 ₹130cr. Margin 22%; other income ₹27cr. No exceptional items are disclosed, so nothing is removed and **₹252cr is the base**.
- **The real question is quality, not one-offs.** ₹1,085cr of FY26 revenue, about 60%, was plants sold to promoter-group and temporarily controlled project companies not yet sold on to outside investors. Management says plain construction earns 5–10% and building plants to sell adds 6–8 points, so a large part of the 22% margin depends on later sales to outsiders. The first such sale only happened on 30 September: 110 MWp to Actis at an enterprise value of ₹501cr. I keep the reported base but haircut growth and the exit P/E for this.
- **P/E check:** ₹2,799cr (site, 15 Oct, ₹275.55) ÷ ₹252cr = **11.1**, matching the site (11.14) and Screener (10.8 at ₹268). The site's earlier stored trailing revenue (₹2,801cr) was wrong; ₹1,814cr is right.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue | Operating margin | Profit after tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | ₹1,814cr | 22% | ₹252cr |
| FY27 | ~₹2,400cr (+32%) | 20% | ~₹290cr |
| FY28 | ~₹3,100cr | 19% | ~₹340cr |
| FY29 | ~₹3,500cr | 18.5% | ~₹370cr |
| FY30 | ~₹3,900cr | 18% | **~₹400cr** |

**Drivers:**
- **Order book.** About ₹6,800cr unbuilt at June (3.7 times FY26 revenue), including the ₹1,200cr floating plant at Maithon Dam. On 18 September it won two Maharashtra (MSEDCL) battery awards: 900 MW / 1,800 MWh, under 15-year storage contracts at ₹2.38–2.40 lakh per MW a month. The ₹3,870cr "value" is 15 years of tariff (900 MW × ~₹2.39 lakh × 180 months), not construction revenue. Oriana will build the batteries in its own project companies with ~₹324cr of government viability-gap funding, then book the construction as revenue. That adds perhaps ₹2,000–2,500cr of construction revenue over FY27–FY29, but also more guaranteed project debt.
- **Plant sales.** The 1 GWp Actis programme: 238 MWp was promised for H1 FY27, then 200 MWp in H2, then ~400 MWp a year in FY28–FY29. Only 110 MWp had closed by 30 September.
- **Guidance.** Management guides 40–50% growth in revenue and profit for FY27 and FY28 ("~70%" if all goes well), with batteries 40% of FY27 revenue and green ammonia 10% of FY28 revenue.
- **My haircuts.** FY26 promises were missed:
  - revenue ₹1,814cr against "over ₹2,000cr";
  - profit +59% against "doubling";
  - 835 MW built against 1 GW;
  - the Actis sale slipped.

  The older targets ("FY28 profit equal to FY25 revenue", i.e. ~₹1,000cr; 1 million tonnes of hydrogen by 2030) were not credible, and the electrolyser gigafactory has been shelved. So I use about 32% growth in FY27, then 10–15% a year.
- **Margin.** It slides from 22% to 18% as battery construction (bought from China, priced at bid) grows and solar construction competition stays fierce.
- **Interest.** Rises from ₹63cr to ~₹150cr as bill discounting and borrowings fund the battery projects. Tax ~26%.
- **Result:** FY30 profit ~₹400cr, **12.2% a year**. Green ammonia (a ~₹3,000cr project, design due late 2026) is not counted beyond a small FY29–FY30 contribution.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**Runway:** India will keep adding solar and storage after 2030. Storage in particular should grow fast as daytime solar is curtailed. Oriana has 4,780 acres with grid connections and is one of the few firms to have commissioned batteries. The market in 2030 could expect 12–15% growth from a developer that keeps selling plants to investors.

**Why it trades at 11 (from ~90 two years ago):**
- Profits are largely booked on sales to its own project companies.
- Off-balance-sheet guarantees of about 2.1 times net worth.
- Short-term bill funding not counted as debt.
- SME listing with half-yearly, late results.
- Repeated missed promises.
- A tender drought in the sector: new solar awards were only 4.7 GW in FY27 to August.

Some of this could clear (main-board migration, regular Actis sales), but the business model is the related-party structure itself (74 subsidiaries, "100-plus group companies"), so I do not expect the discount to close by March 2030.

**Exit P/E 11**, the same as today. The spec's 18–26 band for 12–16% growth would apply only to clean, cash-backed profit.

## Returns
Formula: ((FY30 profit × exit P/E) ÷ (market value × (1 + d)))^(1/3.45) − 1 + dividend yield. Market value ₹2,799cr (site, 15 Oct). No dividend.

- **Normal case:** ₹400cr × 11 = ₹4,400cr. I assume 5% more shares (d = 0.05). No raise has been announced, but the 1,800 MWh battery equity, ₹1,640cr of group guarantees on a ₹763cr net worth, and the planned move to the main board make one likely; its return is in the path. (4,400 ÷ 2,939)^(1/3.45) − 1 = **12.4% a year**.
- **Bad case:** plant sales keep slipping and buyers pay less, and battery margins compress (rivals bid down to ~₹1.46 lakh). Revenue reaches only ~₹2,600cr at a 15% margin, interest stays ~₹130cr and profit is ~₹180cr. Exit P/E 8; a 10% share sale to shore up the balance sheet. (1,440 ÷ 3,079)^(1/3.45) − 1 = **−19.8% a year**.
- **Tail risk:** a lender calls a guarantee on project-company debt (₹1,385cr against ₹763cr of net worth), or the ₹375cr of loans and investments in group companies the auditor flagged has to be written off.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet: 1.** Borrowings ₹509cr, plus ~₹1,020cr of bill-discounting, letter-of-credit and advance liabilities, plus ₹1,385cr of guarantees on project-company debt outside reported contingent liabilities (₹1,640cr of group guarantees in all, 2.1 times net worth). Large battery projects come next.
- **Earnings quality: 1.** Screener shows 88% of five years' operating profit as cash. But FY26's ₹337cr of operating cash coincided with a ~₹690cr rise in discounted bills and similar short-term liabilities, so the cash was likely financed. 60% of revenue was sold to its own project companies, and the auditor made ₹375cr of group loans a key audit matter.
- **Management: 1.** It missed nearly every FY26 promise, has made outsized claims (FY28 profit of ~₹1,000cr, 1 million tonnes of hydrogen), shelved the electrolyser plant and stalled the Middle East plans. It files late with 100-plus subsidiaries, and promoters had 11.31% of the company pledged until 29 September 2026.
- **Moat and runway: 2.** A grid-connected land bank and real battery delivery are useful. But construction is competitive, it won no battery tender for months until September, and its margin depends on selling plants at a profit.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **₹1,085cr (60%) of FY26 revenue was sold to promoter-group or temporarily controlled project companies.** Growth is cut to ~12% and the exit P/E held at 11. The bad case assumes these sales slow.
- **₹1,385cr of project-company guarantees are outside reported contingent liabilities; group guarantees are ₹1,640cr, 2.1 times net worth.** Balance sheet scores 1; named as the tail risk.
- **About ₹1,020cr of short-term liabilities (bill discounting, letters of credit, advances) are not in Screener's ₹223cr net debt.** Interest is raised to ~₹150cr; earnings quality scores 1.
- **The auditor's key audit matter on impairment of ₹375cr of loans and investments in group companies.** Tail risk.
- **Missed FY26 on revenue, profit, megawatts and the Actis timing.** Guidance is haircut from 40–50% to ~32% in FY27 and 10–15% after.
- **Only 110 of the 238 MWp promised to Actis for H1 FY27 had closed by 30 September.** This is the first next check.
- **Outsized past targets (FY28 profit ≈ FY25 revenue, 1 million tonnes of hydrogen); electrolyser plant shelved.** Ignored in the path.
- **Promoters had pledged 11.31% of the company until 29 September; promoter holding fell from 61.4% to 58.0%.** Management scores 1, though the pledge release is a plus.
- **Repeated related-party votes:** 13 transactions approved in April, Sunpulse/Dynospark in July, Oriana USolar JV from 10 October. Exit P/E kept low.
- **Half-yearly, late results; 100-plus group companies.** No re-rating assumed.
- **CFO appointed in May 2023 with about two years of experience.** Noted.
- **The new 1,800 MWh battery award adds construction revenue, but also project debt it will likely guarantee.** In the path (revenue) and in the dilution assumption.
- **Sector air pocket: tender awards fell by about two-thirds in FY26; 4.7 GW in FY27 to August.** Growth slows to 10–15% after FY27.
- **Stock down 49% in a year (high ₹613, low ₹235).** The case starts from today's price.
