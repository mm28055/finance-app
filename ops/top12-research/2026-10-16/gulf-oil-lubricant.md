# Gulf Oil Lubricant

## Verdict for the portfolio
Not now. A well-run lubricant brand: volumes have grown two to three times the industry for 17 years, it holds net cash and it pays out 73% of profit (₹51 a share, a 4.7% yield). At about 13.6 times clean profit it is fairly priced. Q1 FY27's 17% volume jump was partly customers stocking up, and price rises will be partly given back, so I expect profit to grow about 9–10% a year to ~₹533cr by FY30. At an unchanged P/E of 14 plus the dividend, that is about 15% a year: a good steady holding, but not a 25% idea.

## Clean profit base
- **Reported:** FY26 profit ₹345cr (consolidated). Trailing four quarters to June 2026 ₹371cr (₹84cr + ₹76cr + ₹90cr + ₹121cr).
- **Removed:** the one-off ₹22.8cr charge for India's new labour codes in FY26 (~₹17cr after tax). That gives **clean TTM ≈ ₹388cr** and clean FY26 ≈ ₹362cr.
- **Kept:** Tirex (EV-charger subsidiary) losses, about ₹7cr in Q1 FY27 (consolidated ₹120.8cr against standalone ₹127.5cr), because they recur. Other income (~₹75–80cr) is interest on ~₹1,100cr of cash, but it is largely offset by ~₹56cr a year of interest on ₹462cr of short-term import credit for base oil, so the net treasury effect is small.
- **Not removed, but flagged:** Q1 FY27 profit of ₹121cr (+27%) included customers stocking up during the Hormuz disruption and price rises that management says will be partly rolled back. The FY27–FY28 path fades it.
- **P/E check:** site 13.54 (₹5,279cr) and Screener 13.8 (₹5,365cr) both imply ~₹389cr of trailing profit, matching my clean ₹388cr. No disagreement.

Drivers:
- **Latest calls (4 Aug 2026 Q1 FY27; 28 Sep 2026 investor conference, which leads):** volumes to keep growing two to three times an industry that grows 3–4% (6–8% in value, per the consultancy Kline); "we are not anticipating any major further price increases"; per-litre margin is the right measure for now, with 12–14% the medium-term band and 14–16% the aim over three to five years through premium oils (synthetics below 10% of volume, +1–1.5 points a year); 70% more capacity "in the next 6–8 months" for ~₹55cr; Tirex to reach ₹300–400cr of sales in three to four years, with its plant capacity up three to four times; cash kept for possible acquisitions, and payouts raised meanwhile. In August: "some of the growth this quarter has been due to more supply security", and B2C price rises cannot all be kept ("some rollbacks will happen").
- **Track record:** delivered. Volume +11% in FY26, +14% in Q4 FY26, +17% in Q1 FY27; margin 12.5–13.5% in every quarter for three years; EBITDA up 16% a year FY22–FY26 (₹285cr → ₹514cr). But profit growth slowed in FY26 (−3% reported, +1.5% clean) as interest rose to ₹56cr and Tirex lost money, and Tirex's Q1 was "slightly subdued".
- **Volume and per-litre profit:** lubricant volume +10% in FY27, then +8% a year (2–2.5 times the industry, a little below the claim). Operating profit per litre, about ₹31 in FY26 (my estimate, on roughly 1.68 lakh kL) and ₹35 in Q1 FY27 (₹166cr on 48,000 kL), eases to ~₹33 for FY27 and dips in FY28 as part of the price rises is handed back, then rises ~3% a year with premium mix. EBITDA ₹593cr → ₹629cr → ₹698cr → ₹775cr (about 11% a year from FY26).
- **Revenue:** +23% in FY27 on price rises (Q1 alone +31%), then flat-ish in FY28 as prices ease, then +10% a year; the percentage margin looks low (12%) because price rises inflate sales, as management warns.
- **Below the line:** depreciation ₹80cr → ₹100cr (new capacity and the Tirex plant); interest ~₹45cr; other income ₹80–85cr; tax 25.5%; Tirex near break-even by FY29.
- **Growth rate:** about 9% a year from clean TTM ₹388cr (10% a year from clean FY26). The note's estimate is 12%.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
In March 2030 the market will see an industry still growing 3–4% in volume (6–8% in value) despite electric vehicles. Electric two-wheelers were 6.5% and cars 4.6% of FY26 sales, and personal vehicles are only ~20% of lubricant demand. Add Gulf's record of share gains (7–8% share overall, 13–14% in vehicle-makers' franchise workshops, under 5% in industrial oils) and premium mix. The market will expect perhaps 8–10% growth → the 14–20 band. Upper-half features: net cash, 26% return on capital, a 73% payout and a 17-year record. Lower-half features: base oil is a crude-linked cost it cannot control; its 12–14% margin is about half of Castrol's; ₹43.8cr a year of royalty goes to a promoter-group company; and engine oil faces slow erosion from electric vehicles.

Peers: Castrol India trades at about 17–19 (Value Research, Craytheon, Share.market, Jul–Sep 2026). Gulf's own year-end P/E was 18.3 (2021), 10.1 (2022), 8.5 (2023), 15.0 (2024) and 15.7 (2025) (Value Research), and MoneyWorks4Me put 14.6 above its five-year median. So its normal is about 14–15, and today's ~13.6 is close to it. I see no discount that needs to clear, and I use **14**. At 15 the return is 17.5%; at today's 13.6 it is 14.3%.

## Returns
Market value ₹5,279.15cr (site, 15 Oct; Screener ₹5,365cr on 16 Oct). No meaningful dilution: share capital has stayed at ₹10cr; the promoter stake fell from 71.9% (Dec 2023) to 67.2% (Sep 2024) by a share sale, then to 66.8% (Sep 2026). Dividend yield 4.7%: ₹51 a share for FY26 on ₹1,065. The payout rose from 53% (₹25, FY23) to 57% (₹36), 66% (₹48) and 73% (₹51), funded from ₹299–355cr of yearly free cash.
- **Normal case:** ₹533cr × 14 = ₹7,462cr. (7,462 ÷ 5,279)^(1/3.45) − 1 + 4.7% = **15.3% a year**.
- **Bad case:** after the stock-up, volume growth falls to ~5% a year (barely above the industry). Price rollbacks and costly Group III base oil hold operating profit at ~₹30 a litre, and Tirex keeps losing money. FY30 EBITDA ~₹612cr, profit ~₹396cr; exit P/E one band down at 11 (it traded at 8.5–10 in 2022–2023). ₹396cr × 11 = ₹4,356cr → **−0.7% a year** (the 4.7% dividend included).
- **Tail risk (separate):** the cash pile goes into a large, poorly priced acquisition or a Hinduja-group deal (management says it is "evaluating" acquisitions), or a long Hormuz closure cuts base-oil supply outright.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 5/5:** net cash ₹641cr (Screener; ~₹680cr on the annual report: ₹1,141cr of cash against ₹462cr of short-term import credit) and no long-term debt, though gross borrowings rose from ₹365cr (FY24) to ₹567cr (FY26).
- **Earnings quality 4/5:** 66% of five years' operating profit came in as cash, but that includes a −₹24cr FY22. FY23–FY26 ran at about 100%, the cash cycle fell from 115 to 37 days, and the only one-off was a ₹22.8cr labour-code charge. Inventory rose ₹108cr in Q1 FY27 as stock got dearer.
- **Management 4/5:** has delivered its promise of two to three times industry volume and a 12–14% margin for years. Against that, ₹43.8cr of royalty goes to the promoter group, ~₹225cr was put into a loss-making EV-charger arm, promoters sold about 4.6% in 2024, and the head of strategy and the company secretary resigned in Aug–Sep 2026.
- **Moat and runway 3/5:** India's number-two private brand, 50+ vehicle-maker partners and a dense mechanic network win share. But it has no control of base-oil costs, it faces state-owned majors and Castrol, its margin is half Castrol's, and its core market grows only 3–4% a year with slow electric-vehicle erosion.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- Q1 FY27's 17% volume growth was partly customer stocking → FY27 volume +10%, then +8%; bad case 5%.
- Price rises will be partly rolled back ("some rollbacks will happen") → FY28 per-litre profit dips; FY28 profit growth only ~5%.
- FY26 profit fell 3% (labour-code charge, interest up to ₹56cr, Tirex losses) → labour charge removed; interest and Tirex kept.
- Group III base oil scarce; supply not normal until at least mid-2027 (Argus, ICIS) → bad case; tail risk.
- ₹43.8cr a year royalty to a promoter-group company (~12% of profit) → lower-half P/E; management 4, not 5.
- ~₹225cr into Tirex, still losing money, Q1 "subdued" → no Tirex profit before FY29; bad case keeps losses.
- Promoter stake fell from 71.9% to 66.8% → noted; no dilution.
- Head of strategy (31 Aug) and company secretary (16 Sep 2026) resigned → management 4; watch.
- Margin about half of Castrol's; 14–16% aim not yet shown → margin held at ~12% in the path.
- Electric vehicles slowly erode engine-oil demand (personal mobility ~20% of the market) → growth after FY30 capped at 8–10%.
- Management "evaluating" acquisitions with the cash → tail risk.
- Gross borrowings up from ₹365cr to ₹567cr in two years → balance sheet note.
