# InfoBeans Technologies

## Verdict for the portfolio
The cheapest and cleanest of this group, at 18.6 times profit, with no debt and a 29% return on capital. Revenue is growing about 20–23% in dollars. But it is a mid-size IT exporter: the market will not pay much more than today's multiple, and part of this year's growth is a weak rupee. My base case is about 16% a year, with a modest bad case of about −5%. **Not now** against a 25% target. It remains a fair small holding for someone happy with mid-teens returns.

## Clean profit base
- **Filed figures (Screener, consolidated):**
  - FY26: revenue ₹514cr (+30%), operating profit ₹113cr (22%), other income ₹25cr, pre-tax ₹112cr, net ₹87cr.
  - Last four quarters (Sep-25 to Jun-26): revenue ₹554cr, operating profit ₹121cr, other income ₹17cr, pre-tax ₹113cr, net **₹85cr**.
- **What I removed.**
  - **FY26 grant.** FY26 included a one-off US employee-retention grant (₹6.3cr in Q1 FY26). That quarter is no longer in the TTM.
  - **Tax catch-up.** Q1 FY27 carried a one-time tax charge from moving to the new tax regime (rate 26%). I leave it in, since it is small.
- **Clean profit:** about ₹85cr (TTM Jun-26), and FY26 about ₹82cr. Treasury income is about ₹17cr pre-tax (15% of pre-tax profit), roughly ₹13cr after tax. I keep it but note it.
- **The P/E.** The site's 18.6 matches Screener's 19.4 at today's slightly higher price (₹170).
- **Cash.** Screener's net cash is ₹209cr; true cash and investments are about ₹230–240cr, which is **13–15% of today's value**. The company's slides show ₹360cr by counting ₹108–119cr of receivables as cash.

## Profit path FY27–FY30

|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | 514 | 22% | 87 (about 82 clean) |
| FY27 | 650 | 21% | ~95 |
| FY28 | 748 | 21.5% | ~112 |
| FY29 | 852 | 22% | ~131 |
| FY30 | 963 | 22% | **~148** |

**Drivers.**
- **Revenue.**
  - Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹153cr, +37% in rupees. In dollars that is roughly +23%: about $16.2m against $13.1m, at average rates of ₹94.7 and ₹85.6.
  - FY27 assumes quarterly growth of 3–4% to about ₹650cr (+26%, including about 7 points of currency).
  - After that, +15%, +14%, +13% a year with a steady rupee. Management's goal is to "double every three years" (26% a year) including acquisitions. I assume none, and haircut organic growth because only about 7% of revenue comes from new clients.
- **Margin.** Management targets 24% EBITDA, including other income. It is deliberately stepping down to fund AI tools and US sales, with a 9–12-month lag before the spending brings revenue. I hold the filed margin at 21–22% (FY26 22%, Q1 21%).
- **Below the margin.** Depreciation rises from ₹24cr to about ₹32cr as the Indore campus is built. Other income is about ₹18cr. Tax is 25%.
- **Growth rate:** about 16% a year (₹85cr to ₹148cr over 3.75 years).
- **Track record.** Q1 FY27 met the 14% net-margin target. The 24% EBITDA target was missed, but management had warned about that. The FY23–FY24 slowdown is the warning: revenue fell, the margin dropped from 24% to 14%, and profit fell from ₹55cr (FY22) to ₹22cr (FY24).

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
**The runway.**
- InfoBeans serves 50 large clients, 18 of them in the Fortune 500, with 94% returning each year and an average tenure of 9+ years. Europe has grown from nil in 2016 to 38% of revenue.
- In 2030 the market will expect perhaps 10–13% growth for a small custom-engineering firm in an AI era where clients want more work for the same money. That sits in the 14–20 to 18–26 bands.

**Anchors.**
- Peers trade at 14.2 times (Saksoft), 21.7 times (Happiest Minds) and 20.6 times (Newgen) (Screener, 16 Oct).
- InfoBeans itself traded at about 13.5–29 times trailing profit in the past year (₹119–₹258).

**Choice.** Net cash and a 29% return on capital argue for more; promoter selling, client concentration and the risk of AI cutting billable work argue for less. **I use 18**, about today's multiple, and do not assume a re-rating.

## Returns
Market value ₹1,581cr (site, 15 Oct). Dilution 0.5% (small option grants). Dividend yield 0.3% (Screener).
- **Normal case:** ₹148cr × 18 = ₹2,664cr. (2,664 ÷ (1,581 × 1.005))^(1/3.45) − 1 + 0.3% = **+16.5% a year**.
- **Bad case:** the rupee recovers towards ₹88 (InfoBeans is unhedged), dollar growth slows to about 8%, and the margin slips back to about 18%, as in FY24. FY30 profit is about ₹95cr and the exit P/E is 14. ₹1,330cr gives **−4.7% a year**.
- **Bull check:** ₹160cr at 20 times gives about 23% a year. It still does not clear 25%.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 5/5:** about ₹230–240cr of cash and investments against ₹15cr of lease borrowings, and no guarantees noted.
- **Earnings quality 4/5:** five-year operating cash of ₹337cr against operating profit of ₹369cr (91%), and free cash of ₹71cr in FY26 against ₹87cr of profit. Debtor days are 77, and three clients owe 66% of receivables.
- **Management 3/5:**
  - It met its 14% profit-margin target and has delivered growth for six quarters.
  - But promoters cut their stake from 74.40% (Dec 2025) to 72.17% (Sep 2026), including a share gift to a trust and open-market sales.
  - Its slides count receivables as cash.
  - It dropped currency hedging in January 2026 and gives no revenue guidance.
- **Moat and runway 3/5:** sticky, long relationships and elite Salesforce/ServiceNow partner status. But custom engineering is crowded, the top five clients are 40% of revenue, and AI may shrink billable hours.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- **About a third of recent rupee growth is currency, and InfoBeans has been unhedged since January 2026.** The base case assumes a flat rupee. The bad case takes it back towards ₹88.
- **Promoters are selling: 74.40% down to 72.17% in nine months.** They had a window to sell or pledge up to 3% until October 2026. Management is scored 3. If selling continues after October, that is a break trigger.
- **Slides count ₹108–119cr of receivables as cash.** I use Screener's net cash of ₹209cr and true cash of about ₹230–240cr.
- **The margin is below its own 24% target and being stepped down for AI and US sales.** It is held at 21–22% in the base case and 18% in the bad case.
- **Top five clients are 40% of revenue and the top ten 57%; three clients owe 66% of receivables.** This is a reason for the middle-of-band exit P/E. No single client is above 10%.
- **Only about 7% of revenue comes from new clients.** Growth after FY27 is cut to 13–15%.
- **In the last slowdown profit fell 60% (₹55cr in FY22 to ₹22cr in FY24).** That is the shape of the bad case.
- **AI may deflate custom-engineering hours.** Management says clients ask for efficiency, not discounts. The exit P/E is not lifted.
- **A one-off US grant (₹6.3cr) flattered FY26.** Excluded from the clean base.
