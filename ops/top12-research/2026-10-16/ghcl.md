# GHCL

## Verdict for the portfolio
Not now. GHCL is a low-cost soda-ash maker with over ₹1,000cr of net cash (about 30% of its ₹3,550cr market value) and a steady ₹12 dividend (3.1%), priced at about 7.4 times clean profit. But its plant is full, so profit can grow only if soda-ash prices recover or the new bromine unit adds a little, and the price is set by Chinese, Turkish and US imports with no duty protection. I get ~₹590cr of profit by FY30 (about 6% a year). At its own five-year P/E of about 8 that is roughly 12% a year including the dividend. The low P/E has lasted five years and I do not expect it to clear: the commodity price stays out of its hands, and a ₹4,000–5,000cr greenfield hangs over the cash.

## Clean profit base
- **Reported:** FY26 profit ₹472cr (consolidated). Q1 FY27 profit ₹191cr, of which ₹40cr (net of tax) was an exceptional gain from a one-time settlement by the company's ESOS (employee share) trust; profit before it was ₹151cr.
- **Removed:** the ₹40cr exceptional. Trailing four quarters: ₹107cr + ₹106cr + ₹116cr (Q2–Q4 FY26, Screener consolidated) + ₹151cr = **clean TTM ≈ ₹480cr**.
- **Not removed, but flagged:** Q1 FY27's 29.1% margin (company basis) included higher war-time prices and cheap raw-material and energy stock that the MD called transient ("I would caution against reading this as a new normal"). At FY26's ~23% margin, Q1 would have earned roughly ₹34cr less after tax, so the normal run-rate is nearer ₹445–470cr. The FY27 path below carries that fade.
- **Treasury income:** ~₹65–70cr a year of other income comes from ~₹1,000cr of cash and investments, about 10% of pre-tax profit. I keep it in profit, but it means the operating business trades at only ~6 times its earnings once the cash is set aside.
- **P/E check:** site 7.78 (₹3,550cr ÷ ~₹456cr) and Screener consolidated 7.87 use slightly different trailing figures, because Screener had not loaded the consolidated Q1 FY27 quarter; Screener standalone shows 7.39. On my clean ₹480cr it is 7.4. No large disagreement.

Drivers:
- **Latest call (3 Aug 2026, Q1 FY27):** global soda ash still oversupplied, Chinese stocks high, "a meaningful reduction in the supply remains some way off"; Indian demand soft near term; margins to "moderate from current level and revert to more normalized trend"; judge margins on the year, "broadly in the range we have delivered over the last year". Bromine and vacuum salt: commercial production in Q2, revenue "maybe in the fourth quarter", full use in FY28 at ₹150–170cr of revenue and 40–45% margins. FY27 capex ₹140–150cr. Greenfield: land acquisition unresolved, no timeline. Solar glass soda-ash use from ~1.5 to ~3.5 lakh tonnes once plants start in January–March 2027 (~8% of Indian demand). Safeguard limit on imports "still under consideration".
- **Track record:** costs and plant use delivered (96–100% use; dense ash raised from 40% to 55% of output without capex). But the new unit slipped from Q4 FY26 to Q1 to Q2 FY27 (bromine started 12 Oct 2026; vacuum salt not yet filed), and the FY27 contribution fell from ₹120cr of revenue (May) to Q4 only. The greenfield was first approved in FY2017 and has not started.
- **Volume:** soda ash flat at capacity (12 lakh t); bicarbonate from 80–85% use towards 90%; core revenue +3% a year from FY28 (a little volume, rupee weakness raising the import-parity price, solar-glass demand).
- **Price and margin (core):** FY27 23.6% (Q1 27% standalone, then ~22.5%), 23% in FY28, 24% in FY29–FY30. That is a slow move back towards the FY23–FY26 average of about 27%, not all the way, because China has added ~7 million tonnes of cheap natural capacity and Tata Chemicals sees world demand growing only ~0.2% in FY27 against 3.8 million tonnes of new supply.
- **Bromine and vacuum salt:** ₹40cr of revenue in FY27, ₹130cr in FY28, ₹160–165cr after, at 35–40% margins (below the 40–45% guided, since bromine prices swing).
- **Below the line:** depreciation ₹111cr → ₹125–136cr as the ₹330cr unit is capitalised; interest ~₹8–10cr; other income ₹70–75cr on the cash; tax 25.5%. No greenfield spending of size before FY30 in the base case.
- **Growth rate:** about 6% a year (₹480cr → ₹593cr). The note's estimate is 4% (range −3% to 9%).

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
In March 2030 the plant is still full, so the market will expect growth for FY31–FY33 only from soda-ash prices, which it cannot forecast, or from the greenfield, which would add volume only with ₹2,000–2,500cr of debt and about ₹0.3 of sales a year per rupee spent. That is under 8% expected growth, the 10–15 band, and the lower half because the price is set by imports. But GHCL has not traded at 10 in five years: year-end P/Es were 6.7 (2021), 8.1 (2022), 4.2 (2023), 5.3 (2024) and 9.4 (2025) (Value Research), the five-year median is about 8.3–8.5 (MoneyWorks4Me) and the average 7.2 (Trendlyne). Today's ~7.4 is in that range.

The reasons for the discount are the commodity price, no duty protection, the greenfield and a 19.8% promoter stake. None is likely to clear by March 2030. A safeguard limit would help, but India never notified the anti-dumping duty its own trade authority recommended, so I give it no credit. I use **8**, its own median. Allowing for the cash, that equals about 6 times operating profit plus ~₹1,500cr of cash in March 2030 at face value, so it does not undervalue the cash pile. At 10 the return would be 19.1%; holding today's 7.4 gives 9.4%.

## Returns
Market value ₹3,550.25cr (site, 15 Oct; Screener ₹3,596cr on 16 Oct). No dilution; the FY26 ₹300cr buyback cut the share capital from ₹96cr to ₹92cr, and any repeat would be extra upside I do not count. Dividend yield 3.1%: ₹12 a share in each of FY24, FY25 and FY26 (payout 14%, 18%, 23%; the policy was raised to 25% of profit in May 2026), on ₹385.
- **Normal case:** ₹593cr × 8 = ₹4,744cr. (4,744 ÷ 3,550)^(1/3.45) − 1 + 3.1% = **11.9% a year**.
- **Bad case:** imports keep flowing at 73–74,000 tonnes a month, the Indian price slips again, and the core margin settles near 20% (FY26's weakest quarters were 21–22%). Bromine earns less (~₹45cr), and the board starts spending cash on the greenfield, so other income falls to ~₹40cr and interest rises. FY30 profit ~₹402cr; exit P/E 6 (its 2021–2024 lows were 4.2–6.7). ₹402cr × 6 = ₹2,412cr → **−7.5% a year** (dividend included).
- **Tail risk (separate):** the board commits to the ₹4,000–5,000cr greenfield at today's prices with ₹2,000–2,500cr of debt; or an outage at Sutrapada, its only soda-ash plant.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 5/5:** net cash ₹1,049cr (Screener; "more than ₹1,000cr" at June), borrowings ₹83cr, CARE AA-; the only shadow is the undated greenfield that would need ₹2,000–2,500cr of debt.
- **Earnings quality 4/5:** 78% of five years' operating profit came in as cash (₹3,621cr of ₹4,651cr) and about 100% in FY26, but stocks run at 199 days (four to five months of raw materials) and Q1 FY27 carried a ₹40cr exceptional gain and transient inventory benefits.
- **Management 3/5:** a cost leader that paid out 87% of FY26 profit, but the bromine unit slipped two quarters, the greenfield approved in FY2017 is still not started, its green-tribunal challenge was disclosed in the annual report but not raised on either call, and the MD was paid ₹14.9cr in FY26 (about 3% of profit).
- **Moat and runway 2/5:** a low-cost plant with ~26% of Indian demand, but no pricing power (the Indian price is the landed cost of imports), no duty protection, and no volume growth without a low-return greenfield.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- Profit fell from ₹1,142cr (FY23) to ₹472cr (FY26); FY26 soda-ash price −7% → base assumes only a slow recovery to a 24% margin; bad case 20%.
- Imports set the price; anti-dumping duty recommended but never notified; only 7.5% customs duty → no credit for protection; lower-half P/E.
- Imports rebounded to 73–74,000 t a month in Q1 FY27 from 45–46,000 → bad case.
- China added ~7 Mt of natural soda-ash capacity in three years; record Chinese stocks → margin held below the FY23–FY26 average.
- Q1 FY27 margin of 29.1% called transient; ₹40cr exceptional gain → exceptional removed; FY27 margin fades to ~22.5% after Q1.
- Bromine and vacuum salt slipped from Q4 FY26 to Q2 FY27; FY27 revenue from ₹120cr guided to Q4 only → ₹40cr in FY27, margins below guide.
- ₹4,000–5,000cr greenfield, first approved in FY2017, under NGT challenge, would need ₹2,000–2,500cr of debt → kept out of the base; in the bad case and tail risk; exit P/E held at 8.
- Plant at 96–100% use, so no volume growth → growth ~6% a year; exit band under 8%.
- One soda-ash site (Sutrapada) → tail risk.
- Inventory at 199 days → earnings quality 4.
- Promoters own 19.8%; foreign funds cut from 26.4% (Jun 2025) to 22.0% (Sep 2026) → noted; part of why the P/E stays low.
- MD pay ₹14.9cr in FY26 → management 3.
- Coal up from ~$120 to $136 a tonne; limestone imported from the Middle East → margin assumption below FY25's 28%.
