# TD Power Systems

## Verdict for the portfolio
The best business story in this group: global engine and turbine makers are short of generators, and TD Power is one of the few independent suppliers they trust. But at 87 times profit the share price assumes the boom lasts well beyond 2030. Even if profit grows about 26% a year to ₹596cr in FY30, a realistic exit P/E of 29 gives a **loss of about 9% a year**. **Not now.** It would need a price near ₹290 to earn 20% a year.

**Where it sits among the five:**
- Most expensive at 87 times profit (Atlanta 61, Pitti 31, Dynamic 22, Transrail 15).
- Strongest balance sheet: about ₹900cr net cash after the October share sale.
- Second-fastest grower I expect (about 26% a year from FY26).
- Weakest cash conversion after Transrail and Atlanta, and the most concentrated customer list.
- Ranks last of the five on base return (−9% a year).

## Clean profit base
- **Reported (Screener, consolidated):** FY26 profit ₹239cr; last four quarters to June 2026 ₹275cr (Q2 FY26 ₹60cr + Q3 ₹56cr + Q4 ₹72cr + Q1 FY27 ₹86cr).
- **One-offs:**
  - Q4 FY26 carried a one-off late-delivery penalty on a Turkish contract that cut the gross margin by about 3 points (about ₹18cr before tax). I did **not** add it back; such penalties can recur in a business running at full capacity. Adding it back would give about ₹288cr.
  - Treasury income is excluded by management from its margin, but on Screener other income is only ₹20cr in the last four quarters. Kept.
- **Clean base: ₹275cr, last four quarters.**
- **P/E check:** the site shows 83.5, using the old share count of 31.25cr shares × ₹734.75. Screener shows 86.9 (₹23,901cr ÷ ₹275cr). Screener's market value already includes the 12.5 lakh shares issued to the promoters (21 Sep) and the 77.4 lakh QIP shares (12 Oct). I use Screener so that no further dilution needs adding.

## Profit path FY27–FY30
| Year | Revenue | Operating margin | Profit after tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (actual) | ₹1,856cr | 18.0% | ₹239cr |
| FY27 | ₹2,600cr | 18.5% | ₹360cr |
| FY28 | ₹3,250cr | 18.5% | ₹449cr |
| FY29 | ₹3,950cr | 18.5% | ₹519cr |
| FY30 | ₹4,600cr | 18.5% | ₹596cr |

**Drivers.**
- **FY27, as guided: ₹2,600cr (+40%), raised in August from "₹2,400cr-plus".** Q1 revenue was ₹640cr (+72%). Q1 orders were ₹734cr, against a guided ₹700cr a quarter (₹2,800cr for the year). The ₹2,208cr order book "will be executed this year".
- **FY28: ₹3,250cr.** Management says capacity is about ₹3,200cr "plus or minus" after ₹50cr of debottlenecking, and orders should grow 20–25% next year (Q4 FY26 call).
- **FY29–FY30: ₹3,950cr and ₹4,600cr.** The next capacity round is meant to take sales capacity "to ₹4,000cr and above". It includes rotor capacity for generators up to about 200 MW. Machine tools take 15–16 months to arrive, so the big ramp-up lands in calendar 2028 (FY29). The plan and its cost were promised for the Q2 call.
- **Margin 18.5%:** the middle of the 18–19% management has guided for years; Q1 was 19.1% on Screener. Price clauses, currency and mix hold it. "We are not going to make the same kind of money that the turbine guys make."
- **Below the operating line.**
  - Other income: ₹30–45cr. The ₹675cr raised plus about ₹240cr of cash earns interest until spent on the large-generator plant and on working capital ("all our retained earnings are basically going into working capital").
  - Depreciation: rises from ₹30cr to ₹70cr once the new plant arrives in FY29.
  - Interest: about ₹5cr. Tax 26%.
- **Track record.** Management has met or raised its guidance for several years; Q1 beat Q4 as promised. I take its numbers, but I do not add the extra growth some brokers expect from large generators in FY29. That business is still unannounced and competes with Siemens, Baker Hughes (Brush), Mitsubishi, Toshiba and Fuji.
- **Result:** FY30 profit is ₹596cr, about 26% a year from FY26 and 23% a year from the last four quarters.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway.**
  - Its customers are doubling engine and turbine capacity by 2030 (management). GE Vernova reported 116 GW of gas backlog plus slot reservations in Q2 2026, is taking 2031 reservations, and plans to raise output from 20 GW a year to 30 GW in 2030.
  - Large generators (100–200 MW) open a much bigger market, and combined-cycle conversion of today's open-cycle plants would add steam-turbine generators later.
  - India adds only 10–12% a year (management).
- **Against that.**
  - By 2030 this will be a five-year-old boom built partly on slot reservations (about 63 of GE Vernova's 116 GW), which are softer than orders.
  - Ten customers are 84% of revenue.
  - TD supplies what its customers build, so a pause in US data-centre gas plants would cut its orders about a year later.
  - The market in March 2030 is likely to price **about 17% a year** growth, not the 25%+ of today.
- **Exit P/E: 29, inside the 24–34 band for 16–20%.**
  - Pulled down: 36% cash conversion over five years and 84% customer concentration.
  - Pushed up: net cash, 34% return on capital and a ten-year Siemens Energy agreement.
  - So the middle of the band.
- **Anchors:**
  - Peers on Screener (16 Oct): Triveni Turbine 46, Elecon 35, Siemens Energy India 68, GE Vernova T&D 75, CG Power 93.
  - TD Power's own five-year P/E ranged 17–92, with a median of 40 (22–23 in 2021–22, 45–47 in 2024–25). Today's 87 is near the top.
  - I do not assume today's P/E survives to 2030. Even at 40, the five-year median, the return is about 0% a year.

## Returns
Market value used: **₹23,901cr (Screener, 16 Oct; includes the September and October share issues)**. New shares d = 0, since they are already counted. The cash they raised is in the other-income line. Dividend yield 0.14%.

- **Normal case:** ₹596cr × 29 = ₹17,284cr. ₹17,284 ÷ ₹23,901 = 0.723. 0.723^(1/3.45) − 1 = −9.0%, plus 0.14% dividend = **−8.8% a year**.
- **Bad case** (US data-centre gas build pauses in FY28–FY29; orders fall back toward ₹500cr a quarter):
  - FY30 revenue ₹3,400cr at a 17% margin. Profit before tax ₹543cr, profit ₹402cr.
  - Exit P/E one band down: 22 (18–26 band).
  - ₹402cr × 22 = ₹8,844cr. ₹8,844 ÷ ₹23,901 = 0.370. 0.370^(1/3.45) − 1 = **−24.9% a year**.
- **Tail risk:** a top customer (INNIO, Siemens or a US turbine maker) moving generator work in-house or to a rival. Ten customers are 84% of sales.
- **Price that works:** ₹9,252cr market value (about ₹288 a share) for 20% a year; ₹8,035cr (about ₹250) for 25%.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 5/5.** Borrowings ₹18cr against about ₹240cr of cash in June, plus ₹675cr raised in September–October. CRISIL AA−. No material guarantees seen.
- **Earnings quality 2/5.** Five-year operating cash of ₹353cr against ₹972cr of operating profit (36%). FY26 free cash flow ₹19cr. Debtor days rose from 110 to 146 and inventory days are 148. Management says payment terms "cannot be altered significantly because otherwise we will lose business".
- **Management 3/5.**
  - Delivered: it beats and raises (FY27 guide lifted to ₹2,600cr; Q1 beat Q4 as promised).
  - But it raised ₹600cr from funds plus ₹75cr from promoters at ₹600, below the then market price of about ₹719, while holding net cash.
  - The MD cut his stake from 11.2% to 8.3% in FY26, and the promoter group went from 34% to 27% over three years.
  - It declines to give data-centre exposure or customer advances.
  - The head of operations left in March 2026 after five months; a new CEO joined in April.
- **Moat and runway 4/5.** Qualified with all major engine and turbine makers (INNIO since 2009, a Siemens licence since 2012, a ten-year Siemens Energy agreement from August 2026). The generator is under 5% of a plant's cost, so buyers do not switch lightly. The runway beyond 2030 depends on one end-market cycle.
