# Pitti Engineering

## Verdict for the portfolio
A good, sticky business: its parts are designed into motors for Wabtec, Siemens, Cummins and Caterpillar, and steel costs pass through. But the headline P/E of 31 flatters it. About ₹22cr of the last year's ₹124cr profit was a one-off government incentive that does not recur this year. On clean profit the P/E is about 39. Debt-funded plants also keep returns on capital near 15%. On my numbers profit grows to ₹245cr by FY30, about 20% a year from FY26, helped by the incentive returning from FY28. At an exit P/E of 22 that gives **about 10.5% a year**. **Not now**; it fits below about ₹770.

**Where it sits among the five:**
- Middle on price: 31 times reported profit, 39 times clean (Transrail 15, Dynamic 22, Atlanta 61, TD Power 87).
- Middle on growth: about 20% a year.
- Best cash conversion of the five (84% of operating profit over five years).
- Most net debt (₹491cr) and the lowest return on capital (15%).
- Third on base return (10.5%), behind Dynamic (17.4%) and Transrail (14.5%).

## Clean profit base
- **Reported (Screener, consolidated):** FY26 profit ₹118cr; last four quarters to June 2026 ₹124cr.
- **Removed:**
  - **Incentive income:** about ₹29cr before tax (₹22cr after) of other income, mostly a state investment incentive. Other income in the last four quarters was ₹34cr: ₹21cr in Q2 FY26, then ₹7cr, ₹5cr and ₹1cr. Its normal level is about ₹5cr a year, and management says no incentive will be booked in FY27.
  - **Low tax:** about ₹3cr from Q1 FY27's unusually low 19% tax rate (a deferred-tax effect on leased assets; management guides 25% for the year).
- **Clean base: about ₹99cr, last four quarters.**
- **P/E check:**
  - **Reported:** site 30.6, Screener 30.9 (₹3,846cr ÷ ₹124cr).
  - **Clean basis: 38.8.** The site's P/E understates the price by about a quarter.

**Drivers.**
- **FY27.**
  - **Volumes:** targets raised in August to 82,000 tonnes of laminations (69,500 in FY26) and 17,000 tonnes of castings (12,012).
  - **Revenue, ₹2,250cr:** management's "about ₹2,300cr at current steel prices" from May, slightly haircut. Q1 was ₹529cr (+16%).
  - **Operating profit, about ₹360cr:** management guides about ₹370cr before ₹10cr of share-option costs. That is a margin of about 16%, because pass-through steel inflates revenue.
- **FY28: ₹2,500cr at 17–17.2%, management's own figure at about 90,000 tonnes.** The ₹150cr expansion (started a quarter early) and modular machining additions cover it. Machining runs at 86% and is the bottleneck, not orders.
- **FY29–FY30: ₹2,800cr and ₹3,150cr.**
  - The ₹290cr Hyderabad foundry and machining plant (₹60cr spent) starts in Q1 FY30, adding about ₹350cr of sales at full use, at a 25–28% margin per management.
  - Machining comes in steps from FY28.
  - A further lamination expansion: management says ₹400cr in Bengaluru, not yet approved, takes sales to "₹3,000–3,300cr comfortably".
  - Margin rises to 17.5%, below the "18–19%" management talks of, because each new plant carries costs before it fills.
- **Below the operating line.**
  - **Incentive:** about ₹30cr a year before tax is booked again from FY28 (management: about ₹40cr a year on the ₹400cr Aurangabad investment, if the 9-year claim period is granted). I take ₹30cr because the claim period is not settled.
  - **Interest:** ₹82–95cr (net debt about ₹491cr, plus most of the foundry and part of Bengaluru on debt).
  - **Depreciation:** ₹125–165cr (in line with Arihant's ₹132–152cr for FY27–FY29). Tax 25%.
- **Cross-check.** Arihant Capital (BUY, 12 Aug) has FY29 revenue of ₹2,740cr and profit of ₹191cr. My FY29 ₹209cr is a little higher: I use the 25% tax management guides, Arihant about 30%.
- **Track record.** Recent promises were kept or beaten (targets raised, expansion early, net debt cut faster). But FY26 profit fell (₹122cr to ₹118cr) and Q1 pre-tax profit was flat. Plants keep arriving before the last one fills.
- **Result:** FY30 profit is ₹245cr: 20% a year from FY26 reported, 27% a year from the depressed clean base. Without the incentive, FY30 is about ₹222cr, 24% a year from the clean base.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
- **Runway.**
  - The Hyderabad foundry fills in FY31–FY32 at higher margins.
  - Global makers are moving lamination, casting and machining from China and Europe to India: Progress Rail and Siemens Mobility were added, and there is a ₹500cr multi-year Wabtec deal.
  - Every motor and generator needs a laminated core.
  - Mining, oil and gas doubled to 10% of revenue; data-centre generators are 5%.
  - The market in 2030 will likely expect **about 15% a year**.
- **Exit P/E: 22, the middle of the 18–26 band for 12–16%.**
  - **Pulls it down:** return on capital of 15% and net debt of about 1.5× a year's operating profit, in a capital-hungry business.
  - **Pulls it up:** 84% cash conversion and a long, diversified customer list.
  - **Anchors:**
    - Its own five-year P/E was 12–49, median 27.8. That median was earned while returns on capital rose; they are now falling.
    - Arihant values it at 25 times FY29 earnings.
    - Pitti has no close listed peer. Its customers' Indian arms trade much higher (Siemens Energy India 68, CG Power 93), but they are not comparable.
  - I do not use today's 31 because it rests on a one-off incentive.

## Returns
Market value used: **₹3,846cr (Screener, 16 Oct)**. No new shares assumed. The September 2026 merger of Pitti Industries and Dakshin Foundry folds in companies already owned. Dividend yield 0.25%.

- **Normal case:** ₹245cr × 22 = ₹5,390cr. ₹5,390 ÷ ₹3,846 = 1.401. 1.401^(1/3.45) − 1 = 10.3%, plus 0.25% = **10.5% a year**.
  - At an exit P/E of 26 it is 16.0%.
- **Bad case** (data-centre and mining orders cool, which management itself warns "can grow very fast and correct very fast"; margin stuck at 16%; Bengaluru built on debt):
  - FY30 revenue ₹2,700cr. Interest ₹105cr; incentive ₹20cr. Profit before tax ₹187cr, profit ₹140cr.
  - Exit P/E one band down: 17 (14–20 band).
  - ₹140cr × 17 = ₹2,380cr. ₹2,380 ÷ ₹3,846 = 0.619. 0.619^(1/3.45) − 1 = −13.0%, plus 0.25% = **−12.7% a year**.
- **Tail risk:** a rating downgrade, or a fresh share sale, if net debt rises well above twice operating profit while two plants are built (Ind-Ra's trigger is 2.0×; it was 2.4× at December 2025).
- **Price that works:** ₹2,894cr market value (about ₹768 a share) for 20% a year; ₹2,513cr (about ₹667) for 25%.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 3/5.** Net debt about ₹491cr at June 2026 (company), about 1.5× the last year's ₹327cr operating profit. Ind-Ra's net-leverage measure was 2.4× at December against a 2.0× downgrade trigger. ₹290cr more capex is under way, mostly debt-funded, with a possible ₹400cr after that.
- **Earnings quality 4/5.** Five-year operating cash ₹884cr against ₹1,052cr of operating profit (84%), the best of the five. Debtor days fell from 78 to 39. But about ₹40cr of FY26 profit was incentive income, inventory days are 124, and free cash flow was negative or nil in FY24–FY26 because of capex.
- **Management 4/5.** It raised volume targets, finished the ₹150cr expansion a quarter early, cut net debt from ₹555cr to ₹491cr in a quarter, and talks openly about falling returns. Minor flags: a ₹78 lakh state GST settlement (October 2026), and land leased from the founder and his wife.
- **Moat and runway 4/5.** Each part is approved for a specific motor, so switching means re-testing. It is one of few suppliers offering laminations, castings, machining and assembly under one roof. The top ten customers fell from 68% of revenue (FY23) to 51% (FY25). Loose laminations themselves are commodity-like, and China has the most capacity.
