# DDev Plastiks

## Verdict for the portfolio
Not now. A real market leader (a third of India's cable-insulation compound market, 31% return on capital, no net debt) at about 13 times clean profit. But it is a thin-spread converter: filed profit per kilo has been flat at about ₹14.2 for three years, only 47% of five years' operating profit turned into cash, Q1 FY27 volume grew 1% against a 15% target, and the battery-storage venture has already slipped and had its margin guide cut. On ~10% a year volume growth, profit reaches about ₹308cr by FY30; at an unchanged P/E of 13 that is about 13% a year, roughly 15% at 14.

## Clean profit base
- **Reported:** FY26 profit ₹202cr; trailing four quarters to June 2026 ₹213cr (₹47cr + ₹48cr + ₹55cr + ₹64cr).
- **Removed:** Q1 FY27 carried a war-risk premium on exports that the CFO put at "approximately 2.5–3 rupees" a kilo and said "may not be sustainable in each and every quarter". On 52,163 tonnes that is ₹13–16cr before tax, about ₹11cr after. Removing it gives **clean TTM ≈ ₹202cr**, the same as FY26.
- **Not adjusted:** Q4 FY26 other income of ₹12cr included a ~₹10cr mark-to-market gain on export receivables, but the CFO said ~₹11cr of forward-contract losses sat in other expenses, so the net was ~₹1cr. Normal other income is ~₹20cr (₹17cr in FY25).
- **P/E check:** site 12.61 (₹2,692.67cr ÷ ₹213cr = 12.6) and Screener 12.8 (₹2,719cr) agree. On clean ₹202cr it is 13.3.

Drivers:
- **Latest call (11 Aug 2026, Q1 FY27):** FY27 volume target 2,31,000t (+15%) kept, which needs about 60,000t a quarter for the rest of the year after Q1's 52,163t (+1%); Bhiwadi (48,000t, ~₹80cr) ran at 20–25% and should average ~50% in FY27; capacity 3,16,400t now, 3,34,400t by March 2027; FY27 capex ₹150–175cr from own cash; normal EBITDA ₹15–17 a kilo (company basis, which includes other income), "at least 200 rupees per ton every quarter" from mix; cash cycle to return to 55–60 days; ₹5,000cr core revenue by FY30 "firmly on track". Battery storage "delayed by a couple of quarters", moved from Gujarat to West Bengal, phase-one margin 6–8%.
- **Track record:** FY26 volume +6% ("would have approached 10%" without the war); FY25 profit +2%, FY26 +9%. Battery storage was announced in January 2026 for supply from 1 August 2026; in May it was ₹200–250cr of FY27 revenue and a 10%+ margin from FY28; by August it was delayed with a 6–8% margin. Filed operating profit per kilo was about ₹14.2–14.3 in FY24, FY25 and FY26, so the quarterly mix gains have not yet shown in the annual figures.
- **Volume:** I run about a year behind the plan: +7% in FY27, then +12%, +11%, +10% as Bhiwadi, HFFR (10,000 → 20,000t) and the eastern medium-voltage plant fill. That is ~10% a year, in line with management's own 12–13% for its cable customers less some slippage. FY30 core revenue ~₹4,425cr against the ₹5,000cr target.
- **Profit per kilo (filed):** ₹15.1 in FY27 (Q1 at ₹17.4 with the premium, ~₹14.3 after), ₹14.7 in FY28, rising to ₹15.1 by FY30 on more HFFR and higher-voltage XLPE. Revenue uses ~₹150 a kilo (₹165 in FY27, as resin prices stay high for part of the year); revenue moves with resin, profit per kilo does not.
- **Battery storage:** 1 GWh (₹800–900cr of sales at full use) starting late FY28; ₹5cr of profit in FY29 and ₹15cr in FY30 after interest on ₹100–150cr of working capital and depreciation, i.e. well below management's numbers.
- **Below the line:** depreciation ₹24cr → ₹40cr; interest ₹38cr in FY27 (Q1 ₹11cr) then ~₹32cr; other income ~₹20cr; tax 25.5%.
- **Growth rate:** about 11–12% a year (₹202cr → ₹308cr), matching the note's 11%. Management's full plan (₹5,000cr core at ~11% plus ₹2,000–2,500cr of battery storage) would imply roughly ₹450–500cr; I give it about 60% of that.

## Growth after FY30 and the exit P/E
In March 2030 the market will see a long runway: India's transmission spending (about ₹9 trillion to FY32), renewables and data centres, a move up to 220 kV compounds (certification expected around 2030) and exports helped by the EU trade deal. It will expect perhaps 10–12% growth for FY31–FY33 → the 14–20 band. Lower-half features dominate: only 47% of five years' operating profit became cash; margins are a thin pass-through spread (a 10% cost rise not passed on would wipe out most of the margin); battery storage is low-margin, uses Chinese cells and needs bank lines to scale to 5 GWh; and institutions own almost none of it (domestic funds 0.06%, foreign 1.56%) because promoters hold the legal maximum 74.99%. Upper-half features: 31% return on capital (45% in FY22), no net debt, a third of the market and long customer approvals.

The stock's own year-end P/E was 7.1 (FY23), 9.8 (FY24), 14.2 (FY25) and 9.7 (FY26) (StockAnalysis), with a three-year median of about 15 (MoneyWorks4Me). Today's ~13 on clean profit is in the middle of that range. The reasons for the low multiple (cash that lags profit, a commodity-like spread, a tiny free float) are structural and I do not expect them to clear by 2030. I use **13**, unchanged from today and just below the band floor because of the cash conversion. At 14 the return is 15.3%.

## Returns
Market value ₹2,692.67cr (site, 15 Oct; Screener ₹2,719cr on 16 Oct). No dilution: no equity raised since the 2022 demerger listing; battery storage and capex funded from cash and bank lines. Dividend yield 0.7%: ~₹1.75 a share for FY26 (9% payout; final ₹1.25 proposed on the Q4 call) on ₹260; payout has been 4–10% of profit.
- **Normal case:** ₹308cr × 13 = ₹4,004cr. (4,004 ÷ 2,693)^(1/3.45) − 1 + 0.7% = **12.9% a year**.
- **Bad case:** volume keeps growing only ~6% a year as in FY26 (≈2,54,000t by FY30), profit per kilo stays at ₹14.2, interest stays near ₹40cr as the cash cycle sits at 85 days, and battery storage loses ~₹5cr; FY30 profit ~₹219cr; exit P/E one band down at 10 (its FY24 and FY26 year-end levels). ₹219cr × 10 = ₹2,190cr → **−5.1% a year**.
- **Tail risk (separate):** battery storage scaling toward 5 GWh on borrowed working capital in a market where bidders are forfeiting deposits, or large cable makers starting to make their own compounds.

## Quality
- **Balance sheet 4/5:** Screener shows ₹57cr of borrowings against ~₹64cr of cash and investments (₹7cr net cash) and CRISIL rates it A+/Stable, but CRISIL counts ~₹223cr of working-capital lines and Q1 interest rose to ₹11cr.
- **Earnings quality 2/5:** ₹509cr of operating cash on ₹1,081cr of operating profit over FY22–FY26 (47%); free cash was −₹11cr in FY26; the cash cycle stretched from 41 days (FY23) to 85 (FY26) and inventory of ₹392cr was an auditor's key audit matter.
- **Management 3/5:** clean promoter record (74.99% steady, no pledges, small group purchases), but Q1 volume grew 1% against a 15% guide and battery storage went from "supply from 1 August 2026" to a two-quarter delay, a new state and a margin guide cut from 10%+ to 6–8% within seven months; an independent director resigned in July 2026.
- **Moat and runway 4/5:** a third of XLPE, half of Sioplas, the only Indian maker for 66–132 kV, 3–4 years of field approval per voltage step, zero rejections and a grid build-out to FY32; held back by a fixed spread that gives no pricing power and customers who could compound in-house.

## Worst facts and how the case deals with them
- Q1 FY27 volume +1% against a 15% full-year target → FY27 volume +7%, a year behind plan.
- Q1 profit per kilo included a ₹2.5–3 war premium → removed from the base; FY28 per-kilo profit set below FY27.
- Filed profit per kilo flat at ~₹14.2 for FY24–FY26 → only ₹0.2 a kilo a year of mix gain assumed.
- 47% five-year cash conversion; FY26 free cash −₹11cr; cash cycle 41 → 85 days → exit P/E kept at 13; earnings quality 2.
- Inventory ₹392cr a key audit matter → earnings quality 2.
- Q1 interest ₹11cr; ~₹223cr of working-capital lines (CRISIL) → interest held at ₹32–38cr; bad case ₹40cr.
- Battery storage delayed, relocated and margin guide cut to 6–8% → only ₹15cr of FY30 profit; tail risk.
- Profit grew only 2% in FY25 and 9% in FY26 → growth capped at ~11%.
- Cable makers could make their own compounds; no anti-dumping protection; Dow and Borealis import → lower-half P/E; tail risk.
- Domestic funds hold 0.06% and foreign funds 1.56%; promoters at the 75% cap → small float noted as a reason the P/E stays low.
- Independent director resigned (July 2026) → management 3.
