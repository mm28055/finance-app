# Indian EMS & Electronics — sector research (28 Sep 2026)

Scope: electronics manufacturing services (EMS) and electronics system design & manufacturing (ESDM) — PLI and the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS), mobiles, room ACs and consumer electronics, semiconductor assembly/test (OSAT/ATMP), working capital and margins. Note on method: web search was unavailable for part of this session, so sources are official/government pages, rating-agency rationales and company documents opened directly (all listed at the end).

## Market size and growth
- India's electronics exports rose 24.7% to US$48.0 bn in FY26, driven mainly by smartphones; the electronics import bill crossed US$100 bn for the first time at US$116.2 bn (+17.8%). Electronics is now India's third-largest export category (US$37.7 bn in FY25). [IBEF ESDM page, updated Sep-26, citing MeitY]
- Smartphones were 62% of the ESDM market in FY25, growing at a 28% CAGR FY20–FY25; smartphone units rose from 2 in 2014 to 300 in FY26; India passed China as the largest smartphone exporter to the US in Q2 FY26. [IBEF Sep-26]
- Mobile-phone manufacturing reached ₹5.45 lakh cr in FY25 (28x since FY15) and mobile exports ₹2.06 lakh cr (130x). [ICEA website, chairman's message]
- Government/industry target: US$500 bn electronics production by 2030 (ICEA says with US$180–200 bn exports); MeitY's ECMS portal quotes "more than US$150 bn today". [ECMS portal; ICEA]
- Forecasts: IBEF expects the ESDM industry to grow at a 20–25% CAGR over five years and roughly double to ₹7–8 lakh cr (US$80–91 bn) by 2030; KPMG (as cited by Centum) puts the Indian EMS market at US$40–45 bn in FY25 rising to ~US$150 bn by FY30, and the global EMS market at US$640–650 bn in 2025 rising to US$1.2–1.25 trn by 2035 (APAC 68% of the global market). [IBEF Sep-26; Centum IP Sep-26 citing KPMG]
- Room ACs: ICRA estimates industry volumes hit an all-time high of 12.5–13.0 m units in FY25, contracted in FY26 on a weak summer and high base, and should grow 10–12% a year over the medium term. [ICRA Epack Durable rationale, Jan-26]
- Defence/space electronics: Deloitte/FICCI-EY figures used by Centum — India defence production ~₹1,541 bn (FY25) → ~₹3,000 bn by FY29 (~18% CAGR); India space economy ~US$12 bn (2025) → US$44 bn (2033). [Centum IP Sep-26]

## Demand drivers
- China+1 sourcing by global OEMs and the US tariff reshuffle (India overtook China in smartphone exports to the US). [IBEF Sep-26; Aimtron and Centum calls]
- Domestic under-penetration (ACs, appliances), a GST cut on ACs in FY26 and replacement demand; ICRA expects RAC demand to recover after the FY26 dip. [ICRA Epack Jan-26]
- Defence indigenisation, space programmes and railway signalling for high-reliability EMS (Centum order book ₹1,797 cr, Vinyas ₹1,309–1,400 cr). [CARE Centum Sep-26; CRISIL Vinyas Aug-26]
- AI data-centre build-out: pulls memory and power-electronics demand (Sahasra memory exports, Aimtron data-centre UPS order) but also creates component shortages. [Sahasra H2 call; Aimtron H2 call; Centum Q4 call]

## Policy (subsidies, duties, rules)
- **PLI (mobiles/LSEM):** the large-scale electronics PLI attracted ₹15,172 cr of investment and 1.71 lakh jobs by Dec 2025. Dixon met thresholds under four PLIs (mobiles, telecom, LED, AC PCBs) for FY25 and shares part of the incentive with clients. [IBEF Sep-26; ICRA Dixon Jan-26]
- **PLI (white goods, ACs/LED):** Virtuoso says AC PLI is worth ~1% of AC EBITDA and ends after FY27, to be renegotiated with brands. [Virtuoso Q1 FY27 call]
- **ECMS (component scheme):** Budget 2026–27 raised the outlay to ₹40,000 cr; MeitY approved 29 more projects with ₹7,104 cr investment and ₹84,515 cr expected production (14,246 jobs). Incentives are turnover-linked (6 years incl. 1-year gestation), capex-linked (5 years) or hybrid, across display and camera modules, multilayer and HDI/flexible PCBs, passives, Li-ion cells, enclosures, capital equipment and optical transceivers. The application window for segments A, B, C and E closed on 30 Sep 2025; segment D (supply chain/capital equipment) stays open to 30 Apr 2027. [IBEF Sep-26; ECMS portal]
- **Semiconductors:** Semicon 1.0 (₹76,000 cr) approved 12 manufacturing units worth over ₹1.64 lakh cr (one silicon fab, one SiC fab, one GaN micro-LED display fab and nine packaging units); Micron, Kaynes and CG Semi are in commercial production; first fab due in 2028. Cabinet approved **Semicon 2.0 on 15 Jul 2026 with ₹1,27,500 cr** across six pillars including "further strengthening the ATMP/OSAT industry"; the minister said ~₹1 lakh cr of investment commitments had been received by 17 Sep 2026, to materialise over 2–3 years. Rajasthan's first OSAT (Sahasra, Bhiwadi) opened May 2026. [PIB 15-Jul-26; PIB 17-Sep-26; ISM website]
- **Budget/MeitY:** MeitY's FY27 allocation ₹21,633 cr, 6.9% below FY26 BE; ISM 2.0 given ₹1,000 cr for FY27. [IBEF Sep-26]
- **Trade rules:** compressor import cap at 40% of FY25 base (extended to March 2027) and QCOs on copper/aluminium push localisation but raise input costs in the short run. [Virtuoso Q4 & Q1 calls]

## Cost drivers
- Components are mostly imported: Virtuoso, Vinyas (70–75% imported inputs) and Dixon (cells and components) are exposed to forex and supply shocks. [ICRA Dixon Jan-26; CRISIL Vinyas Aug-26]
- 2026 shock: West Asia war lengthened logistics; copper-clad laminate and memory lead times stretched; some semiconductor lead times went from 4–6 weeks to over a year with prices doubling; copper and aluminium rose. [Centum Q4 call; IKIO Q1 call; Virtuoso Q1 call]
- Pass-through is the norm (BOM-plus/cost-plus) but with a 1–3 month lag, so margins dip when costs spike. [IKIO Q4 call; Virtuoso Q1 call]
- Labour: minimum-wage revisions and the new labour codes raised staff costs in 2026. [IKIO Q1 call; Sahasra AR FY26]

## Competitive structure and share
- Scale end (mobiles, TVs, appliances): Dixon is the largest; clients include Motorola, Xiaomi, Samsung, Longcheer, Airtel, Jio, HP, ASUS; five-year revenue CAGR ~55%. [ICRA Dixon Jan-26]
- RAC contract manufacturing: Amber, PG Electroplast, Epack, Virtuoso compete for Voltas, Havells, Godrej, Blue Star and others. [ICRA Epack Jan-26; ICRA Virtuoso Sep-26]
- High-mix industrial/defence/medical EMS: Kaynes, Syrma, Centum, Avalon, Cyient DLM, Vinyas, Aimtron — many players, low entry barriers in pure EMS (CARE), higher barriers where qualification is long (defence, space, aerospace, NADCAP/AS9100). [CARE Centum Sep-26]
- New entrants: L&T commissioned an EMS facility (raised on Aimtron's call); two new refrigerator-compressor makers expected FY27–FY28. [Aimtron H2 call; Virtuoso Q4 call]

## Where profit sits
| Model | Typical EBITDA margin | Working capital | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prescriptive mobile/consumer EMS (customer buys/nominates parts) | ~3.5–4% | near zero (NWC/OI 0–3%) | ICRA Dixon Jan-26 |
| RAC and appliance contract manufacturing | 6–10% (AC ~6–8%, components 10–12%) | high, seasonal (NWC ~40% of sales at Virtuoso) | ICRA Virtuoso Sep-26; Virtuoso IP |
| Industrial/defence high-mix EMS (cost-plus) | 9–12% | 115–250+ days | Centum Q4 call; CRISIL Vinyas Aug-26; Aimtron/IKIO Screener |
| ODM / design-led, niche lighting | 13–20% | 200+ days inventory | IKIO Q1 call; Aimtron H2 call |
| Build-to-spec defence/space systems | 18–20%+ | 225–350 days | Centum IP Sep-26 |
| Memory ATMP/OSAT | break-even at small scale; 16–20% targeted at scale | inventory-heavy | Sahasra H1/H2 calls |
- Pattern: the more design and qualification the supplier owns, the higher the margin — and the heavier the working capital. Pure-assembly volume players earn thin margins but high ROCE; high-mix players earn wider margins but tie up cash (all six of the small caps researched here — Centum, Aimtron, Vinyas, Virtuoso, IKIO, Sahasra — had negative free cash flow in FY26 on Screener).

## Working capital (why it matters)
- Dixon's model keeps NWC at 0–3% of revenue because suppliers are often tied to customers (back-to-back payments) and customers guarantee some creditors. [ICRA Dixon Jan-26]
- By contrast, Centum carried ~250 inventory days (auditor's key audit matter), Vinyas a 232-day cash cycle, Aimtron 256 working-capital days, IKIO 253 days; CARE and CRISIL both flag working-capital intensity as the main credit weakness for these profiles. [CARE Centum Sep-26; CRISIL Vinyas Aug-26; Screener]
- The 2026 component shortage pushed companies to stock ahead (spot buying, annual orders), so inventory rose again in H1 FY27. [CARE Centum Sep-26; IKIO Q1 call; Aimtron H2 call]

## Cycle position (Sep 2026)
- Structural growth phase for exports and components (ECMS approvals, Semicon 2.0 commitments), but a **cost up-cycle** for inputs (memory, CCL, copper/aluminium) that squeezes margins with a lag.
- Room ACs: FY26 was a down year after the FY25 peak; FY27 recovery under way (Virtuoso Q1 revenue +85%), ICRA expects 10–12% medium-term volume growth.
- Memory: consumer memory prices "recently gone down" after an AI-driven spike (Sahasra) — volatile.
- Valuations of listed EMS names already assume multi-year 25–40% growth (P/Es 34–100x for our six small caps on Screener).

## Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecaster named)
- **KPMG** (via Centum): Indian EMS market US$40–45 bn (FY25) → ~US$150 bn (FY30), implying ~27–30% a year.
- **IBEF**: ESDM 20–25% CAGR, ~₹7–8 lakh cr by 2030; electronics production ambition US$500 bn by 2030 (government/ICEA target, not a forecast).
- **ICRA**: RAC volumes +10–12% a year medium term (Jan-26); Dixon OPM 3.5–3.7% near-to-medium term (Jan-26); Virtuoso revenue growth >50% in FY27 (Sep-26).
- **CARE Ratings**: Centum 20–25% revenue CAGR with 50–100 bp margin gain (Sep-26).
- **CRISIL**: Vinyas 20–30% growth at 11–12% margin as the upgrade path (Aug-26).
- **MeitY/PIB**: Semicon 2.0 investments of ~₹1 lakh cr to land over 2–3 years; first Indian fab 2028.
- My synthesis: the sector should compound revenue at 20–30% a year through FY29, but margins in volume EMS stay low single digits; value accrues to firms that own design, qualification (defence, aerospace, medical) or scarce components (PCBs, compressors, memory packaging) — provided they can finance the working capital without heavy dilution.

## Key risks for the sector
- Policy dependence: PLI tapering (AC PLI ends after FY27), import caps/QCO reviews (compressors March 2027), ECMS approvals turning into production on time.
- Component and logistics shocks (2026 war) and forex; customer concentration (one brand often 25–60% of a supplier's sales).
- Equity dilution to fund capex and working capital (Aimtron ₹205 cr preferential; Virtuoso repeated issues; Vinyas warrants).
- Governance at smaller listed EMS firms: related-party trading and promoter-group mergers (Aimtron, Vinyas, Virtuoso, Sahasra) — see company files.
