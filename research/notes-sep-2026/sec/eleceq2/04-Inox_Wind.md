## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Inox Wind

## Executive Summary
Inox Wind exists to facilitate India's structural energy transition from infirm renewable generation to firm, round-the-clock (RTC) power by providing end-to-end wind energy solutions. The company solves a critical bottleneck for independent power producers (IPPs) and Commercial & Industrial (C&I) clients by supplying technologically advanced wind turbine generators (WTGs) and maintaining them through their lifecycle. 

Historically a highly cyclical EPC player, Inox Wind is currently transitioning from a mid-tier **Challenger** into a highly integrated **Category Leader** in the Indian wind ecosystem. The firm is engineering a major strategic pivot—moving away from cash-intensive turnkey projects toward a lighter equipment-supply model, heavily backstopped by massive captive order flows from its promoter group. While this internal synergy guarantees volume and insulates the firm from broader market cycles, the true test of its leadership will be navigating severe industry-wide delays in customer site readiness, optimizing a bloated working capital cycle, and overcoming self-inflicted wounds from erratic execution and volatile margin guidance.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** Wind Turbine Generators (currently transitioning to a new 4X / 4.4 MW platform with prototype installation in August), limited-scope EPC services, and high-margin, long-term Operations & Maintenance (O&M) services via its subsidiary, Inox Green. 
- **Who pays them?** Marquee IPPs (e.g., First Energy, Juniper), C&I customers (Aditya Birla, Jindal), and increasingly, its own promoter group's IPP arm (Inox Clean/Inox Green Energy). 
- **How do they make money?** Project-based equipment sales and EPC milestones form the volatile top line, while 15-to-20-year O&M contracts form the highly visible, recurring cash flow base. 

### Segment & Geographic Mix
*Note: As Inox Wind operates exclusively in India, geographic concentration is 100% domestic. Segment breakdown reflects current strategic shifts.*

| Segment | Revenue Dynamics | Commentary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wind Turbine Equipment Supply** | Growing Substantially | The strategic focal point. The company is deliberately shifting away from turnkey projects to preserve working capital, with equipment supply now constituting 59% of the third-party order book. |
| **Turnkey EPC** | Declining by Design | Being phased down to selective projects (currently 41% of third-party orders) due to historic working capital blockages and execution bottlenecks. |
| **O&M Services (Inox Green)** | High Growth / High Margin | Represents the crown jewel of profitability. Margins hover near ~50%. Set to see multi-fold growth as NCLT approval paves the way for the consolidation of a 6.5 GW inorganic portfolio. |

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
- **Is the moat Durable or Fleeting?** The moat is shifting from fleeting to **Durable** on paper, but **narrowing** in practice due to erratic execution and deteriorating transparency.
- **Evidence:** The company has successfully backward-integrated into critical supply chain nodes (expanding its Jaipur factory for 4.9 MVA and 100 MVA transformers, proprietary cranes, and power electronics), buffering it against geopolitical supply shocks and maximizing ALMM indigenization benefits. Furthermore, Inox Green’s scale (13.3+ GW peak portfolio) introduces high switching costs for O&M clients. The ultimate demand backstop was confirmed via a massive 1.5 GW MoU with Inox Green (500 MW already firmly signed), assuring Inox Wind of a massive captive offtake. 
- **The Bear Case:** The pivot to pure equipment supply commoditizes the firm's offering relative to end-to-end turnkey solutions. Furthermore, erratic execution and an increasingly combative, evasive management team erode investor trust, effectively narrowing their investable moat despite strong structural tailwinds.

## Growth Vectors
- **Where is the next $100M coming from?** 
  1. **New Product Launch:** The commercial rollout of the 4X (4.4 MW) turbine by the end of FY26 will drive deeper market penetration and yield unit-level margin expansion.
  2. **Inox Green Consolidation:** The impending Q2 FY27 financial consolidation of Wind World India's 4.5 GW operational O&M assets will dramatically scale recurring EBITDA.
  3. **Demerger Synergies:** The completed (August 2026) demerger of the evacuation infrastructure business into Inox Renewable Solutions (IRSL)—slated for automatic listing—eliminates ~₹1,000 crore in gross block and ₹50-55 crore in annual depreciation, immediately lifting ROE and bottom-line profitability.
- **Management Execution:** Management is pragmatic but increasingly defensive and evasive. They correctly identified EPC as a cash trap and initiated a painful pivot to an equipment-supply model. However, their decision to withdraw megawatt (volume) guidance in favor of financial (revenue) guidance is a classic masking tactic to obscure volume volatility. This masking became glaring in Q1 FY27, where revenues dropped sequentially from ₹1,306 crore in Q4 to just ₹872 crore, severely lagging the run-rate needed for their maintained 75% YoY growth target. Compounding this, management quietly slashed full-year EBITDA margin guidance to 14-15% (down from 20-25% guided just a quarter prior), while turning hostile toward analysts questioning the misses and relying heavily on a promised "H2-heavy" execution cycle.

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Forensic Red Flags:** The "virtuous cycle of group synergies" is a double-edged sword. With a 1.5 GW MoU recently signed with the promoter entity (Inox Green), related-party transactions (RPTs) will heavily pad the order book and dominate the revenue mix. Investors must strictly scrutinize transfer pricing to ensure the listed entity isn't subsidizing the promoter's IPP ambitions. Additionally, management's recent refusal to disclose basic operating metrics (MW execution, specific receivable days) is a major red flag for corporate transparency.
- **Business Risks:** 
  - *Margin Compression:* Management's sudden downgrade of FY27 EBITDA margin guidance to 14-15% signals severe, unexplained pricing pressure or raw material cost blowouts as equipment supply volumes ramp up.
  - *Working Capital Bloat:* Despite the pivot away from EPC, working capital remains a heavy overhang. Management claims trade receivable days are falling QoQ but refuses to share actual metrics, suggesting cash remains stuck in legacy EPC disputes.
  - *Customer Site Readiness:* The equipment supply model remains highly vulnerable to delays at customer sites, which continues to throttle offtake.
- **What could derail the thesis?** A collapse in battery energy storage (BESS) and solar panel prices could tilt the economics of FDRE (Firm and Dispatchable RE) projects away from wind, though currently, hybrid mandates require a wind component. Furthermore, a failure to flawlessly execute an H2-heavy revenue ramp would mathematically obliterate their stated growth targets.

## Investment Thesis
- **The Bull Case:** Own Inox Wind for its transition into a de-risked, net-cash, structurally higher-margin business with phenomenal revenue visibility. The order book has swelled to an exceptional 4.4 GW (including the NLC India LOA and 1.5 GW captive MoU), offering up to 36 months of clear runway. By offloading capital-intensive turnkey EPC work, capturing 100% indigenization ALMM benefits, stripping out dead-weight depreciation via the completed IRSL demerger, and impending financial consolidation of Wind World India, the company is primed for immense operating leverage once the "transitional" disruptions conclude in H2.
- **The Bear Case:** Stay away if you are uncomfortable with high related-party dependency, obfuscated volume metrics, and combative leadership. The sudden 14-15% margin guidance cut—down from 20-25% just 90 days ago—points to structural profitability issues. With Q1 revenues contracting sequentially, the business needs a near-impossible ~100% sequential growth over the next three quarters to meet its lofty 75% FY27 top-line guidance. Until working capital definitively normalizes and management stops hiding behind "annualized" targets to excuse poor quarterly execution, the promised growth will remain trapped in inventory rather than converting to free cash flow.
