# Apar Industries (APARINDS) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Figures from Screener's consolidated page unless tagged otherwise.
- Price ₹17,776; market cap ₹74,432 cr; 52-week range ₹6,800–19,269; trailing P/E 61.8; ROCE 31.8%; ROE 20.3% [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].
- Promoters 55.42% (Aug 2026), down from 57.76% (Jun 2026) after the ₹2,500 cr QIP in August 2026 [Screener; CARE Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- **What it sells:** three businesses [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 MD&A]:
  - **Conductors** (52% of FY26 gross segment revenue): bare overhead power-line conductors, both conventional (ACSR/AAAC) and "premium" (HTLS high-temperature low-sag, ACCC carbon-core under licence from CTC Global, OPGW fibre-optic earth wire, copper transposed conductor for transformers, railway catenary, busbars). It calls itself the world's largest aluminium/alloy conductor maker.
  - **Transformer and speciality oils** (22%): transformer oil (world's third-largest maker), industrial and automotive lubricants (Eni licence), white oils. Plants in India and Hamriyah (Sharjah, UAE).
  - **Cables** (25%): elastomeric and e-beam cables for railways, defence, solar, wind; medium-voltage power cables; optical fibre cable; a young housing-wire (B2C) business.
- **Customers:** KEC, Kalpataru, L&T, BHEL, Indian Railways, state utilities, renewables developers; top 10 customers only ~19% of FY26 sales [CARE Sep-26]. Sole supplier of HVDC transformer oil to Hitachi Energy, GE and Siemens so far [Q4 call].
- **Geography:** exports ~30% of FY26 revenue (27.5% in Q1 FY27); presence in 140+ countries. US revenue grew ~50% in FY26 but fell 11.1% year on year in Q1 FY27 (conductor US revenue −34%) [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].
- **FY26 segment numbers [AR FY26 MD&A]:**
  - Conductors: revenue ₹12,712 cr (+32.7%), volume 2,41,788 MT (+8.6%), EBITDA ₹1,040 cr, **₹43,012 per MT** (+17.3%), premium share 45.8%.
  - Oils: revenue ₹5,373 cr (+5.6%), volume 6,31,985 KL (+9%), EBITDA ₹376 cr, ₹5,942 per KL (−3.3%).
  - Cables: revenue ₹6,220 cr (+25.8%), EBITDA ₹633 cr, margin 10.2%. Cables overtook oils as the second-largest segment for the first time.
- **Capacity and use:** installed capacity 5,55,262 MT conductors, 9,34,600 KL oils, 9,50,656 km cables at March 2026 [CARE Sep-26]. Management put utilisation at 90–95% (conductors), 85–90% (cables), 65–70% (oils; lubricant filling 85–90%) in May, and "80% to 90%" across divisions in July [Q4 call; Q1 call]. Note: FY26 conductor volume is only ~44% of the stated MT capacity, so the capacity figure must be on a different product basis; utilisation is taken from management.

## Key numbers
- **Quarterly sales / operating profit / OPM / PAT (₹ cr) [Screener]:**
  - Q1 FY26: 5,104 / 452 / 9% / 263
  - Q2 FY26: 5,715 / 465 / 8% / 252
  - Q3 FY26: 5,480 / 452 / 8% / 209
  - Q4 FY26: 6,603 / 496 / 8% / 253
  - **Q1 FY27: 6,591 / 758 / 11% / 467** (PAT +78% year on year; record quarter)
- Company-defined EBITDA (after "open-period forex", before corporate costs) was ₹814 cr, 12.4%, in Q1 FY27 vs ₹501 cr, 9.8% a year ago [IP Q1FY27]. CARE's PBILDT margin: 11.50% vs 8.86% [CARE Sep-26].
- **What drove Q1:** oil EBITDA jumped to ₹329 cr (₹25,482 per KL vs ₹7,004) **after** a ₹93 cr inventory provision, because cheap contracted stock was sold at war-inflated prices. Conductor EBITDA rose 14% to ₹285 cr on a 50.3% premium mix, even though volume fell 6.7% to 53,279 MT. Cables EBITDA ₹194 cr (+36.7%), margin 10.6% [IP Q1FY27]. CARE says the oil profit "is not expected to sustain at the same level" [CARE Sep-26].
- **Annual [Screener]:** FY24 sales ₹16,153 cr, OPM 10%, PAT ₹825 cr; FY25 ₹18,581 cr, 9%, ₹821 cr; FY26 ₹22,902 cr (+23%), 8%, ₹977 cr (+19%). TTM sales ₹24,389 cr, PAT ₹1,182 cr. 5-year sales CAGR 29%, profit CAGR 44%.
- **Unit economics (my calculation):** conductor revenue per MT ~₹5.3 lakh (FY26) and ~₹6.3 lakh (Q1 FY27), the rise reflecting metal prices and premium mix. EBITDA per MT went ₹36,683 (FY25) → ₹43,012 (FY26) → ₹53,418 (Q1 FY27). Oil EBITDA per KL before the ₹93 cr provision would have been ~₹32,700.
- **Order book (30 Jun 2026):** conductors ₹10,190 cr (56.8% export), up from ₹7,671 cr at March; cables ₹1,925 cr (₹1,653 cr a year ago). Q1 conductor inflow ₹5,245 cr, including more than ₹2,800 cr from one US and one European utility spread over four years [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call].
- **Q1 also said:** Section-232 tariff muddle caused "very little ordering" for a quarter; US deliveries are DDP, so revenue lags shipment [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- **Q4 call (28 May 2026):**
  - Conductor EBITDA "in the range of INR35,000 to INR36,000 per metric ton plus the tailwinds" over the medium to long term (raised over the years from ₹8,000–10,000) [Q4 call].
  - Conductor volume growth ~10% a year; cables revenue growth 25% a year toward a ₹10,000 cr cable business in five years, "on track, perhaps slightly ahead" [Q4 call].
  - Cable EBITDA margin 10–11% [Q1 call, "in line with our guidance of 10-11%"].
  - US sales to be higher in FY27 than FY26; warned of a short-term slowdown from metal prices, freight, war and election-related labour shortages [Q4 call].
  - FY27 capex ~₹1,500 cr [Q4 call].
- **Q1 call (24 Jul 2026):** no guidance at all — the company was in a quiet period ahead of the share issue [Q1 call].
- **Delivery vs promise:**
  - Conductor EBITDA/MT ₹53,418 — far above the ₹35–36k base guide (mix plus metal tailwinds). **Beat.**
  - Conductor volume −6.7% vs +10% guide. **Missed** (customers delayed clearances while aluminium spiked).
  - Cables +29.5% vs 25% guide, margin 10.6% within 10–11%. **Delivered.**
  - US revenue −11.1% year on year vs "larger presence in FY27". **Behind so far**, though the ₹2,800 cr utility orders and hyperscaler approvals support later quarters.
- Guidance was neither raised nor cut; it was suspended for the QIP.

## Capex and capacity
- **FY26 capex ~₹740 cr** (conductor ₹223 cr, oil ₹104 cr, cable ₹401 cr) [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A]. CWIP rose to ₹540 cr at March 2026 from ₹130 cr [Screener]. Capital commitments ₹465 cr (FY25: ₹316 cr) [AR FY26 note 53].
- **FY27 plan ~₹1,500 cr:** cables ~₹850 cr (medium-voltage XLPE and rubber/EPR cables for US data centres and utilities; wind, solar, railway, defence cables), conductors ~₹400 cr, oils ~₹200 cr (lubricant filling debottlenecking) [Q4 call]. About ₹400 cr of the cable spend was pulled forward from later years because equipment lead times have lengthened [Q4 call].
- **Funding:** internal accruals plus the ₹2,500 cr QIP (net ₹2,472 cr). QIP use: ₹1,875 cr for working capital, ₹597 cr general purposes — none earmarked for capex [CARE Sep-26].
- **Payback:** not disclosed. Fixed-asset turns were 10.2x in FY26 (11.1x FY25) [AR FY26 MD&A]. At even 5x turns, ₹1,500 cr of capex could support ~₹7,500 cr of extra revenue (my estimate). US data-centre cables need no US plant; customers audited the Indian plants [Q1 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- **Debt:** borrowings ₹956 cr (March 2026, up from ₹585 cr). But the real funding is supplier credit and LC acceptances: "other liabilities" ₹7,362 cr [Screener]. CARE gearing including acceptances 1.11x; TOL/TNW 1.54x; debt/PBILDT 3.11x [CARE Sep-26].
- **After QIP:** CARE projects gearing 0.59x and TOL/TNW 0.83x in FY27, and upgraded Apar to **CARE AA/Stable** from AA− on 9 Sep 2026 [CARE Sep-26].
- **Working capital:** requirement ₹8,520 cr in FY26 (₹6,672 cr in FY25), ~37% of revenue; inventory ₹3,186 cr and debtors ₹5,334 cr at March 2026. Non-fund limit utilisation averaged ~88% for the 12 months to May 2026 (75% a year earlier) [CARE Sep-26]. Screener: debtor days 85, inventory days 84, payable days 138, cash conversion cycle 30 days [Screener]. Management: "Working capital is in the range of 45 to 50 days" [Q1 call].
- **Cash flow [Screener]:** operating cash ₹968 cr in FY26 (₹1,291 cr FY25), 70% of operating profit; free cash flow ₹235 cr (₹784 cr FY25). FY24 operating cash was negative (−₹283 cr). Interest cost ₹474 cr in FY26 — high because LC/acceptance costs sit in finance cost.
- **Liquidity:** cash and liquid investments ₹713 cr at March 2026 [CARE Sep-26].
- **Dividend:** payout ~25% for five years [Screener].

## Moat evidence
- **Scale and approvals:** largest conductor maker globally; Power Grid-type tenders require past field performance, so "new parties... will not be given easy access"; ACCC costs up to 3x ACSR, so utilities avoid untested suppliers [Q4 call].
- **Premium mix:** 45.8% of conductor revenue in FY26 (40.6% FY25), 50.3% in Q1 FY27 [CARE Sep-26]. Reconductoring leader — 272+ projects, 7,075+ ckm [Screener about]; record reconductoring installations in Q1 [Q1 call].
- **US access:** approvals from Google, Microsoft and Meta for data-centre cables in Q1 FY27; 144-fibre OPGW approval from one of the largest US utilities; 19 UL approvals [Q1 call; AR FY26]. Earlier ~$15 million of cables supplied to three US data-centre projects [Q4 call].
- **Pricing power:** EBITDA per MT up ~46% in two years (₹36,683 → ₹53,418) while volume grew in single digits.
- **Against the moat:** domestic capacity additions "increasing some amount of pricing pressure"; Chinese competition severe in some regions; UltraTech and Adani entering wires [Q4 call]. Conventional exports are largely ACSR/AAAC — low-tech [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Oil profit is not repeatable:** Q1's ₹329 cr oil EBITDA was mostly an inventory gain on cheap contracts; with gas-oil prices falling at quarter-end a ₹93 cr provision was needed. 65% of oil is bought on contract (backward-priced) — in a falling market the effect reverses [Q1 call; CARE Sep-26].
- **Metal-price timing:** unhedged customers held back clearances in Q1; MJP (aluminium premium) cannot be hedged [Q1 call]. Iraq conductor contracts pushed out by the war [Q4 call].
- **Tariffs:** US Section 232 duty — 50% on bare aluminium products and conductors, 25–50% on cables — plus basic duty and a Section 301 surcharge of about 10% [Q1 call]. So far absorbed because US makers also pay 50% on imported aluminium.
- **Working-capital intensity:** acceptances utilisation at 88%; ₹8,520 cr tied up. The QIP buys headroom but dilutes equity ~4% (promoter stake 57.76% → 55.42%) [CARE Sep-26; Screener].
- **Auditor and accounts:** unmodified opinion; key audit matters are revenue cut-off and capitalisation of PPE/CWIP (including interest capitalisation). No CARO qualifications [AR FY26 auditor's report]. FY26 had a ₹33 cr exceptional loss; Q4 had ₹31 cr of one-offs (gratuity ~₹8 cr, ECB mark-to-market, and an old arbitration case moved from contingent liability into a provision on auditors' advice) [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]. Remaining contingent liabilities are small (tens of crores of tax, labour and other claims) [AR FY26 note 53].
- **Related parties:** routine; KMP pay ₹40.1 cr in FY26 (₹32.5 cr FY25), ~4% of PAT; purchases from associates ₹6.5 cr [AR FY26 note 52]. Promoter-family trusts and a university/foundation are listed related parties.
- **Disclosure:** no US sales figure by segment given on calls; guidance withheld during the quiet period.

## What changed between the two calls
- **Tone:** Q4 was expansive (₹1,500 cr capex, US data centres, medium-term targets) but warned of a near-term slowdown; Q1 was guarded because of the QIP.
- **Numbers:** EBITDA margin 8.8% → 12.4%; conductor EBITDA/MT ₹44,919 → ₹53,418; conductor order book ₹7,671 cr → ₹10,190 cr; oil EBITDA/KL ₹5,656 → ₹25,482.
- **New issues:** the ₹93 cr oil provision; Hamriyah port closure; hyperscaler approvals; the two multi-year utility orders; the capital raise.

## My read
The business is clearly improving: premium conductors now half the mix, cables compounding at ~25–30%, and multi-year export utility contracts plus US hyperscaler approvals widen the runway. But Q1 FY27 flatters: about ₹224 cr of the ₹313 cr year-on-year EBITDA jump came from the oil division, much of it an inventory gain that will not repeat, and conductor volume actually fell. At ~62 times trailing earnings the price assumes the ₹1,500 cr capex fills at today's margins and the US ramps quickly. Watch conductor volume recovery in Q2–Q3, whether oil EBITDA/KL falls back toward ₹6,000–8,000, and US revenue turning positive. A slide back to guided ₹35–36k per MT would hurt the earnings base the market is paying for.
