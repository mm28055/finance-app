# TD Power Systems (TDPOWERSYS) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Figures from Screener's consolidated page unless tagged otherwise.
- Price ₹752; market cap ₹23,577 cr; 52-week range ₹284–819; trailing P/E 85.7; ROCE 34%; ROE 24.7% [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].
- Promoters 26.87% (unchanged since Jun 2025, when it fell from 33.22%). FIIs 26.2%, DIIs 23.9% [Screener].

## Business and segments
- **What it makes:** custom AC generators — for steam turbines (up to 250 MW), gas turbines (up to 250 MVA), gas and diesel engines (up to 25 MVA), hydro (up to 45 MW) — plus induction, synchronous and railway traction motors [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 MD&A].
- **Who buys:** global engine and turbine makers (the "prime movers") who bolt a TDPS generator onto their machine: Siemens, INNIO Jenbacher, Triveni Turbine and others [CRISIL Jun-26]. One engine or turbine takes one generator [Q4 call].
- **Mix:** gas-engine generators became the largest contributor in FY26, followed by steam-turbine generators; steam is "around 25%" of revenue [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call]. Service is 5–6% of sales [Q1 call]. Gas split by application (data centres etc.) is not disclosed [Q1 call].
- **Geography:** exports ~70% of FY26 sales (40–50% five years ago) [CRISIL Jun-26]; standalone exports and deemed exports 66% of FY26 sales [AR FY26 MD&A]. 93% of Q1 FY27 order inflow was export [IP Q1FY27].
- **Installed base:** 8,375 units in 113 countries at June 2026, up from 8,055 at March — about 320 machines in one quarter [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Jun-26].
- **Plants:** two in Bengaluru (one a dedicated large-generator unit) and a third commissioned in December 2025 (~₹140 cr, internally funded); a small plant in Turkey; UK R&D office for 40–200 MW designs [CRISIL Jun-26; AR FY26].
- **Utilisation:** the factory is "running very full" — management calls high utilisation "a new normal" [Q4 call].

## Key numbers
- **Quarterly sales / operating profit / OPM / PAT (₹ cr) [Screener]:**
  - Q1 FY26: 372 / 69 / 19% / 50
  - Q2 FY26: 452 / 83 / 18% / 60
  - Q3 FY26: 443 / 80 / 18% / 56
  - Q4 FY26: 589 / 98 / 17% / 72
  - **Q1 FY27: 640 / 122 / 19% / 86** (sales +72%, PAT +72% year on year)
- **Gross margin:** 34.7% in Q1 FY27 vs 31.4% in Q4 FY26 (hit by a Turkey late-delivery penalty) and 35.3% a year earlier [IP Q1FY27].
- **Annual [Screener]:** FY24 sales ₹1,001 cr, OPM 17%, PAT ₹118 cr; FY25 ₹1,279 cr, 18%, ₹175 cr; FY26 ₹1,856 cr (+45%), 18%, ₹239 cr (+37%). TTM sales ₹2,124 cr, PAT ₹275 cr. 5-year profit CAGR 43%.
- **Unit economics (my estimate):** ~320 machines shipped in Q1 on ₹640 cr of sales gives an average of roughly ₹2 cr per unit (mixes generators, motors and railway parts). Management says future growth is "both: units as well as realization" [Q1 call].
- **Order book:** manufacturing order book ₹2,207 cr at 30 Jun 2026 (generators and motors ₹1,929 cr, railways ₹211 cr, aftermarket ₹22 cr, Turkey ₹46 cr), up from ₹1,973 cr at March. Q1 inflow ₹734 cr (+87%); FY26 inflow ₹2,238 cr (+51%) [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]. Execution cycle is 3–6 months, so the book is about one year of sales [CRISIL Jun-26].
- **Returns:** ROCE 16% (FY22) → 34% (FY26) [Screener].

## Guidance history
- **Q4 call (15 May 2026):** FY27 revenue "INR2,400-plus crores", with "an extremely high probability" of raising it; capacity for ₹3,000–3,200 cr by FY28 with ~₹50 cr capex in FY27 and ~₹50 cr in FY28; gross contribution back to ~34% after the Turkey hit; Q1 to beat Q4; order inflow to grow 20–25% [Q4 call].
- **Q1 call (12 Aug 2026):** FY27 revenue **raised to ₹2,600 cr** "with a small chance that we may even cross this number"; FY27 order inflow about ₹700 cr a quarter, ₹2,800 cr-plus for the year; ₹3,200 cr capacity for FY28 ("not written in stone"); a further expansion toward ₹4,000 cr-plus of capacity for FY29–FY30, details promised on the next call; gross contribution margin held within ±1%; railway orders stopped [Q1 call].
- **Delivery vs promise:** Q1 revenue ₹640 cr beat Q4's ₹589 cr as promised; gross margin recovered to 34.7%; inflow ₹734 cr met the ~₹700 cr/quarter pace. **Delivering / raised.**
- **Large generators:** promised in May for "2 or 3 months", partly delivered — a 10-year build-to-print agreement with Siemens Energy, Inc. for 2-pole generators, effective 13 Aug 2026 [News: Trade Brains, Aug-26]. Capex size still not disclosed. Management said full large-generator capacity is possible only in calendar 2027, with the ramp-up in calendar 2028 (FY29), because big machine tools take 15–16 months to arrive [Q4 call]. FY27 guidance includes no large-generator revenue [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- **Done:** third plant (~₹140 cr) commissioned December 2025 [CRISIL Jun-26]. FY26 investing outflow ₹105 cr [Screener]. Capital commitments ₹40 cr at March 2026 [AR FY26 note 36].
- **Planned:** ~₹50 cr in FY27 for debottlenecking to reach ~₹3,200 cr capacity for FY28 [Q1 call]. CRISIL expects ₹60–80 cr a year funded from accruals of ₹240–300 cr [CRISIL Jun-26]. On top of that, an undisclosed large-generator investment (rotor machining up to ~200 MW) and a new capacity round for FY29–FY30.
- **Funding:** board approved on 14 Aug 2026 a QIP of up to ₹600 cr plus a ₹75 cr preferential issue to promoters Nikhil Kumar and Mohib Khericha (6.25 lakh shares each, 12.5 lakh in all, at ₹600 post-split / ₹1,200 pre-split); shareholders approved on 10 Sep; shares allotted 21 Sep 2026 [News: Investywise; Screener announcements]. A ₹600 cr QIP at today's price would dilute about 2.6% (my estimate).
- **Payback:** very high asset turns — ₹50 cr of debottlenecking supports ~₹600 cr more revenue (₹2,600 cr → ₹3,200 cr), per management's own numbers [Q1 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- **Debt:** essentially nil (₹18 cr borrowings at March 2026); interest cover 47x [Screener; CRISIL Jun-26].
- **Cash:** ₹199 cr cash and liquid investments at March 2026 (₹120–130 cr unencumbered), ~₹240 cr at June [CRISIL Jun-26; Q1 call].
- **Working capital is the weak spot:** debtor days rose to 146 in FY26 (110 in FY22); inventory days 148; cash conversion cycle 173 days [Screener]. Trade receivables ₹785 cr at June 2026 vs ₹742 cr at March [IP Q1FY27]. Operating cash was ₹129 cr in FY26 (59% of operating profit) and free cash ₹19 cr; FY25 free cash was −₹16 cr [Screener]. Management: payment terms cannot be changed "because otherwise we will lose business" [Q1 call]. "All our retained earnings are basically going into working capital" [Q4 call].
- **Dividend:** payout 11% [Screener].
- **Rating:** CRISIL upgraded to AA−/Stable/A1+ from A+/Positive on 22 Jun 2026 [CRISIL Jun-26].

## Moat evidence
- **Qualification and relationships:** long-term supply with INNIO since 2009; Siemens licence for 2-pole generators up to 250 MVA since 2012 [IP Q1FY27]; a capacity commitment agreement with INNIO running to 2030 [Q4 call]; the new 10-year Siemens Energy framework [News: Trade Brains].
- **Structure:** "there's no possibility for new entrants" among prime-mover makers, and those makers are "on an average, planning to double capacity by 2030" [Q4 call].
- **Pricing:** price-variation clauses pass through copper; margins held at 17–19% OPM through a copper spike. But management admits limited pricing power: "we are not going to make the same kind of money that the turbine guys make" [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Demand inelasticity:** the power plant is "less than 5%" of a data-centre project's cost [Q1 call].
- **Against it:** top 10 customers were **84%** of FY26 consolidated revenue [AR FY26 MD&A]; CRISIL puts top-10 at 60–70% of generator revenue [CRISIL Jun-26]. Large-generator incumbents are Siemens, Baker Hughes (Brush), Mitsubishi, Fuji, Toshiba [Q4 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Customer concentration** (84% top-10) and dependence on the US gas/data-centre build-out; an OEM re-sourcing or a data-centre pause would bite quickly [AR FY26; CRISIL Jun-26].
- **Working capital:** receivables and inventory growing with sales; FY26 free cash of only ₹19 cr despite ₹239 cr profit [Screener].
- **Dilution:** the QIP comes while the company holds ~₹240 cr cash and no debt; management declined to discuss debt vs equity [Q1 call].
- **Promoter stake is low** (26.87%) after a ~6-point sale in the June 2025 quarter; the ₹75 cr preferential issue at ₹600 is a partial re-up [Screener; News].
- **Execution:** factory at full stretch; any machine breakdown delays output [Q4 call]. Large-generator entry is late relative to peers [Q4 call].
- **Turkey:** one Turkish contract hit by a shipping delay led to a "high LD penalty" in Q4 FY26; the Turkish plant now has only EUR3–3.5 million of orders for FY27 and no pipeline beyond [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Accounts and auditor:** unmodified opinion; key audit matter is revenue recognition on custom products [AR FY26 auditor's report]. A further ₹3 cr impairment of the loss-making Indian subsidiary DF Power Systems was booked as an exceptional item, taking the write-down to the full ₹20.4 cr investment [AR FY26 note]. Contingent liabilities: performance guarantees ₹122 cr; a disputed ₹19.9 cr transfer-pricing tax demand (AY 2017-18), recovery stayed by the Karnataka High Court [AR FY26 note 36].
- **Related parties:** MD Nikhil Kumar's pay about ₹5.1 cr in FY26, including ₹4.1 cr commission (commission ₹3.3 cr in FY25); professional fees to Trilegal, where independent director Rahul Matthan is a founding partner; vehicle servicing from promoter-linked Trident Automobiles [AR FY26 note 44]. Small, but worth noting.
- **Management change:** first CEO, Deepak Kumar Sinha (ex L&T-MHI, GE), from 2 Apr 2026 [AR FY26; IP Q1FY27].

## What changed between the two calls
- **Tone:** from "one-off Turkey hit, Q1 will be better" to raised guidance and a bigger capacity plan.
- **Numbers:** FY27 guide ₹2,400 cr+ → ₹2,600 cr; order book ₹1,973 cr → ₹2,207 cr; gross margin 31.4% → 34.7%.
- **New issues:** railway orders halted to free space for generators; fund-raise announced; Siemens Energy framework signed a day after the call; Turkey downgraded to a service back-up.

## My read
The business is improving fast and the evidence is hard: revenue up 45% in FY26 and 72% in Q1 FY27, margins steady at 18–19%, ROCE 34%, and a new 10-year Siemens Energy agreement. The weak points are cash conversion (146 debtor days, near-zero free cash), extreme customer concentration, and a narrow driver — US gas-fired power for data centres. At ~86 times trailing earnings the price needs the FY28 ₹3,200 cr step and the FY29–30 ₹4,000 cr-plus plan to arrive at today's margins. Watch the large-generator capex plan on the Q2 call, whether receivables stop outgrowing sales, and quarterly inflow holding near ₹700 cr.
