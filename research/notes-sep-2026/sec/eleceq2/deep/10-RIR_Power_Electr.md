## Business and segments
- Makes silicon power semiconductors at Halol, Gujarat: thyristors (SCRs) and diodes (low-power "LPD" and high-power "HPD" press-pack devices up to 5–8 kV), power modules, rectifier assemblies and systems (e.g. rectifiers for BARC, Hindalco, NFC, a 15 MW green-hydrogen unit). Customers: railways (locomotives), defence, Navy, industrial OEMs, global distributors; 400+ customers. [IP Jun-26; Q4 call]
- One reportable segment ("Power Electronics"). [AR FY26 note 40]
- Exports: 20% of FY26 order intake came from overseas; presentation says "35 to 40% Export"; imports and exports roughly match, so rupee moves are a wash. First overseas order for 120 x 125 mm 5 kV SCRs (US, for HVDC use), to be completed by end-2026. [Q4 call; IP Jun-26]
- New business: India's first silicon-carbide (SiC) epitaxial-wafer and device plant at Bhubaneswar, Odisha (Phase 1 epitaxy + packaging; Phase 2 fabrication). Two epi reactors (4/6/8-inch). SiC devices are outsourced to foundries (1,200 V and 1,700 V with one partner; a Korean source for 3.3 kV). [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Halol runs one shift; MD says low-power devices have "reached its peak saturation level" — growth must come from high-power devices, equipment, railways and defence. [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- **Q4 FY26 call (late May 2026):** epitaxy start in Q2 FY27 ("August, September, definitely"); 33 kV power by end-June (earlier promised by March); Halol revenue 2–3x in 2–3 years; ₹5 cr Halol upgrade; NSE listing "in due course"; bank loan for packaging "in principle approval"; whole Odisha capex done by Dec-27/Mar-28. [Q4 call]
- **Q1 FY27 call (13 Aug 2026):** power transformer charged the day before; epitaxy start "end of second quarter", revenue from Q3 FY27; H2 FY27 epi-wafer revenue ₹12–15 cr at 20–25% EBITDA; Halol to "easily cross about Rs. 30 plus crores per quarter" this year and ₹50 cr/quarter in 6–9 months; FY27 growth "3 to 4 times" last year's growth rate (FY26 grew 5.4%, so this implies roughly 15–20%+), bottom line faster; long-run margin aim 15–17%; packaging revenue from Q2 FY28. NSE listing done (16 Jul 2026). [Q1 call]
- Verdict: **Delivering, after long delays** — Q1 numbers beat and two promises (NSE listing, power line) were met, but the Odisha start has slipped repeatedly (power was promised for March, then June 30; delivered mid-August) and the ₹70 cr bank loan is still not sanctioned. Analysts on the Q1 call noted past targets "were not able to achieve".

## Capex and capacity
- Odisha SiC project: ₹618 cr total — Phase 1 ~₹225 cr (epitaxy + packaging), Phase 2 ~₹395–400 cr (fab). Government of Odisha funds 50% as capital subsidy. Spent so far: ~₹70 cr by RIR + ₹58 cr subsidy (Q1 call); ~₹100 cr of Phase 1 left, for which a ₹70 cr bank loan has been applied. FY27 capex ₹100–120 cr for Phase 1; Phase 2 funding "under discussion". [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Subsidy accounting: ₹47.84 cr received from Odisha in FY26, booked as deferred income (to be released over asset life). [AR FY26 note 44]
- Balance sheet: capital work-in-progress ₹46.9 cr; intangible assets under development ₹51.1 cr, of which ₹41.95 cr is SiC "process know-how" bought from a related party (see risks). [AR FY26 notes 38, 43]
- Expected returns: Chairman says SiC fab breaks even at 60–65% utilisation and earns "40% plus" margins above that; asset turnover ~0.65x of gross assets (so ₹618 cr of assets → roughly ₹400 cr revenue at maturity). [Q4 call]
- Halol: ₹5 cr from internal accruals for high-power testing and throughput; can add shifts. [Q4 call]

## Moat evidence
- One of very few makers of high-power thyristors in India; management says it is now "only one" domestic supplier of low-power devices; qualified by a US OEM for HVDC-grade 125 mm SCRs; replaced ABB as a supplier for an Indian defence application (claim by Chairman). [Q4 call]
- 20-year technology link with Silicon Power Corporation (USA), founded by Chairman Dr Harshad Mehta. [AR FY26; Q4 call]
- R&D spend "8–10%" of revenue claimed. [Q1 call]
- Pricing: passed on 80–85% of metal-cost rise in one quarter — some pricing power in niche, specification-driven products. [Q1 call]
- Weakness: small scale (₹91 cr revenue), thin ₹17 cr backlog, one-off defence/Navy orders without repeat so far (acknowledged by management). [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Related-party technology purchase:** ₹41.95 cr of SiC fabrication know-how bought from Sicamore Semiconductor Inc. USA, "an entity in which director/relative of director are interested", and carried as intangible under development. [AR FY26 note 36, note 43]
- **Subsidiary sold to promoter-linked firm:** 100% of Visicon Power Electronics (SiC wafers/epitaxy subsidiary) sold to Silicon Power Corporation, USA (Chairman's original company) for ₹2.16 cr, effective 26 Jul 2025; RIR bought ₹27.5 cr of plant and machinery from Visicon (table layout makes the year unclear). The auditor added an **emphasis of matter** on this disposal in the consolidated report. [AR FY26 Board's report; note 36; consolidated auditor's report]
- **Execution and funding:** a ₹618 cr project for a company with ₹91 cr revenue and ₹140 cr net worth; depends on 50% state subsidy and bank debt not yet sanctioned; Phase 2 funding open; strategic-investor talks "going on for some time". [Q1 call; Q4 call; Screener]
- **Repeated delays:** power connection slipped from March to June to August 2026; epitaxy start moved from Q2 to "end of Q2". [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Auditor (Kirtane & Pandit LLP): unmodified standalone opinion, no key audit matters; CARO notes "minor discrepancies" between bank statements and books (forex and late entries). [AR FY26 auditor's report & CARO]
- High valuation versus earnings: market cap ~₹1,500 cr on ~₹8 cr profit; stock −52% from high. [Screener]
- Management change: new MD & CEO N. Ramesh Kumar (ex-CG Power) from 11 Feb 2026; new CFO Ankit Shah (Aug 2026). [AR FY26 note 36; Q1 call]

- Tone shifted from frustration with Odisha power delays (Q4: "equally frustrated", DG-set fallback uneconomic) to confidence (power line done, NSE listing done).
- First concrete numbers for Odisha: ₹12–15 cr H2 FY27 epi revenue, 20–25% EBITDA; first Halol run-rate targets (₹30 cr+/quarter, ₹50 cr/quarter in 6–9 months).

## My read
The old Halol business is small but improving under a new CEO: Q1 revenue +29% and margin back near 15%. The whole valuation, though, rests on the Odisha SiC plant, which has slipped repeatedly, still lacks full funding, and is tangled with promoter-linked entities (₹42 cr of know-how bought from Sicamore; the SiC subsidiary sold to Silicon Power Corp). At ~₹1,500 cr market cap the price assumes Odisha works; the realistic near-term earnings are ₹10–15 cr a year. I would want to see epi wafers actually shipped and billed in Q3 FY27, the ₹70 cr loan sanctioned, and clear disclosure of what RIR paid for and owns in the SiC technology.
