# Ester Industries — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code ESTER (consolidated). Price ₹86.1, market cap ₹898 cr, book value ₹80.2, ROCE 2.76%, ROE −3.5%; **no P/E shown because trailing-12-month profit is a loss of ₹2 cr** [Screener, 28-Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- Three businesses plus a JV [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Sep-26]:
  - **Polyester (BOPET) film**: 108,000 t a year across Khatima, Uttarakhand (parent) and Hyderabad (subsidiary Ester Filmtech, 48,000 t line started Q4 FY23). Q1 FY27 film revenue ₹399.5 cr (+38%), volume 22,120 t (+2.7%), segment EBIT ₹39.1 cr (9.8% vs 2.4%).
  - **Specialty polymers** (patented polyester resins, 30,000 t capacity): Q1 FY27 revenue ₹32.7 cr (−32%), volume 725 t (−24%), EBIT ₹14.8 cr (45.3% vs 31.7%). FY26 revenue ₹179.3 cr (+16%), EBIT ₹58.7 cr (32.7%).
  - **rPET** (recycled PET, 28,000 t incl. a 20,000 t extruder at Hyderabad started FY26): Q1 FY27 external sales ₹17.5 cr, 1,394 t; most output is now used in-house.
  - Polyester chips: 67,000 t capacity, mainly captive; ₹5.3 cr external sales in Q1.
  - **ELITe** — 50:50 JV with NASDAQ-listed Loop Industries for chemical recycling of polyester textile waste at Dahej, Gujarat: 70,000 t, ~₹1,600 cr project, commercial operations targeted CY2028 [AR FY26 MD&A p.49].
- Value-added (VAP) films were 29% of film volume in Q1 FY27 (6,368 t, +23%) vs 24% a year ago; target 35% by FY27 end and 50–60% in 2–3 years [Q1 call].
- Capacity use: film 84% consolidated (Ester 85%, Filmtech 83% — Filmtech's highest yet) [Q1 call].
- Specialty polymer revenue depends heavily on a few patented products; one "marquee" product's demand fell from Q4 FY26 [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly sales: Jun-25 338 → Sep-25 354 → Dec-25 339 → Mar-26 344 → Jun-26 432 (+28%). Operating profit 20 → 14 → 16 → 42 → 49; OPM 6% → 4% → 4.8% → 12% → 11% [Screener].
- Net profit: −7 → −16 → −12 → 8 → 19. Interest ₹15–17 cr and depreciation ₹17–18 cr a quarter absorb most operating profit in weak quarters [Screener].
- Annual: sales FY24 1,063 → FY25 1,282 → FY26 1,375; operating profit −23 → 147 → 93; net profit −121 → 14 → −27; ROCE −5% → 6% → 3% [Screener]. Earlier peak FY21: OPM 23%, profit ₹137 cr, ROCE 32% (company figure) [Screener; Q4 call].
- FY26 reported EBITDA ₹110.6 cr (7.9%); excluding ₹37.4 cr mark-to-market loss on euro loans, ₹147.9 cr (10.6%) [Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27 company EBITDA ₹58.9 cr (13.3%, incl. ₹9.8 cr other income — ₹3 cr treasury, ₹3.5 cr FD interest, ₹1 cr FX), PAT ₹18.6 cr [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Unit economics: commodity 12-micron film spread ₹28–30/kg in Q1 FY27 (₹32–33/kg in Q4 FY26); VAP earns ₹25/kg and more above that [Q1 and Q4 calls]. Chips cost ~₹100/kg in May 2026 [Q4 call].
- Filmtech Q1 FY27: income ₹159.6 cr (+63%), EBITDA ₹19.5 cr (vs −₹2.7 cr), PAT ₹4.7 cr (vs −₹16.5 cr) [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (15 May 2026): margins "far better" than the last 12–15 months and sustained for 18–24 months; specialty polymers to grow 20% a year and reach ~₹200 cr in FY27 ("we stick to that"); FY27 capex ~₹70 cr (₹15 cr growth, >20% IRR; rest maintenance); debt repayment ~₹85 cr with new borrowing under ₹40 cr; ROCE above 20% "in the following 8 to 10 quarters"; full-capacity revenue ₹2,000–2,200 cr; Q4 EBITDA run-rate "maintain or even exceed" [Q4 call].
- Q1 call (18 Aug 2026): specialty polymers **cut** to "flat or single-digit growth" in FY27, 20% CAGR pushed to 3–5 years; debt reduction **raised** to ₹100 cr (gross debt ₹720 cr → ₹620 cr by Mar-27); capex now "sustenance and maintenance" only; good earnings for "the next 6–8 quarters"; ₹2,000–2,200 cr revenue "in the next 2–3 years"; no FY27/FY28 numbers [Q1 call].
- Delivery: Q1 EBITDA ₹58.9 cr beat the Q4 run-rate of ₹43.3 cr — **delivered** (helped by one-off other income); specialty polymers — **missed**; debt — too early.
- CRISIL's own projection: revenue growth 18–20% and EBITDA margin 12–13% over the medium term [CRISIL Sep-26].

## Capex and capacity
- Big capex is done: gross block rose from ~₹390 cr (FY22) to ~₹978 cr (FY23) with the Hyderabad BOPET line; FY26 added the 20,000 t rPET extruder [Screener; AR FY26 Board's report].
- FY27 capex: ~₹70 cr guided in May (₹15 cr growth), then "no major capex" beyond maintenance in August [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **ELITe is the real capex.** ~₹1,600 cr project at Dahej; debt:equity 70:30; FEED done by Tata Consulting Engineers, detailed engineering by Toyo; land by Oct 2026; construction from Q3 FY27; start CY2028 (commercial benefit from FY30) [AR FY26 p.49; CRISIL Sep-26; Q4 call].
  - Ester raised ₹165.25 cr through warrants (₹175 cr issue; ₹9.75 cr unsubscribed) and has ₹140 cr earmarked for its JV equity [AR FY26 Board's report; Q1 call]. At 30% equity, total equity is ~₹480 cr, so Ester's half is ~₹240 cr — **about ₹100 cr more than earmarked** (my arithmetic; not disclosed by the company).
  - Shareholders approved related-party transactions with ELITe of up to ₹1,463 cr for FY27 [AR FY26 secretarial audit].
  - Management: at full use the 70,000 t plant would earn ~US$150 mn revenue at 40–45% EBITDA margin; Nike is anchor buyer (multi-year offtake) and a second global sports brand signed an LOI for up to 15,000 t a year; Taro Plast and Hyosung TNC have partnership deals [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26 p.49].
  - JV debt stays off Ester's balance sheet (50:50, not consolidated) [Q1 call].
- Payback: the existing plants can do ₹2,000–2,200 cr revenue at full use vs ₹1,375 cr in FY26 without new lines [Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹729 cr at Mar-26 (₹660 cr Mar-25; ₹816 cr peak Mar-23) [Screener]. Gross debt ₹722 cr, liquidity ₹236 cr at Jun-26 (₹60 cr funds, >₹160 cr FDs), of which ₹140 cr is for ELITe — sustainable liquidity ~₹100 cr [Q1 call].
- Long-term debt ~₹380 cr; consolidated cost 9–10%; Filmtech's euro term loans cost under 3% but caused ₹37.4 cr of MTM losses in FY26 as the euro rose from ~₹88 to ~₹112 [Q4 call].
- Working capital is heavy: inventory 86 days, debtors 49 days, payables only 27 days → cash cycle 108 days in FY26 [Screener].
- Cash from operations ₹89 cr (FY26), ₹112 cr (FY25); free cash flow ₹27 cr and ₹82 cr [Screener].
- Dividend ₹0.25/share for FY26; the promoter waived his dividend [Q4 call; AR FY26].
- CRISIL A-/Stable/A2+ reaffirmed 24 Sep 2026 on ₹524 cr of bank lines; gearing 0.93x, interest cover 2.3x in FY26 (3.5x Q1 FY27); downgrade if EBITDA margin falls below 8% or debt-funded capex/investment [CRISIL Sep-26].
- Loans are backed by personal guarantees of promoter Arvind Singhania and the holding company Wilemina Finance Corporation (shareholders approved up to ₹380 cr each of such "transactions" for FY27) [AR FY26 borrowing notes; secretarial audit].

## Moat evidence
- Specialty polymers: patents granted for 10 products globally; EBIT margins 32–45% show pricing power on patented grades [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- Food-grade and recycled-content approvals (US FDA, EFSA, FSSAI, GRS) for rPET films — needed as brands prepare for the Plastic Waste Management Rules; won a Marico supplier award for scaling rPET [IP Q1FY27].
- VAP films price at ₹25/kg+ over commodity film and grew 23% in Q1 [Q1 call].
- Limits: commodity BOPET is ~70% of film volume; margins swung from −2% OPM (FY24) to 12% (Q4 FY26) — pricing follows industry supply, not Ester [Screener]. CRISIL flags "intense competition" [CRISIL Sep-26].

## Risks and red flags
- **Customer/product concentration in specialty polymers**: one high-margin product's demand fell; management "still assessing" in May, now expects recovery only by Q4 FY27 [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **ELITe funding and execution**: ~₹1,600 cr first-of-its-kind plant; Ester's likely equity (~₹240 cr) exceeds the ₹140 cr set aside; timeline already slipped ("albeit with some delay") [AR FY26; Q4 call].
- **FX**: euro loans in Filmtech generated ₹37.4 cr of FY26 MTM losses; hedging only "as appropriate" [Q4 call].
- **Earnings quality**: Q1 FY27 other income ₹9.8 cr, called "one-time" by the CFO [Q1 call].
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; key audit matter only revenue recognition; excise/customs/service-tax disputes ₹11.9 cr, income tax ₹0.3 cr [AR FY26 auditor's report; note 38]. The parent gives a letter of comfort and corporate guarantees for Filmtech's loans [AR FY26 note 38].
- **Governance/succession**: Arvind Singhania moved from MD & CEO to non-executive chairman from end-March 2026; his son Ayush Vardhan Singhania is whole-time director and now chairs a board committee; promoter shares were transferred to the son ("internal family matter") [AR FY26 Board's report; Q1 call]. Promoter holding 62.32% [Screener].
- Industry supply: 2 BOPET lines added in 18 months, 2–3 more expected in 1.5–2 years; anti-dumping duty on Chinese BOPET was recommended by DGTR in May 2026 but formal notification was still "expected" then [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "inflection point" on US tariff relief and anti-dumping duty to self-help: VAP mix, rPET in-house use, operating leverage.
- Specialty polymers went from "₹200 cr this year" to "flat or single digit"; debt paydown went up from ~₹45 cr net to ₹100 cr; the ₹70 cr capex plan faded to maintenance.
- New: a second global sports brand LOI for ELITe; promoter share transfer to his son; rating review (completed 24 Sep 2026, reaffirmed).

## My read
Ester is a cyclical BOPET maker enjoying an upswing: spreads of ₹28–33/kg, Filmtech finally profitable, and Q1 FY27 the best quarter in two years. But the specialty-polymer engine that was supposed to smooth the cycle has stalled on one product, reported ROCE is still ~3%, and debt of ~₹720 cr is large for a company earning ₹120 cr of operating profit a year. The share price (~1.1x book) is betting on ELITe, a ₹1,600 cr first-of-a-kind plant three years away, which Ester will probably need more equity for. The view improves if spreads hold through FY27, debt actually falls to ~₹620 cr and specialty polymers recover by Q4; it worsens if new BOPET lines or Chinese imports push spreads back under ₹20/kg.
