# Huhtamaki India — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code HUHTAMAKI (standalone; the consolidated page is stale, last updated 2016). Price ₹243, market cap ₹1,837 cr, P/E 13.5, book value ₹179, ROCE 12.0%, ROE 9.0% [Screener, 28-Sep-26].
**Calendar-year company.** The dashboard's "Q1 FY27" = Apr–Jun 2026 = the company's Q2 CY26; "Q4 FY26" = Jan–Mar 2026 = Q1 CY26. Annual report = CY2025 (year to 31 Dec 2025).

## Business and segments
- Flexible packaging converter (printed laminates, pouches, tube laminates, pressure-sensitive labels, cylinders) for FMCG majors such as Unilever, Nestlé, Mondelez plus regional brands [Q4 call; CRISIL Aug-26].
- Parent Huhtamaki Oyj (Finland) owns 67.73% through Huhtavefa B.V.; public holds ~30.6%, institutions under 2% [Screener].
- Only one reportable segment (packaging); no product, volume or capacity split is disclosed. Management declined to give utilisation "due to the competitive nature of disclosures" [Q1 call; AR CY25].
- Exports ~30% of sales volume (South-East Asia, Africa, Europe, US); export and domestic grew at about the same rate in Q2 CY26 [Q1 call].
- Revenue from operations CY21 ₹2,625 cr → CY22 ₹2,983 cr → CY23 ₹2,549 cr → CY24 ₹2,521 cr → CY25 ₹2,469 cr: shrinking for three years as the company dropped low-margin business [AR CY25 MD&A p.83; Screener].
- Footprint changes: Hyderabad plant stopped (2023), three label sites merged (2023), Thane land sold (2024), Daman property held for sale, Baddi pressure-sensitive label plant to close from 30 Oct 2026 (₹21.9 cr turnover in 2025, 0.92% of revenue); labels consolidated at Ambernath and Bengaluru [AR CY25 notes 20, 40; BSE filing 1-Sep-26].
- Blueloop (recyclable mono-material laminates): customer adoption still "below 30%", but those lines run above 70% because they make other products that used to be outsourced [Q1 call].

- Other income is lumpy: ₹376 cr in Dec-23 quarter (land sale), ₹36 cr Jun-24, ₹22 cr Mar-26 (incl. ₹6.5 cr interest on tax refund), ₹1 cr Jun-26 [Screener; Q4 call].
- No per-kg economics disclosed.

## Guidance history
- Company gives no numeric guidance on revenue or margin [Q4 and Q1 calls].
- Q4 call (13 May 2026, new MD Kamal Taneja's first full quarter, new CFO Amit Gupta one month in): sales flat (+0.1%), EBITDA +24.8%; raw-material costs up "low to medium double digit" after the West Asia war but "most" passed on; large FMCG clients grow 2–5%, regional 5–7%; capacity "not a constraint"; solar captive plant at Khopoli to go live "in the next few months"; ECB of ₹100 cr due June 2027 [Q4 call].
- Q1 call (27 Jul 2026): sales +23.1%, EBITDA +55%; part of the jump is customers stocking up ahead of price rises ("some ... is going to sustain and some ... will even out"); "we do not expect 23% growth every quarter"; happy to grow with the market at 3–5%; solar (≈50% of Khopoli power) in Q3 CY26; plans to repay the parent ECB early [Q1 call; IP Q2CY26 slide 11].
- Delivery vs Q4 promises: margin protection — **delivered** (EBITDA margin 10.5%); growth — **beat** (flat to +23%), partly inventory pull-forward; solar — on time so far. Q3 CY26 results (27 Oct 2026) will show how much volume was pull-forward [Q1 call].
- CRISIL's own marker: operating margin 7.7% in CY25 (4.6% in CY24) and 8.4% in H1 CY26 (6.4%) [CRISIL Aug-26].

## Capex and capacity
- Capex CY25 ₹55.8 cr, "focused on capacity optimisation, efficiency improvement and maintenance"; fixed assets flat at ~₹639 cr gross block [AR CY25 MD&A p.83].
- Capital commitments fell to ₹15.5 cr (Dec-25) from ₹25.3 cr (Dec-24) — no growth project in the pipeline [AR CY25 note 44].
- Bengaluru (KIADB) plot: building finished and occupancy certificate received 21 Aug 2025 after years of extensions; the board approved a capex plan to meet the 50% land-use condition — amount not disclosed [AR CY25 note 44].
- EPCG export obligation: ₹295 cr of export commitments against duty-free imported machines; ₹153 cr done, discharge certificate awaited [AR CY25 note 44].
- Growth capacity comes from productivity work ("enough to cater to volume growth for the next couple of years") and modernisation spend; no new line announced [Q1 call].
- CWIP fell to ₹8 cr at Jun-26 from ₹131 cr at Dec-23 [Screener].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Gross debt ₹101.5 cr = one ECB (rupee bonds, 6.5%) from fellow subsidiary Huhtamaki Finance Co. V B.V.; first ₹100 cr tranche prepaid Sep 2024; second tranche's due date **moved from Feb 2026 to Jun 2027** [AR CY25 note on borrowings]. Screener borrowings ₹145 cr (incl. leases) [Screener].
- Cash and bank ₹270.6 cr + liquid funds ₹125.3 cr at Jun-26; unused bank lines ₹427 cr; net debt nil [IP Q2CY26 slide 7].
- Operating working capital jumped to ₹464 cr at Jun-26 from ₹305 cr at Dec-25 (higher inventory by choice, higher receivables from higher prices) [IP Q2CY26 slide 8].
- CY25 working capital: debtor days 82, inventory days 46, payable days 102, cash cycle 26 days [Screener].
- Cash from operations CY25 ₹238 cr (CFO/operating profit 120%); free cash flow ₹184 cr [Screener].
- Dividend ₹2/share for CY25 (payout ~13%); ₹10.2 cr of the dividend went to the parent [AR CY25 p.83; note 47].
- CRISIL AA-/Stable reaffirmed 12 Aug 2026; net worth ₹1,228 cr, debt ₹213 cr (CRISIL's definition), liquidity "strong" with over ₹400 cr of cash/investments [CRISIL Aug-26].

## Moat evidence
- Parent's global buying: during the war "availability is not an issue"; customers "value that reliability" — management says big FMCG clients shifted volume to large suppliers [Q4 and Q1 calls]. No share data given.
- Price pass-through: index-linked contracts reset monthly or quarterly; by end-March 2026 "almost all future orders were locked in with new pricing" [Q4 call].
- Brand/tech: Blueloop recyclable laminates, FSC-certified label plants, PCR content — but adoption is slow [Q1 call; IP slide 11].
- Limits: CRISIL says "intense competition restricts pricing flexibility" in a fragmented industry; raw materials are 65–70% of sales [CRISIL Aug-26]. Margins of 4–10% over five years show little pricing power.

## Risks and red flags
- **Parent charges are large.** Payments to Huhtamaki Oyj for software/IT and "centralised services" were ₹55.9 cr + ₹49.4 cr = ₹105.3 cr in CY25, up from ₹80.5 cr in CY24 — about 4% of revenue and roughly two-thirds of CY25 PBT of ₹158 cr. No trademark royalty was payable in either year. Amount due to the parent ₹99.1 cr at Dec-25 [AR CY25 note 47].
- **Accounting error.** An ₹8.8 cr depreciation shortfall for 2024–25 (assets depreciated on the wrong method) was booked in Q1 CY26 instead of restating, as management judged it immaterial [Q4 call]. The auditor's only key audit matter was revenue cut-off [AR CY25 auditor's report].
- **Management churn.** CFO Jagdish Agarwal resigned 1 Dec 2025; MD Dhananjay Salunkhe resigned 15 Jan 2026; Anil Kaul appointed CFO 27 Feb 2026, then Amit Gupta was CFO by April 2026; company secretary changed Aug 2026 [Screener announcement list, 19-Aug-26]; three parent nominee directors left in 2025–26 [AR CY25 Board's report; Q4 call].
- ECB tenure extended by the parent rather than repaid while the company holds ~₹396 cr of cash [AR CY25; IP slide 7].
- Title deeds of some land (Silvassa, Daman) still in former names such as Huhtamaki PPL Ltd [AR CY25 CARO i(c)].
- Contingent liabilities are small (largest item excise duty in appeal ~₹9 cr; other tax disputes a few crore each) [AR CY25 note 44]. Silvassa planning authority levied a ₹17.5 lakh penalty for a late occupancy certificate (Sep 2026) [BSE filing 11-Sep-26].
- Demand pull-forward in Q2 CY26 could reverse in Q3 [Q1 call].
- Low float (30%) and almost no institutional holding [Screener].

## What changed between the two calls
- From "flat sales, better margin" to "23% growth, 10.5% margin". Management moved from defending lost volume to saying it is happy to grow with the market.
- Working capital became an issue: inventory roughly doubled and receivables rose with prices.
- New disclosures after Q1: Baddi label plant closure (Sep 2026), small Silvassa penalty, new company secretary.

## My read
The turnaround is real on margins: operating margin has gone from under 5% in CY24 to ~10% in mid-2026, cash is ₹396 cr against ₹100 cr of parent debt, and the stock sits at about 1.4x book. But sales have shrunk for three years, the latest 23% jump is partly customers hoarding stock and price pass-through, and there is no growth capex, so volumes can only grow as fast as productivity allows. The large and rising parent charges (~₹105 cr a year) and repeated management churn cap what minority holders get. The price needs only margins holding at 8–9%; the view improves if Q3–Q4 CY26 volumes hold after the pull-forward and parent charges stop rising, and worsens if margins slip back toward 5–6%.
