# Pyramid Technoplast — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code PYRAMID (standalone; no subsidiaries — the consolidated page is empty). Price ₹162, market cap ₹597 cr, P/E 19.0, book value ₹75.2, ROCE 12.3%, ROE 11.0% [Screener, 28-Sep-26]. Listed Aug 2023 (IPO).

## Business and segments
- Makes rigid industrial packaging for chemical, agrochemical, specialty-chemical and pharma customers: HDPE (polymer) drums, IBCs (1,000-litre intermediate bulk containers in a steel cage) and mild-steel (MS) drums; 9 plants in Gujarat (Bharuch, Silvassa) and Maharashtra (Wada), own truck fleet [IP Q1FY27 slide 4; AR FY26].
- Capacity: polymer drums 33,909 t, IBCs 5.7 lakh units, MS drums 16,176 t — 83,745 t a year in total; plus a 5,000 t recycling plant (Unit 9, Oct 2025) [IP Q1FY27 slide 4].
- FY26 revenue split [IP Q1FY27 revenue table]: polymer drums ₹289 cr (23,057 t), MS drums ₹70 cr (8,515 t), IBCs ₹246 cr (3.36 lakh units), other operating income ₹76 cr (raw-material and scrap sales). IBCs rose from 34% to 41% of revenue in FY26; management expects IBC 43–45%, polymer drums ~45%, MS 10–12% [Q4 call].
- Customers are diversified: top customer 6% of revenue, top 10 = 29% [IP Q1FY27 slide 4]. Exports (mainly IBCs) only ~2–4% of sales, but many customers export chemicals in Pyramid's drums [Q1 call].
- Utilisation has fallen as capacity rose: 75.3% (FY23) → 73.1% (FY24) → 69.5% (FY25) → 68.7% (FY26) → 62% in Q1 FY27 (12,992 t on 20,936 t quarterly capacity) [IP Q1FY27 revenue table].
- Wada (new plant, production from 20 Jun 2025): ₹65 cr revenue in FY26; ₹43 cr (19% of revenue) in Q1 FY27; HDPE 71%, IBC 77%, MS 70% utilised; Phase 1 revenue potential ~₹200 cr, ~₹400 cr with Phase 2 machines [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 8].
- An analyst put Pyramid's domestic IBC share at 40% with Time Technoplast the other large player; management did not confirm the figure [Q4 call].

## Key numbers (Screener, standalone, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly sales: Jun-25 164 → Sep-25 161 → Dec-25 161 → Mar-26 195 → Jun-26 222 (+36%). Operating profit 13 → 12 → 11 → 20 → 20; OPM 8% → 7% → 7% → 10% → 9% [Screener].
- Net profit 8 → 6 → 5 → 10 → 10. Interest rose from ₹1 cr to ₹4 cr a quarter and depreciation from ₹2 cr to ₹4 cr in a year [Screener].
- Annual: sales FY24 532 → FY25 591 → FY26 681; operating profit 44 → 43 → 56; OPM 8% → 7% → 8%; net profit 29 → 27 → 29; ROCE 21% → 14% → 12% (it was 31–33% in FY21–FY23) [Screener].
- Company Q1 FY27 (incl. other income): EBITDA ₹21 cr (9.5%), PAT ₹10.5 cr (+32%); gross margin 23.0% vs 26.3% a year ago and 28.6% in Q4 [IP Q1FY27 P&L slide].
- Unit economics: EBITDA per tonne ₹16,380 in Q1 FY27 vs ₹11,252 a year ago and ₹15,053 in Q4 FY26 [Q1 call]. Drum selling price rose from ~₹138/kg (March) to ~₹193/kg; management expects ~₹160 → ₹140/kg as polymer prices ease; conversion margin ~₹25/kg vs ₹20 earlier [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Volume: HDPE drum tonnage −4% YoY in Q1; MS drums +51%, IBC-polymer +7%; IBC exports hit by the West Asia war and freight [IP Q1FY27 slide 11]. FY26 volume +20% [IP revenue table].

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (13 May 2026): FY27 revenue ~₹800 cr; EBITDA "between 75 and 80 crores, at a minimum" and margins "in double digits"; "major capex cycle is now largely behind us" with only ~₹20 cr of maintenance/balancing capex in FY27; utilisation to ~80%; recycling licence by June–July; solar saving ~₹10–12 cr in FY27 and ₹15 cr from FY28 [Q4 call].
- Q1 call (12 Aug 2026): revenue growth ~15% (≈₹780 cr) and EBITDA margin above 10%, "11–12%" once polymer prices fall; still "fully hopeful" of ₹80–86 cr EBITDA; **new Kutch IBC plant** (₹22–25 cr, 10,000 IBCs a month, by March 2027) announced; FY27 capex now ₹20–25 cr "primarily towards the Kutch expansion"; overall utilisation to 70–75% this year [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Delivery vs Q4 promises: margin — **short** (9.5% vs double digits); volume — **missed** (utilisation fell to 62%); recycling licence — **late** (partial licence; GPCB third-party waste authorisation came only on 18 Sep 2026); solar — on track (₹2 cr saving in Q1, 1 MW still pending); "capex over" — **changed** (Kutch) [Q1 call; BSE filing 23-Sep-26].
- Longer term: management talks of doubling the business "from here" via new locations (south, north) [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ~₹90 cr, "the largest single-year capex in our history" (Wada, recycling unit, 14.25 MW solar); about ₹120 cr over two years — ~₹60 cr solar, ~₹50–60 cr Wada, ₹8–12 cr recycling [AR FY26 highlights; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Solar: 14.25 MW captive (6.6 MW Oct 2025; 5 MW Bharuch + 2.25 MW Maharashtra Feb 2026; 1 MW pending); ~₹60 cr cost; ~₹15 cr a year saving at full run-rate, i.e. ~4-year payback [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Recycling: 5,000 t a year (10–12% of polymer needs); processed ~150 t in Q1 for ₹25 lakh EBITDA; FY27 EBITDA ~₹2 cr expected; GPCB now allows collection of up to 960 t a year of contaminated third-party containers and 9,000 cleaned drums a month [Q1 call; BSE filing 23-Sep-26]. Management said direct buying saves ₹10–15/kg [Q4 call].
- Kutch: own land; ~₹10 cr land and building, rest machinery; orders placed; 4-year payback; revenue ₹50 cr initially, ~₹90–100 cr at full use; to be "funded internally" [Q1 call].
- Wada Phase 2: machines only, ~₹20 cr per phase, after March 2027 (subsidy rules bar adding capacity before then) [Q1 call].
- State subsidies approved: ₹24.9 cr (Wada) + ₹10.5 cr (Bharuch Unit 7), received in cash over 10 years (~₹3.5 cr a year), timing of first receipt unclear [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 6].
- Capital commitments only ₹3.7 cr at Mar-26 [AR FY26 note 42].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- **Cash flow turned negative in FY26**: cash from operations −₹41 cr, investing −₹79 cr, free cash flow −₹118 cr; borrowings jumped from ₹55 cr (Mar-25) to ₹184 cr (Mar-26) [Screener]. Net debt/equity 0.64x at Mar-26 [IP Q1FY27 slide 4]; Acuité: gearing 0.67x vs 0.22x [Acuité Sep-26].
- Working capital stretched: inventory days 49 → 79, debtors 71 → 77, payables 35 → 31 → cash cycle 124 days in FY26 (86 in FY25) [Screener]. Inventory ₹107 cr at Mar-26 [AR FY26 auditor's report]. Cause: switch from credit-backed imports to cash local buying, plus polymer prices up ~₹50/kg [Q4 call].
- Management on the Q1 call: total loans ₹225–230 cr; term loans ~₹80–85 cr in repayment; interest ₹3.5 cr a quarter falling to ~₹3 cr by year-end; cash cycle "65–70 days" [Q1 call] — the last figure does not match Screener's 124 days.
- Management said in May the company had "a fund flow of 50 crores" in FY26; Screener shows negative operating cash flow [Q4 call; Screener].
- Acuité A-/Stable and A2+ (25 Sep 2026): bank lines raised to ₹235 cr from ₹138 cr (new ₹97 cr long-term incl. an HDFC term loan of ₹50 cr and Federal Bank ₹7.6 cr); interest cover 7.8x; cash accrual ₹40.8 cr vs ₹3.9 cr maturing debt; downgrade if debt/EBITDA above 3.5x [Acuité Sep-26].
- Loans carry personal guarantee of MD Bijay Kumar Agarwal and guarantees from promoter entities (Anmol Monower Plastics, Yash Synthetics, Credence Financial) [AR FY26 borrowings note; note 37].
- Dividend ₹0.50/share (payout ~6%) [Q4 call; Screener].

## Moat evidence
- Location: plants inside the Dahej–Bharuch–Vapi chemical belt and now Wada/Kutch; empty drums are bulky, so freight (~₹1,000 per IBC to Kutch) favours the nearest maker [AR FY26 chairman's letter; Q1 call].
- Scale in IBCs: 3.36 lakh IBCs in FY26, volume +42% in Q2 FY26 "despite increased competitive intensity" [AR FY26 operational highlights; IP revenue table].
- Price pass-through: orders booked in the first week of each month at current polymer prices; diesel and freight passed on within 15–30 days [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Integration: in-house caps, closures, handles; captive solar and recycling are cost edges few peers have [AR FY26; Q1 call].
- Limits: HDPE drums are commodity; Acuité calls the industry "highly competitive and fragmented"; gross margin fell 3–5 points in Q1 [Acuité Sep-26; IP Q1FY27].

## Risks and red flags
- **Cash conversion and debt**: negative operating cash flow in FY26 and debt tripling while management describes cash flow as healthy; the new Kutch plant was announced three months after "capex is behind us" [Screener; Q4 and Q1 calls].
- **Falling utilisation**: capacity up ~10% in a year but output flat at ~13,000 t a quarter for four quarters [Q1 call].
- Promoter stake 74.94% — at the 75% ceiling for listed companies, so free float is thin; FIIs + DIIs ~4% [Screener].
- Raw-material exposure: imports were ~42% of raw-material buying (Acuité), now mostly local; polymer spikes swell working capital [Acuité Sep-26; Q1 call].
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; key audit matters revenue recognition, trade-receivable recoverability (credit loss), capital expenditure capitalisation and inventory valuation; no CARO adverse remarks found in the parts read [AR FY26 auditor's report].
- Contingent liabilities small: bank guarantees ₹2.1 cr; no tax disputes [AR FY26 note 41]. Related-party dealings small: director pay ₹2.25 cr, rent ₹0.18 cr to a promoter company [AR FY26 note 37].
- Disclosure: quarterly volume and per-tonne data are given, but management's cash-cycle and "fund flow" statements conflict with reported numbers.

## What changed between the two calls
- May: "milestone year", capex over, double-digit margin, ₹800 cr revenue, ₹75–80 cr EBITDA. August: volume dip blamed on export freight, margin 9.5%, a new ₹22–25 cr Kutch plant, targets restated as 15% growth and >10% margin.
- Recycling licence moved from "June or July" to partial approval; full third-party authorisation arrived in September.
- New talking point: EBITDA per tonne (₹16,380) instead of margin percent.

## My read
Pyramid has done what it said on building capacity — Wada, solar, recycling — and per-tonne profit is at a record, but the capacity is not yet being filled: utilisation fell from 75% to 62% and ROCE from 31% to 12% in three years. The bigger worry is cash: FY26 operating cash flow was negative and debt tripled, while management spoke of healthy cash generation and then added a new plant. At ~19x earnings the price assumes margins recover to 11–12% and volumes grow. The view improves if utilisation climbs toward 70% in H2 FY27, polymer prices ease and working capital releases cash; it worsens if volumes stay flat and debt keeps rising.
