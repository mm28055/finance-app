# Knack Packaging Ltd — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code: KNACK (consolidated). Price ₹182, market cap ₹2,227 cr, ROCE 35.3%, ROE 43.6%, no dividend [Screener, 28 Sep 2026].

Context: listed 8 July 2026 (IPO ₹439.5 cr at ₹170: fresh issue ₹380 cr + ₹59.5 cr offer for sale; subscribed 83x; listed at ₹188 on NSE, +10.6%) [News: India TV, 8 Jul 2026]. Only **one** earnings call exists (Q1 FY27, 10 Aug 2026), so there is no second call to compare. No annual report has been published as a listed company; I used the draft red herring prospectus (DRHP, Sep 2025, restated FY23–FY25 accounts) in its place.

## Business and segments
- Ahmedabad-based maker of printed and laminated woven polypropylene (PLWPP) bags for 5–50 kg packs — rice, pulses, flour, spices, sugar, pet food, animal feed, seeds, fertiliser — and premium "pinch-bottom" bags (brick-shaped, six-side branding) [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Integrated from tape extrusion to weaving, gravure printing, lamination and conversion [Q1 call].
- Share: ~10.1% of India's flexible bulk PLWPP bag market; claims to be the largest PLWPP and pinch-bottom maker in India by FY25 revenue (Technopak report cited by the company) [IP Q1FY27 p.14].
- Mix: exports ~55–56% of sales, domestic ~45%; 74 countries (71 in FY26); 2,000+ customers, 13,493 SKUs [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]. Pinch-bottom share of sales 10% (2023) → 16% (2024) → ~20% (2025) → 22–23% now [Q1 call].
- Export gross margin is 5–6 points higher than domestic [Q1 call].
- Customers: Cargill (US and 8 countries) ₹140 cr a year; Cargill was 19.0% of FY25 revenue, 22.3% of FY24; top 5 = 35.2%, top 10 = 43.9% (FY25); US customers 26.6% of revenue [DRHP p.276, risk factors; Q1 call]. Management variously called Cargill "16–17%" and "12%" of sales on the call [Q1 call].
- Capacity: >48,000 tonnes a year incl. rented plants, ~91% utilised; owned installed capacity ~43,300 t at listing [Q1 call]. FY25 effective capacity 36,400 t at 86% utilisation; production 31,297 t [DRHP p.126].
- Mexico JV (Sayem Knack, 50:50 with customer Sacos y Empaques Internacionales) commissioned April 2026; South Africa subsidiary [IP Q1FY27 p.12–13; DRHP].

## Guidance history
- No formal numeric guidance. Management said Q1 sales levels "will be maintained" through the year using two rented plants (one added ~a month before the call, ~5,040 t a year) until the new plant starts [Q1 call].
- Order book ₹130 cr on hand (about half a quarter's sales) [Q1 call].
- New plant "on track" for October 2027; main construction "after the rains" [Q1 call].
- No prior call, so no guidance track record exists yet.

## Capex and capacity
- IPO-funded greenfield at Borisana, Kadi (Mehsana, Gujarat): DRHP earmarked ₹435 cr of proceeds for a new line with pinch-bottom machines; final fresh issue was ₹380 cr, and the CFO said "some ₹320 crores" will go into the plant [DRHP p.124–126; Q1 call]. Capacity to rise to ~70,000 t a year (+62% on owned 43,300 t) by October 2027 [Q1 call].
- Gross block ₹416 cr at Jun-26 vs ₹273 cr a year earlier (+₹141 cr; +₹31 cr in Q1) [Q1 call].
- FY26 spend: two pinch-bottom machines and one lamination machine ~₹47 cr from internal accruals [CARE Apr-26].
- Asset turns: 2.53x gross block, 4.12x net block (company) [Q1 call]. The new plant roughly doubles gross block; at 2.5x gross-block turns, ₹320–380 cr of new assets would support ₹800–950 cr of extra sales at full use [my arithmetic].
- Interim: leased plants for semi-finished fabric so owned lines can focus on finishing [Q1 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹188 cr at Mar-25 [Screener]; debt/equity 0.59x at Jun-26 (0.74x a year earlier) [IP Q1FY27 p.25]. The ₹380 cr IPO cash arrived in July 2026, after the June balance sheet [News: India TV].
- Cash from operations ₹92 cr FY26 (₹93 cr FY25); free cash flow −₹10 cr FY26 after ₹100 cr investing outflow [Screener].
- Working capital 86 days (company) [IP Q1FY27 p.24]; Screener FY25: debtor days 60, inventory days 76, payable days 30, cash conversion cycle 106 days [Screener].
- Buys raw material "in cash" for discounts [Q1 call]. No dividend paid [Screener].
- Rating: CARE A-/Stable, A2+ reaffirmed 10 Apr 2026; gearing 0.72x at Dec-25; interest cover 12x in 9M FY26 [CARE Apr-26].

## Moat evidence
- First mover (2004) in retail-quality printing on 25–50 kg bags; claims first in India/Asia with laser-cut easy-open pinch-bottom bags [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Switching costs: 90–94% customer continuity (93.75% in FY26); packaging is <1–2% of the customer's product cost; proprietary colour recipes [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.5].
- Cargill kept buying from India through an 8-month 50% US tariff and refused an Oman alternative [Q1 call].
- Cost: Indian labour/power cheaper than Thailand/Cambodia; US competitor Polytex sells a dog-food bag at ~$0.70–0.75 vs Knack's $0.35–0.40 [Q1 call].
- Pass-through: 45–50% of customers on "polymer price plus fixed conversion" contracts (e.g., Cargill, Dawat); others repriced order by order [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Promoter-owned premises:** registered office, manufacturing plants and even the land for the IPO-funded plant are leased from the promoters and promoter group [DRHP risk factor 34]. This is a significant governance point: the public company's new ₹300 cr+ asset sits on promoter land.
- **Related-party flows:** aggregate related-party transactions were 20.4% of revenue in FY25 (mostly promoter loans taken/repaid ~₹11–12 cr each, purchases from promoter-group "Knack" entities, a ₹5.6 cr loan to a group entity) [DRHP p.31, summary RPT].
- **Customer concentration:** Cargill ~19%, US ~27% of revenue; no contracts with top-10 customers (DRHP) though Cargill now has a 2+1-year contract [DRHP; Q1 call].
- **Statutory dues delays:** DRHP discloses past delays in PF/ESI payments; also historical MCA filing discrepancies (FY2015-16) [DRHP risk factor 31].
- **Asset-turn questions:** analysts challenged the 4x net-block turn vs 1.5–2x for peers; answer relied on older depreciated assets and product mix [Q1 call]. Screener ROCE (35%) is far below the company's quarterly annualised 54.7% — the company's figures are flattering.
- **Disclosure quality:** the results table appears to show Q4 FY26 consolidated EBITDA margin as 20.65% while its own figures (₹44.6 cr on ₹224.5 cr) give 19.86%; claims ROE fell because of the "enlarged equity base following the recent listing" though the June balance sheet predates the IPO [IP Q1FY27 p.24–25].
- Execution: first large greenfield; relies on moving a trained workforce from leased plants [Q1 call].
- Litigation small: 9 cases filed by the company (₹0.7 cr), one tax case against it (₹0.07 cr); no auditor qualifications in restated accounts [DRHP p.29–30].
- Promoters hold 70.6% (lock-in applies post-IPO) [Screener].

## My read
The business is genuinely improving: volumes +21%, richer pinch-bottom mix, solar savings and a first-mover position in branded bulk bags have lifted operating margins from 11% (FY23) to ~20%. The next leg (to ~70,000 t by late 2027) is funded by IPO cash, so balance-sheet risk is low, but returns will fall as a large new asset base is added and filled. Governance needs watching — promoter-owned land under the new plant, heavy related-party flows and inconsistent investor figures. I would turn more positive after two or three quarters of clean reporting and on-time plant progress; more negative if margins slip below 18% as polymer prices swing or if Cargill's share rises further.
