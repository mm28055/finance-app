# Indian packaging — sector research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Scope: consumer and industrial packaging makers in the note — flexible films (Cosmo First, Ester), flexible converters (Huhtamaki India, TCPL's flexibles), laminated tubes (EPL), glass (AGI Greenpac), paperboard cartons (TCPL), rigid plastic (Mold-Tek, Pyramid, Knack), woven PP/FIBC (Kanpur Plastipack). Company figures come from the deep files in this folder; sector figures carry their own source tags.

## Market size and growth
- Indian packaging market ~US$101 bn in 2025, forecast US$170 bn by 2030 (10.7% a year); flexible packaging is 54% of it and forecast to grow 11.5% a year — **Mordor Intelligence**, as cited in Huhtamaki India's annual report [AR Huhtamaki CY25 MD&A]. Food is ~48.5% of packaging demand [same].
- Industrial packaging (drums, IBCs, bulk bags) ~US$5.2 bn in 2025, forecast US$9.5 bn by 2034; Indian IBC market ~US$1.26 bn by 2030 — market-research figures cited by Pyramid Technoplast [AR Pyramid FY26 MD&A].
- BOPP film: India ~9 lakh t a year, ~₹13,000 cr, growing 8–10% [Cosmo IP Q1FY27 slide 15]. BOPET film: Indian capacity ~1.35 mn t, operating ~85% because much is exported [Ester Q1 call]. Flexible packaging volumes grow 8–10% a year [Cosmo Q4 call].
- Container glass: AGI is ~17–18% of the organised market; HNG has the largest installed capacity [CARE Apr-26 via deep/02-AGI].
- Reality check on volumes: the research-firm growth rates (10–11% in value) are far above what end-markets delivered in 2026 (next section). Value growth in 2026 is mostly price pass-through.

## Demand drivers — FMCG and pharma volumes
- **FMCG volumes fell 2% YoY in Apr–Jun 2026**; value +0.8% on 2.8% price growth; rural volume −5%, urban +0.1%; 68% of categories lost volume; traditional trade −6%, modern trade +17.5%, e-commerce +34.8% (quick commerce +40%); small manufacturers (<₹100 cr sales) −10.5% — **NielsenIQ**, reported 27 Sep 2026 [News: ET Small Business via RetailIntel, 27-Sep-26].
- Previous quarter (Oct–Dec 2025): FMCG value +7.8%, rural volume +2.9%, urban +2.3%; NIQ expected GST 2.0 benefits to show from Jan–Mar 2026 [NIQ, 13-Mar-26].
- Implication: packaging makers reporting 20–46% revenue growth in Q1 FY27 (Cosmo +46%, Huhtamaki +23%, Pyramid +36%) are riding price, not FMCG volume; Huhtamaki said part of its Q2 CY26 volume was customers stocking up ahead of price rises [deep/07]. Expect volume payback in Q2–Q3 FY27.
- Channel shift helps premium formats: e-commerce is ~21% of FMCG sales in the top 8 metros and ~7% nationally [NIQ via RetailIntel, Sep-26] — more secondary packaging, cartons and tamper-evident packs.
- **Pharma (Indian Pharmaceutical Market): July 2026 value +12.1%, of which price 5.9%, new launches 3.8%, volume only 2.4%; moving-annual-total value +10.2% with unit growth 1.1%** — **Pharmarack** [News: BioSpectrum/Business Standard, Aug-26]. Pharma packaging volume (blisters, cartons, tubes, glass vials) is therefore growing slowly; value is price-led.
- Chemicals and agrochemicals (for drums/IBCs/FIBCs): Pyramid says Indian chemical customers' realisations rose while volumes fell in Q1 FY27; exports to the Middle East stalled on freight [deep/10].
- Liquor/beer (for glass): weather-hit beer season hurt AGI's inventory in FY26 [deep/02].

## Policy — duties, EPR, trade
- **Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules, 2026** (G.S.R. 237(E), 31 Mar 2026) — mandatory recycled content for brand owners [Corporate Professionals summary of notification]:

| Category | FY26 | FY27 | FY28 | FY29+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I – rigid plastic | 30% | 40% | 50% | 60% |
| II – flexible (single layer) | 10% | 10% | 20% | 20% |
| III – multilayer | 5% | 5% | 10% | 10% |

  Food-contact shortfalls can be carried forward up to three years from FY27 (one-third a year); certificates can be traded on the CPCB portal; rigid reuse targets 10% → 25% by FY29 [same]. Winners: BOPET/rPET makers (Ester says brands are switching from BOPP/BOPE to BOPET because it is the only food-grade substrate that can take enough recycled content) and recyclers (Pyramid's 5,000 t plant) [deep/08; deep/10]. Enforcement is lax — Ester says recycled-content enforcement "has yet to take place" and the EPR portal was shut ~3–5 months for an upgrade in 2026 [deep/08; deep/10].
- **BOPET anti-dumping**: DGTR opened a case on PET film from Bangladesh, China, Taiwan, Thailand and the US on 30 Sep 2025 (applicants Chiripal, Ester, Vacmet); preliminary findings 21 Mar 2026 recommended provisional duties of US$56–330/t on Bangladesh, China and Thailand; oral hearing 13 Jul 2026; **no final findings published yet** [DGTR case page; duty range per ICIS 23-Mar-26 headline — article body not readable]. Cosmo's management (Aug 2026) spoke of the duty as "levied recently" [deep/06].
- **Paperboard minimum import price** ₹67,220/t (CIF) on virgin multi-layer board (Aug 2025), extended by DGFT Notification 14/2026-27 **to 30 Sep 2026** — renewal is due now; IPMA also sought anti-dumping on Indonesian board [A2Z Taxcorp, Apr-26; IPMA petition per search results, not opened]. Board prices stable in Aug–Sep 2026: SBS ₹88–96/kg, duplex ₹40–50/kg [Indian Printer & Publisher, 21-Sep-26]. TCPL says "drip-by-drip" board price rises are hard to pass on [deep/03].
- **Import duty waiver on PP** (March–June 2026, Customs Notification 12/2026) to fight the war shock; it made DFIA export licences worthless for a while — Kanpur did not book ₹7.0 cr of DFIA benefits [deep/09].
- **US tariffs**: the US Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs on 20 Feb 2026 (6–3); the 25% extra "Russian oil" tariff on India had already been lifted on 7 Feb 2026; a replacement Section 122 tariff of 10% on all countries started 24 Feb 2026, later raised to 15% [WilmerHale, 20-Feb-26]. Refunds are flowing: Cosmo's US arm got ~US$7 mn in July 2026 [deep/06]. Ester and Cosmo both say US volumes are recovering [deep/06; deep/08].
- **UK FTA** in force from mid-July 2026 — Kanpur delayed UK shipments to benefit [deep/09].
- EU Ecodesign (ESPR) textile-waste rules drive Ester's ELITe chemical-recycling JV (start CY2028) [deep/08].

## Cost drivers — polymer and energy
- The West Asia war (from late Feb/March 2026) caused a polymer spike: PP rose from ~US$1,000/t to ~US$1,700/t; Indian petrochemical plants were told to prioritise LPG [Kanpur Q4 call, deep/09; Cosmo Q1 call, deep/06]. HDPE drum prices went from ~₹138/kg (March) to ~₹193/kg [deep/10].
- Prices are now falling: Reliance cut HDPE/LLDPE by ₹9,000/t on 9 Jul and HDPE/LDPE by ₹4,000/t and PP by ₹6,000/t on 17 Jul 2026; Indian plants run ~95% vs ~70% globally, and analysts see prices "nearing a floor" [Industrial Front, Jul-26]. Management "new normal": PP US$1,200–1,350/t (Kanpur); drum resin to fall ₹20/kg to ~₹140/kg (Pyramid) [deep/09; deep/10].
- Pass-through is near-complete but lagged: commodity film reprices at once, specialty film 1–3 months, converters 2 weeks to a quarter; percentage margins shrink when prices rise even if ₹/kg holds (Cosmo EBITDA/kg +15%, Pyramid EBITDA/t record ₹16,380) [deep/06; deep/10].
- Working capital is the hidden cost: Cosmo +₹85 cr in Q1, Huhtamaki operating WC ₹305 cr → ₹464 cr, Pyramid FY26 operating cash flow negative [deep/06, 07, 10].
- Other costs: ocean freight US$2,000 → US$5,000 per container (Kanpur); state minimum-wage revisions (+₹3.5 cr a quarter at Kanpur); new labour codes (Nov 2025) created one-off gratuity charges across the sector; gas/fuel is 20–30% of glass costs [deep/09; deep/02; deep/03].

## Competitive structure and share
- **Films (BOPP/BOPET)**: oligopoly of ~7 large players (Cosmo, Jindal, SRF, Uflex, Polyplex, Vacmet, Chiripal/Ester etc.) with >60% of capacity [CRISIL, 16-Sep-24]. Cosmo holds ~30% of Indian BOPP capacity [CRISIL Sep-25, deep/06].
- **Flexible converting**: highly fragmented; Huhtamaki (MNC) and a few organised players vs many small converters [CRISIL Aug-26, deep/07].
- **Tubes**: EPL ~20% of the global laminated-tube market, ~35% of oral care [CARE Apr-26, deep/01].
- **Glass**: AGI and HNG dominate; HNG's insolvency tightened supply in FY25–FY26 [CARE Apr-26, deep/02].
- **Cartons**: TCPL among the largest organised folding-carton makers; tobacco, FMCG, pharma, liquor [deep/03].
- **Industrial rigid**: Pyramid and Time Technoplast lead IBCs (an analyst put Pyramid at 40% — unconfirmed); HDPE drums fragmented [deep/10].
- **FIBC**: India supplies ~60% of Europe's FIBC imports; Turkey/Eastern Europe 20–25% [Kanpur Q4 call, deep/09].

- Profit pools sit where the product is specified and hard to swap: glass (few furnaces, long lead times), tubes (global share, customer lines built around the tube), niche rigid (Knack, Mold-Tek IML). Commodity films earn cost of capital only at the top of the cycle (film OPM fell to a decadal low ~6% in FY24, historical average 12–13%) [CRISIL, 16-Sep-24].

## Cycle position
- **Films**: FY21–FY24 capacity additions (BOPP +~20%, BOPET +~45%) created a glut; FY24 margins hit ~6% [CRISIL, 16-Sep-24]; oversupply was absorbed in FY25 [ICRA Vacmet rationale, 18-Aug-25]. Now a new wave: Cosmo's 81,000 t BOPP line (Jun 2025) plus lines reported for SRF (Indore BOPP/BOPE) and Jindal (Nashik) — the last two from market-report search snippets, not verified; Cosmo counts 7–8 new lines in India over three years; Ester counts two BOPET lines in 18 months and 2–3 more in 1.5–2 years, but says BOPET demand (PWM switching) is outpacing supply [deep/06; deep/08]. Spreads today: BOPP base ₹20–30/kg (incl. stock gains), BOPET ₹9/kg (Cosmo) vs ₹28–33/kg for 12-micron (Ester) — the figures are not like-for-like, but both say BOPET is tighter than BOPP [deep/06; deep/08]. **Position: mid-cycle, with BOPP at risk from new lines and BOPET supported by duties and PWM demand.**
- **Glass**: tight in FY25–FY26 (HNG weak); CARE expects AGI's margins to "normalise at 22–24%" as HNG's supply returns; AGI adds +25% capacity (Gwalior, Mar-27) [CARE Apr-26, deep/02]. **Late-cycle peak margins.**
- **Paperboard**: import pressure contained by the MIP; board prices flat; renewal due 30 Sep 2026 [above]. **Neutral.**
- **Industrial rigid / FIBC**: capacity just added (Pyramid +22% FY26, Kanpur Unit 3, non-woven), utilisation falling (Pyramid 75% → 62%); freight and chemical-export weakness hurt Q1 FY27 [deep/09; deep/10]. **Early in a fill-the-capacity phase.**
- **Demand**: FMCG volumes contracting (−2%) and pharma units ~1–2% — the weakest end-market volume backdrop since 2020; price has masked it.

## Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecaster named)
- **Mordor Intelligence**: Indian packaging market +10.7% a year to 2030; flexible +11.5% a year [via AR Huhtamaki CY25]. Treat as value growth including inflation.
- **CRISIL Ratings** (company level, Sep 2026): Ester revenue growth 18–20% and EBITDA margin 12–13% "over the medium term" [CRISIL Sep-26, deep/08]; Kanpur upgrade needs 20% growth at 10–11% margin [CRISIL Jul-26, deep/09].
- **CARE Ratings** (Apr 2026): AGI margins normalise to 22–24% [deep/02].
- **CRISIL Ratings** (Sep 2024, sector): film industry margin 7.5–8% in FY25 recovering from ~6%, still below the 12–13% average; domestic demand ~75% of film volumes [CRISIL, 16-Sep-24]. No newer sector-wide CRISIL/ICRA/CARE packaging forecast for FY27–FY29 was found in the last 12 months.
- **Company guidance (FY27)**: Cosmo revenue ~+20%, ROCE 15–20% in 12–24 months; Ester debt −₹100 cr, 6–8 quarters of good spreads; Huhtamaki "grow with the market" (3–5%); Kanpur ₹900–950 cr by FY28, ₹1,000–1,050 cr by FY29; Pyramid +15% and >10% margin [deep/06–10].
- **My synthesis**: FY27 sector revenue growth of 12–18% is mostly price; volumes +3–6% at best given NIQ and Pharmarack data. FY28–FY29 should see volume recovery (FMCG base effects, GST 2.0, quick commerce) of ~6–8% a year, with value growth ~8–10% if polymer prices settle. Margin direction diverges: films face BOPP supply in FY28; glass normalises lower; converters and rigid players gain as resin prices fall and new capacity fills; PWM recycled-content steps (rigid 40% → 60%, flexible 10% → 20% in FY28) favour integrated recyclers and BOPET.

## What to watch
- Paperboard MIP renewal after 30 Sep 2026; final DGTR findings on PET film.
- NIQ Jul–Sep 2026 volumes (festive season) — whether the −2% turns positive.
- Polymer prices after the July cuts; ocean freight; Section 122/301 US tariff actions.
- Commissioning of new BOPP lines (SRF Indore, Jindal) and HNG's output under its new owner.
- CPCB EPR portal reopening and actual enforcement of recycled-content rules.
