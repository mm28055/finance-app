# Cosmo First — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code COSMOFIRST (consolidated). Price ₹849, market cap ₹2,230 cr, P/E 13.0, book value ₹616, ROCE 11.2%, ROE 10.4% [Screener, 28-Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- India's largest BOPP film maker: BOPP capacity 2,77,000 t a year (up from 1,96,000 t after an 81,000 t line started June 2025), plus BOPET 30,000 t, CPP 30,000 t, coated films 45,000 t, metallised 57,000 t, thermal lamination 26,000 t, synthetic paper 7,200 t [AR FY26 MD&A p.77].
- Films are ~90% of revenue: film revenue FY26 ₹3,265 cr (+25%); Q1 FY27 ₹1,034 cr (+45%) with film EBITDA ₹140 cr vs ₹122 cr (+15%) [IP Q1FY27 slides 5, 15].
- BOPP/CPP are ~90% of film volume, BOPET ~10% [Q4 call]. Specialty + semi-specialty films were 61% of film volume in Q1 FY27 (56–58% in FY26, ~70% before the new line diluted the mix); target back to ~70% in 18–24 months [IP Q1FY27 slide 17; Q4 call].
- Claims: world's largest thermal lamination film maker, #2 in specialty label films, top-4 globally in BOPP specialty films [AR FY26 p.74; IP slide 21]. CRISIL puts its share at ~30% of domestic BOPP capacity [CRISIL Sep-25].
- Exports ₹1,592 cr in FY26 = 44% of sales, to 100+ countries; US and Japan are the premium markets [AR FY26 p.80; IP slide 20].
- Adjacencies (Q1 FY27 revenue / growth / EBITDA) [IP Q1FY27 slides 5, 13, 25]:
  - Specialty chemicals (coatings, masterbatch, adhesives; ~80% sold captively to films at arm's length): ₹66 cr, +34%, EBITDA ₹17 cr (26%). FY26 ₹204 cr, EBITDA ₹53 cr.
  - Cosmo Plastech (thin-wall rigid containers): ₹36 cr, +58%, EBITDA ₹5 cr incl. a one-time ₹2.4 cr state incentive (7% normalised). FY26 ₹92 cr.
  - Cosmo Consumer (window film, paint-protection film, ceramic coatings): ₹11 cr (4.5x), EBITDA −₹1 cr. FY26 ₹23 cr.
  - Zigly pet care: ₹18 cr, +70%, EBITDA −₹14 cr (vs −₹10 cr). FY26 ₹55 cr; 47 centres; two vet hospitals bought (Mumbai, Bengaluru) [AR FY26 p.79].
- Utilisation: films ~85% in Q1 FY27; specialty chemicals has 15–20% headroom; Plastech adding 50% capacity with little capex [Q1 call].

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly sales: Q1 FY26 800 → Q2 919 → Q3 899 → Q4 1,021 → Q1 FY27 1,166 (+46% YoY). Operating profit 92 → 103 → 68 → 121 → 136; OPM 11% → 11% → 8% → 12% → 12% [Screener].
- Net profit: 43 → 47 → 30 → 37 → 54 (Q1 FY27 +26% YoY). Interest ₹38 cr and depreciation ₹38 cr a quarter now, up from ₹23–24 cr each in FY25, eat most of the operating gain [Screener].
- Annual: sales FY24 2,587 → FY25 2,895 → FY26 3,639; operating profit 183 → 288 → 384; net profit 62 → 133 → 156; ROCE 7% → 10% → 11% [Screener]. Peak was FY22: sales 3,038, operating profit 572, profit 397, ROCE 31% — the cycle high.
- Company "EBITDA" includes other income: FY26 ₹479 cr (= Screener operating profit 384 + other income 95), Q1 FY27 ₹147 cr (12.6%) [IP Q1FY27 slide 10; Screener].
- Unit economics per kg (contribution): base BOPP ₹30 in Q1 FY27 (incl. one-off stock gain) vs ₹20 in Q4 and ₹23 a year ago; BOPET ₹9 vs ₹18 and ₹13; semi-specialty ₹45 vs ₹36; specialty ~₹63, steady above ₹60 for five quarters [Q1 call; IP slide 17].
- Revenue grew 46% on only 9% volume growth: the rest is raw-material cost passed on after the West Asia war lifted polymer prices; management says EBITDA per kg rose ~15% [Q1 call].
- Export volume fell 13% in Q1 because of port congestion (goods in transit not booked) and one line was down for maintenance [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (21 May 2026): "double-digit" revenue growth in FY27; ROCE ~14–15% in FY27 from 11% in FY26; net debt/EBITDA below 2x in 12–18 months; capex under ₹100 cr in FY27; Plastech high-single-digit EBITDA margin in FY27 and mid-teens by FY28; Cosmo Consumer loss-making for ~2 years, 50%+ growth; US business +15–20%; US tariff refund "more than ₹60 cr" in 6–12 months [Q4 call].
- Q1 call (7 Aug 2026): guidance **raised** — revenue ~20% in FY27, new businesses ~60%; ROCE target widened to 15–20% over 12–24 months; US business +25–30%; specialty chemicals now expected to beat its FY30 ₹400–500 cr target by FY29; Plastech ₹150–160 cr in FY27 and ₹200–250 cr in FY28; debt cut of ₹400–500 cr over two years [Q1 call].
- Delivery vs Q4 promises: US refund arrived early (~US$7 mn in July 2026; not yet booked, part is owed to customers) — **delivered**. Plastech normalised 7% EBITDA — **on track**. Net debt flat at ₹1,166 cr (2.3x) because working capital rose ₹85 cr — **deleveraging delayed** a quarter, though the ratio fell from 2.4x [Q1 call; IP slide 12].
- Longer targets (FY30): specialty chemicals ₹400–500 cr (~10% of group revenue, 20%+ ROCE); Plastech ₹350–400 cr; Cosmo Consumer ₹200 cr [IP Q1FY27 slides 25, 28, 30; AR FY26 p.78].
- On FY28, management declined to give a number on the Q1 call ("still evaluating") [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- About ₹1,200 cr spent over FY24–FY26 (BOPP, CPP and BOPET lines, metallisers, coating lines, window film/PPF, Plastech, Zigly). Capex was ₹502 cr in FY25 and ₹412 cr in FY26 [AR FY26 p.79–80]. Screener cash flow shows investing outflows of ₹420 cr (FY25) and ₹361 cr (FY26); free cash flow −₹325 cr and −₹8 cr [Screener].
- Still to come: "one of the world's largest" coating lines, 18,000 t a year, due in FY27 [AR FY26 p.79]; the presentation mentions an expansion at Bidkin (Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar) "underway" for the next phase — no amount given [IP Q1FY27 slide 19]. Capital commitments not yet executed fell to ₹43 cr at Mar-26 from ₹123 cr [AR FY26 standalone contingencies & commitments note].
- FY27 capex guided below ₹100 cr; non-film businesses need ₹50–75 cr a year for FY27–FY28 unless Zigly makes acquisitions. About ₹270–300 cr has already gone into Plastech, Consumer and Zigly [Q4 call].
- Plastech is adding 50% capacity "with minimal capex" over two quarters [Q1 call].
- Two private solar/wind power deals should save ~₹20 cr a year (ESG slide says ₹25 cr); none of it in Q1 — first from Q3 FY27, second from Q1 FY28 [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 ESG slide].
- Payback: the film assets are ~85% used, so the next 15% of volume needs no capex; this is the core of the ROCE-recovery case. Asset turn on Screener: FY26 sales ₹3,639 cr on gross fixed assets ~₹2,480 cr + ₹189 cr CWIP ≈ 1.4x [Screener].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹1,680 cr at Mar-26 (₹1,438 cr Mar-25, ₹760 cr Mar-21) [Screener]. Company net debt ₹1,159 cr at Mar-26 and ₹1,166 cr at Jun-26 (2.3x EBITDA, 0.7x equity; peak ₹1,234 cr in Jun-25 at 2.9x) [IP slide 12].
- Cash and liquid investments ₹420 cr at Mar-26 [AR FY26 p.80].
- Cash from operations ₹397 cr in FY26 (CFO/operating profit 112%) after ₹166 cr in FY25 [Screener].
- Working capital: debtor days 40, inventory days 110, payable days 140 → cash cycle ~10 days in FY26 — Cosmo leans heavily on supplier credit (payable days 94 → 143 → 139 → 140 over FY23–FY26) [Screener]. Q1 FY27 added ₹85 cr of working capital as polymer prices jumped [Q1 call].
- Interest cost ₹141 cr in FY26 vs ₹89 cr in FY24 [Screener].
- Dividend ₹4/share for FY26 (payout ~7%) [Q4 call; Screener].
- CRISIL AA-/Stable and A1+ reaffirmed Sep 2025 on ₹2,214 cr of facilities; upgrade needs debt/EBITDA below 1.25x; downgrade trigger is debt/EBITDA near 3x or more debt-funded capex [CRISIL Sep-25].

## Moat evidence
- Scale and cost: ~30% of Indian BOPP capacity; newest lines are "most cost-efficient"; management calls itself the lowest-cost producer [CRISIL Sep-25; AR FY26 p.79; Q4 call].
- Specialty pricing power: specialty contribution ~₹63/kg vs base BOPP ₹20–30/kg; semi-specialty ₹15–20/kg above base. Specialty films have grown ~10% a year for 6–7 years [IP slides 16–18; Q1 call].
- Switching costs: specialty films need customer approvals; specialty prices reset monthly/quarterly with a 1–3 month lag (base films reprice immediately) [Q4 call].
- IP: 6 patents granted, 11 pending; R&D ~0.5% of revenue [IP slide 22; AR FY26 p.81].
- Customer loyalty: during the US tariff period "we did not lose any customers" and signed new ones [Q4 call].
- Limits: base film is a commodity with 7–8 new BOPP lines coming to India over three years; base margins swing ₹13–30/kg quarter to quarter [Q4 call; IP slide 17].

## Risks and red flags
- **Promoter pay is very high.** Chairman & MD Ashok Jaipuria took ₹24.4 cr in FY26 (salary ₹7.5 cr + commission ₹12.1 cr + perquisites ₹4.8 cr), 387x the median employee, up 15%; whole-time director Yamini Kumar ₹7.0 cr (+162%); total key-management short-term pay ₹52.7 cr vs ₹30.7 cr in FY25 — about a third of FY26 consolidated profit of ₹156 cr [AR FY26 Annexure & note 44].
- Related-party loans: ₹10 cr loan to the chairman in FY25 (repaid in the year); a small loan to CEO Pankaj Poddar (₹0.47 cr repaid in FY26); CSR money flows to promoter-linked foundations (₹2.8 cr to Sitaram Jaipuria Foundation, ₹3.3 cr Cosmo Foundation) [AR FY26 note 44].
- Auditor (standalone): unmodified opinion; key audit matters were derivative valuation and **recognition of state incentive income** (judgement on eligibility and timing) [AR FY26 auditor's report]. CARO: stock statements to banks differed from books (unreported advances from subsidiaries; provisional figures) — minor; no defaults [AR FY26 CARO (ii)(b), (ix)].
- Contingent liabilities (consolidated): bank guarantees to third parties ₹101 cr (₹61 cr), excise/service-tax disputes ₹48.6 cr (₹34.0 cr), income tax ₹9.1 cr, claims ₹7.2 cr [AR FY26 consolidated note 38]. Small relative to ₹1,676 cr net worth.
- Earnings quality: other income ₹95 cr in FY26 (25% of operating profit) and volatile — ₹24 cr, ₹25 cr, ₹35 cr, ₹10 cr, ₹11 cr over the last five quarters; management blamed currency swings [Screener; Q1 call]. Q4 FY26 had ₹12 cr of one-offs (₹7.2 cr Netherlands subsidiary provision; ₹5.3 cr deferred-tax reversal in Korea) [Q4 call].
- Base-film oversupply (7–8 lines) and BOPET overcapacity; BOPET contribution fell to ₹9/kg in Q1 [Q4 and Q1 calls].
- Cash burn in consumer bets: Zigly loss rose to ₹14 cr in Q1; break-even needs ~₹250 cr revenue, "a couple of years" away; a carve-out with outside capital is planned [Q1 call; IP slide 13].
- Promoter holding fell from 45.0% to 41.0% in Dec-24 and is 40.78% now; ESOP trust holds 1.11% [Screener; IP slide 36].
- Disclosure gap: annual report shows only two segments (packaging, specialty chemicals); film volumes are not disclosed ("the information we can share is in the presentation") [AR FY26 note 44; Q4 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "capex done, sweat assets" to explaining why margin % fell while ₹/kg rose. Guidance went from vague (double-digit) to specific (20% revenue, 60% new businesses, 15–20% ROCE).
- New issues in Q1: West Asia war (polymer shortages, LPG diversion), port congestion (−13% export volume), ₹85 cr working-capital rise. BOPET went from "spreads should rise" (Q4) to ₹9/kg with hopes pinned on anti-dumping duty.
- New plans: Plastech +50% capacity; TV advertising for Cosmo PPF from July 2026; first car-care centre in Pune.

## My read
The film business is past its capex hump: capacity is 45% bigger, 85% used, and specialty mix is recovering (61%), so volume and mix can lift profit with little new spending. But reported profit growth is being eaten by higher interest and depreciation, and base-film spreads depend on a market with 7–8 new lines coming. The stock at ~13x earnings and ~1.4x book prices in little, so it needs only modest delivery — the 20% revenue guide and net debt falling toward ₹800–900 cr by FY28. The view improves if ROCE reaches 14–15% in FY27 and debt actually falls; it worsens if base BOPP spreads slip back to ₹13/kg, if the consumer bets keep burning cash, or if promoter pay keeps rising faster than profit.
