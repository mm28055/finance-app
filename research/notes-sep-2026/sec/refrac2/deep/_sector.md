# Sector research — Electrodes & Refractories (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Two different businesses share this note. **Graphite electrodes** (HEG Graphite, Graphite India) are consumables for electric arc furnaces (EAF); ~65–70% of Indian output is exported, so the driver is *global* EAF steel. **Refractories** (RHI Magnesita India, IFGL) are heat-proof linings sold mostly inside India, so the driver is *Indian* steel and cement output and new plant builds.

## 1. Market size and growth
- India crude steel output FY26: **168.4 Mt, +10.7%** (JPC). Capacity rose 10% to **233 Mt** in FY26 and is projected at ~**298 Mt by FY30** (BigMint). [Mysteel/JPC, Apr 2026] [BigMint, 2026]
- FY27 so far is slower on output: Apr–Aug 2026 crude steel **71.0 Mt, +2.8%**, while finished-steel consumption rose **7.4% to 70.5 Mt**; finished imports **+29.5%** and exports +34.1% (JPC, 24 Sep 2026). Calendar H1 2026 was stronger (India +7.1–7.5% to 87.0 Mt per worldsteel). [Chartforest/JPC, Sep 2026] [Graphite India IP Q1FY27] [HEG Q1 call]
- Route mix Apr–Aug 2026: blast furnace/BOF 43.9% (31.1 Mt), **induction furnace 38.9%** (27.6 Mt), **EAF only 17.2%** (12.2 Mt). EAF capacity 46 Mt in FY26, projected to rise "only modestly to 55 mnt by FY'30" while BOF capacity rises to 142 Mt. [Chartforest/JPC] [BigMint]
- Global: world crude steel H1 2026 **930.6 Mt, −0.7%**; ex-China **430.6 Mt, +2.1%** (worldsteel). [HEG IP Q1FY27 p.6]
- Global EAF share ex-China rose from 44% (2015) to 50% (2023) and 51% (2024); China ~11% (worldsteel, cited by HEG). [HEG IP Q1FY27 p.2]
- Indian refractory market ~**US$2.6 bn (2025) → ~US$3.5 bn (2034)**, ~3% a year in dollars (IMARC estimate cited in RHIM's annual report); steel is the largest end-user. Cement output ~485 Mt in FY26 (+8–9%). [RHIM AR FY26 MD&A]

## 2. Demand drivers
- **Indian steel build-out** — Choice Broking (Jun 2026) projects crude steel from 168.4 Mt (FY26) to **255 Mt by FY31**, with induction-furnace output rising from 45.8 Mt to 69.4 Mt (good for ramming-mass refractory makers). RHIM estimates steel-sector capex of ₹50,000–60,000 cr in FY27. [News: ANI/Choice Broking, 8 Jun 2026] [RHIM Q1 call]
- **New blast furnaces, coke ovens, DRI and pellet plants** in India (OECD: India added 41.4 Mt capacity in 2021–25 and plans 31.8 Mt more by 2028, mostly integrated) → first-fill and project refractory orders (RHIM's coke-oven and SAIL Bhilai SMS-4 pipeline). [OECD Steel Outlook 2026, via search summary] [RHIM Q1 call]
- **Global EAF shift** — OECD Steel Outlook 2026: of **138.8 Mt** of capacity under way or planned for 2026–28, **51% is EAF** (~71 Mt). HEG counts ~20 Mt of greenfield EAF commissioned in 12–18 months and ~60 Mt more by 2028; management estimates ~200,000 t of extra electrode demand by 2030 ex-China (company estimate). Each tonne of EAF steel uses 1.5–2 kg of electrode (Ind-Ra). [OECD 2026] [HEG Q4 call, official] [Ind-Ra May-26]
- **EU CBAM** (carbon border tax, live from 2026) and the EU tariff-rate quota regime push European steel toward domestic EAF output. [HEG Q4 call]
- **Cement**: capex of ₹1.2–1.25 lakh cr for 130–170 MTPA additions over FY25–FY27; top four players ~54% of capacity — consolidating buyers with strong bargaining power. [RHIM AR FY26 MD&A]

## 3. Policy
- **India steel safeguard duty**: definitive duty on flat products for three years (21 Apr 2025–20 Apr 2028), 12% in year one (Notification 02/2025-Customs (SG), 30 Dec 2025); sources differ on the year-two rate (5% per the notification summary on TaxGuru; others report ~11–11.5%). Imports rose 29.5% in Apr–Aug 2026, consistent with a lower year-two rate. ICRA expected imports to "remain muted" in FY27. [TaxGuru, Dec 2025] [ICRA Steel Outlook, May 2026] [JPC]
- **US trade case on large-diameter graphite electrodes** (petitioners: Resonac Graphite America, Tokai Carbon GE): preliminary countervailing duties (30 Jul 2026) — **HEG 6.99%, Graphite India 3.68%, others 5.87%**; China 103.49%. Anti-dumping preliminary for India was due by 22 Sep 2026 (petition alleged 42.59–73.40% margins); finals due by ~7–8 Dec 2026. The US was 17% of HEG's FY26 revenue. [US Federal Register, 13 & 30 Jul 2026] [HEG AR FY26]
- **Brazil** opened an anti-dumping case on electrodes ≥350 mm from China and India (12 Mar 2026; petitioner GrafTech Brasil); estimated margins China 54.9%, **India 57.3%**. [Baker McKenzie, Mar 2026]
- **Japan** imposed anti-dumping duty on Chinese electrodes in 2025, which lifted HEG's sales to Japan (₹67 cr in FY26 vs ₹30 cr). The Eurasian Economic Union duty on Indian electrodes (HEG 16.04%, others 32.83%) runs to Mar 2029. [Ind-Ra May-26] [HEG AR FY26] [EEC, Apr 2024]
- **Press Note 3** (Chinese investment approvals) blocked IFGL's China JV at its chosen site. [ICRA IFGL Aug-26]
- Refractories have no sector-specific support scheme (RHIM AR lists this as an industry weakness). [RHIM AR FY26]

## 4. Cost drivers
- **Needle coke** (the key electrode raw material; only 3–4 non-Chinese suppliers, no greenfield plant in decades): up **$200–300/t** since the West Asia war; total electrode cost up 10–15%; lands in P&Ls around Nov 2026–Jan 2027. GrafTech also flagged tighter merchant needle-coke pricing for H2 2026. Needle coke is made from decant oil, so it tracks crude. [HEG Q1 & Q4 calls, official] [Graphite India IP Q1FY27] [GrafTech 10-Q/8-K Q2 2026 via search]
- **Refractory raw materials**: magnesite up 6–8% in two months (to Aug 2026); fused magnesia/dead-burnt magnesia costs rose with Chinese energy costs and freight; alumina stable. Much of India's refractory raw material is imported from China (ICRA on IFGL; RHIM AR). [RHIM Q1 call] [ICRA IFGL Aug-26] [RHIM AR FY26]
- **Energy and freight**: LPG shortages hit IFGL in Q1 FY27; West Asia freight "from $20–30 to more than $300" per tonne on some electrode routes. [IFGL Q1 call] [HEG Q4 call]
- **Rupee**: weaker rupee helps exporters (HEG, Graphite India) but raises import costs for refractory makers (a reason for RHIM's goodwill write-down). [HEG Q4 call] [RHIM AR FY26]

## 5. Competitive structure and share
- **Electrodes (ex-China ~600–650 kt market)**: a handful of players — GrafTech (US/Europe/Mexico), Resonac, Tokai, HEG, Graphite India, plus Chinese exporters (~400 kt exported, ~100 kt of it UHP per HEG). HEG's 100 kt Mandideep plant is the largest single site; next two are ~70 kt. Utilisation: **HEG >90%, Graphite India 97% (Q1 FY27), GrafTech 74% (Q2 2026)**. Only HEG has announced new Western-market capacity (+15 kt by early 2028); Graphite India's first expansion phase is due in FY27. [HEG Q1/Q4 calls] [Graphite India IP Q1FY27] [GrafTech Q2 2026 release]
- **Refractories (India)**: RHIM is the leader (~30% share, 512 kt capacity, 850+ customers); others include Calderys, Vesuvius India, IFGL, Dalmia-OCL legacy players (now inside RHIM), and many small makers. Industry utilisation is "often 60–70%" (RHIM AR); RHIM says Calderys, IFGL and smaller players "fill up their plant at any cost" in cement. [RHIM AR FY26] [RHIM Q4 call, official]
- **Consolidation**: RHIM bought Dalmia Bharat's refractory business (2023) and Ashwath Technologies (via Intermetal, Aug 2025); IFGL bought Sheffield Refractories (2023). Dalmia Bharat Refractories sold its entire 13.08% RHIM stake in May–Sep 2026 (11.11% block to SBI MF and others at ₹360 on 23 Sep). Meanwhile new capacity keeps arriving at the low end (e.g., Monolith expanding ramming-mass capacity from 210,000 to 575,000 t/yr by early FY27, per Choice Broking). Net: consolidation at the top, overcapacity at the bottom. [RHIM AR FY26] [BSE SAST, 24 Sep 2026] [News: Upstox 23 Sep 2026] [News: ANI/Choice, Jun 2026]

## 7. Electrode price cycle — where are we?
- Prices bottomed in 2025: GrafTech's Q2 2026 realised price ~$3,900/t was still **7% below** a year earlier. Graphite India's inventory write-down for falling electrode prices shrank from ₹77 cr (Jun 2025) to ₹24 cr (Jun 2026) — the fall has stopped. [GrafTech Q2 2026] [Graphite India IP Q1FY27]
- Turn signals: GrafTech announced **+$600–1,200/t** (Mar 2026) and has booked new orders >15% above Q1 prices; a Japanese peer announced +$930/t. Indian makers are booked to Sept–Oct at old prices, so any increase shows from Q3–Q4 FY27. Utilisation across the industry is rising (GrafTech 65% → 74%; Graphite India 82% → 97%). [GrafTech Q2 2026] [HEG Q1 call]
- Against: needle coke +$200–300/t, US/Brazil trade cases against Indian exports, Middle East demand down (Middle East crude steel −22% YoY in Q2 CY26), global steel demand growth only 0.3% in 2026. [Graphite India IP Q1FY27] [worldsteel SRO Apr 2026]
- My read: **early upturn** — volumes and utilisation have turned; prices are trying to follow, but spreads (price minus needle coke) widen only if the price hikes stick in the Oct–Dec bookings. Nothing like the 2018 spike (driven by Chinese closures and needle-coke scarcity) is in sight.

## 8. Refractory cycle — where are we?
- Volume steady to good (India steel/cement growth, project pipeline), but **price discipline weak**: overcapacity, surcharge-based pricing, long-term contracts without escalation clauses (RHIM AR). Margins are recovering from FY26 lows (RHIM 11.9% → 14.5% in Q1; IFGL still 7–8%), helped by temporary surcharges. [RHIM AR FY26] [RHIM IP Q1FY27] [IFGL IP Q1FY27]
- Structural positives: 4PRO/TRM (full refractory management) contracts raise switching costs; captive raw materials (RHIM's quartzite mines, MINPRO recycling JV). Structural negatives: import dependence for magnesia/alumina; goodwill write-downs (RHIM ₹556 cr in FY26) show acquired capacity earning less than paid for.
- My read: **mid-cycle, low-margin** — volume grows with steel, but returns (RHIM ROCE ~6%, IFGL ~5%) stay poor until overcapacity clears.

## 9. Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecaster named)
- **Indian steel demand**: worldsteel SRO (Apr 2026) — **+7.4% in 2026, +9.2% in 2027**; ICRA (May 2026) — **+9–10% in FY2027** after 7.6% in FY2026, industry OPM ~flat (−25 bps) in FY27; OECD — +6.7% in 2026. [worldsteel] [ICRA] [OECD 2026]
- **Indian steel output**: Choice Broking — 168.4 Mt (FY26) → **255 Mt (FY31)**, ~8.7% a year (derived); BigMint — capacity ~298 Mt by FY30 with EAF share steady at ~19–20%. [Choice via ANI] [BigMint]
- **Global steel demand**: worldsteel — +0.3% (2026) to 1.72 bn t, **+2.2% (2027)** to 1.76 bn t. OECD warns global excess capacity rises to **745 Mt by 2028** (from ~640 Mt in 2025). [worldsteel] [OECD via Eurometal]
- **Electrode demand**: OECD pipeline ~71 Mt of new EAF capacity 2026–28 → at 1.5–2 kg/t and ~80% use, roughly 85,000–115,000 t/yr of extra electrode demand by 2028–29 (derived from OECD + Ind-Ra figures), against ~600–650 kt ex-China demand — meaningful (+15%) but spread over three years. Ind-Ra expects HEG's operating performance to improve in FY27 with price revisions; free cash flow negative in FY27. [Ind-Ra May-26]
- **Refractory demand**: tracks steel + cement volume (~7–9% a year per the above); IMARC's ~3%/yr dollar market growth to 2034 implies continued price pressure. [RHIM AR FY26 citing IMARC]
- **Key dates**: US final AD/CVD on Indian electrodes by ~7 Dec 2026; HEG Graphite listing H2 Oct 2026; Brazil AD preliminary (date not found); India safeguard year three (11%) from 21 Apr 2027.

## 10. Three findings that matter for the note
1. **India is not an EAF story; it is a blast-furnace and induction-furnace story.** EAF is only ~17% of Indian output and EAF capacity grows to just ~55 Mt by FY30, while BOF capacity jumps to ~142 Mt. That favours refractory makers (RHIM: blast-furnace, coke-oven, DRI, pellet projects; ramming-mass makers for induction furnaces) far more than electrode makers, whose growth depends on exports and the global EAF build-out.
2. **The electrode cycle has turned on volume, not yet on spread.** Utilisation is 90%+ at the Indian makers and prices have stopped falling, but needle coke is up $200–300/t and US (HEG 6.99% preliminary CVD, AD pending) and Brazil (57.3% alleged margin for India) cases target Indian exports. FY27 H2 margins hinge on October–December price bookings.
3. **Refractories are consolidating at the top but remain over-supplied.** RHIM absorbed Dalmia's business, Dalmia has now fully exited RHIM's shares, and IFGL's two greenfield projects are on hold — yet utilisation is "often 60–70%" and new low-end capacity keeps coming. Margins will depend on surcharges sticking and on captive raw materials, not on volume.
