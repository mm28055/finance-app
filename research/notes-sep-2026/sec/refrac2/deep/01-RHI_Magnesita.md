# RHI Magnesita India (RHIM) — deep research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Figures in ₹ crore unless stated. Historical numbers are Screener consolidated (raw page, read 27 Sep 2026) unless tagged otherwise. "Adjusted EBITDA" is the company's own measure (includes other income, excludes exceptional items), so it runs a little above Screener's "operating profit".

## Business and segments
- India's largest refractory maker (heat-resistant bricks, mixes and flow-control parts that line steel ladles, furnaces and cement kilns). 56.07% owned by RHI Magnesita N.V. (Austria/Netherlands group); serves 850+ customers; 8 plants, 2 mines, installed capacity 512 kt a year. [AR FY26 p.74] [Screener shareholding Jun-26]
- One reported segment (refractories). Steel is the bulk of sales; the "Industrial" division (cement, glass, non-ferrous, petrochemicals) was about 13% of FY26 revenue [AR FY26 p.73]; cement alone fell from ~13% of revenue in FY25 to ~11% in FY26 as RHIM walked away from low-price orders [Q4 call].
- Flow-control products (slide gates, nozzles, isostatic "ISO" parts) are the high-margin core. On the Q1 call the CFO said "35%" of revenue — the official transcript attributes it to "core business" in answer to a flow-control question, so treat the 35% flow-control share as approximate. [Q1 call, official]
- The ex-Dalmia business (bought in 2023, now the subsidiary RHI Magnesita India Refractories Ltd, "RHIMIRL") grew revenue 14% to ₹1,153 cr in FY26 (FY25: ₹1,013 cr) at an EBITDA margin of 10.8% (FY25: 11.5%); management says it was ~4% at acquisition. In Q1 FY27 "Dalmia is mostly flat and the growth has come from RHIM India" (flow-control plants at Bhiwadi and Jamshedpur). [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Volumes: FY26 shipments 523 kt (+5%); quarterly shipments 129 kt (Q1 FY26) → 117 kt (Q4 FY26) → 122 kt (Q1 FY27). FY26 shipments (523 kt) exceed the 512 kt own capacity because shipments include traded/group-sourced goods; the company does not publish plant utilisation. [Q4 call, official] [IP Q1FY27 p.11]
- Exports are small and shrinking ("reduced from last quarter to this quarter"); strategy is "local for local". [Q1 call]

## Key numbers
- Quarterly revenue: Q1 FY26 960 → Q2 1,035 → Q3 1,092 → Q4 932 → Q1 FY27 1,014 (+6% YoY, +9% QoQ). Screener operating profit: 102 → 109 → 143 → 87 → 138; OPM 11% → 11% → 13% → 9% → 14%. [Screener]
- Company adjusted EBITDA Q1 FY27 ₹147 cr, margin 14.5% (Q4 FY26 ₹113 cr / 12.1%; Q1 FY26 ₹103 cr / 10.8%). [IP Q1FY27 p.9–11] [Q1 call, official]
- Reported net profit: Q1 FY26 35, Q2 38, Q3 62, Q4 −518 (goodwill impairment), Q1 FY27 65. [Screener]
- Annual: revenue FY24 3,781 → FY25 3,674 → FY26 4,020; operating profit 555 → 482 → 443; OPM 15% → 13% → 11%; reported net profit −100 → 203 → −383. Company adjusted EBITDA FY26 ₹477 cr (11.9%) vs ₹505 cr (13.7%) in FY25; adjusted PAT FY26 ₹180 cr. [Screener] [AR FY26 p.76] [Q4 call]
- Unit economics: average realisation ₹83,175/t in Q1 FY27 vs ₹79,948/t in Q4 FY26 and ₹74,317/t in Q1 FY26 (+12% YoY) while shipments fell 6% YoY — i.e. Q1 growth was price/mix ("war surcharge"), not volume. [IP Q1FY27 p.12] [Q1 call]
- Returns are poor because of acquisition goodwill and intangibles: ROCE 9% (FY24) → 7% → 6% (FY26); 5-year profit CAGR −5%. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- Margin guidance has been cut step by step: an analyst on the Q4 call noted it was once 15–16%, then 14%, now 13% for FY27. Management: without its initiatives "it might have gone to 11%/10% also". [Q4 call, official]
- Q4 FY26 call (30 May 2026) promises: FY27 adjusted EBITDA margin 13%; Q1 "should be a strong quarter"; volume = market growth + 1–2% ("if the market is growing by 6%–7%, we will be growing at 9%"); price increases of 1–3% (some double-digit); FY27 capex ~₹150 cr (maintenance ₹40–50 cr); a 30,000+ tonne coke-oven order securing ~18 months of kiln loading; own quartzite mines transferred. [Q4 call, official]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: margin 14.5% — beat; 13% full-year guide kept, not raised. Volume 122 kt, −6% YoY — missed; management now says 7–8% for FY27, "9% is a bit of stretch". Capex only ₹8 cr in Q1; management reverted to "₹80–100 cr every year" — effectively a cut from ₹150 cr. The coke-oven order was still "at the final stage of negotiation… a bit of pricing adjustment", production to start "next month" (September) — so the Q4 claim of a secured order was premature. No project revenue in Q1; silica and glass project orders pushed to Q3–Q4. [Q1 call, official]
- Verdict: margin **delivered/beaten**, volume **missed**, capex **cut**, order book **delayed**.

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ~₹135 cr: robotics at customer sites, a new hydraulic press, a new tunnel kiln, X-ray/CT inspection, shooter system. [AR FY26 p.17]
- FY27: guided ₹150 cr on the Q4 call, then "₹80–100 cr every year" on the Q1 call; Q1 spend ₹8 cr. Covers Dalmia plant modernisation, 4PRO robots/machinery and maintenance. Funded from own cash. Open capital commitments fell to ₹23 cr at Mar-26 from ₹77 cr a year earlier — consistent with a slower spend. [Q4 call] [Q1 call] [AR FY26 Note 34a]
- MINPRO JV (51% RHIM, 49% Khemka Refractories): greenfield mineral processing / refractory recycling plant at Dhenkanal, Odisha; ~₹35 cr initial investment over two years shared 51:49; target EBITDA margin 8–10%, payback under three years after start; start "towards Q4 of this financial year". Small (under 1% of revenue) but aimed at raw-material cost and supply security. [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27 p.25]
- Quartzite mines (Chiraipani and Bhikampali, Odisha, acquired with Dalmia): all licences received; opening expected by end-Q2 FY27, benefit "from next quarter". Management said market-bought material cost "almost double" its own mining cost, but refused to quantify savings ("work in progress"). [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Product transfers from the parent: "4 or 5 more products" localised within a year (magnesia-chrome, magnesia-spinel, thin-slab ISO, high-chrome bricks for petrochemicals). [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27 p.23]
- Growth pipeline: coke-oven project (14–16 months of loading once signed), 4–5 more coke-oven batteries in 2–3 years (management expects to win "at least 2"), SAIL Bhilai SMS-4 (~6–6.5 Mtpa) first-fill orders "6 or 8 months" away; management estimates steel-sector capex of ₹50,000–60,000 cr in FY27. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Asset turn: FY26 revenue ₹4,020 cr on fixed assets of ₹2,128 cr (incl. intangibles) — about 1.9x; new capex is small relative to the base, so growth depends on utilisation and price, not new plants.

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Cash and equivalents ₹452 cr at Jun-26; **net cash ~₹140 cr** (net cash/trailing EBITDA 0.27x), up from ₹69 cr at Mar-26. Screener borrowings ₹448 cr at Mar-26 (FY25: 380). [IP Q1FY27 p.14] [Q1 call] [Screener]
- Cash from operations FY24 271 → FY25 373 → FY26 409; free cash flow 191 → 255 → 274; CFO/operating profit 107% in FY26 — the best feature of the company. Q1 FY27 operating cash flow ₹81 cr (−7% QoQ) as inventory was built. [Screener] [IP Q1FY27 p.9]
- Working capital: inventory days 163, debtor days 64, payable days 101, cash conversion cycle 126 days (FY26; FY25: 148). Working-capital intensity 36% of sales at Jun-26. [Screener] [IP Q1FY27]
- Dividend yield 0.65% at ₹386; FY25 payout 25% of profit. [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Scale: largest Indian refractory maker, ~30% India share (company/Screener summary; the 32% steel / 40% cement figures in the stored profile could not be verified). [Screener about] [AR FY26 p.74]
- Parent technology: access to RHI Magnesita group R&D, recipes and the parent's magnesite mines; group also sells RHIM ~₹390–400 cr a year of materials. [AR FY26 Note 36]
- Switching costs are building through "4PRO" (full refractory management plus robots, scanning and digital monitoring): India's first robotic caster solution running two robots at JSW under a 5-year all-inclusive contract; technical evaluations with 4–5 more steelmakers; Lining Evaluation Scan service at 24 sites. [IP Q1FY27 p.20–22] [Q1 call]
- Pricing power is partial: it pushed "war surcharges" and got some double-digit increases after the West Asia cost spike, but the AR itself says many long-term contracts "lack adequate price escalation provisions". [Q4 call] [AR FY26 p.75]
- Flow-control share gain: share with "one of the big group" nearly doubled in six months. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Repeated write-downs**: ₹556 cr impairment of the Dalmia investment in Q4 FY26 (FY26 loss after tax ₹383 cr). Screener also shows large exceptional charges inside "other income" of −₹646 cr (FY23) and −₹322 cr (Q4 FY24); their nature was not re-checked in older annual reports. ₹324 cr of goodwill remains on the consolidated books and is a key audit matter. The reasons given — falling exports, rupee weakness, "new capacities by competitors" and import competition — are structural, not one-off. [AR FY26 p.17, KAM] [Screener]
- **Auditor resignation mid-year**: PwC resigned as statutory auditor of RHIM and of subsidiary RHIMIRL (effective 14 Aug 2026) to align with a group-wide auditor change; BSR & Co. appointed to the casual vacancy. The board states no other concerns were raised. Low governance risk, but worth watching the first BSR-audited results. [AR FY26 Board's report] [Q1 call]
- **Top-management change**: Parmod Sagar (MD & CEO since 2013) resigned as MD "due to personal reasons" effective 1 Jul 2026 and is now non-executive chairman; Pankaj Malhan is new MD & CEO; CFO Azim Syed stepped down as whole-time director but stays CFO. [AR FY26 Board's report]
- **Big shareholder exit**: Dalmia Bharat Refractories sold its entire 13.08% stake (2.70 cr shares) between May and September 2026, including a 2.29 cr share (11.11%) block on 23 Sep at ₹360; SBI MF bought 8.07%. The overhang is now cleared. [BSE SAST 24 Sep 2026] [News: Upstox, 23 Sep 2026]
- **Related parties**: royalty to the parent ₹35 cr in FY26 (FY25: ₹32 cr), material purchases from group firms ~₹390–400 cr a year. Ordinary course and approved, but a structural drain on margin. [AR FY26 Note 36]
- **Overcapacity**: the AR says industry utilisation is "often 60–70%"; competitors are adding greenfield/brownfield capacity — in cement, "Calderys, IFGL, smaller players… try to fill up their plant at any cost". [AR FY26 p.76] [Q4 call, official] [Q1 call]
- **Raw materials**: magnesite up 6–8% in two months to August; fused magnesia/DBM costs rose with Chinese energy and freight. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Contingent liabilities are small: ₹29 cr at Mar-26 (₹36 cr a year earlier), mostly customs and tax. [AR FY26 Note 33]
- No rating rationale for RHIM found in 2025–26 (Screener lists only a 2015 CRISIL note).

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from defence (Q4: freight, cement price war, impairment) to confidence (Q1: 14.5% margin, new CEO, MINPRO, mines).
- Numbers: margin up 240 bps QoQ; volume guide trimmed from ~9% to 7–8%; capex guide trimmed from ₹150 cr to ₹80–100 cr; coke-oven order moved from "secured" to "final negotiation".
- New issues: auditor resignation, MD change, magnesite inflation, and (after the call) Dalmia's full stake sale.

## My read
The underlying business is steady and very cash-generative (₹409 cr operating cash flow in FY26, net cash), but it is not yet a growth story: FY26 volume grew 5% and Q1 FY27 volume fell 6% YoY, and the Q1 margin beat came from surcharges that management itself frames as temporary. Repeated write-downs (₹556 cr in FY26 alone) show that the Dalmia purchase has not earned its price, and ROCE of ~6% says the same. For the share to work, FY27 must show the promised 13% margin *with* 7–8% volume, the coke-oven order must actually start (Sept 2026), and mines/MINPRO must lift gross margin in H2. A second quarter of falling volume, or margin slipping back under 12% once surcharges fade, would argue for a lower growth estimate; a clean H2 with project revenue and a lower cost base would support mid-teens earnings growth.
