## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# HEG

## Executive Summary
HEG is in the final stages of a definitive corporate bifurcation, transitioning from a conglomerate structure into two pure-play entities: a dominant, cash-generative **Graphite Electrode** franchise and a high-growth **Greentech** platform. Historically a cyclical commodity player powering Electric Arc Furnaces (EAF) for steel production, HEG is pivoting to solve critical energy transition bottlenecks—specifically the non-Chinese supply of battery anode materials and energy storage solutions. As of Q1 FY27, the composite demerger scheme has completed its NCLT hearings and is merely awaiting the final pronouncement of the order. The company remains a **Category Leader** in graphite electrodes (world’s largest single-site plant) and an aggressive **Challenger** in the battery supply chain, attempting to capitalize on "China+1" mandates. The investment narrative hinges entirely on the successful execution of this demerger, designed to unlock value by separating a highly resilient graphite business—which recently delivered massive earnings beats despite global macro shocks—from the capital-hungry, higher-multiple renewable ventures.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
HEG’s revenue model is bifurcating into two distinct streams under the proposed demerger:

1.  **Legacy Graphite (New "HEG Limited"):**
    *   **What they sell:** Ultra-High Power (UHP) graphite electrodes used in EAF steelmaking.
    *   **Who pays:** Global steel manufacturers shifting toward decarbonized steel production (EAF vs. Blast Furnace).
    *   **Monetization:** Driven by cyclical pricing power, raw material spreads (needle coke), and capacity utilization (currently >90%).

2.  **Greentech Platform (Renamed "HEG Greentech"):**
    *   **Anode Materials (TACC):** Selling synthetic graphite anode active materials to Lithium-ion cell OEMs. Monetization is volume-based with premiums for non-Chinese origin compliance (US FEOC regulations). Management expects steady ~35% EBITDA margins.
    *   **Hydroelectric Power:** Selling baseload and peaking power from run-of-the-river assets (Malana, AD Hydro). High EBITDA margin (80%) annuity cash flows.
    *   **Energy Storage (RE+):** Selling Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) and EV battery packs to utilities and C&I consumers.
    *   **IPP Model:** An asset recycling strategy—build renewable projects, stabilize operations, and flip to InVITs/Yieldcos for 10-11% yields, targeting 16-20% equity IRR.

The growth narrative has shifted entirely to Greentech. The Hydro segment acts as the internal funding engine, generating steady cash flows to service initial battery material capex.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)

*   **Graphite Electrodes (Widening Moat):** HEG operates the world’s largest single-site plant, offering significant operating leverage. The moat is protected by high barriers to entry and a structural lowest-cost producer advantage. This was conclusively proven in Q1 FY27, where HEG sustained >90% utilization and expanded standalone EBITDA margins to 29%, while Western peers struggled at 60-65% utilization.
*   **Anode Materials (Developing Moat):** The company is leveraging its 50-year expertise in graphitization to enter the battery market. The primary defensive moat here is regulatory: India’s PLI scheme and US regulations restricting Chinese battery materials create a protected market for non-Chinese suppliers.
*   **The Bear Case:**
    *   **Tech Obsolescence:** The Greentech moat is fragile. A shift in battery chemistry (e.g., Sodium-ion) could render the heavy capex in synthetic graphite anodes less relevant, though management argues 8-10 year OEM commitments mitigate this.
    *   **Chinese Dumping:** While tariffs exist, China controls 97% of the synthetic graphite market. Aggressive pricing from Chinese incumbents could crush HEG's projected margins before the Indore plant reaches scale.

## Growth Vectors

*   **The Next $100M (Anode & Storage):** The immediate growth vector is the TACC facility (Anode materials), commencing commercial production in Q1 FY28. Management has exceptional visibility, expecting to lock 70% of the initial 20,000-ton capacity into 3-5 year contracts with Tier-1 global OEMs imminently. Revenue is guided at ₹600-700 crores in Year 1 (40-50% utilization), scaling to ₹1,500-1,600 crores by Year 3 at 35% margins. A Phase 2 expansion (+10,000 tons for an ₹800 crore capex) is already being evaluated.
*   **Asset Consolidation & Expansion:** HEG consolidated 100% ownership in AD Hydro and is aggressively expanding its renewable footprint, targeting a 75 MW Hydro project addition and a 300 MW DC Solar project by 2030. The combined Greentech entity is targeting a "four-digit" (₹1,000+ crore) EBITDA by the end of the decade.
*   **Core Capacity Upgrades:** In the legacy business, HEG is tracking to expand capacity from 100,000 to 115,000 tons by early 2028 to capture the 71 million tons of global EAF capacity coming online. 
*   **Management Execution:** The tone is triumphant and highly confident. Having shattered prior conservative margin guidance, management is deploying capital efficiently. The ₹2,200 crore TACC Phase 1 capex is 40% spent, with 90-95% expected to be deployed by the end of FY27.

## Key Risks & Governance

*   **Governance & Structure:** The NCLT demerger hearings are complete. The swap ratio (8 shares of Greentech for 7 of Bhilwara) and the subsequent promoter holding increase to ~62% warrant scrutiny to ensure minority shareholder value wasn't diluted to favor the promoter's private energy holdings.
*   **Capital Allocation Risks:** The projected Greentech capex is massive. While the graphite business is ring-fenced, the Greentech entity is taking on significant execution risk, attempting to rapidly scale synthetic graphite production with a 4-5 year payback period timeline.
*   **Regulatory & Trade Risks:** The looming threat of US Anti-Dumping (AD) and Countervailing Duties (CVD) could force HEG to reroute volumes to lower-margin geographies, though management downplays current US exposure at <10% of sales.
*   **Forensic Watch:** The debt split is now explicit. Post-demerger, the Greentech entity will carry approximately ₹1,500 crores of gross project debt (mostly via SBI). The legacy Graphite entity remains completely debt-free with a robust treasury of ₹858 crores. Additionally, monitor the volatility of "Other Income," as past painful GrafTech Mark-to-Market write-downs have reversed into fair valuation gains, artificially padding earnings.

## Investment Thesis

**The Bull Case:**
HEG represents a unique **Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) arbitrage**. The market currently values HEG largely for its cyclical Graphite business, which is an absolute cash cow proving its ability to effortlessly absorb macro shocks and sustain 27-29% margins. The imminent demerger forces a re-rating of the Greentech business. If TACC achieves its projected 35% margins and ₹1,500+ crore Year 3 revenue, HEG Greentech becomes one of the few ex-China, highly derisked plays on the EV supply chain, commanding a significantly higher multiple than a steel-ancillary company.

**The Bear Case:**
The "Conglomerate Discount" may persist as a "Holding Company Discount." The Greentech business is entering a hyper-competitive arena against entrenched Chinese giants and faces structural technology risks. In the legacy business, needle coke inflation ($200-$300/ton) may outpace HEG's ability to push price hikes if global macro softness curtails steel demand, and aggressive US AD/CVD duties could further impair margins. If the demerger fails to create liquidity, investors may be left holding a cyclical asset facing secular trade headwinds alongside a debt-burdened startup.
