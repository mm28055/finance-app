# Caplin Point Laboratories (NSE: CAPLIPOINT) — deep research, Sep 2026

## Business and segments
- Two businesses. (1) **Emerging markets** ("Rest of the World" segment): branded and plain generics (tablets, ointments, injectables) sold mainly in Central and Latin America — Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Ecuador, Colombia, and newer Chile and Mexico — through its own subsidiaries, warehouses and 700+ local staff; ~78% of Q1 FY27 revenue. (2) **US/regulated markets** via Caplin Steriles Ltd (CSL, Gummidipoondi, Chennai): sterile injectables and eye drops sold to US wholesalers, group purchasing organisations and hospital networks, partly under its own label (Caplin Steriles USA); ~22%. [IP Q1FY27 press release; AR FY26 note 43]
- Segment revenue (₹ cr): Q1 FY27 RoW 476.1, USA 134.3 (Q1 FY26: 403.5 and 106.7). Segment profit before tax: RoW 158.4 (vs 151.5; margin down from 37.5% to 33.3%), USA 33.3 (vs 9.9). FY26: RoW 1,733.8, USA 453.4. [Q1FY27 results, segment note]
- LatAm model: fast-moving products (about 20% of the range) kept in warehouses next to customers for ~6 months; 40–50% of LatAm supply is outsourced (25–30% from China). In Central America 18–20% of sales go to governments, the rest to private pharmacies (Caplin products hold ~41% of shelf space in independent pharmacies surveyed). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- US: 60 ANDAs approved (50 own, 15 bought — company figures), 5 under review, 40+ in development; 38 launched; CSL B2B/own-label split 70/30; own label (CSU) did ~₹100 cr in FY26 and targets ~₹200 cr in FY27. CSL FY26 EBITDA ₹142 cr at 30% margin (₹102 cr, 27.9% in FY25); Q4 33%. [IP Q1FY27 press release; Q4 call]
- Capacity: CSL has 6 lines plus 1 oncology line (8 with line 7 coming in 6–7 months); Phase 3 (COL-II building) adds 5 injectable/ophthalmic/BFS lines by Mar-27, with room for 3–4 more ("17 lines" in 2–3 years). Management says existing plants CP1 (Puducherry) and CSL are capacity-constrained; CSL "booked out till almost February". [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (14 May 2026): no formal guidance; CSL growth 25–30% in FY27 and own label doubled to ~₹200 cr; ~₹510 cr of the ₹1,000 cr capex plan still to spend over 18–24 months (excluding Mexico plant and Chile labs); receivables comfort range 100–120 days; long-term 25–29% PAT margin and double-digit revenue growth "confident". [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: US segment revenue +26% (CSU own label 3x to ₹43.7 cr); total revenue +20%; gross margin 59.8% vs a committed floor of 55%; PAT margin 27.8%. Cash flow from operations only ₹95 cr (₹118 cr a year earlier) due to ₹76 cr more warehouse stock, ₹15 cr more raw material and ₹46 cr more government receivables; free cash flow ₹40 cr after ₹55 cr capex. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 guidance: gross margin ~60% for the year; CFO to normalise from Q2 (government receivables collected by Q3); 13 injectable lines visible, last 3–4 "2029 and beyond"; Mexico distribution acquisition — 3–4 targets under review; RoW profitability "might even improve". [Q1 call]
- Kept: 55% gross-margin / 25% PAT floors; ₹1,000 cr+ capex from internal cash. Nothing cut. [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Capex plan ₹1,000 cr+ ("around 50% nearing completion", balance over 2–3 years), fully from internal cash: COL-II injectable/ophthalmic/BFS block (Mar-27), oncology OSD/injectable plant at Kakkalur (inspected, 6 products' submission batches done, 18 more in 12 months), oncology API at Thervoy (Q3–Q4 FY27), Vizag general API (6 APIs scaled up; first DMFs 2026–27), new OSD/derma plant in Puducherry (2x capacity), Amaris Clinical BE centre to 120 beds (end FY27), land bought in Mexico for a plant (16% tender price preference for local makers). [IP Q1FY27 project table; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- FY26 capex ₹248 cr; ₹154 cr capitalised and ₹143–144 cr in CWIP (oncology plant, capitalised April 2026) plus ₹109 cr equipment advances. Fixed-asset turnover 3.91x. [Q4 call]
- Payback: a product moved to a new line needs ~9–10 months (post-approval supplement) before US sales. Line additions are therefore revenue-lagged into FY28. [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Essentially debt-free (Screener borrowings ₹221 cr are mostly lease/other; management: "not taken a single dollar ... bank loan"). Free cash reserves ₹1,502 cr at Jun-26 (₹1,471 cr at Mar-26); "liquid assets" (cash + receivables + inventory) ₹2,875 cr. Net worth ₹3,776 cr. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- CFO: FY24 ₹318 cr, FY25 ₹432 cr, FY26 ₹523 cr (80% of PAT); FCF ₹173 cr / ₹241 cr / ₹275 cr. [Screener; Q4 call]
- Working capital is heavy and rising: Screener debtor days 117 (FY24) → 119 → 138 (FY26); inventory days 183 → 159 → 181; working-capital days 166 → 174 → 199. Company: receivables 128 days at Jun-26; inventory ₹505 cr (47% in overseas warehouses, 23% in transit, 30% in India). Management attributes ~11 days of FY26 receivables to currency translation. [Screener; IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- FY26 net worth rise included a ₹141 cr foreign-currency translation gain (dollar assets). [Q4 call]
- Dividend: final ₹4/share recommended (payout ~9%). Board discussing moving part of the ~₹1,000 cr treasury into credit/index funds. [Board outcome 12 Aug 2026; Q1 call]
- Credit rating: none located (no bank debt).

## Moat evidence
- Pricing and margin: gross margin ~60% and PBT margin ~37% (FY26) in plain generics — far above peers — from owning distribution in small, fragmented LatAm markets where big companies sell only through importers. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Share: ~41% shelf share in independent Central American pharmacies (company survey); 29 Mexico approvals (120+ filings planned in 18 months); Chile $12 mn of tenders to be supplied over 18 months; $7 mn emergency tenders in Central America. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- US: company says revenue per ANDA is more than 50% above the industry average and only one approved product has been withdrawn for lack of sales. [Q4 official transcript]
- Switching costs: 6-month local stock and local collection teams make Caplin the reliable supplier; weekly orders from the four largest US wholesalers. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Audit coverage:** the group auditor (Brahmayya & Co, Chennai) did not audit five subsidiaries and eight step-down subsidiaries holding ₹3,463 cr of assets, ₹2,038 cr of revenue and ₹303 cr of profit — i.e., over 90% of group revenue is audited by other (mostly overseas) auditors. One step-down subsidiary's accounts were unaudited. Audit-trail (edit log) was not enabled at database level for payroll and ledger software. Opinion otherwise unmodified; key audit matter includes inventory valuation. [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report]
- **Working capital:** debtor days up ~20 in a year; government customers (El Salvador) pay late; inventory deliberately increased; Q1 CFO fell y/y. [Screener; Q1 call]
- **Emerging-market concentration:** RoW segment profit margin slipped from 37.5% to 33.3% in a year; management explains higher marketing cost. Political/currency risk in Central America (most currencies stable or dollarised per management). [Q1FY27 segment data; Q4 call]
- **Execution:** 7+ new plants at once (injectables, oncology, API, OSD, Mexico), CSL old lines mixing terminal/aseptic processes cause "breakdowns" (management admission). [Q1 call]
- **Idle cash:** ₹1,500 cr cash earns treasury rates and dilutes ROCE; acquisitions talked about for several quarters but none made. [Q1 call]
- **Related parties (small):** rent/consultancy ~₹3.7 cr to promoter-linked Ashvich companies; solar power ₹2.2 cr from associate Sunsole; Guatemala subsidiary 69% owned. Contingent liabilities tiny (₹0.6 cr tax; ₹10 cr LCs, ₹6.7 cr guarantees). [AR FY26 notes 37, 43]
- **Tariffs:** chairman's answer on US generic tariffs was "cross the bridge when we reach it"; no US plant planned. [Q1 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- Q4: celebration of a 5-year doubling; CSL guided 25–30%, own label to ₹200 cr; ₹510 cr capex to go.
- Q1: growth accelerated to 20%, US segment +26% and own label 3x; but cash flow weakened on deliberate inventory and government receivables; new emphasis on digitisation (paperless CSL in 6 months, "video masters", AI cameras), Mexico distribution M&A and treasury-yield changes. MD Dr Sridhar Ganesan re-appointed for two years.

## My read
Caplin is a very profitable, cash-rich compounder whose next leg depends on US injectables and larger LatAm markets (Mexico, Chile). Growth has picked up to ~20% and the US arm is now profitable, but emerging-market margins are edging down, receivables and inventory keep rising, and most of the group is audited by other auditors. At a low-30s P/E the stock needs mid-to-high-teens earnings growth, which looks achievable if the new injectable lines ramp on time in FY28. A Mexico acquisition done at a sensible price, or CSL margins above 33%, would improve the view; a further rise in debtor days or delays at COL-II would weaken it.
