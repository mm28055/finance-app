# Eris Lifesciences — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code ERIS (consolidated). Price ₹1,259, market cap ₹17,466 cr, 52-week range ₹1,200–1,740 (28% below the high); promoter holding 54.14% (Jun 2026) [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].

## Business and segments
- Eris sells branded prescription medicines in India, mostly for long-term (chronic) conditions — diabetes tablets, insulin, heart, skin, women's health — to specialist doctors through about 4,000 medical representatives. It also owns Swiss Parenterals, an injectables maker that exports to Africa, Asia and Latin America and is building a European contract-manufacturing (CDMO) book [Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A].
- FY26 split: Domestic Branded Formulations (DBF) revenue ₹2,778 cr (+11%), DBF EBITDA ₹1,026 cr (37% margin); International ₹348 cr (+7%, vs ₹375 cr target), EBITDA ₹110 cr (32%) [Q4 call; IP Q4FY26 p.3, p.11].
- DBF mix Q1 FY27 (IQVIA): chronic 72.7% of the portfolio, chronic + sub-chronic 85%. OAD (diabetes tablets) ~20% and cardiac ~15% are the two largest therapies; both lagged — OAD grew 1.9% vs market 11.5%, cardiac 11.1% vs 16.9%. Insulin grew 27.7% vs market 4.5%; derma, VMN (vitamins), CNS and women's health grew double digits [IP Q1FY27 p.4].
- Insulin: the "represented" market (human insulin RHI + glargine) is ₹2,778 cr and shrinking 1.2% a year; Eris sales ₹412 cr (+26.4%), share 16% (9% at the Biocon deal). RHI cartridge share rose from 13% to 24% in FY26. The faster-growing analog market (₹2,309 cr, +9.4%) is not yet served; the Q1 deck says the 5-product analog pipeline launches "starting FY28" [IP Q1FY27 p.5; Q4 call].
- Semaglutide (Sundae, launched Mar 2026 after patent expiry): first full quarter generic market ₹88 cr, Sundae ₹12 cr; share ~21% of prescriptions, ~20% of units (#1) and ~14% of value (#2). Vial ₹1,290, pen ₹3,200 — about 30% below most rivals' ₹4,200 [IP Q1FY27 p.6; Q4 call].
- Swiss Parenterals Q1 FY27: revenue ₹72 cr (+5%), EBITDA ₹18 cr (−19%). Geography: ~30% Africa, ~30% Asia, ~25% LatAm; no EU sales in the base business [IP Q1FY27 p.9; Q4 call].
- Plants: six (Guwahati, three in Ahmedabad, Bhopal, Chennai). Eris BioNxt (Bhopal) is a sterile fill-finish site for vials, cartridges and pens; Phase-1 pen capacity 5 mn units a year; it received its licence in July 2026 and commercial production was to start in August 2026 [Ind-Ra May-26; Q4 call; Q1 call]. Drug substance for analogs/recombinant semaglutide comes from Levim Lifetech, a joint venture of Eris BioNxt [Q1 call; AR FY26 note 30].

## Key numbers
- Quarterly sales (₹ cr): Jun-25 773 → Sep-25 792 → Dec-25 807 → Mar-26 757 → Jun-26 873. Operating margin: 36% → 36% → 35% → 36% → 34%. Net profit: 125 → 134 → 109 → 279 → 143 [Screener].
- Q1 FY27: revenue +13%, EBITDA ₹296 cr (+7%), margin 33.9% vs 35.8%; gross margin 72.6% vs 76.1% (DBF gross margin 74% vs 77%); PAT ₹143 cr (+14.5%); tax rate 20%. Profit grew faster than EBITDA because finance cost fell to ₹47 cr [IP Q1FY27 p.11–12; Screener].
- Q4 FY26 profit ₹279 cr included a one-off ₹150 cr deferred-tax gain (Screener shows a −75% tax rate). Continuing PAT was ₹132 cr; FY26 continuing PAT ₹498 cr (+34%) vs reported ₹648 cr [Q4 call; Screener].
- Annual (Screener): sales FY24 ₹2,009 cr → FY25 ₹2,894 cr → FY26 ₹3,129 cr; operating margin 34% → 35% → 36%; net profit ₹397 cr → ₹375 cr → ₹648 cr (FY26 incl. ₹150 cr tax credit). TTM sales ₹3,230 cr, TTM profit ₹666 cr (~₹516 cr without the one-off). Interest ₹193 cr and depreciation/amortisation ₹280 cr in FY26 still eat a third of operating profit. ROCE 14% (FY26), vs 25%+ before the acquisitions [Screener].
- Unit economics: yield per MR averages ~₹6.5 lakh a quarter; the biologics team runs at ₹15–16 lakh, so insulin earns ~30–31% EBITDA despite much lower gross margin [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (20 May 2026): DBF growth 1.3× the covered market (CVM) growth; DBF EBITDA 37%, consolidated 36%, H2 better than H1; international (existing business) +18–20% with similar margins, EU-CDMO revenue (₹120–140 cr) excluded; tax ~21%; ROCE from 15% to 23–25% by FY28; debtor days to ~60 within two quarters; Aspart and Esaxerenone launches in FY27; Bhopal to lift margins in H2 [Q4 call, official].
- Q1 FY27 call (29 Jul 2026): DBF guidance kept as "300 bps ahead of market" (~14% if the market grows 11%). International cut to "flattish" (± low single digit) with margins 200–300 bps lower because of remediation work at Swiss Parenterals; clearer view after Q2. DBF gross margin to stay ~74% in Q2, improve in Q3, and "a fair chance" of returning to the old 76–77% band in Q4. OCF/EBITDA ~77% for the year [Q1 call, official].
- Delivery: DBF +14.2% met the guide; DBF EBITDA margin 35% vs the 37% annual guide (management had flagged a softer H1); consolidated margin 34% vs 36% guide. Analog timing drifted from "FY27" (Q4) to "Q3, but I am skeptical of calling a date" (Q1) and "starting FY28" in the Q1 deck. Esaxerenone launch slipped from FY26 to "first fortnight of August 2026" [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.5].
- Verdict: DBF growth guidance **kept**; international **cut** (from +18–20% to flat); margin guidance **kept for the year but back-loaded**; analog launches **delayed**.

## Capex and capacity
- Capex FY26 ₹297 cr (Q4 ₹102 cr), "largely on biologics and sterile injectables"; Q1 FY27 ₹88 cr [IP Q4FY26 p.15; Q1 call].
- Cumulative strategic spend: ~₹150–180 cr in drug-substance capability and ~₹250 cr in Bhopal; "nothing has started coming out of these yet" (May 2026). A second Swiss Parenterals plant (Unit-3) doubles parenteral capacity; commissioning targeted in FY28 [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 p.9].
- Capital commitments ₹191 cr at March 2026 (₹3.7 cr a year earlier); plus up to ₹100 cr more equity and up to ₹50 cr of debt/founder funding for Levim [AR FY26 note 31b].
- Acquisitions in FY26: remaining 30% of Swiss Parenterals for ₹423 cr paid in Eris shares (23.06 lakh new shares to Naishadh Shah); a second ₹100 cr tranche into Levim Lifetech; Velbiom probiotics brands for ₹50 cr (Mar 2026). Management says no acquisitions for now — "manufacturing is the priority" [AR FY26 MD&A p.63, note 27; Q1 call].
- Payback: management targets ROCE 23–25% by FY28 as Bhopal insources insulin (it cost ~₹50 cr of lost insulin sales in FY26 and a similar amount in FY25 because third-party supply fell short) [Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net debt ₹2,255 cr at March 2026 = 2.0× EBITDA (4× in FY24). Borrowings ₹2,359 cr [Q4 call; Screener].
- Maturities are heavy: NCDs of ₹625 cr due 7 Dec 2026 and ₹625 cr due 7 Jun 2027; Ind-Ra lists scheduled repayments of ₹920 cr in FY27 and ₹820 cr in FY28, to be repaid or refinanced. In Aug–Sep 2026 Eris drew new Axis Bank loans of ₹64.4 cr and ₹45 cr "to meet other liabilities"; the Sept loan is secured by a charge on the company's brands [Ind-Ra May-26; NSE filings 6 Aug and 9 Sep 2026].
- Cash conversion slipped: FY26 cash from operations ₹538 cr, free cash flow ₹241 cr, CFO/operating profit 59% (Screener); company figure OCF/EBITDA 48% vs 105% in FY25. Debtor days rose from 58 to 79–80; inventory days 200 (Screener). Management built ~9 months of raw/packing stock ahead of API price rises of 70–80% and could not ship ~₹30 cr of export goods in Q4 [Screener; AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call]. Q1 FY27 OCF recovered to 77% of EBITDA [IP Q1FY27 p.11].
- Dividend payout 16% in FY26; unencumbered cash only ₹56 cr at end-H1 FY26 per Ind-Ra; working-capital lines used ~58% [Screener; Ind-Ra May-26].
- Rating: India Ratings IND AA/Stable/A1+, affirmed 8 May 2026 on ₹1,198 cr bank lines and ₹1,250 cr NCDs; expects net leverage below 1.5× in FY27 "in the absence of acquisition-led debt"; downgrade trigger is net leverage above 2.0× sustained [Ind-Ra May-26].

## Moat evidence
- Specialist franchise: IPM rank 19th (22nd in 2022); 5th in anti-diabetes, 12th in cardiac, 5th in derma (AIOCD MAT Mar-26). Top 25 "mother brands" are ~65% of branded sales; 13 of them rank top-5 in their segment; 6 brands above ₹100 cr [Ind-Ra May-26; AR FY26 MD&A p.65].
- Share gains in a flat market: insulin grew 32% in FY26 vs covered market 6.7%; RHI cartridge share 13% → 24%; all of the 16% insulin share came from switching patients [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 p.5].
- Semaglutide: #1 by units and prescriptions within months, despite pens arriving later than vials — evidence that the insulin cold-chain and patient-support system transfers to GLP-1s [IP Q1FY27 p.6].
- Price control exposure 17% of revenue (NLEM), in line with the market [AR FY26 MD&A].
- Weakness: the two legacy engines (OAD 20%, cardiac 15% of sales) are losing share; OAD grows ~6% internally vs market 11–12% [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Key executive exit.** V. Krishnakumar, Executive Director and COO — who presented results on both calls — resigned on 11 Sep 2026, effective 30 Sep 2026, "to pursue professional opportunities outside" [NSE filing 11 Sep 2026].
- **Refinancing load.** ₹1,250 cr of NCDs mature within nine months (Dec 2026, Jun 2027) with low free cash; the Sept term loan is secured on the brands [Ind-Ra May-26; NSE 9 Sep 2026].
- **EU GMP failure at Swiss Parenterals.** Both injectable sites were inspected 9–13 Mar 2026 and found non-compliant (operator behaviour, area classification, material flow). Sites to be "audit-ready by December 2026"; EU-CDMO revenue (₹120–140 cr planned for FY27) is postponed and re-inspection timing is up to the agency [Q4 call; IP Q4FY26 p.12; Q1 call].
- **Disclosure sloppiness.** The 9 Sep 2026 loan filing says ₹45 cr in figures but "Sixty Four Crores forty Lakhs" in words; the next day Eris issued a rumour-clarification pointing readers back to its own filing. BSE and NSE each fined Eris ₹4.25 lakh (27 May 2026) for board-composition non-compliance in Q4 FY26 [NSE filings 9, 10 Sep and 28 May 2026].
- **Related-party / promoter dealings.** In FY25 Eris bought 19% of Swiss Parenterals "from the promoters of the Company" for ₹237.5 cr; in FY26 it bought the last 30% from the Swiss founder for ₹423 cr in new Eris shares. Eris has lent ₹47 cr to the Levim joint venture [AR FY26 note 27, note 30].
- **Auditor (Walker Chandiok).** Unmodified opinion; key audit matter is revenue recognition (discounts, returns). Database-level audit trail was not enabled for the parent and five subsidiaries. Six subsidiaries (₹545 cr revenue) were audited by other auditors [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report].
- **Contingent liabilities** are small: DPCO pricing notices ₹20.9 cr, tax/GST ₹17.0 cr, others ₹13.7 cr; unfulfilled export obligation under EPCG rose to ₹97 cr (duty at risk ₹16.2 cr) [AR FY26 note 31a].
- **Semaglutide adoption slower than hoped**: "patient resistance at the clinic level" and no plan to cut price yet [Q1 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "roaring/robust start" for Sundae to "a little slower than what we expected… patient resistance".
- International guidance fell from +18–20% to flat, with margin down 200–300 bps.
- Gross margin: a 300 bps DBF drop became the new issue; recovery now tied to Bhopal ramp-up in H2.
- Analogs slid from FY27 to "Q3 at best" (call) / "starting FY28" (deck).
- Positives: Bhopal licence finally received; OCF/EBITDA back to 77%; DBF growth 14% in line.

## My read
The India franchise is sound — 14% growth, insulin and semaglutide share gains, 35–37% margins — but the group is in a costly transition: gross margin fell 350 bps, exports are stalled by an EU GMP failure, and the Bhopal plant that should fix both margins and insulin supply only started in August 2026, a year late. Debt of ~₹2,250 cr (2× EBITDA) with ₹1,250 cr of NCDs due by June 2027, weak FY26 cash conversion and new brand-secured loans make execution matter more than usual, and the COO who ran the operating detail is leaving. The price already needs profit growth well above the 10–13% EBITDA growth now visible, because the trailing P/E is flattered by a ₹150 cr tax credit. The view improves if Q3 shows gross margin back towards 76% and debt falling; it worsens if the Swiss re-inspection slips past early 2027 or refinancing lifts interest costs.
