# Orchid Pharma — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code ORCHPHARMA (consolidated). Price ₹1,065, market cap ₹6,377 cr, 52-week range ₹471–1,144, trailing P/E 353 on trailing profit of about ₹11 cr [Screener]. **Important context:** Dhanuka Laboratories (the unlisted promoter company) merged into Orchid, effective 10 July 2026 with an appointed date of 1 April 2024. FY25 and FY26 are now restated as one combined business, so all older Orchid-only numbers (and most quarterly history on the dashboard) are not comparable [AR FY26 letter to shareholders; Q1 call].

## Business and segments
- Orchid makes cephalosporin antibiotics — the active ingredients (APIs) and some intermediates — mainly for other drug makers. Oral APIs are about two-thirds of the core business, sterile (injectable) APIs about one-third. Core cephalosporin API is "95% of revenue"; 44 of 50 cephalosporins ever invented are in its portfolio [IP Q4FY26 p.16].
- Concentration: two products, Cefixime and Cefuroxime Axetil, were about 67% of consolidated revenue in FY25. Cefixime is under "maximum stress" on price [CARE Apr-26; Q1 call].
- Geography (restated, FY26): India ₹414.7 cr, rest of world ₹795.8 cr of ₹1,210 cr product revenue (FY25: ₹387.8 cr and ₹996.1 cr) — exports fell 20% [AR FY26 note 44]. Regulated markets were 30% of sales in FY26 (one-third was the historic norm); the rest are price-driven "rest of world" markets in Africa and South Asia [Q4 call].
- Single reported segment ("Pharmaceuticals"); no segment margin is disclosed [AR FY26 note 44].
- Platform: sites at Alathur (Chennai), Manesar/Gurgaon, Keshwana (Rajasthan, from Dhanuka) and the Jammu 7-ACA site under construction. The Alathur API unit is one of three global US FDA-approved sterile cephalosporin API sites [CARE Apr-26; AR FY26].
- New lines: **Exblifep/Orblicef** (cefepime-enmetazobactam, Orchid's own invented antibiotic; global rights bought back from bankrupt Allecra in Q3 FY26), an Antimicrobial Stewardship (AMS) hospital division (Q1 FY27 revenue about ₹5 cr, EBITDA loss about ₹50 lakh), Ceftaroline (Ortaro) launched in India, and the non-penicillin non-cephalosporin (NPNC) business from Dhanuka (₹21 cr in Q1 FY27) [Q1 call; Q4 call].

## Key numbers
- Annual, restated combined (Screener consolidated): sales FY25 ₹1,398 cr → FY26 ₹1,233 cr (−12%); operating profit ₹151 cr → ₹48 cr; OPM 11% → 3.9%; net profit ₹135 cr → ₹10 cr. FY24 (Orchid only) sales ₹819 cr, profit ₹92 cr [Screener]. The AR letter confirms FY26 revenue ₹1,232.78 cr and PAT ₹9.96 cr [AR FY26].
- Gross margin fell from about 36% in FY25 to about 32% in FY26; volumes and prices both fell 15–20% in key products — "among the most challenging environments" in 15–20 years [Q1 call].
- Quarterly (Screener consolidated): Jun-25 ₹263 cr (OPM −2%), Sep-25 ₹194 cr (−1%), Dec-25 ₹207 cr (1%), Mar-26 ₹368 cr (8%), Jun-26 ₹304 cr (4.6%). Net profit −₹5.7 cr, −₹5.7 cr, −₹12.6 cr, +₹25.8 cr, +₹3.2 cr. Caution: Sep-25 and Dec-25 are pre-merger Orchid-only figures and Mar-26 is a restated balancing figure, so the series is not like-for-like [Screener].
- Q1 FY27 (restated, company): revenue ₹304 cr vs ₹263 cr (+15%); gross margin about 33% vs 30%; EBITDA ₹25 cr vs ₹10 cr [Q1 call]. Screener shows operating profit of only ₹14 cr (4.6%) for the same quarter — the gap is classification (company EBITDA includes items Screener treats differently); use Screener's ₹14 cr for consistency.
- Old Orchid-only basis (Q4 call, before restatement): FY26 revenue ₹811 cr vs ₹922 cr; EBITDA ₹101 cr vs ₹155 cr; Q4 FY26 revenue ₹238 cr, EBITDA ₹42.3 cr [Q4 call]. Dhanuka alone had FY26 revenue about ₹450 cr (₹500 cr in FY25) at "~7–8%" operating margins [Q4 call; CARE Apr-26].
- Unit economics: 7-ACA (the key raw material) price about $61/kg, long-run average $60, range $55–65 [Q4 call]. Cefiderocol vials sell at about $150 each in current markets [IP Q4FY26 p.21].

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (26 May 2026): base-business revenue growth target "10% to 15%" for FY27 and "something around" 12% EBITDA margin; merger to add 1–2% EBITDA margin over time; 7-ACA commissioning Q1 CY2027; cefiderocol plant commissioning Dec-2026, India launch Q2/Q3 CY2027; at least one Exblifep licensing deal "within this quarter"; about 50% of licensing deals signed "for sure" within six months [Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27 call (21 Aug 2026): management **dropped** numeric guidance — "I don't think it will be prudent to give you any number at this time." It still expects H2 to be better (regulated-market demand is seasonal) and called Q1 "mediocre" for regulated products. Timelines kept: cefiderocol commissioning Dec-2026, validation Jan–Mar 2027, India approval 6–9 months later (Q3 FY28) if the trial waiver comes; 7-ACA first commercial batch by March 2027; 7-ACA revenue from FY28 [Q1 call].
- Delivery vs Q4 promises: one licensing deal did arrive — Russia with Pharmasyntez, about $178 m estimated value over 10 years (July 2026) — but no US deal yet; Q1 revenue +15% was at the top of the old 10–15% range, but EBITDA margin (company 8.2%; Screener 4.6%) is far below the 12% target [Q1 call; News: Medical Dialogues, 8 Jul 2026].
- Verdict: guidance **dropped**; project timelines **kept**; Exblifep licensing **partly delivered** (Russia yes, US/Japan not yet). Longer record: 7-ACA was originally due in FY25 — now about two years late [CARE Apr-26].

## Capex and capacity
- 7-ACA plant (subsidiary Orchid Bio Pharma, Kathua, Jammu): 1,000 tonnes a year under the government's PLI scheme. CARE puts project cost at ₹596 cr (debt ₹447 cr, internal accruals/QIP ₹149 cr), ₹285 cr (48%) spent by Dec-2025; management quotes ₹750 cr for "7-ACA + downstream" [CARE Apr-26; IP Q4FY26; Q1 call].
- Use of 7-ACA output: long-term 75–80% in-house (25% replaces current purchases, about 50% goes into new downstream intermediates sold from Chennai), 20–25% sold to others. Ramp to 80–100% utilisation by end of first year is the plan, but management admits fermentation scale-up (800x from the pilot) is "a little unpredictable" and Aurobindo's experience suggests it may take longer [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Downstream intermediates capex at Alathur (about ₹100 cr) has been deferred after QIP money was reallocated; management is trying to "re-engineer" existing plants instead [CARE Apr-26; Q4 call]. Management says downstream products add about 5% EBITDA margin [Q4 call].
- Cefiderocol sterile vial plant (sub-licence from GARDP, capacity 1 million vials): cost ₹190 cr (debt ₹142.5 cr), ₹96 cr spent by Dec-2025. Supply is cost-plus with a guaranteed fixed profit; early demand estimated about 400,000 vials a year, a third in India. Equipment rerouted from China via Italy but timeline unchanged [CARE Apr-26; Q4 call; Q1 call].
- US sterile formulations: 5–6 products (ceftaroline, ceftazidime-avibactam, ceftolozane-tazobactam, cefepime, ceftriaxone, cefazolin) for a ~$1.2 bn US market "by 2030"; first two ANDAs via a contract manufacturer; fill-finish add-on about ₹50 cr; nothing before 2028 [Q4 call official; Q1 call].
- PLI incentive: because of the delay, Orchid now expects to be entitled for only 2 years of incentives; may seek an extension after start-up [Q1 call].
- FY26 capex about ₹389.6 cr; CWIP rose from ₹81 cr to ₹414 cr [AR FY26 letter; Screener]. Management says no "very significant capex" beyond these projects in FY28 [Q1 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹307 cr at Mar-2026 vs ₹154 cr at Mar-2025 (Screener consolidated). CARE counted ~₹185 cr of project debt by Dec-2025 (₹150 cr at the Jammu subsidiary); repayments start H1 FY28 (7-ACA) and Nov-2026 (cefiderocol) [Screener; CARE Apr-26].
- Standalone corporate guarantees for subsidiary loans: ₹579.6 cr (Mar-26) vs ₹447.2 cr [AR FY26 note 45].
- QIP (June 2023, ₹394.5 cr net): ₹62 cr still unused in fixed deposits at Mar-2026 [AR FY26 note 56].
- Cash flow: operating cash flow ₹126 cr in FY26 (₹51 cr in FY25); free cash flow −₹255 cr after capex [Screener; AR FY26].
- Working capital is heavy: debtor days 113, inventory days 161, payable days 150, cash conversion cycle 124 days (FY26) [Screener].
- No dividend. Rating **downgraded** to CARE BBB+ (Stable)/A2 from A- on 8 Apr 2026 because of weak 9M FY26 results and the 7-ACA delay; gearing expected to rise [CARE Apr-26].
- Promoter holding 69.84%; FIIs 0.80%, DIIs 20.75% (Jun-26) [Screener].

## Moat evidence
- Regulatory: one of three global US FDA-approved sterile cephalosporin API makers; 48 US DMFs, 6 ANDAs [CARE Apr-26].
- Breadth: "we are the only company which can make about 20 to 25 products", versus 3–4 for Indian rivals [Q1 call].
- IP: Exblifep is India's first home-discovered antibiotic with US FDA approval; Orchid owns it worldwide. Lifetime sales potential stated at $1–2 bn (peak in year 4–5 of launch) — an unchanged, unverified management number; about 30,000 patients treated last year [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Cost position is the weak point: pricing power in rest-of-world APIs is low (price fell 15–20%), 100% of GCLE is bought from Otsuka Chemical and 7-ACA is imported from 3–4 Chinese makers until Jammu starts [Q1 call]. Management argues 7-ACA prices have been stable for 10–12 years and Chinese makers are now profit-driven; the bear case is the Pen-G experience where China cut prices after India's PLI plants started [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Auditor qualification (consolidated), repetitive**: three US subsidiaries (assets ₹3.86 cr) are consolidated from unaudited accounts. Immaterial in size, but a qualified opinion every year [AR FY26 Statement on Impact of Audit Qualifications]. Standalone opinion unmodified.
- **Audit trail gap**: the merged Dhanuka division's accounting software did not have audit trail enabled through the year [AR FY26 note].
- **Related-party purchases**: goods bought from Otsuka Chemical (India) — a related party — of about ₹245 cr in FY26 (₹351 cr in FY25); ₹68.5 cr owed to it at year-end. An analyst noted this share of material cost has risen for five years; management said it depends on demand and price [AR FY26 related-party note; Q1 call].
- **Promoter loans**: Dhanuka family members and Dhanuka Agritech lent unsecured loans to Dhanuka Labs; large repayments in FY26 (e.g. ₹24 cr to Shashwat Dhanuka, ₹17.5 cr to Mridul Dhanuka, ₹10 cr to Dhanuka Agritech) [AR FY26 related-party note].
- **Execution/debt**: two debt-funded projects, both late historically; debt repayment starts close to start-up; PLI entitlement cut to two years; rating already cut [CARE Apr-26; Q1 call].
- **Merger dilution of margins**: Dhanuka runs at 7–8% operating margin and sells mostly to unregulated markets [CARE Apr-26].
- Contingent liabilities are small (tax/electricity/customs claims of a few crore); pre-insolvency claims were extinguished by the 2020 resolution plan [AR FY26 note 43/45].
- Disclosure gaps: no product or segment margins, no Exblifep sales figure, Dhanuka EBITDA not given at Q4.

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "worst phase of the pricing cycle may now be behind us" (Q4) to "industry is still facing overcapacities leading to cutthroat competition" and "premature to assume a straight-line recovery" (Q1).
- Numbers moved to a restated combined basis; revenue guidance and the 12% margin target were withdrawn.
- New: Russia Exblifep deal ($178 m est.), South Africa registration done, GCC launch delayed by the regional conflict, talks in Latin America, Mexico, Philippines, Thailand, Morocco and Australia; PLI entitlement now two years; 7-ACA capex quoted at ₹750 cr.

## My read
The core API business is in a genuine downcycle and the merger has made reported margins look worse, not better; Q1 FY27 recovery (company EBITDA ₹25 cr, Screener ₹14 cr) is still far below the ₹151 cr operating profit of FY25. The stock at about ₹6,400 cr is priced almost entirely on 2027–2030 projects — 7-ACA, cefiderocol, Exblifep licensing and US injectables — each of which has slipped before. For the price to work, 7-ACA must start by March 2027 and reach high utilisation within a year, margins must return to the low teens, and Exblifep deals must bring cash. What would change the view: a US Exblifep deal and a clean 7-ACA start (positive), or another delay, Chinese price cuts on 7-ACA or a second rating cut (negative).
