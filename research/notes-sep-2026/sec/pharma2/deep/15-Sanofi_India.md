# Sanofi India — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code SANOFI. **Use the standalone page**: the consolidated page stops at Dec 2024 because the consumer-health subsidiary was demerged in 2024. Price ₹3,140, market cap ₹7,232 cr, 52-week range ₹3,049–4,898. Financial year = calendar year (Jan–Dec), so the "Q1 FY27" quarter in the dashboard is Sanofi's Q2 CY2026 (Apr–Jun 2026) [Screener standalone].

## Business and segments
- Listed arm of Sanofi SA (promoter holding 60.4%). After the 2024 consumer-health demerger and the 2024–25 hand-over of legacy brands to partners, it has three revenue lines: (1) its own diabetes insulins — Lantus, Toujeo, Soliqua; (2) "partnership" sales of legacy cardiac (Clexane, Cardace etc.) and CNS brands promoted by Emcure and Cipla, and oral diabetes brands (Amaryl) promoted by Emcure; (3) exports from the Goa plant through a group company in Singapore [Aug-26 call; AR CY25 p.193].
- CY2025 revenue from operations ₹1,837 cr: India ₹1,593 cr, Singapore affiliate ₹231 cr (CY24 ₹392 cr), others ₹14 cr. Domestic net sales ₹1,511 cr, about half diabetes and half partnerships [AR CY25 note 33; Feb-26 call].
- Q2 CY26 domestic net sales ₹355.9 cr (+8%): diabetes insulins ₹203.1 cr (+14%, 57% of domestic), partnerships ₹145.7 cr (+2%), other ₹7.1 cr. Exports ₹60.3 cr (−2%). H1 CY26: diabetes +17%, partnerships −15%, exports −7%, domestic total −2% [IP Aug-26 p.4, p.8].
- Market position: Lantus ~47% of basal insulin by value; Toujeo 11%; together 58% by value and 61% by volume (IQVIA MAT Jun-26). Soliqua (insulin + GLP-1 combination) growing about 16% [IP Aug-26 p.5; Aug-26 call].
- Two customers exceed 10% of revenue: the Singapore affiliate (₹229 cr) and Emcure (₹504 cr in CY25 vs ₹318 cr in CY24) [AR CY25 p.194].

## Key numbers
- Quarterly sales (₹ cr), Screener standalone: Jun-25 406 → Sep-25 475 → Dec-25 420 → Mar-26 472 → Jun-26 438. Operating margin 23% → 28% → 22% → 30% → 26%. Net profit 70 → 76 → 62 → 103 → 84 [Screener].
- Q2 CY26: sales +8% YoY; profit before tax and exceptional items ₹112.3 cr (+19%), 27% of net sales vs 24%; operating costs −5% (H1 −15%) [IP Aug-26 p.9–10; Screener].
- Annual (Screener): sales CY23 ₹2,851 cr → CY24 ₹2,013 cr → CY25 ₹1,837 cr; operating margin 28% → 24% → 27%; net profit ₹603 cr → ₹414 cr → ₹327 cr. TTM sales ₹1,805 cr, TTM profit ₹324 cr. Sales have fallen 9% a year over five years, mainly from the demerger, brand hand-overs and loss of tolling for Zentiva [Screener; Feb-26 call].
- ROCE 58%, ROE 43% — high because the company pays out almost all its profit [Screener].
- CY25 EPS ₹142; dividend ₹123 a share (payout 87%) [Feb-26 call; Screener].

## Guidance history
- Sanofi gives no numeric guidance ("we don't give forward-looking statements") [Feb-26 call; Aug-26 call].
- Feb-26 call (for Q4 CY25): partnerships to "stabilise by end-2026"; no new product launches in 2026; diabetes to keep double-digit growth; volatility in partner sales would continue [Feb-26 call].
- Aug-26 call (for Q2 CY26): diabetes delivered (+14% in Q2, +17% in H1). Partnerships did not: +2% in Q2, and the CFO said "2026 I'm not expecting that we'll reach the industry growth; 2027 is what we are evaluating" — the stabilisation target has in effect slipped by a year. Dividend payout expected to be "minimum at last year payout" [Aug-26 call].
- Verdict: diabetes promise **met**; partnership recovery **pushed out** to 2027.

## Capex and capacity
- Minimal capex: fixed assets ₹291 cr, CWIP ₹19 cr (Jun 2026); capital commitments only ₹6 cr at Dec 2025. Depreciation about ₹36 cr a year [Screener; AR CY25 note 34].
- One plant (Goa) exporting to about 25 countries; strategy is to make Goa a specialised global site and bring back products from contract makers. Losses in Australia are being offset with France, Italy, Turkey and Russia; South Africa tenders awaited [Aug-26 call; Feb-26 call].
- No plan to buy back shares ("not in the strategy") [Aug-26 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Debt-free (₹15 cr lease liabilities). Cash up 34% year on year at June 2026 [Screener; Aug-26 call].
- Cash from operations CY25 ₹438 cr, free cash flow ₹413 cr — nearly all paid out as dividends (financing outflow ₹448 cr) [Screener].
- Debtor days 35, inventory 127 days, cash conversion cycle 83 days (CY25) [Screener].

## Moat evidence
- Brand and doctor loyalty in insulin: Lantus has kept ~47% share by value even after biosimilars, helped by matching biosimilar prices; volume grew 6% [IP Aug-26 p.5; Aug-26 call].
- Public-sector push: entry into government "care" accounts (CGHS, ESI, railways, defence, state contracts) grew 70% with Toujeo and Soliqua [Aug-26 call].
- Parent gives the trademarks and product rights to the listed company free of cost (no royalty); products and APIs are imported from Sanofi-Aventis Singapore [AR CY25 p.30, AGM notice].
- Weakness: the listed company gets no new group products — new launches go to the unlisted Sanofi Healthcare India; "no new products in 2026" [Feb-26 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Parent dependence / related parties.** Purchases from group companies ₹537 cr in CY25 (₹462 cr from Sanofi-Aventis Singapore, ₹75 cr from Sanofi Healthcare India), sales to them ₹254 cr. Shareholders are asked to approve up to ₹1,385 cr of 2026 transactions with the Singapore affiliate and ₹530 cr with Sanofi Healthcare India [AR CY25 note 37; AGM notice].
- **Two-company structure.** Management evaluated merging the listed and unlisted Indian entities and "didn't find it would bring value for the shareholders" [Feb-26 call].
- **Contingent liabilities**: income-tax demands ₹318.6 cr (₹79.7 cr where the department appealed after losing; ₹238.9 cr under the company's appeal) — this is the auditor's key audit matter. Separate provisions for DPCO/NPPA price-overcharging demands (₹36.7 cr) and ₹16.2 cr retained for NPPA demands remanded in 2019 [AR CY25 p.146, notes 36, 40, 42].
- **Auditor (Price Waterhouse & Co)**: unmodified opinion. Audit-trail gaps: core accounting software does not log changes by certain privileged users and the database-level trail was not enabled; the auditor could not comment on the payroll provider's trail for Oct–Dec 2025 [AR CY25 p.149].
- **Management churn**: MD Rodolfo Hrosz left April 2025; CFO Rachid Ayari was interim MD Jul–Oct 2025; Deepak Arora MD from Oct 2025; analysts wished Ayari well on his "next venture" on the Aug-26 call; three company secretaries in 2025 [AR CY25 note 37; Aug-26 call].
- **Partner under-performance**: partnership sales +2% in Q2 against a growing cardiac/CNS market; H1 −15% [Aug-26 call].
- **Insulin disruption**: Novo Nordisk's once-weekly insulin (Awiqli) and cheap generic semaglutide [Aug-26 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- Feb-26: defensive — domestic sales flat for 2025, partnership sales fell to ₹153 cr in Q4 from ~₹200 cr in Q3, EBITDA margin an "8-quarter low" of 21.5%.
- Aug-26: confident — PBT +19%, margin 27%, diabetes +14%; but partnerships now "not as per our expectation" and industry-level growth pushed to 2027.
- New in Aug: explicit dividend comfort ("minimum at last year payout"); buyback rejected; CFO departure signalled.

## My read
Sanofi India is now a small, cash-rich insulin franchise with a slow-growing tail of partner-sold legacy brands and a shrinking export arm. The insulin business is genuinely healthy (+14–17%) and cost cuts lifted the margin to 27%, but total sales are still roughly flat because partnerships and exports offset it. At about 21 times trailing profit with a ~2.4% dividend yield, the stock is priced as a steady dividend payer, which fits if diabetes keeps double-digit growth and partnerships return to growth in 2027. What would change the view: a group decision to route new products into the listed company (positive), or a further slide in partnership sales and a lower payout (negative).
