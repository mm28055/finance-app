# Natco Pharma — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code NATCOPHARM (consolidated). Price ₹839, market cap ₹15,033 cr, 52-week range ₹789–1,227 [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].

## Business and segments
- Natco makes hard-to-copy generic medicines, mostly cancer drugs, plus a small crop-protection (pesticide) arm. FY26 segment revenue: pharmaceuticals ₹3,940 cr, agro-chemicals ₹138 cr; segment profit: pharma ₹1,547 cr, agro a loss of ₹19.8 cr (FY25 loss ₹145.8 cr, which included a ₹50 cr write-down of agro plant) [AR FY26 p.362, note 36].
- Geography FY26: USA ₹2,381 cr (FY25 ₹3,149 cr), India ₹691 cr (₹567 cr), rest of world ₹1,006 cr (₹714 cr). The US fell 24% as the Revlimid (lenalidomide) profit share faded; the rest grew 32% [AR FY26 p.362].
- One customer (the US Revlimid partner) contributed ₹1,687 cr in FY26 (41% of revenue) vs ₹2,686 cr in FY25 (61%). Receivable from it fell from ₹731 cr to ₹20.5 cr — the jackpot has run off [AR FY26 p.363].
- Subsidiaries: Brazil FY26 revenue ₹287.5 cr; Canada ₹251.7 cr; group subsidiary revenue about ₹730 cr [AR FY26 MD&A p.108; Q4 call]. On the Q4 call the CEO put Canada at ₹229 cr, which is lower than the annual report's ₹251.7 cr [Q4 call vs AR FY26].
- Q1 FY27 mix (company revenue incl. other income ₹794 cr): international formulations ₹477 cr (incl. Brazil ₹178 cr, +180%; Canada ~₹56 cr), domestic formulations ₹136 cr (Q1 FY26 ₹107 cr), API ₹66.7 cr, crop health ₹40.8 cr, other operating and non-operating income ₹73.4 cr [IP Q1FY27 p.4; Q1 call].
- Associate: 49% of Adcock Ingram, South Africa's second-largest private-market drug company. Adcock FY26 (June year) revenue US$592 m, profit US$57 m (FY25 US$549 m / US$48 m). Natco paid about ZAR 4.0 bn (~US$226 m; ICRA: ₹2,000 cr) for the first 34.88% in Nov 2025 and ZAR 1.81 bn (~US$111 m; ₹1,060 cr per call) for 13.25% more in July 2026 [AR FY26 p.6; IP Q1FY27 p.3; ICRA Nov-25; Q1 call].
- Plants: four US FDA-inspected sites (two API, two finished dosage). Kothur, Chennai and Nellore were cleared in 2025 with EIRs; Vizag inspection pending. The Kothur warning letter (FY24) was lifted in Sep 2025 [Q4 call; ICRA Nov-25].

## Key numbers
- Quarterly sales (₹ cr), Screener: Jun-25 1,329 → Sep-25 1,363 → Dec-25 647 → Mar-26 739 → Jun-26 735. Operating margin: 43% → 42% → 25% → 17% → 25%. Net profit: 480 → 518 → 151 → 269 → 206 [Screener].
- Q1 FY27 vs Q1 FY26: sales −45%, operating profit ₹186 cr vs ₹571 cr (−67%), net profit −57%. Of the ₹206 cr profit, ₹84 cr (41%) was Natco's share of Adcock profit; profit before the associate share was only ₹122 cr [Screener; IP Q1FY27 p.5].
- Q4 FY26 profit of ₹269 cr included a one-time ₹115 cr deferred-tax gain from moving to the new tax regime (Screener shows a −62% tax rate that quarter) [Q4 call; Screener].
- Annual trend (Screener): sales FY24 ₹3,999 cr → FY25 ₹4,430 cr → FY26 ₹4,078 cr; operating margin 44% → 50% → 36%; net profit ₹1,388 cr → ₹1,883 cr → ₹1,418 cr. TTM sales ₹3,485 cr, TTM profit ₹1,145 cr. ROCE fell from 33% (FY25) to 17% (FY26) [Screener].
- Other income is large: ₹272 cr in FY26 and ₹295 cr TTM (interest, PLI incentive, licensing fees such as the India semaglutide licence) [Screener; Q4 call].
- Semaglutide (India): own brand about ₹2 cr a month; with partners Cadila and Glenmark about ₹4 cr a month extra; management hoped for ₹75–100 cr annualised. On Q1 the CEO said it "does not lose money, but it does not make much money yet" [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (29 May 2026): FY27 revenue ₹3,400–3,500 cr; PAT ₹700–750 cr. The official transcript says the Adcock profit share is **included** in that PAT range. Adcock itself was guided at US$580–600 m revenue and US$47–48 m profit. R&D 7–9% of sales. Tax ~25%. Earnings to compound 15–25% a year from FY28, with FY27 as the base [Q4 call, official].
- Q1 FY27 call (14 Aug 2026): PAT guidance "remains around ₹750 cr — I am not changing the guidance"; revenue restated as ₹3,300–3,400 cr, i.e. ₹100 cr below the Q4 range, without comment. Domestic revenue to grow about 25% in FY27. Crop health to break even for the year [Q1 call, official].
- Delivery so far: Q1 PAT ₹206 cr is 27–29% of the ₹700–750 cr range, but 41% of it came from Adcock during a strong South African flu season, which management told analysts not to annualise. Q1 sales of ₹735 cr annualise to about ₹2,900 cr, short of the ₹3,300–3,400 cr revenue range; management expects "all-round growth" from Brazil, Canada and two US orders [Q1 call; Screener].
- Brazil: Q4 target US$55–60 m for FY27 (about ₹470–510 cr); Q1 ₹178 cr is on track. Canada: +10–15% guided; Q1 ₹56 cr is flat-ish against the ₹229–252 cr FY26 base [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Crop-health demerger: annual report says effective October 2026; on the Q1 call it was pushed to about March 2027 because of the fundraise [AR FY26 p.110; Q1 call].
- Verdict: guidance **kept** on profit, **trimmed** on revenue.

## Capex and capacity
- Organic capex "around ₹250–300 cr" a year [Q1 call]. Capital work-in-progress rose to ₹294 cr (Screener) — ₹84 cr of it is 1–2 years old; the auditors report no overdue or over-budget projects [AR FY26 p.364, note 39].
- FY26 capacity additions at Kothur and other sites (a liquid capsule line of 75 lakh capsules a year, etc.); the Kothur plant is being upgraded for the carfilzomib launch, to finish by end-2026 [AR FY26 p.12; Q1 call].
- Acquisitions are the real capex: about ₹3,000 cr into Adcock in 12 months. Management says the return is currently "only slightly better than bank interest"; target 15–20% return on capital over time [Q4 call].
- Fundraise: on 25 Sep 2026 the board approved a 2-for-21 rights issue at ₹750 a share, raising up to ₹1,279.36 cr; record date 1 Oct, opening 12 Oct 2026. It funds "a couple" of acquisitions (a small one in India, a very large one abroad), short-term loan repayment and capex [BSE filing 25 Sep 2026 via Screener; Q1 call]. The Q1 call had spoken of up to ₹2,000 cr.

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net cash: about ₹2,400 cr at March 2026; about ₹1,400 cr after the July Adcock payment [Q4 call; Q1 call]. Borrowings ₹714 cr at March 2026 (₹279 cr a year earlier), including a ₹400 cr term loan used for Adcock [Screener; ICRA Nov-25].
- Cash flow from operations FY26 ₹1,768 cr, free cash flow ₹1,379 cr; CFO/operating profit 148% as the US partner paid down receivables [Screener].
- Working capital: debtor days 79 (FY25 103), inventory days 480, cash conversion cycle 343 days. ICRA flags "high working capital intensity" (43% of revenue in H1 FY26) and inventory of about 193 days [Screener; ICRA Nov-25].
- Dividend payout only 6% (FY25 and FY26); buyback ruled out in favour of acquisitions [Screener; Q4 call].
- Rating: ICRA AA (Stable)/A1+, reaffirmed Nov 2025 on ₹2,150 cr of limits; ICRA calls any large debt-funded acquisition "an event risk" [ICRA Nov-25].

## Moat evidence
- Litigation and complex-chemistry skill: 28 US Para IV filings with over 20 first-to-file (sole or shared), 17 approved (tentative or final); pipeline includes carfilzomib (Kyprolis), ibrutinib, olaparib (Lynparza), semaglutide pens [IP Q1FY27 p.6]. Revlimid produced ₹2,686 cr from a single partner in FY25 — proof that the model pays when it hits [AR FY26 p.363].
- India: the only generic semaglutide vial at launch, priced under ₹1,000 against ₹3,000–4,000 for pens [Q4 call]. Strong domestic oncology position per ICRA [ICRA Nov-25].
- Brazil: sole source of everolimus for the fourth year; among the first to launch apixaban [AR FY26 p.108].
- Weakness: the moat is product-by-product and temporary. Pomalidomide was launched with four or five other day-one filers and gave "no bump" [Q4 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Earnings cliff and concentration.** 41% of FY26 revenue from one customer; that stream has almost gone. Next US exclusivities come only in FY28 [AR FY26 p.363; Q4 call].
- **Capital allocation.** ₹3,000 cr into a minority stake earning near bank-interest returns; now a rights issue at ₹750 (about 11% below the ₹839 price) to fund two undisclosed deals. Promoter holding 49.41% [Q4 call; BSE 25 Sep 2026; Screener].
- **Associate dependence.** Adcock gave 41% of Q1 profit; Bidvest holds 51% and control; Natco has only a right of first refusal [Q1 call].
- **Auditor.** Unmodified opinion. Key audit matters: revenue under profit-share deals (estimates of variable consideration), MAT-credit deferred tax asset tied to past tax disputes, and impairment of agro plant valued on a market-multiple basis because of the demerger. The accounting software's database-level audit trail was not enabled from 1 Apr to 10 Jul 2025 and was not enabled for certain tables for the rest of FY26 [AR FY26 p.287–292].
- **Contingent liabilities** are small (disputed tax about ₹3 cr) but patent suits against launched products cannot be estimated [AR FY26 p.363–364].
- **Land:** 15–20% of the land bank is tied up in litigation [Q1 call].
- **Management change:** CFO S.V.V.N. Appa Rao left 12 Feb 2026; Amit Parekh took over 13 Feb 2026 [AR FY26 note 35].
- **Olaparib** trial outcome and exclusivity still undetermined [Q1 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "we have ₹2,400 cr, comfortable for one or two large acquisitions" (Q4) to "we want to raise money from the market" (Q1), even though ₹1,400 cr net cash remained.
- Revenue guidance slipped from ₹3,400–3,500 cr to ₹3,300–3,400 cr; PAT kept at ~₹750 cr.
- Demerger pushed from October/December to about March 2027.
- Semaglutide: from "good share, ₹75–100 cr annualised" to "competition is very intense… not much money yet".
- New positives: Brazil +180%; Adcock profit share doubled to ₹84 cr on a flu season; US FDA final approval for eribulin injection.

## My read
The business is in the trough management promised: Revlimid is gone, core operating margin has halved to about 25%, and nearly half of profit now comes from a South African associate that Natco does not control. The base is genuinely broader (Brazil, India, Adcock), but FY27 profit of ₹700–750 cr and a 15–25% compounding path from FY28 both depend on US launches (carfilzomib, olaparib) that are litigated and undisclosed. The stock is cheap on trailing numbers, but trailing numbers still include Revlimid; on the ₹750 cr guidance it is at roughly 20 times profit, before dilution from a ₹1,279 cr rights issue at ₹750. The view improves if the rights money goes into an acquisition with visible returns and carfilzomib launches on time in 2027; it worsens if the big overseas deal is expensive or a US launch slips.
