# Wockhardt — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code WOCKPHARMA. Price ₹2,266, market cap ₹36,814 cr, 52-week range ₹1,087–2,422 [Screener]. Wockhardt does not hold quarterly earnings calls; the two stored "transcripts" are the annual investor meets of 4 Jun 2026 [IM Jun-26] and 5 Jun 2025 [IM Jun-25].

## Business and segments
- Three verticals: pharmaceuticals (~75% of sales; UK, Ireland, India branded, emerging markets), diabetes biosimilars (human insulin, glargine; 5 more in pipeline incl. aspart, degludec, semaglutide), and novel antibiotics (Zaynich, Emrok, Miqnaf). 11 plants, 2 R&D centres [IM Jun-26].
- FY26 revenue ₹3,373 cr; specialty (biosimilars + novel antibiotics) 23% of sales; biosimilars +27% (insulin output doubled, glargine 1.5x) [IM Jun-26]. Geography: UK 39% (+13%, top-5 generic player, 18% share of its covered market), emerging markets 28% (+35%, partnerships in Brazil, Thailand, Algeria, Malaysia), India and Ireland the rest [IM Jun-26].
- US generics exited 3–4 years ago (was ~5% of sales and loss-making, legacy of 2013 import alerts) [IM Jun-26].
- **Zaynich (cefepime/zidebactam)**: USFDA approval for complicated urinary tract infection (cUTI) in late May/early June 2026 — first Indian-discovered drug approved by USFDA; label covers Enterobacterales and Pseudomonas with breakpoints covering ~95% of US carbapenem-resistant cases; also approved by India's CDSCO; European filing done, approval expected by end-2026 [IM Jun-26].
- Capacity/utilisation: auditor notes "lower capacity utilisation" at some plants; biosimilar capacity to double in 12–15 months [AR FY26 KAM; IM Jun-26].

## Guidance history
- June 2025 meet: Zaynich US filing "coming quarter", launch "by middle of next year" (mid-2026); India approval early 2026 and launch mid-2026; India Zaynich ₹200–400 cr from 20,000–30,000 patients in the first 6–9 months, 80,000–100,000 patients in 3 years; biosimilar business to double in 3 years; US route either out-licence or own team [IM Jun-25].
- Delivered by June 2026: USFDA approval achieved roughly on time; India approved; own US team chosen (with an outsourced "operating partner" for sales-force logistics, distribution and pharmacovigilance) [IM Jun-26].
- June 2026 meet: US launch execution "in the next six to nine months" (i.e. commercial ramp from ~early 2027); first three years of new US anti-infectives typically $30–50 mn cumulative; "hockey stick" after 12–18 months; Zaynich unit to break even in 12–18 months with a small loss before; global peak sales $1.5–2 bn a year (US ~40%); target 20–25% of resistant cases; biosimilars to double in 24–36 months; normal capex ₹200–300 cr; R&D spend steady as % of sales; Emrok/Miqnaf India only; next NCE (Odrate, Foviscu) at least 4 years away [IM Jun-26].
- Verdict: **kept on regulatory milestones**, but the US commercial timeline moved out (launch mid-2026 promised in 2025 → ramp "6–9 months" from June 2026), and the ₹200–400 cr India first-year number was not repeated.

## Moat evidence
- Zaynich: 20% higher composite cure than meropenem in Phase 3 (96.8% composite cure); >90% efficacy in carbapenem-resistant patients in an Indian study; 85 compassionate-use patients; patent to 2038 plus QIDP five-year exclusivity [IM Jun-26].
- Six antibiotic programmes with USFDA QIDP status — "no other company in the world has more than one or two" (chairman's claim) [IM Jun-26].
- UK: top-5 generic player, 18% share of covered market [IM Jun-26].
- Diabetes biosimilars: end-to-end (API, device, sales) with 6–7 competitors in India/EM, registered in 30 markets [IM Jun-26].
- Limits: competitor cefiderocol (Shionogi) does ~$250 mn (questioner's figure) after 7 years; hospital antibiotic stewardship restricts new antibiotics to last-resort use [IM Jun-26].

## Risks and red flags
- **Customs penalty (order received 21 Aug 2026)**: ₹5 cr penalty plus ₹10 cr redemption fine for mis-declared/mis-classified API exports, and ₹1 cr penalty on a company officer; disclosed late on 25 Aug 2026 citing "inadvertent administrative oversight"; company will appeal [BSE Reg-30 filing 25 Aug 2026].
- **Capitalised R&D**: ₹1,707 cr of intangibles under development on a book value of ~₹4,940 cr; if an NCE fails, write-offs follow. Auditor flags recoverability as a key audit matter [AR FY26 KAM].
- **Idle assets**: ₹707 cr of plant/CWIP depends on deferred projects [AR FY26 KAM].
- **Promoter pledge**: 1.50 crore of the promoter trust's 6.52 crore shares pledged (23% of promoter holding, ~9% of company), up from 1.45 crore [AR FY26 note on share capital]. Promoter holding fell from 74% (2017) to 49.09% via QIPs [Screener].
- **Dilution risk**: ₹3,000 cr fund-raising enabling approval [AR FY26].
- Contingent liabilities ~₹675 cr: income tax ₹345 cr, sales tax/GST ₹117 cr, NPPA overcharging ₹136 cr, excise ₹45 cr [AR FY26 note 45]. Texas Medicaid settlement ($36 mn) fully paid [AR FY26 note 45].
- Related parties: promoter family runs Wockhardt Hospitals (unlisted); Zahabiya Khorakiwala (MD of Wockhardt Hospitals) now leads the US Zaynich business — first-time US commercial leadership [IM Jun-26; AR FY26].
- Execution: building a US hospital sales organisation from scratch; history of a 2013 USFDA import alert [IM Jun-26].
- Quarterly profit still volatile (Q1 FY26 loss) [Screener].

## My read
Wockhardt has turned a corner operationally — margins went from 3% to 19% in two years and it is now profitable — and Zaynich's USFDA approval is a genuine first for India. But the market cap (~₹37,000 cr on FY26 profit of ₹199 cr) already prices a large Zaynich success that management itself says will be slow for 12–18 months, with typical new antibiotics doing only $30–50 mn in their first three years in the US. The accounts carry large capitalised R&D and idle assets, a new ₹3,000 cr dilution window, a 23% promoter pledge and a fresh customs penalty with late disclosure. The view improves if US hospital formulary wins and early prescriptions show up by mid-2027 and if the base business keeps 20%+ margins; it worsens on write-downs, big equity dilution or a slow US launch.
