# Rubicon Research (NSE: RUBICON) — deep research, Sep 2026

## Business and segments
- Rubicon is an R&D-led maker of US generic and "specialty" medicines (tablets, oral liquids, nasal sprays). It defines "specialty" as a product where it faces 0 or at most 1 competitor for at least a year after launch. [IP Q1FY27 slide 16, 25]
- The business is almost entirely American: of FY26 revenue of ₹1,754 cr, the USA was ₹1,737 cr (99%), India ₹6.3 cr and others ₹10.5 cr. The group reports one segment (pharmaceutical products). [AR FY26 consolidated note 32]
- Customer concentration is extreme, as usual for US generics: five customers each gave 10% or more of FY26 revenue — 19%, 16%, 13%, 13% and 11% (≈72% together; same pattern in FY25). These are the big US wholesalers/purchasing groups. [AR FY26 note 32]
- Portfolio: 86 active approved products at 30 Jun 2026 (78 ANDAs + 8 NDAs), 76 commercialised (88% commercialisation rate); 21 specialty products in FY26 vs 3 in FY23. Specialty products gave 36% of gross profit in Q1 FY27 (₹127 cr), up from 33% in Q4 FY26 and 27% for FY26. [IP Q1FY27 slides 15, 25, 28; Q4 call]
- US branded arm Validus markets 4 brands with no FDA-approved substitutable generic; specialty contribution after Validus costs was ₹311 cr in FY26 (specialty gross profit ₹377 cr less Validus opex ₹66 cr). [IP Q1FY27 slide 25]
- Product concentration has fallen over time: top-5 products 39% of Q1 FY27 revenue (77% in FY23), top-10 55% (vs 56% a year earlier). [IP Q1FY27 slides 9, 28]
- India entry: bought 85% of **Arinna Lifesciences** (CNS brands, ~60 brands, 4,000+ prescribers) in April 2026 at an enterprise value of ₹200 cr; deal value about ₹176 cr; Arinna had ~₹72 cr revenue and ~₹10 cr EBITDA. It added ₹12.8 cr revenue and no material EBITDA in Q1 FY27. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 5]
- Plants: Ambernath (oral solids >6 bn units/yr, nasal sprays >50 mn vials/yr), Satara (oral liquids, nasal), Pithampur/Indore (bought from Alkem in FY26; hormones, steroids, high-potency; not yet commercial), and a new US site in East Brunswick/East Windsor, New Jersey (bought July 2026 for $2.9 mn). R&D centres in Thane and Toronto. [IP Q1FY27 slides 30–32; Q1 call]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly revenue: Jun-25 352 → Sep-25 412 → Dec-25 476 → Mar-26 514 → Jun-26 534. Operating profit (EBITDA): 79 → 94 → 108 → 119 → 129. OPM: 22% → 23% → 23% → 23% → 24%. Net profit: 43 → 54 → 73 → 77 → 85. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27 vs Q1 FY26: revenue +52%, EBITDA +63%, PAT +96% (company: ₹84.8 cr; includes a one-off insurance claim of ₹3.6 cr in other income). [Screener; IP Q1FY27 slide 6]
- Annual: revenue FY24 ₹854 cr → FY25 ₹1,284 cr → FY26 ₹1,754 cr (+37%); OPM 18% → 21% → 23%; PAT ₹91 cr → ₹134 cr → ₹247 cr (+84%). TTM revenue ₹1,936 cr, PAT ₹288 cr. 3-yr sales CAGR 65%. [Screener]
- Unit economics: gross margin 67.7% in Q1 FY27 (66.3% in Q4 FY26); pre-R&D EBITDA margin 35.0%; R&D 10.9% of revenue (₹58 cr). USD revenue $55 mn, +32% y/y from $42 mn but slightly down q/q because low-margin business was dropped. Portfolio average unit price rose 8.0% over FY22–25 against an industry erosion of –5.2% (Frost & Sullivan data cited by company). [IP Q1FY27 slides 6, 9, 10, 23]
- ROCE: Screener 28% (FY26); company's own pre-tax ROACE ex-cash 36%, with ₹354 cr (about a quarter of capital) in pre-revenue assets — Pithampur ₹150 cr, New Jersey/CSN ₹28 cr, Arinna ₹176 cr. [Screener; IP Q1FY27 slide 7]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (29 May 2026): FY27 operating EBITDA margin 22–23% "even for the coming quarters", including ESOP, Arinna, Pithampur and input-cost escalations; more reliance on contract makers "for at least a couple more quarters"; Pithampur ramp-up from Q1 CY27; R&D ₹500 cr over FY26–Q1 FY28; capex ~₹300 cr over two years; NWC around 126 days. [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: EBITDA margin 24.2% (above the band); gross margin up 140 bps q/q; R&D ₹251.5 cr spent in 5 of the 9 quarters, ~₹60 cr/quarter run rate — on track. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 12]
- Guidance **raised**: FY27 operating EBITDA margin now "at least 23%" (was 22–23%), even after ESOP costs, Arinna growth spending and pre-revenue plant costs. Management said Q2 FY27 is "tracking well" for sequential USD revenue growth. [IP Q1FY27 slide 10; Q1 call]
- Longer term: R&D productivity (incremental revenue ÷ lagged 9-quarter R&D) was 3.3x (FY21→FY24), 4.1x, 5.9x (FY23→FY26) and 5.5x on annualised Q1 FY27; management says "5-plus" is a fair assumption, which with ₹500 cr+ R&D "gives strong visibility for FY29/30". Arinna: beat Indian market growth from FY28, profitability later. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 24]
- Pipeline disclosure narrowed: 24 products under FDA review was given at Q4; the company declined to update it at Q1, saying the number will be given once a year. [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ₹80 cr plus Pithampur acquisition ₹149 cr; Q1 FY27 capex ₹37 cr plus Arinna ₹176 cr; guidance ~₹300 cr over FY27–FY28 "across various sites". Philosophy: capex lags proven demand; outsourcing fills the gap. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 8]
- Pithampur: unannounced USFDA inspection in July 2026, Form 483 with 2 procedural observations; response filed and a product filing approved after the inspection, but the formal EIR (closure report) was still "awaited" in the Q1 deck. Ramp-up from Q1 CY27; 12–18 months to decent utilisation; site is ~30 acres, only 5–6 acres used. Pre-revenue capital ₹150 cr. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 slides 7, 20]
- New Jersey: bought for $2.9 mn in a US bankruptcy (section 363) auction; May 2026 inspection had 6 observations but the FDA site now shows VAI (acceptable). Shares a wall with the AimRx US warehouse; aimed at specialty products and US government (VA) business needing US-made supply; commercial in CY27. [IP Q1FY27 slide 11; Q1 call]
- Fixed-asset turnover 4.1x on average net fixed assets in Q1 FY27 (4.6x in FY25), diluted by pre-revenue assets. [IP Q1FY27 slide 29]
- Payback: management would not quantify the margin gain from in-house vs outsourced making, saying own manufacturing is "far more efficient". [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Cash from operations: FY24 ₹21 cr, FY25 ₹159 cr, FY26 ₹205 cr; free cash flow FY26 –₹24 cr after the Pithampur purchase. Q1 FY27 operating cash flow only ₹28.5 cr (vs ₹139 cr before working-capital changes) because GST refunds were delayed; company says they began normalising after quarter end. [Screener; IP Q1FY27 slide 8]
- Balance sheet (company): borrowings ₹259 cr and cash ₹346 cr at Mar-26 (net cash ~₹87 cr); at Jun-26 borrowings ₹327 cr, cash ₹241 cr (net debt ~₹86 cr) after the Arinna purchase, which was partly debt-funded. Equity ₹1,391 cr. Screener shows borrowings ₹311 cr at Mar-26 (likely includes leases). [IP Q1FY27 slide 7; Screener]
- Working capital: company NWC 114 days at Jun-26 vs 126 days at Mar-26; guided back to 125–130 days. Inventory ₹746 cr and receivables ₹592 cr at Jun-26. Screener FY26 inventory days 550 (cost-based), debtor days 106, cash conversion cycle 371 days. Management calls inventory "fuel for growth"; about a third of factorable US receivables are factored (cost booked in finance costs). [IP Q1FY27 slide 7; Screener; Q4 call]
- IPO in Oct 2025 raised primary money used mainly for debt repayment; ₹100 cr unused for acquisitions at Q4. First dividend ₹1.5/share (10% payout). [Q4 call]
- Credit rating: India Ratings IND A-/Positive (July 2025; outlook raised from Stable). Rationale: 97%+ US revenue, regulatory, currency and tariff risk; net leverage 2.5x in FY24 then; working-capital cycle "elongated"; need to hold high finished-goods inventory at the US arm to avoid supply-failure penalties. [IndRa Jul-25]

## Moat evidence
- Market-share execution (IQVIA volume data): #1 in Metoprolol tartrate (48.0% FY26), Baclofen (33.9%), Diclofenac potassium (32.6%), Cyclobenzaprine (34.8%) even as a late entrant against 5–8 competitors. [IP Q1FY27 slide 22]
- Pricing: average unit price +8% over FY22–25 vs industry –5.2%; "pricing continues to remain stable". [IP Q1FY27 slides 9, 23]
- Compliance: clean USFDA record — Ambernath NAI/VAI history, Canada R&D zero observations (Apr 2026), Thane R&D zero (Mar 2025); one of 9 sites globally chosen for the FDA's Quality Management Maturity pilot in 2024. [IP Q1FY27 slides 20–21]
- Nasal spray niche: 5 approved nasal drug-device products; claims one of the largest single-site nasal spray capacities; only 28 companies had nasal approvals in 2019–24. [IP Q1FY27 slide 19; AR FY26 MD&A]
- R&D efficiency: fully expensed R&D (no capitalisation), 88–92% commercialisation of approvals, R&D productivity 5.5–5.9x. [IP Q1FY27 slides 12, 24]
- Capital-light M&A: Satara (from Cipla), Pithampur (from Alkem), NJ plant for $2.9 mn; only ~$25 mn equity raised from 1999 until the IPO. [IP Q1FY27 slides 27, 32]

## Risks and red flags
- **Promoter selling:** General Atlantic (promoter) sold 8.38% (1.39 cr shares) on 26 Aug 2026 at about ₹1,660 for ₹2,300 cr; GA now holds 27.45%, promoter group 51.38% (from 59.76%). Buyers included HDFC, Kotak, ICICI Pru, SBI and Axis MFs and Temasek's Aranda. A further GA overhang remains. Founder family holds only ~24%. [News: Outlook Business, 27 Aug 2026; IP Q1FY27 slide 17]
- **Concentration:** 99% US revenue; five customers ≈72% of revenue. [AR FY26 note 32]
- **Working capital and cash conversion:** CFO/EBITDA 78% in FY26, FCF negative; Q1 FY27 CFO only ₹28.5 cr; inventory high and exposed to launch delays. [Screener; IP Q1FY27 slide 8]
- **Tariffs:** an analyst asked how a US pharma tariff would be handled with ~97% US exports; management gave no specific plan. The New Jersey plant is partly a hedge. [Q1 call; IndRa Jul-25]
- **Disclosure getting thinner:** pipeline count now annual only; no product names; gross-margin gap of own vs outsourced not given; an analyst questioned standalone material cost vs revenue growth (transfer pricing) — CFO said fully compliant, offered to take it offline. [Q1 call]
- **Management change:** CFO Nitin Jajodia moves to Chief Commercial Officer; Rohit Saraogi (ex-Marico, Reckitt) is CFO-designate. [IP Q1FY27 slide 13]
- **Auditor:** Deloitte Haskins & Sells gave an unmodified opinion on FY26; key audit matters were revenue cut-off and US chargebacks/rebates/returns accruals (judgement-heavy); 11 subsidiaries (₹263 cr revenue, ₹350 cr assets) audited by other auditors. CARO: no defaults, IPO money used as stated. [AR FY26 auditor's report, CARO]
- **Contingent liabilities (small):** income-tax demands ₹18.4 cr and sales-tax ₹0.7 cr under appeal at Mar-26 (₹10.6 cr and ₹1.6 cr a year earlier). [AR FY26 consolidated note 30]
- **Related parties (small):** office leased from Medone Pharma Labs (partners are MD/CEO relatives) — ₹5.1 cr rent in FY26; three family members employed (combined pay ~₹4.6 cr); CEO pay ₹5.1 cr. [AR FY26 note 43]
- **Arinna economics:** ~5% return on the price paid at acquisition; phase 1 is "fix growth levers", profits later. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "gross margin under pressure, more outsourcing for a couple of quarters" (Q4) to "gross margin up 140 bps, low-margin business dropped, margin guidance raised" (Q1).
- New: US manufacturing (New Jersey) and government/VA angle; Pithampur inspected earlier than expected (surprise inspection, 2 observations); CFO transition; 24-product pipeline number withdrawn from quarterly disclosure.
- Cash flow weakened (Q4 CFO ₹65.5 cr → Q1 ₹28.5 cr) on GST refund delays; NWC days improved but guided back up.

## My read
The business is clearly improving: revenue up about 50% a year, margins rising, specialty share of profit climbing, and two new plants that should lift gross margin from CY27 once outsourced volumes come home. The price (P/E in the high 90s at ₹1,676) already assumes 30%+ earnings growth for many years, so the stock needs continuing FDA approvals, a clean Pithampur ramp in 2027 and a turn in cash flow. The overhang from General Atlantic's continuing sell-down, 99% US exposure (tariff risk) and thin, falling disclosure are the main worries. A US pharma tariff, a warning letter at any site, or cash flow staying weak while inventory climbs would change the view; strong Q2 cash flow and specialty share reaching 40% of gross profit would support it.
