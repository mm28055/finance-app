# Indian logistics — sector research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Covers the four listed names in this note: express parcel / e-commerce and quick-commerce delivery (Delhivery, Shadowfax), contract logistics and freight forwarding (TVS Supply Chain Solutions), and LPG/chemical port terminals plus gas distribution (Aegis Logistics).

## Market size and growth
- **Whole-economy logistics cost:** ₹24.01 lakh crore in 2023-24, **7.97% of GDP** (9.09% of non-services output) — the first government-backed estimate (NCAER for DPIIT, released Sep 2025), replacing the old 13–14% figure. Growth in logistics cost is slowing relative to output. [NCAER/DPIIT, Sep-25]
- **Cost by mode (₹ per tonne-km):** road 3.78, rail 1.96, waterways 2.30 (coastal 1.80, inland 3.30), air 72. Light trucks 11.03 vs 55-tonne trailers 1.51. Rail-based multimodal beats road beyond ~600 km. Fuel is 42.1% of road transport cost. [NCAER/DPIIT, Sep-25]
- **Logistics cost burden by firm size:** 16.9% of output for firms under ₹5 cr turnover vs 7.6% above ₹250 cr — why SMEs/D2C sellers pay more and value 3PL networks. [NCAER/DPIIT, Sep-25]
- **Express parcels:** 10–11 billion shipments in FY25, e-commerce >50% (4.8–5.5 bn); forecast **24–29 bn by FY30**, e-commerce shipments **+23–24% CAGR to 15–16 bn** (Redseer). Traditional courier ~2 bn, +7% CAGR. [Redseer via IBEF, Apr-25]
- **Online retail:** ~$80 bn GMV in FY26 (+21%), 320–340 million annual shoppers. **Quick commerce** ~$13–14 bn (~17% of online retail), +~120% in FY26; value commerce 12% (+~40%). Redseer forecasts QC at **25–30% of online retail GMV by FY30** and value commerce 15–18%. [Redseer, 6-May-26]
- **Warehousing (contract logistics proxy):** Grade A industrial & warehousing leasing ~22 mn sq ft in H1 CY2026 (+12% YoY); **3PL firms 30% of leasing**, e-commerce 16%; new supply ~25 mn sq ft (ahead of demand); Q2 leasing ~11 mn sq ft, −1% QoQ on West Asia disruption. [Colliers via CargoNet, Jul-26]
- **LPG (Aegis):** FY26 consumption **33.21 mt (+6%)**, fastest since FY19 (PPAC). India imports ~60% of LPG, ~90% of imports via the Strait of Hormuz. Jan–Jun 2026 consumption 14.74 mt (−8%); May 2026 2.13 mt, June 2.18 mt (−17% YoY). The US became the largest supplier in Mar–Jun 2026; online refill bookings back to 98%. [PPAC via BusinessToday, 2-Jul-26; search summary of PPAC data]
- **Ammonia (Aegis):** domestic capacity ~12–13 mt/yr vs demand ~15–17 mt, imports 3–5 mt/yr (industry estimates); CRISIL (in the Aegis Vopak DRHP, cited by Aegis's CFO) sees a ~3 mt supply–demand gap by 2029. [Search summary; Aegis Q4 FY26 call]

## Demand drivers
- E-commerce penetration: Shadowfax expects India's digital retail penetration to rise 120–150 bps a year from 7–8% (FY25) to 14–15% by FY30 (company view). [Shadowfax Q4 FY26 call]
- Mix shift to D2C/SME sellers (grow 40–45% a year at Delhivery; 2.7x at Shadowfax) and heavier parcels. [Delhivery Q1 FY27 call; Shadowfax Q1 FY27 call]
- Quick commerce and "vertical QC" (fashion, beauty, pet care) outsourcing dark stores and last-mile delivery; Shadowfax estimates QC outsourcing at 12–15% of platforms' volumes, possibly 20–25%. [Shadowfax Q1 FY27 call]
- Marketplaces moving volume back from in-house arms to 3PLs as labour and fuel costs rise (claimed by both Delhivery and Shadowfax; not independently verified). [Delhivery Q4 FY26 call; Shadowfax Q4 FY26 call]
- China+1 and MNC manufacturing in India for contract logistics (TVS SCS cautious: "wait to see"). [TVS SCS Q4 FY26 call]
- Energy: India's clean-cooking and industrial shift to gas (LPG, ammonia) vs policy push to piped natural gas (below).

## Policy
- **National Logistics Policy (Sep 2022) / PM Gati Shakti:** aims to cut logistics costs to global benchmarks and reach the top 25 of the World Bank LPI by 2030; 434 projects worth ₹11.17 lakh crore identified under Gati Shakti; LEADS 2025 state rankings released May 2026. [LEADS 2025 summaries; PIB NLP 3-year note]
- **GST 2.0 (56th GST Council, effective 22 Sep 2025):** GST on trucks, buses and commercial vehicles cut **28% → 18%**; third-party insurance for goods carriages 12% → 5%; goods-transport services can charge 5% (no input credit) or 18% (with credit). Lowers fleet acquisition cost ~10% for operators that cannot claim input credit. [Taxmann/LKS summaries, Sep-25]
- **Labour codes (in force 21 Nov 2025):** aggregators (incl. logistics and e-marketplaces) must contribute **1–2% of annual turnover** to a gig-worker social-security fund, capped at 5% of payouts to gig workers; minimum-wage revisions in Haryana, Karnataka, UP and Punjab in Q1 FY27 raised delivery costs (Shadowfax ~₹2–2.5 cr a month; Delhivery part of a ₹30–35 cr Q1 hit). [EY alert, Nov-25; Shadowfax and Delhivery Q1 FY27 calls]
- **LPG → PNG:** Natural Gas and Petroleum Products Distribution Order, 2026 (March 2026) makes households in PNG-covered areas switch within three months of notice; ~60 lakh LPG users live in PNG areas; India has ~16.2 million PNG vs ~330 million LPG users. Government also cut subsidised cylinders (Aegis Q4 call: 9 → 4). Negative at the margin for household LPG volumes; industrial/commercial LPG (Aegis's distribution focus) less affected. [BusinessToday, 26-Mar-26; Aegis Q4 FY26 call]
- **Fuel:** diesel raised ~₹7.5/litre in May 2026. [ICRA via Logistics Insider, 12-Jun-26]

## Cost drivers
- Fuel (42% of road transport cost; 50–60% of fleet operators' opex per ICRA) — pass-through clauses exist but lag by up to a month. [NCAER; ICRA Jun-26; Delhivery Q1 call]
- Labour: minimum wages, labour codes, gig-worker social security; tight labour supply during elections/heat. [Company calls]
- Warehouse rent (Ind AS 116 leases) — TVS SCS right-of-use depreciation ₹114 cr a quarter; Grade A supply now exceeds absorption, which should cap rent inflation. [TVS SCS IP Q1FY27; Colliers Jul-26]
- For Aegis: LPG and freight prices (VLGC vs medium carriers), war-risk insurance, port land allotments.

## Competitive structure and share
- **Express parcel (3PL):** three listed players after Delhivery bought Ecom Express (July 2025): Delhivery (~1 bn parcels FY26), Shadowfax (28–30% of 3PL e-commerce express by its estimate, up from ~17–18% a year earlier), Blue Dart (premium/air). Unlisted Xpressbees remains; captive arms (Amazon, Flipkart's Ekart, Meesho's Valmo) are the larger threat. Delhivery's CEO: "we don't see significant pricing pressure in the market any longer." [Delhivery Q4/Q1 calls; Shadowfax IP Q1FY27]
- **Customer concentration:** Shadowfax's top two customers ~64% of FY26 revenue; Delhivery's largest customer 19.2%. [Shadowfax AR FY26; Delhivery AR FY26]
- **PTL (B2B part-truckload):** Delhivery says it is No. 2 and fastest-growing (tonnage ~1.8x in 2.5 years); Shadowfax does not plan PTL. [Delhivery Q4 FY26 call; Shadowfax Q4 FY26 call]
- **Quick-commerce delivery:** Shadowfax claims >50% of outsourced QC delivery; Delhivery deliberately avoids dark stores and QC last-mile, calling it undifferentiated with no network effects. [Shadowfax Q1 call; Delhivery Q1 call]
- **Contract logistics:** fragmented, low-margin, many global and domestic players; TVS SCS with 100 Fortune 500 clients and 25 mn sq ft; typical ISCS EBITDA 8.5–10%, freight forwarding 2–5%. [TVS SCS calls]
- **LPG terminals:** Aegis Vopak terminals handled ~61% of India's LPG import volumes and ~23% of liquid import volumes (June 2024, per its DRHP). [Aegis Vopak DRHP via Angel One]

## Where profit sits
- Infrastructure with scarce port land and long contracts earns the most: Aegis Liquids ~62% EBITDA margin (FY25, CARE); LPG terminalling ~₹1,000/t EBITDA; gas distribution ₹4,000/t normally, ~₹7,000/t in FY26–Q1 FY27 (war premium). [CARE Mar-26; Aegis calls]
- Network parcel businesses earn thin margins on large revenue: Delhivery adjusted EBITDA 4.4% (FY26), 2.6% (Q1 FY27); Shadowfax 4.9%; statutory profit at Delhivery is smaller than its treasury income. [Company filings]
- Contract logistics: ~7% adjusted EBITDA but ~1% PBT at TVS SCS because leases absorb EBITDA. [TVS SCS IP Q1FY27]
- Service EBITDA "normative" bands: Express 16–18%, PTL 16–18% (Delhivery targets); corporate overheads ~9% of revenue are the gap to group profit. [Delhivery calls]

## Cycle position
- Volumes are strong (e-commerce, QC, India freight forwarding +84% at TVS SCS) but margins are being squeezed by the fuel and wage shock of Q1 FY27 — early-to-mid upcycle for volume, cost-shock phase for margins.
- ICRA (Jun-26) expects road-logistics **industry revenue +8–10% in FY27, mostly from higher freight rates**, and **operating margins down 150–200 bps** for its listed sample.
- Freight forwarding rates are elevated by West Asia routing — cyclical tailwind for TVS SCS's GFS that may reverse.
- LPG: supply shock easing (bookings 98% normal by mid-2026); the distribution-margin windfall is likely to normalise in FY28.

## Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecaster named)
- **Redseer:** e-commerce parcel shipments +23–24% CAGR to FY30; total express parcels 24–29 bn by FY30 (from 10–11 bn FY25) — implies roughly 18–22% a year across FY27–FY29. Quick commerce to 25–30% of online retail by FY30.
- **ICRA:** FY27 road logistics revenue +8–10%, margins −150–200 bps (fuel).
- **Colliers:** warehousing leasing +12% in H1 CY2026 with 3PLs the largest occupier; supply running ahead of absorption.
- **Company guidance (not independent):** Delhivery express volumes +20–30%, PTL +18–22% in FY27; Shadowfax revenue +38–40% FY27, adjusted EBITDA margin +100–120 bps a year to FY28; TVS SCS mid-teen revenue growth, 4% PBT by FY28; Aegis EPS +25% CAGR, 2 mt gas distribution by FY28–FY29, $5 bn capex by Dec-2030.
- **CRISIL (via Aegis):** ~3 mt ammonia supply gap by 2029 supports import-terminal demand.

## Three key findings
1. **Volume is not the problem; cost pass-through is.** Parcels (+55% Delhivery, +83% Shadowfax orders) and QC are booming, but fuel (+₹7.5/l diesel) and labour-code/minimum-wage costs cut margins in Q1 FY27; ICRA expects 150–200 bps margin compression for road logistics in FY27.
2. **The parcel market has consolidated to two scale 3PLs plus captive arms.** Pricing has stabilised, but concentration risk is extreme (Shadowfax ~64% from two clients; Delhivery's top client 19%) and marketplaces' in-house arms remain the swing factor.
3. **Energy-logistics profits are riding a war windfall and a policy headwind.** Aegis's distribution margin (~₹7,000/t vs ₹4,000/t normal) and India's LPG supply shock are temporary, while the 2026 LPG→PNG order and cylinder-subsidy cuts trim household LPG; industrial LPG, ammonia imports (~3 mt gap by 2029, CRISIL) and port terminals are the durable part.
