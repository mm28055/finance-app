# Shadowfax Technologies — deep research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Price ₹282, market cap ₹16,577 cr, P/E 98.0 on TTM profit ₹169 cr, ROCE 10.0%, ROE 10.4%, book value ₹30 [Screener, 27-Sep-26]. Listed 28 Jan 2026 (IPO ₹1,907 cr: fresh issue ₹1,000 cr + offer for sale ₹907 cr). Promoters hold 16.5%. [AR FY26 Directors' report; Screener]

## Business and segments
- Third-party last-mile logistics for e-commerce and quick commerce; 100% of last-mile delivery is crowdsourced gig riders paid per order (~2.9 lakh active partners a quarter); sort centres and hubs are leased and run by own staff; trucking is hired. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 FY27 revenue split: **Express** ₹997 cr (73%; +87% YoY, +8% QoQ; 19.8 crore orders, +95% YoY); **Hyperlocal** (quick commerce, food, on-demand) ₹272 cr (20%; +53% YoY, +17% QoQ; 4.9 crore orders); **Other logistics** (CriticaLog high-value, dark stores) ₹89 cr (7%; **−21.5% YoY**, +11% QoQ after winding down experiments). [IP Q1FY27 p.9]
- Unit revenue: Express ~₹50.4 per order in Q1 FY27 vs ~₹52.2 a year ago and ~₹50.3 in Q4 (weights falling); Hyperlocal ~₹55.5 per order vs ~₹53.9. D2C/SME pricing is 15–20% above enterprise rates. [IP Q1FY27, derived; Q4 call]
- Market share: 28–30% of 3PL e-commerce express volumes (company estimate), up from ~17–18% a year earlier and ~8% in FY22; claims >50% of outsourced quick-commerce delivery; D2C share 12%+. [IP Q1FY27 p.5; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Network (June 2026): 16,372 pin codes (716 added in Q1), 5,095 touch points, 53 lakh sq ft operating space (24 lakh sq ft mid-mile), 3,500+ trucks a day, 5,900 permanent and 20,600 contract staff. [IP Q1FY27 p.4, 18]
- New lines: Prime (same-day/next-day for D2C) revenue 2.7x YoY, 400+ D2C clients; Shadowfax 360 self-serve SME platform 1,200+ transacting sellers in 90 days; Prime Large (heavy parcels) ~₹75 cr ARR (+170%), 10,000 pin codes; 47 dark stores for vertical quick commerce (15 in Q4), ~10–12% of Other logistics revenue. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]

## Guidance history
- **Revenue growth FY27:** Q4 call 27–30% ("28%, 30% maximum" as a forward view; hyperlocal 45–50%). Q1 call **raised to 38–40%** on enterprise volume forecasts, Amazon Now and new D2C clients. Q1 delivered +65% YoY, so the full-year guide implies sharp slowing over Q2–Q4 as the base rises. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Margins:** Q4 call — adjusted EBITDA margin to rise **100–120 bps a year until FY28**, then 200–250 bps a year toward "early double-digit" steady state. Q1 call — "margin trajectory remains unchanged"; excess profit to be reinvested or passed to customers. Q1 delivered +204 bps YoY. (An analyst on the Q1 call quoted "100–200 bps"; management did not correct it — the written Q4 number is 100–120 bps.) [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Capex:** FY27 ₹180–190 cr, flat on FY26 (~₹185 cr, ~4.5% of revenue); Q1 ~₹60 cr (front-loaded before festive season), 77% into network and automation. [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.14]
- **Network:** ~17,000 pin codes by FY27-end, full national coverage by FY28 — on track (16,372). Prime Large 10,000 pin codes target for FY27 — **met in Q1, raised to 12,000**. Dark stores 100 in FY27 — 47 live plus 20 in progress by June. White goods go-live by Q2 FY27. [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- **Lost-shipment cost:** promised reduction — 6.1% → 5.5% delivered. [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ~₹185 cr; more than 60% of three-year capex into sortation centres and ~85% into sort centres plus last-mile hubs. Large automated "OneNCR" sort centre went live in H2 FY26 after six months of double running costs. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Operating space +35% between September 2025 and March 2026, and 45 → 53 lakh sq ft between December 2025 and June 2026. New nodes take 6–8 months to reach efficient truck loads. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Dark stores <10% of capex; store revenue ₹8–15 lakh a month, 3–4 months to profit; top stores at 20%+ gross margin. [Q4 call]
- Capital commitments only ₹31 cr at March 2026. [AR FY26 note 31]

## Moat evidence
- Share gains: 3PL express share ~28–30% vs 17–18% a year ago; CEO says volume is consolidating into "the two large 3PL networks" as weaker players exit. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Only national-scale 3PL offering same-day delivery; largest 3PL in reverse pickups and in quick-commerce delivery. [IP Q1FY27 p.6]
- Cost position: 100% per-order gig network lets one rider serve e-commerce, food and quick commerce on one app — management says no peer does this. Crowdsourced model keeps road costs variable. [Q4 call]
- AI: rider co-pilot resolves ~97% of ~16,000 daily queries; Vision AI at reverse pickup catches ~40% of bad pickups at ~35x lower inference cost than frontier models. [Q1 call]
- Pricing power is limited: volume rate cards pass scale benefits back to large clients; the company absorbed five days of diesel pass-through to protect relationships. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Extreme customer concentration:** two customers generated ₹2,709 cr — **~64% of FY26 revenue** (₹1,493 cr, ~60%, in FY25). Top 2–3 customers' share unchanged Q3→Q4. The two are not named; Flipkart group revenue was ₹494 cr for 1 Apr 2025–27 Jan 2026 (~12% of FY26 revenue), which implies the other large client alone is roughly half of revenue. Both kinds of anchor client (large marketplaces) also run in-house logistics. [AR FY26 note 25, note 33, note 35; Q4 call; derived]
- **Flipkart link:** Flipkart (Walmart) had significant influence until listing and was a large customer (₹451 cr revenue from Flipkart Internet and ₹43 cr from Instakart in FY26 up to 27 Jan). In July 2026, Flipkart, Eight Roads and IMM sought to sell up to 9.08% via block deals at a ₹197 floor after the lock-in ended; Flipkart's holding was to fall to ~2%. [AR FY26 note 33; News: Business Standard 24-Jul-26; BW Disrupt Jul-26]
- **Insourcing risk:** Delhivery's CEO says it caps exposure to "a single account" that drove Shadowfax's growth; if that marketplace insources, Shadowfax's volumes fall. [Delhivery Q4 FY26 call]
- **Auditor (B S R & Co., KPMG network):** unmodified opinion; CARO clean. Key audit matter: **revenue is manually computed** from system delivery reports and contract prices, raising misstatement risk. Audit trail: no service-organisation report for the holding company's accounting software for Jan–Mar 2026, so the auditor could not confirm the database-level audit trail; subsidiary's sales-operations software had no database-level audit trail. [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report]
- **Contingent liabilities** small: GST ₹2.15 cr (FY21–FY22). [AR FY26 note 31]
- **Related parties (minor):** four firms linked to directors' relatives (e.g. Dazzl Technologies, Ooru Health, Choudhary Sales, Pataakha Foods) — amounts small. KMP short-term pay ₹11.7 cr plus share-based pay ₹9.2 cr. [AR FY26 note 33 — table columns partly garbled]
- **Cost shocks:** minimum-wage hikes in four states cost ~₹2–2.5 cr a month; diesel pass-through lags. [Q1 call]
- **Thin margins:** at ~5% adjusted EBITDA, a 1% price cut by a top client wipes out a fifth of profit.

## My read
Shadowfax is the fastest-growing listed parcel company: revenue +65% with margins rising every quarter and a clean, cash-rich balance sheet after the IPO. But nearly two-thirds of revenue comes from two platforms that also run their own delivery arms, and one of them (Flipkart) has just sold down its stake. At about 98 times trailing earnings, the price needs growth of roughly 35–40% for several years and margins moving towards the promised double digits by FY30; guidance (100–120 bps a year to FY28) is slower than that. The view improves if D2C/SME and Amazon cut the top-two share below 50%; it worsens if a top client insources or presses price, or if Q2–Q3 growth falls well short of the raised 38–40%.
