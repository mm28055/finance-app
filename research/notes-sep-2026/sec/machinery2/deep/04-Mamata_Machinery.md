# Mamata Machinery (NSE: MAMATA) — deep research, 27 Sep 2026

Screener snapshot (consolidated), 27 Sep 2026: market cap ₹1,014 cr, price ₹412 (52-week 297–476), P/E 92.4 on TTM profit of ~₹9 cr, book value ₹75, ROCE 12.6%, ROE 9.6%, dividend yield 0.12%. [Screener]

Note: Mamata holds earnings calls only half-yearly (Nov 2025 for Q2/H1 FY26; 1 Jun 2026 for Q4/FY26). There was **no call for Q1 FY27**; only a results presentation (14 Aug 2026). The two stored transcripts are the Q4 FY26 and Q2 FY26 calls.

## Business and segments
- Ahmedabad (Sanand) maker of flexible-packaging machinery in three lines: **converting** (bag and pouch-making machines, ₹15 lakh–₹2.5 cr; legacy business, "top five globally" by brand per management), **packaging** (vertical/horizontal form-fill-seal machines for FMCG brand owners, ₹50 lakh–₹3.5 cr; the growth engine), and **co-extrusion** (blown-film plants up to 9 layers, ₹1.2–9 cr; lumpy). US subsidiary Mamata Enterprises Inc. (MEI; Florida and Illinois) sells to the US, Canada and Latin America. [Q4 call; Q2 call; AR FY26]
- 5,400+ machines installed in 80+ countries; exports ~63% of revenue (FY26 geographic split per AR: India 37%). Aftermarket (spares/service) only 5–7% of sales. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26; Q2 call]
- FY26 revenue by geography (₹ cr): India 83.3 (FY25 72.0, +16%); USA 56.5 (73.4, −23%); Canada 3.8 (9.9); Mexico 2.3 (11.3); rest of world including Europe, South Africa, Portugal, Kuwait ~87 cr. Management said "US business" (US+Canada+Mexico, machines only) fell close to 50%. [AR FY26 Note 56; Q4 call]
- Segment detail (machines only, FY26): packaging ~₹48–49 cr (−1%; domestic +55%, US ₹29.4 cr vs ₹36.8 cr); converting US machines ₹5.9 cr vs ₹31.6 cr (−81%), domestic converting +6%, rest-of-world +23%; co-extrusion slightly down (two 9-layer lines delivered, one ~₹9 cr due H2 FY27). [Q4 call]
- Asset-light: assembly-led plant; subsidiary MEI FY26 turnover ₹63.0 cr, PBT ₹4.5 cr, PAT ₹3.8 cr. [AR FY26 AOC-1]

[Q2 call]

[Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Asset-light: fixed assets ~₹65 cr, capex small (FY26 investing outflow ₹45 cr was mainly fixed deposits moved to non-current, per management). No expansion capex announced; the cash pile is described as a "war chest" for expansion and possible acquisitions. [Screener; Q2 call; Q4 call]
- Growth spending is mainly opex: exhibitions ₹10.2 cr in FY26 (4.5% of sales; ₹6.2 cr FY25), Interpack 2026 (May), sales hires in the US and packaging. [Q4 call; AR FY26]
- Technology: EU patent for cross-sealing device (Jan 2026); RecTech recyclable mono-material film launched (Plastindia/Blast India 2026) and certified 100% recyclable in the EU (Aug 2026), APR (USA) certification awaited; patent application for RecTech die/screw design (18 Aug 2026); patent granted for Quadra 600 4-up BOPP bag machine (21 Aug 2026). Management expects RecTech to take 12–18 months (trials of 6–9 months) before traction. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call; BSE filings Aug 2026]

## Moat evidence
- Converting brand recognised globally ("top five", 80+ countries); only Indian company across the full flexible-packaging chain (film extrusion → converting → form-fill-seal). [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Packaging: management says it is "the most expensive in India" yet wins on cost-to-output; only Indian maker offering pre-USFDA dairy 3A-standard machines; multi-machine order (4 delivered, 18 more) from a leading snacks brand. [Q4 call]
- IP: 5 national/international patents received, 3 pending (per presentation); RecTech could pull demand for all three machine lines if brands adopt it under EPR rules. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Limits: European buyers prefer European brands; Europe TAM ~$800 m but management targets only $2–3 m over 3–5 years; no meaningful recurring revenue. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Earnings collapse and missed guidance:** FY26 EBITDA fell from ₹55 cr to ₹19–22 cr; Q2 FY26 guidance (14% margin, ₹64 cr packaging) was missed; Q1 FY27 is loss-making. TTM profit ~₹9 cr supports a ₹1,014 cr market value. [Screener; Q2 call; Q4 call]
- **US concentration and trade policy:** US + Canada + Mexico fell from ~₹95 cr to ~₹63 cr in FY26; export share and gross margin move together. [AR FY26 Note 56]
- **Related parties and promoter-family pay:** the company buys from and pays several firms owned by relatives of directors/KMP — Mamata Airwings, Srikaram Digital Marketing (proprietor a KMP relative), Shree Maruti Travels, Maruti Engitech, Nirav Industries, Maruti Industries, Maruti Enterprise — with line items of roughly ₹0.1–2.6 cr each (the note's table is garbled in extraction, so exact amounts per firm need confirming; the largest expense line is ~₹2.2 cr in FY26 vs ~₹3.4 cr in FY25). Total director remuneration incl. commission ~₹5.4 cr in FY26 (₹6.1 cr FY25), roughly a third of FY26 profit. Sales to the US subsidiary were ₹19 cr (₹58 cr FY25). [AR FY26 related-party note; AOC-2]
- **Cash conversion:** FY26 CFO only ₹2 cr on ₹15 cr profit; inventory ~250 days. [Screener]
- Auditor's report FY26 (S H B A & Co. LLP, formerly Bathiya & Associates): unmodified; key audit matter on revenue recognition; no emphasis of matter found. Contingent liabilities negligible (tax ₹0.32 cr; guarantees ₹0.12 cr). Promoter holding steady at 62.45%, no pledge disclosed; shareholder count falling (65,272 → 54,773 since listing in Dec 2024). [AR FY26 auditor's report, Note 47; Screener]

## My read
Mamata has real engineering assets — a global converting brand, a fast-growing domestic packaging-machine business (+55% in FY26) and promising recyclable-film technology — and a clean, cash-rich balance sheet. But the numbers have gone the wrong way for five quarters: FY26 revenue −8%, EBITDA margin down to single digits, Q1 FY27 a loss, cash flow near zero, and guidance missed twice. At ~92x trailing earnings the price assumes margins snap back to ~20% on ~15% growth in FY27, which needs H2 FY27 deliveries to arrive on time and US demand to return. What would change the view: positive — H1 FY27 (results due after 1 Oct 2026) showing the ₹80 cr order book delivered and EBITDA back above 15%, first RecTech commercial orders; negative — another half-year of deferrals, a lower order book, or rising related-party spend.
