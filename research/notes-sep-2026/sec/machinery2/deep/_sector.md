# Industrial Machinery (machine tools / CNC, textile and packaging machinery) — sector research, 27 Sep 2026

Companies covered: Jyoti CNC Automation, LMW, Macpower CNC, Mamata Machinery.

## 1. Market size and growth — machine tools (CNC)
- Indian machine-tool **production** FY26: ~₹17,387 cr (US$2.0 bn), +19% YoY. **Consumption** (domestic demand): ~₹36,419 cr (US$4.1 bn), +15%. **Imports**: ₹21,628 cr (US$2.4 bn), +16% — about **59% of consumption**. **Exports**: ₹2,596 cr, +76%. [IMTMA via Modern Manufacturing India, Sep 2026]
- India ranks **4th in the world in consumption** but only **9th in production** (CECIMO World Machine Tool Outlook CY2025). [IMTMA via MMI, Sep 2026]
- Momentum was strongest early in FY26: Q1 FY26 consumption +26%, imports +40%, production +11% — i.e. imports grew fastest when domestic makers were capacity-constrained. [IMTMA via MMI, Q1 FY26 update]
- Company data points agree: Jyoti said imports reached 62% of consumption last year because "none of the Indian machine tool companies had sufficient capacity"; Jyoti booked 1,000+ machines in a single month in India in July 2026 (a first). [Jyoti Q1 FY27 call]
- Size by value tier: of ~32,000 turning machines made in India, ~12,000–14,000 were under ₹15 lakh; machines above ₹1 cr are only ~1,000–1,500 a year; above ₹2 cr ~50–100 (Macpower CMD's estimate). [Macpower Q1 FY27 call]

## 2. Imports from China / Taiwan / Japan
- FY26 import sources: **China 30%, Japan 21%, Germany 12%** (63% of imports together). Taiwan and Korea are the main sources of large (5 m+) double-column machines — India imported 300+ such machines last year at ₹3–5 cr each (Jyoti). [IMTMA via MMI, Sep 2026; Jyoti Q1 FY27 call]
- The imported 55–60% is mostly high-end (5-axis, large, high-precision) that India does not yet make in volume; Chinese entry-level imports are small (~1,000–1,200 units a year per Macpower). [Macpower Q4 FY26 call]
- Indian exports are small and concentrated: Russia 34%, Vietnam 9%, UAE 7% of FY26 exports — a sanctions/export-control exposure. [IMTMA via MMI, Sep 2026]
- Export controls are tightening: 5-axis simultaneous machines need dual-use end-user certificates; EU licences now take much longer (Jyoti's French unit Huron is under judicial investigation and ~₹100 cr of revenue awaits licences; Macpower sells only 1–2 five-axis machines a year because of "300-page compliance agreements"). CNC controllers (Fanuc, Siemens, Mitsubishi) are ~99% imported. [Jyoti Q1 FY27 call; AR FY26 Note 42; Macpower Q1 FY27 and Q4 FY26 calls]

## 3. Demand drivers
- **Defence and aerospace:** MoD budget 2026–27 ₹7.85 lakh cr; capital outlay +22%; modernisation ₹1.85 lakh cr (+24.6%), of which **₹1.39 lakh cr (75%) earmarked for domestic industry**; aircraft and aero-engines +31%. This is the richest pool for high-end CNC (Jyoti A&D = 37–39% of revenue and 38% of its ₹4,848 cr order book). [MP-IDSA, 13 Feb 2026; Jyoti IP Q1FY27]
- **Electronics (EMS) components:** Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme outlay raised from ₹22,919 cr to **₹40,000 cr** in Budget 2026–27; 106 applications approved by Aug 2026 (31 on 17 Aug 2026, ₹7,877 cr), committed capex ~₹69,548 cr. Machine-tool demand from EMS lags approvals: Jyoti's EMS order book (~13%) has been static for ~1.5 years awaiting clearances; Macpower avoids EMS bulk orders that demand one-year credit. [IMPRI, Aug 2026; Jyoti Q1 FY27 call; Macpower Q1 FY27 call]
- **Public capex and infrastructure:** Central capex ₹12.2 lakh cr for FY27; Budget 2026–27 also announced CPSE-run high-tech tool rooms at two locations and a Construction & Infrastructure Equipment scheme. Railways (Vande Bharat, wagons) are a new pull for large machines (Jyoti's NX double-column launch). [Multibagg summary of Budget 2026–27; ICRA FY2027 Outlook; Jyoti Q1 FY27 call]
- **Auto and general engineering / MSME:** job shops, dies and moulds and Tier-3/4 suppliers are the volume base (Macpower's core; LMW MTD ~56% auto). Replacement of machines bought in 2000–05 is starting. [Macpower Q1 FY27 call; LMW Q1 FY27 call; Jyoti Q1 FY27 call]
- **Capital-goods cycle:** ICRA keeps a **Positive** outlook on capital goods for FY2027; the order-book-to-operating-income ratio is ~1.4x (9M FY2026), showing large backlogs (partly slower execution). West Asia (14–20% of India's trade) is the key downside. [ICRA FY2027 Outlook, Apr 2026]

## 4. Policy
- **Capital subsidies drive domestic capacity:** Gujarat's Viksit Gujarat Industrial Policy 2026 gives 25% capital subsidy (35% in less-developed districts) plus 7% interest subsidy — Macpower's ₹50 cr expansion uses it; a separate Gujarat defence/aerospace land policy is still pending (Macpower's 60-acre plot). Jyoti expects ~25% central + ~25% state capital subsidy for an in-house CNC-controller plant under a central incentive scheme. [Macpower Q1 FY27 call; Jyoti Q1 FY27 call]
- **Machine-tool parks:** Tumakuru Integrated Machine Tools Park (Karnataka) — 530 acres, 145 acres allotted; Jyoti bought 20 acres there. [MHI page via web-search summary, not opened; Infomerics Apr-26 on Jyoti]
- **Incentive-scheme capex (PLI):** ICRA estimates ~₹2.5 trillion of the ~₹4.0 trillion capex expected under the PLI schemes will have been spent by March 2026 (65%); utilisation is high in mobiles/drones, lower in solar modules and batteries — i.e. the PLI capex wave is past its midpoint. [ICRA FY2027 Outlook]
- **Plastic packaging rules:** Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules 2026 (31 Mar 2026) set rising recycled-content targets — rigid packaging 30% (FY26) → 60% (FY29+); multilayer 5% → 10% from FY28; food packaging exempt where FSSAI prohibits; shortfalls can be carried forward three years. This supports recyclable mono-material films (Mamata's RecTech) but the food exemption and carry-forward slow the push. [Vajiram & Ravi summary of G.S.R. 237(E); Mamata Q4 FY26 call]

## 5. Textile machinery and the spinning cycle
- Indian **textile machinery demand ~₹32,397 cr in FY26; imports ~₹26,999 cr**; Indian makers hold **under 25% of the market by value** — imports dominate weaving and knitting, while spinning is where Indian makers (LMW) are strongest. [TMMA(I) Chairman at 66th AGM, 18 Sep 2026, via Textile Sphere India]
- Global: short-staple spindle shipments rose by 195,000 to 6.11 million in 2025; India's rotor (open-end) investment +12%; loom shipments −27.5%. [ITMF 2025 shipment statistics via Global Textile Times]
- **Spinners' finances are improving:** CRISIL expects cotton-yarn industry revenue **+9–11% in FY27** (flattish FY26), realisations +6–8%, volumes +2–4%, exports +12–14% (30–31% of revenue), operating margin **11–12% (+150–250 bps)**, cotton-yarn spread ₹108–110/kg despite 10–15% costlier cotton, gearing ~0.55–0.60x. India's effective capacity fell to ~40–44 m spindles after 10–12 m were scrapped. [CRISIL Ratings, 27 Jul 2026]
- **Cycle position:** early recovery from a two-year trough. LMW's textile unit runs at ~60% utilisation (50–55% at March), order book ₹3,200 cr (₹2,400 cr active), and management expects a *gradual* rise in spindle installations, helped by state subsidies (Gujarat, Odisha, MP) and FTAs; LMW describes an ~8-year cycle (last peak FY22–FY23). [LMW Q1 FY27 and Q4 FY26 calls]

## 6. Packaging machinery
- India packaging-machinery market ~US$2.0 bn (2025), forecast CAGR only ~3.9% to 2034 (IMARC); flexible-packaging market US$5.18 bn (2025) growing ~11.4% a year to 2034. (Research-firm estimates vary widely; IMARC figures taken from a web-search summary, page not opened — treat as indicative.) [IMARC via web search]
- Domestic FMCG capacity additions (snacks/namkeen growing ~12%) drive form-fill-seal demand; Mamata's domestic packaging sales grew 55% in FY26 while US sales fell. Converting machinery is export-heavy and was hit by US tariff uncertainty and, from March 2026, polymer-price spikes that stretched converters' working capital and delayed deliveries (Mamata Q1 FY27 revenue −6%, EBITDA negative). [Mamata Q2 FY26 & Q4 FY26 calls; Mamata IP Q1FY27]

## 7. Cost drivers
- Steel/castings, imported CNC controllers, ball screws and linear guides (mostly Japan/Taiwan/Germany) — ~50% of LMW's machine-tool material is imported; rupee weakness vs USD/EUR raised costs in FY27. [LMW Q4 FY26 & Q1 FY27 calls]
- West Asia conflict: input and logistics costs up ~3–3.5% (LMW); availability of gases (helium) disrupted; makers raised prices 2–3% (Jyoti, 1 May 2026) and 4–6% (Macpower, 1 Jun 2026). [LMW, Jyoti, Macpower calls]
- Labour codes (Nov 2025) added one-off provisions (LMW ~₹11.5 cr; Mamata ₹3.05 cr). [LMW Q4 FY26 call; Mamata Q4 FY26 call]
- Working capital is the real capital cost: inventory days — Jyoti 453, Macpower 259, Mamata 252, LMW 106. [Screener]

## 10. Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecaster named)
- **Machine-tool consumption:** IMTMA-linked projections put consumption near **₹59,444 cr by 2031** (via Ken Research) — about **10% a year** from ₹36,419 cr in FY26 (my arithmetic); Macpower's CMD cited IMTMA's Goa summit (May 2026) forecast of **₹54,000 cr by 2030** (~10% a year). Ken Research's own estimate: US$3.7 bn (2025) → US$6.9 bn (2031), ~11% CAGR. [Ken Research; Macpower Q4 FY26 call]
- **Domestic makers should grow faster than the market** (15–30%) as they add capacity and replace imports: Jyoti guides 25–30% FY27 growth (capacity 6,000 → 16,000); Macpower 30%+ (FY27) with a new plant from Q2 FY28. Risk: a wave of entry-level capacity (Jyoti +10,000; Hosur rival +10,000) could pressure prices by FY28–FY29; Jyoti's average price per machine is already drifting down (~₹34–35 lakh). [Jyoti & Macpower Q1 FY27 calls]
- **Capital goods:** ICRA Positive outlook FY2027 on public capex (₹12.2 lakh cr), power, data centres and defence; private capex "measured" outside electronics, semiconductors and EVs. [ICRA FY2027 Outlook]
- **Textile machinery:** CRISIL's FY27 cotton-yarn upturn (+9–11% revenue, margins 11–12%) supports a gradual recovery in spinning-machinery orders through FY27–FY28; a sharp upcycle like FY22–FY23 is not in management's base case. [CRISIL Jul 2026; LMW Q1 FY27 call]
- **Packaging machinery:** domestic FMCG demand healthy (Mamata guides 30–40% domestic packaging growth), exports uncertain (US tariffs, West Asia, polymer prices); recycled-content rules help from FY28 as targets step up. [Mamata Q4 FY26 call; PWM Rules 2026]
- **Key swing factors:** EU/US export-control regimes, ECMS approvals turning into machine orders, West Asia/oil (polymer and logistics costs), rupee, and whether imports keep ~60% share.
