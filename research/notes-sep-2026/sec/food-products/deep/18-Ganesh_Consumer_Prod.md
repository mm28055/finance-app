# Ganesh Consumer Products (GANESHCP, BSE 544528)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Standalone figures (Screener). Listed 29 Sep 2025 at an IPO price of ₹322.

The Q1 FY27 call (5 Aug 2026) and the Q3 FY26 call (5 Feb 2026) were used as the two calls. What management said about Q4 FY26 is taken from references back to it on the Q1 call.

## Business and segments
- Kolkata-based packaged-staples brand ("Ganesh", heritage from 1936), promoted by the Mimani family through Srivaru Agro Pvt Ltd. [IP Q1FY27] [AR FY26]
  - 44 product categories and 256 SKUs in three B2C buckets: Carrier (whole-wheat atta); Value-added (sooji, maida, besan, sattu, dalia); Emerging (spices, instant mixes, ethnic flours, soya chunks).
- B2C mix (Q1 FY27): atta 32%, value-added 59%, emerging 9%. Management describes value-added plus emerging as 68%. Atta was 34% in FY23. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- B2B is the sale of co-products and by-products such as bran, plus bulk flour. It fell 17.9% in Q1 FY27 and ~12% in Q3 FY26 as the company deliberately cut low-margin volumes. [Q1 call] [Q3 call]
- **Contract manufacturing:** the Hyderabad unit (312 t/day) does third-party job work for one large FMCG company, which is not named under a confidentiality clause. [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- Geography: West Bengal is ~93% of B2C (81% in FY24 on a different basis); Jharkhand 3%, Odisha 2%, rest of East 2%. The target is for states other than West Bengal to reach 18–20% of B2C in 2–3 years. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- Manufacturing: 8 plants with 1,478 t/day of capacity, including Hyderabad. [IP Q1FY27] [Q3 call]
  - Plants in Howrah (West Bengal), Agra and Varanasi (Uttar Pradesh), and Hyderabad.
  - The Amta plant (12 t/day, for snacks, sweets and soya chunks) started production in Q1 FY27.
  - Utilisation is ~55–60%.
- Distribution: 3.5 lakh+ general-trade outlets, 1,000+ distributors and 30 C&F (carrying-and-forwarding) agents. General trade runs on a cash-and-carry model. [IP Q1FY27]

## Guidance history
- **At IPO and on the Q3 FY26 call:** ₹1,000 cr revenue is a "medium-term aspiration". In Q3, management expected FY26 growth to be "single digit" (actual: +2.5%) and a "higher single digit" volume rise in Q4, citing January B2C volume +9%. Q4 revenue was down ~1% YoY. **Missed.** [Q3 call] [Screener]
- **Q4 FY26 call (as recalled on the Q1 call):** FY27 volume growth of 7–8%. Q1 revenue fell 7.1%, blamed on a heatwave, LPG shortages, elections and cheap wheat. **Behind; a view promised after H1.** [Q1 call]
- **Margin:** FY27 EBITDA of 9.8–10% (FY26 9.8%). Q1 came in at 11.2%. **Achievable.** [Q1 call]
- **Mix targets:** spices from 6–7% of revenue to 11–12% in three years; soya chunks 2–3% in 2–3 years; 300–400 new distributors; 5 lakh outlets in ~18 months (target set in Q3). [Q1 call] [Q3 call]
- **New categories:** ethnic snacks and packaged sweets launching in Q3 FY27 from Amta. Management would not give margins. [Q1 call]
- **IPO-funded Darjeeling plant** for sattu and besan (gram flour), ₹45 cr: only ₹2.46 cr spent by March 2026. Commissioning has slipped to "towards the end of financial year '27-'28", citing supply-chain and geopolitical reasons. **Delayed.** [AR FY26] [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- The IPO raised ₹130 cr of fresh issue (₹119.7 cr net). By Mar 2026: ₹60 cr went to repay debt, ₹2.46 cr to Darjeeling and ₹7.7 cr to general corporate purposes. ₹49.5 cr is unutilised and sits in fixed deposits. [AR FY26]
- Management says current capacity is enough for 2–3 years of demand. Agra atta and Amta came on stream with little capex. FY26 capex was ~₹44 cr (operating cash flow minus FCF). [Q3 call] [Screener]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net cash ₹17.2 cr at Jun 2026 (₹64.3 cr at Mar 2026; net debt ₹195 cr at Jun 2025, before the IPO). Borrowings ₹5.9 cr. The drop in cash reflects inventory built "during a favourable commodity cycle". [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- Cash conversion cycle: 43 days in Q1 FY27 (23 days in FY26, 61 days in Q1 FY26). Inventory days 53; receivable days ~6. [Q1 call]
- Operating cash flow (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 58 → -13 → 88 → 46 → 80. FCF: 10 → -38 → 63 → 28 → 36. [Screener]
- Dividend payout 47% in FY26 (₹2.50 a share). The ESOP trust bought 5.245 lakh shares on the market for ₹10 cr. [Screener] [AR FY26]
- **Rating:** ICRA A+ (Stable)/A1 on ₹213 cr of bank lines, **withdrawn on 11 Jun 2026** at the company's request after bank NOCs. ICRA said it had no information suggesting credit risk had changed. [ICRA Jun-26]
  - FY26 metrics per ICRA: OPBDIT margin 9.8%; total debt/OPBDIT 0.3x; interest cover 8.1x.

## Moat evidence
- **Regional brand leadership:** citing a Technopak report, the company says it is the largest wheat-products player in West Bengal and a top-2 player in besan and sattu. Nielsen shows a +1 point value share in Q1 even as the category shrank. [IP Q1FY27]
- **Resilience to competition:** Emami's entry into atta, maida and sooji in FY26 caused a price war. Ganesh held B2C roughly flat (+1% in Q3) and raised margins. Management's stated edges: in-house manufacturing close to its market (versus rivals using job work), faster turnaround, and a 70-broker sourcing network. [Q3 call]
- **Limits:** investors pushed back that the products have "no entry barrier". Growth is almost entirely within West Bengal, and FY26 volume growth stalled. The brand defends margins but has not yet shown it can expand beyond its home market. [Q3 call]

## Input costs
- Raw materials are 70–80% of cost: wheat, chana (gram) and spices, bought through brokers in UP, MP, Rajasthan, Punjab and Maharashtra. [Q3 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- Wheat and gram prices were 8–10% lower YoY in Q3 FY26, and soft again in Q1 FY27 ("benign wheat prices"). This helped margins through inventory buying but let unbranded mills undercut on price. [Q3 call] [Q1 call]
- The Gulf-crisis spike in LPG prices (used in sattu roasting) was absorbed rather than passed on. [Q1 call]
- GST: the company's categories stayed at 5% after the September 2025 GST rate cuts, so it got no demand boost from them. [Q3 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Growth stall after the IPO:** revenue has declined for two quarters, the stock trades at half its IPO price, and FII holding fell from 8.2% to 2.3% in three quarters. [Screener]
- **Use of IPO money:** only 5% of the Darjeeling allocation has been spent, the schedule has slipped about two years, and ₹49.5 cr is idle. [AR FY26] [Q1 call]
- **Promoter-entity loans before the IPO:** at Mar 2025 the company had lent ₹26.3 cr to Back Bone Sales Pvt Ltd and Srivaru Agro (the holding company), plus ₹12.6 cr of accrued interest. All of it was recovered in FY26. It was repaid, but it shows how the business was run before listing. [AR FY26]
- **Other related-party dealings:** rent to a promoter real-estate company, Purnaa Real Estate (OPC), ₹1.68 cr, plus rent to the MD and his father. MD pay ₹3 cr; total KMP pay ₹3.24 cr (~8% of PAT). [AR FY26]
- **Contingent liabilities:** income-tax demands under appeal ₹7.45 cr; GST ₹4.31 cr; EPCG duty saved ₹3.01 cr. The auditor gave no qualification; the key audit matter was the estimation of trade discounts. [AR FY26]
- **Concentration:** ~93% of B2C is in West Bengal, making it exposed to a single state's weather, elections and competitors. The job work depends on one unnamed FMCG client. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- **Credibility:** on the Q1 call, management said it did not recall an earlier B2B contract-manufacturing plan an analyst mentioned. This is minor but worth tracking. [Q1 call]
- Promoter holding rose to 65.65% (from 64.08%) in Jun 2026; no pledge reported. [Screener]

## What changed between the two calls
- Q3 FY26 (Feb 2026): Emami's price war was "settled"; January volumes +9%; debt-free with ~₹110 cr of cash; celebrity ambassador and new geographies planned.
- Q1 FY27 (Aug 2026): revenue down 7% on heatwave, LPG and election disruption. The margin was a record 11.2% and advertising rose to 3.1%.
- Cash fell to ₹23 cr as inventory was stocked. The Darjeeling plant slipped to late FY28.
- New in Q1: soya chunks and the timing of the snacks and sweets launches. Guidance was deferred to after H1, with only the EBITDA band (9.8–10%) committed.

## My read
Ganesh is a well-run regional staples brand with a clean balance sheet, a cash-and-carry distribution model, and margins improving from 8.4% to 11% through mix and procurement. But its first year as a listed company has been disappointing. Revenue barely grew, the IPO-funded plant is two years late with the money idle, and the stock has halved. Earlier loans to promoter companies were cleared only around the IPO. At ~14x earnings with a 3% dividend yield, the price reflects that. The view would change if H1 FY27 shows B2C volume back to +7–8% alongside spices and snacks scaling and growth outside West Bengal. Another flat year would make it a margin story in a no-growth staple.
