# KRBL (KRBL, ISIN INE001B01026)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Consolidated figures (Screener) unless marked.

## Business and segments
- India's largest branded basmati company ("India Gate"). It is integrated from seed and contract farming through milling (207 t/hour of milling capacity; 233 t/hour of grading and packing) to branded sales. There is a small power segment (biomass and wind) and by-products (bran oil, furfural). [CARE Aug-26] [IP Q1FY27]
- Revenue mix, Q1 FY27: domestic ₹1,221 cr (82%), exports ₹244 cr (16%), power ₹31 cr. [IP Q1FY27]
- FY26: domestic revenue ₹4,444 cr (+10%) and exports ₹1,555 cr (+6%). [Q4 call]
- Domestic brands and categories: [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
  - Branded basmati grew 9% in FY26.
  - Branded non-basmati/regional rice: ₹271 cr in FY26, +38%.
  - Masalas: annualised run-rate ₹9 cr at the end of Q1; target ₹25 cr by end-FY27.
  - Edible oil (Uplife): ₹12 cr in FY26.
  - Poha launched in 22 cities in Q1.
- Market share in packaged basmati (Nielsen, MAT Jun-26): general trade 37%, modern trade 38%, e-commerce 41% (about 10 points ahead of the next brand). Share in traditional trade fell ~2 points YoY in Q1. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- Distribution: ~3.3 lakh outlets and 850+ distributors, reaching 1.2 crore urban households. [IP Q1FY27]
- Exports go to 90+ countries. The Middle East takes close to three-quarters of Indian basmati exports. Saudi Arabia is served through wholesalers while KRBL looks for a new distributor, after a "wrong partner" set it back ~1.5 years. [Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- **Domestic volume growth ~10% a year** (Q4). Kept on Q1 and extended to "the upcoming 2 to 3 years". Q1 volume was flat to slightly down: an analyst cited +3%, and management spoke of "a bit of a degrowth" because bulk-pack buyers delayed purchases. **Kept, Q1 behind.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Q1 domestic realisation "+2–3%"** (Q4). Q1 delivered ~9% sequentially. **Beat.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Exports:** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
  - Q4 gave no number; the CMD said exports "will double up in next six months" after peace.
  - Q1: exports -50%, but management is "maintaining our guidance" of "meaningful export growth" for FY27, with recovery from Q2.
  - **Kept, but unquantified and back-end loaded.**
- **Margins (new on Q1):** FY27 gross margin ~30% and EBITDA 17–18%. Q1's 23.8% was called "clearly not sustainable". **New.** [Q1 call]
- **Regional rice +25% in FY27** on a ₹270 cr base. Q1 was +25%. **On track.** [Q1 call]
- **No further price increases in Q2**; prices will be revisited once the new crop arrives. [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- The capex needs are low: depreciation is ~₹91 cr a year. [Screener] [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
  - Fixed assets rose ~₹411 cr in FY26 (₹922 cr → ₹1,333 cr).
  - The purchases included a ~130-acre land parcel at Samalkha, Panipat. About 60 acres is intended for company warehousing and the rest is being "evaluated" for monetisation or development. The purchase price was not stated on the calls.
- A new regional-rice facility at Gangavathi (Karnataka) is due by the end of Q3 FY27. [Q1 call]
- Funding is entirely internal. Management describes real-estate spending as "treasury optimisation", which is a capital-allocation question for shareholders. [Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net bank debt (₹ cr): +376 (FY22), +95 (FY23), then net cash of 378 (FY24), 405 (FY25), 789 (FY26) and 1,841 (Jun-26) as inventory ran down. Borrowings ₹170 cr at Mar-26. [IP Q1FY27] [Screener]
- Inventory is the whole working-capital story: [Q4 call] [Q1 call] [Screener]
  - Mar-26: ₹3,714 cr (paddy 230,000 t; rice 427,000 t).
  - Jun-26: ₹2,944 cr (paddy 71,000 t; rice 389,000 t).
  - Inventory days: 425 (FY24) → 329 (FY26).
  - Management plans to buy more paddy in the Oct–Nov 2026 season, which will absorb cash.
- Operating cash flow (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 561 → -360 → 224 → 961 → 933. [Screener]
- Dividends: 450% (₹4.5 a share, ₹103 cr) for FY26, payout ~16%. No buyback, despite analysts asking. [Q4 call] [Screener]
- Ratings: ICRA AA (Stable)/A1+ (per company; latest ICRA action 12 Dec 2025). CARE withdrew its unused commercial-paper rating on 21 Aug 2026; CARE's summary shows FY26 gearing of 0.03x and interest cover of 108.8x. [IP Q1FY27] [CARE Aug-26]

## Moat evidence
- #1 in branded basmati in every channel (37–41% share), a premium export realisation over India's APEDA average, and India Gate's brand strength. [IP Q1FY27]
- Pricing power: ~9% branded price increase in Q1 FY27. Management says prices are now at "the higher extent of the bandwidth". [Q1 call]
- Sourcing and ageing: the largest contract-farming network, and the balance sheet to hold 15–24-month aged rice. This mattered in a 2025 crop split by flood damage in Punjab. [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- Weak spots: share loss in traditional trade (-2 points YoY in Q1) and competitive pressure in modern trade. [Q1 call]

## Input costs
- Paddy (Pusa 1121/1509) is the main cost and is bought once a season (Oct–Feb). [Q1 call]
  - 1509 paddy opened at ~₹2,500–3,200/quintal in Oct 2025, and 1121 reached ₹4,300–4,700/quintal by Feb 2026.
  - KRBL's cheaper inventory plus price increases produced the Q1 margin spike.
- The 2026 crop is at risk: the northwest monsoon was deficient (all-India rainfall -11% as of 5 Aug; reservoirs -27%). Basmati is largely canal- and tubewell-irrigated, so the hit shows up in cost more than acreage. Management expects "firm not soft" prices into the new crop. [Q1 call]
- Freight: West Coast–Middle East container rates rose from ~USD 500 to as much as USD 5,000 at the peak of the Hormuz disruption. Gulf buyers are now accepting freight pass-through as benchmark prices were reset. [Q1 call]
- Forex: about 80% of export sales are hedged when booked. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Qualified audit opinion (FY26, standalone and consolidated).** [AR FY26] [Q1 call]
  - The Enforcement Directorate is investigating JMD Anoop Kumar Gupta under the money-laundering law (PMLA) over the AgustaWestland case.
  - The ED has filed a criminal complaint with allegations against KRBL, its subsidiary KRBL DMCC and the JMD.
  - An independent firm found "no conclusive evidence to ascertain impact", but the auditor cannot comment on adjustments until the investigation ends.
  - Land at Dhuri worth ₹15.32 cr was attached; the tribunal ordered a ₹11.13 cr deposit refunded.
  - Management declines all questions on the matter.
- **Contingent liabilities (standalone):** indirect tax ₹50.67 cr, ED matter ₹15.32 cr, other ₹40.39 cr (₹252.51 cr a year earlier), direct tax ₹1.12 cr. [AR FY26]
- **Geography concentration:** the Middle East is the core export market, and Q1 exports halved. The US now carries an 18% tariff. In March 2026 KRBL temporarily stopped production for its West Asia export basket; the press put West Asia at ~30% of KRBL revenue. [Q1 call] [News: Business Upturn, 4 Mar 2026, updated 14 Jul 2026]
- **Capital allocation:** ₹1,841 cr of cash and investments, a low payout, no buyback, and a real-estate purchase described as "treasury optimisation". [Q4 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Governance churn:** two independent directors resigned (Apr and Sep 2025) and the Company Secretary resigned in FY26. [AR FY26]
- **Earnings quality:** Q1 other income of ₹64 cr included ₹18 cr of higher investment gains and ₹9.5 cr of forex gains. The CFO said Q1 margins are not sustainable. [Q1 call]
- Promoter holding steady at 60.17% with 0% pledged. [Screener] [AR FY26]

## What changed between the two calls
- Exports went from "a matter of days" until settlement (Q4) to a 50% fall in Q1. Hormuz is now partially reopening, and management expects Q2 exports to be "much better".
- Domestic pricing: a ~9% increase delivered; no more increases until the new crop.
- New explicit FY27 margin guidance (EBITDA 17–18%).
- Monsoon risk moved from "early to say" to a measured deficit in the basmati belt.
- The Saudi subsidiary plan was deferred in favour of a distributor.

## My read
The core franchise is sound: dominant brand share, strong pricing in Q1, a big net-cash balance, and low capex needs. FY26 recovered from the FY25 trough (OPM 15% vs 12%). FY27 is likely to show a spike-then-normalise pattern (guided EBITDA 17–18%), with a Gulf export recovery as the main swing factor. The persistent discount (~12x P/E, ~1.6x book) reflects the qualified audit opinion tied to the ED case, idle cash and the real-estate diversion. A clean resolution of the ED matter or a buyback or special dividend would change the view. Further share loss in traditional trade, or a costly 2026 paddy crop combined with weak exports, would argue against it.
