# ADF Foods (ADFFOODS, ISIN INE982B01027)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Consolidated figures (Screener) unless marked.

## Business and segments
- An exporter of ethnic Indian packaged foods, dating back to 1932 as American Dry Fruits. It makes ready-to-eat curries, frozen breads and snacks, pickles, chutneys, pastes and spices. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call] [Q4 call] [AR FY26]
  - Plants: Nadiad (Gujarat), Nashik and the new Surat greenfield frozen-food plant (Phase 1 from March 2026).
  - A US distribution arm, ADF Foods (USA) and Vibrant Foods New Jersey, runs warehouses in the US. Subsidiaries are also in the UK and Australia, and one is being set up in Ireland.
- Brands:
  - **Ashoka:** the flagship brand for the South Asian diaspora, sold in 60+ countries; ~₹308 cr in FY26 and a 20%+ five-year CAGR. [Q4 call] [IP Q1FY27]
  - **Truly Indian:** the mainstream US brand, in ~3,000 US stores (Costco, Whole Foods, Safeway-Albertsons, Raley's) and Germany; ~USD 4–4.5 mn a year. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
  - **Soul:** a domestic "better-for-you" brand, which management says has been slow. [Q1 call]
  - Own brands are ~70–75% of sales; B2B/private label ~20–25%. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Segments, Q1 FY27: processed foods ₹144 cr (+28.5%, segment EBITDA margin 21.6%); agency distribution in the US ₹23.2 cr (11.5% margin). [Q1 call]
- Geography: North America is the core market. GCC countries were "a little under 15%" of revenue until the Mar–Apr 2026 shipping disruption. [Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- ~₹124 cr was spent over FY25–FY26 on Surat plus brownfield work at Nadiad and Nashik. Surat Phases 1 and 2 total just over ₹100 cr. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- FY27 capex is ~₹20–25 cr: a pizza-base line in Phase 2 (Q3 FY27) plus ₹15–20 cr of debottlenecking. Depreciation will rise modestly. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Existing plants ran at ~70–75% utilisation in FY26. A second US warehouse is planned because freezer space is over 100% utilised. [Q4 call]

## Moat evidence
- A long-established diaspora brand (Ashoka) with a 20%+ five-year CAGR. It is winning mainstream US listings: Costco repeats, 60% of Truly Indian growth comes from same-store sales, and it won the NEXTY and Freezies awards in 2026. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Owning manufacturing plus US distribution (its own warehouses and agency business) gives last-mile control. The company received AEO-T3, the highest level of Indian customs' trusted-exporter status, in May 2026. [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- Margins are steady at 17–20% EBITDA over FY22–FY26 despite freight shocks, which points to pricing power in niche categories. [Screener]
- Truly Indian competes with local US makers of Indian-style food, not with Indian brands. [Q1 call]

## Input costs
- Inputs are vegetables, pulses, flour, oils, spices and packaging. A procurement programme buys ahead when prices are low. [Q1 call]
- Freight is the swing cost: ~3–4% of revenue in Mar-26 and ~3% in Q1 FY27. It is now mostly passed on. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Currency: a weaker rupee helps, as it is an exporter. [Q4 call]
- US tariffs: a 10% tariff under Section 122 still applied at the time of the Q1 call, and the 2025 reciprocal tariffs were largely absorbed by ADF. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Freight and shipping:** in June 2026 about 30% of goods ready for shipment could not be shipped for lack of vessels. The GCC business fell 80–85% in Mar–Apr. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- **PLI ends after FY27:** ~₹16 cr a year (~2% of margin). Management expects lower Ashoka advertising as a share of sales to offset it. [Q1 call]
- **US litigation:** Ascot Valley Foods (a former co-packer) sued ADF USA in 2022. The US court granted a permanent injunction and money award in ADF USA's favour (~USD 2.8 mn, June 2026). Ascot appealed to the Second Circuit in Aug 2026, with no stay granted. Recovery is uncertain. [BSE filing 14-Aug-26] [AR FY26] [Q1 call]
- **Contingent liabilities are modest:** service tax ₹4.64 cr, income tax ₹4.41 cr, GST ₹0.55 cr, guarantees ₹1.23 cr. No auditor qualification; the key audit matter was impairment of indefinite-life intangibles (brands). [AR FY26]
- **Pledge:** at Mar-25, 17 lakh promoter-family shares were pledged (Parul Thakkar 16.5 lakh; Sumer Thakkar 0.5 lakh). The FY26 status was not found in the notes read. [AR FY26]
- **Management pay:** the MD's commission is up to 5% of consolidated PBT, payable partly by overseas subsidiaries. [AR FY26]
- **Management exit:** the GM-Accounts resigned in Aug 2026 (per the announcement title listed on Screener; the filing was not opened). [Screener]
- **Promoter holding** is only 36.14%. DIIs hold 21.3%. [Screener]

## My read
ADF is a steadily improving branded exporter: four straight quarters of 20%+ growth, a strong Ashoka franchise, early mainstream traction for Truly Indian, and new Surat capacity that more than doubles headroom. The near-term issues are external: shipping availability, freight costs and the end of the PLI subsidy. The Q1 underlying margin of ~14% sits well below the high-teens guidance. At ~31x earnings the valuation assumes the margin recovers. It needs freight pass-through to work in Q2–Q3 and Surat to ramp as planned. The view improves if H2 margins return to 17%+ excluding one-offs and the Ascot award is collected. It worsens if the Gulf stays shut or the post-PLI margin gap isn't closed.
