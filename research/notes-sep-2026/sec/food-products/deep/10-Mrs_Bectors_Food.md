# Mrs Bectors Food Specialities (BECTORFOOD, ISIN INE495P01012)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Consolidated figures (Screener) unless marked.

## Business and segments
- Biscuits under the "Mrs. Bector's Cremica" brand, and breads, buns and bakery under "English Oven" and the clean-label brand "Naturbaked". It is a preferred supplier of buns to India's largest QSR (quick-service restaurant) chains. [IP Q1FY27]
- Segment revenue, Q1 FY27: [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
  - **Biscuits** (domestic, exports, CSD canteen stores): ₹325 cr, +15.7%. Domestic biscuits grew "high single digits".
  - **Bakery** (retail plus institutional/QSR): ₹215 cr, +17.5%.
- FY26 segment revenue: biscuits ₹1,235 cr (+7%) and bakery ₹769 cr (+14%). [IP Q1FY27]
- Exports are ~35% of revenue, to 70+ countries. The US, South America and Africa are the three largest markets at roughly 20–25% of exports each. Walmart added a peanut-butter cracker SKU. [Q1 call]
- Geography is concentrated: ~85% of biscuit general-trade revenue and ~80% of English Oven revenue come from North India. [Q1 call]
  - Biscuit strategy focuses on a 400 km radius around the Rajpura (Punjab) plant.
  - English Oven is expanding to Kolkata, Hyderabad and Mumbai (Khopoli), with Bengaluru next.
- Capacity (FY26): [IP Q1FY27] [AR FY26]
  - Biscuits 185,880 t: 60% average and 69% peak utilisation.
  - Bakery 107,467 t (+8,541 t planned): 75% average and 82% peak.
  - New plants: Dhar, MP (biscuits, 21,000 TPA, May 2025); Kolkata bakery (Jan 2026); Khopoli bakery (36,464 TPA, run by subsidiary Bakebest Foods, Mar 2026).
- Contract manufacturing is only ~1–1.5% of revenue. [Q1 call]

## Moat evidence
- English Oven is a leading premium bread brand in North India, with high-teens growth for four years. Q-commerce share in Kolkata is already "high single digit". [Q4 call]
- The QSR bun supply relationship is long-standing, and QSR demand turned up in Q1 FY27 after two years of weakness. [Q1 call]
- In biscuits, Cremica has leading variants (Coconut, Bourbon #2, Digestive). North-India weighted availability is ~35% (Nielsen), targeted at 40–45% by 2030. Competition "continues to be very intense" in the North. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Pricing power is modest: 2–2.5% price increases in consumer brands during Q1. In FY26, when Parle and ITC stayed at ₹4.50 price points after the GST cut while Bectors moved to ₹5, it lost share. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Input costs
- Main inputs are wheat flour, palm oil, sugar, milk powder and packaging (crude-linked), plus fuel and freight. The company hedges some wheat, sugar, milk powder and edible oil when it sees fit. [Q4 call]
- Inflation hit ~1.5% of costs in Q1 and was larger in Q2, driven by packaging, palm oil, fuel and minimum-wage increases in UP and Karnataka. QSR wheat is locked in for the year. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Recovery comes through price increases (lagged by a quarter), Project IMPACT and mix. Management expects Q2 to be only partly covered and Q3 fully covered. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Margin guidance keeps slipping:** ~14% was talked about for H1 FY27 earlier; it is now a Q4 FY27 exit target. EBITDA has been stuck at 12–13% for six quarters. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Flat profit:** PAT of ₹140–143 cr for FY24–FY26 despite 26% revenue growth, as depreciation rose from ₹61 cr to ₹90 cr. [Screener]
- **Regulatory:** the CCPA (consumer protection regulator) fined the company ₹0.10 million on 9 Jun 2026 over "100% Atta"/"100% Whole Wheat" bread labels; the company has appealed. The DFIA export incentive is lost. Dominican Republic duty and regulatory pressure hit exports in FY26. [AR FY26] [Q4 call]
- **Contingent liabilities (consolidated):** income tax ₹3.29 cr, sales tax ₹0.51 cr, stamp duty ₹0.91 cr; the GST matter (₹6.51 cr) was cleared in FY26. No auditor qualification or emphasis of matter; the key audit matter was revenue recognition. [AR FY26]
- **Related parties and pay:** promoter-family executive pay in FY26 was Anoop Bector ₹8.33 cr, Ishaan Bector ₹3.62 cr and Suvir Bector ₹3.61 cr, about 11% of PAT. Rajni Bector is paid by the subsidiary Bakebest. [AR FY26]
- **Management change:** the CFO role moved from Parveen Kumar Goel (whole-time director) to Anshul Rastogi from 7 Aug 2026. [AR FY26] [Q1 call]
- **Promoter holding** fell below 50% (51.19% → 49.03%) with the Sep 2024 equity raise; no pledge noted. FIIs cut from 16.0% to 8.1% over FY26–Q1 FY27; DIIs rose to 26.0%. [Screener]

## What changed between the two calls
- Q4 was a soft quarter (+8.9% revenue) blamed on Navratri timing, US tariffs and GST price-point confusion. Q1 was the strongest growth in six quarters (+16%), with exports showing "high double-digit" growth and QSR recovering.
- Margin: "as close as possible to 14%" became a Q4 FY27 exit target of 14%, plus a new FY30 target of 15–16% EBITDA and ₹4,000 cr revenue.
- A new CFO was appointed.
- Q2 was flagged as the peak inflation quarter.

## My read
Revenue momentum re-accelerated in Q1 FY27 (+16%, bakery +17.5%, exports recovering). The new plants give headroom to roughly ₹3,500 cr of revenue with a clean balance sheet. But the story has been "margin to 14% next year" for several quarters while profit has been flat for three years. Costs from new plants, marketing and inflation keep absorbing the operating leverage, and the domestic biscuit business is still sub-scale outside Punjab against aggressive competitors. At ~45x earnings the stock needs the Q4 FY27 14% exit to happen. The view improves if the Q2 margin holds at 13% or better despite inflation and Khopoli ramps up. It worsens if domestic biscuit growth stays in single digits.
