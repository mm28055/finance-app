# Manorama Industries (MANORAMA, ISIN INE00VM01036)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Consolidated figures (Screener) for FY25 onward and for quarters; standalone for FY22–FY24, because the consolidated history begins only in FY25 when the overseas subsidiaries were set up.

## Business and segments
- Makes specialty fats and butters from tree-borne seeds: cocoa butter equivalents (CBE, brand MILCOA), stearin and olein fractions, and exotic butters (sal, mango, shea, kokum and others). They go into chocolate, confectionery, bakery fillings and cosmetics. [IP Q1FY27] [CARE Mar-26]
- Product mix: in FY26, CBE plus stearin was 71.4% of revenue. CBE alone was ~30% of revenue, up from ~10% two years earlier. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- FY26 value-added volumes: stearin ~8,000 t and CBE ~7,500 t. Fractionation utilisation was ~85% before debottlenecking, and ~80% in Q1 FY27. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Customers are global confectionery and cosmetic majors, including Ferrero, Mondelez, Mars, Hershey, Nestlé, Barry Callebaut, AAK, Fuji Oil, Olam, L'Oréal and The Body Shop. New suppliers typically take 3–4 years to get approved. The top 5 customers were 28% of income in FY25 (34% in FY24). [IP Q1FY27] [CARE Mar-26]
- Geography: exports were 60% of Q1 FY27 revenue (domestic 40%). Exports were 73% in FY25 and 56% in 9M FY26. The company sells to 39+ countries, and no single export country is above 25% of sales. Middle East exposure is only 2–3% of revenue. [IP Q1FY27] [CARE Mar-26] [Q4 call]
- Sourcing:
  - India: sal, mango and other seeds, collected by tribal communities in Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra.
  - West Africa: shea nuts and butter, bought through 8–9 wholly owned subsidiaries (Nigeria, Ghana, Ivory Coast, Benin, Togo, Burkina Faso and Chad, plus Taang Kaam Industries SA in Burkina Faso).
  - Imported seeds and butters are ~50–60% of the raw material. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call] [AR FY26]
- Capacity: [IP Q1FY27] [Q4 call]
  - Fractionation: 47,500 TPA. Plant 1 is 15,000 TPA; plant 2 is 32,500 TPA after a 7,500 TPA debottleneck in Mar 2026. It goes to ~52,000 TPA in Q3 FY27.
  - Seed milling 60,000 TPA, solvent extraction 90,000 TPA and refinery 45,000 TPA.
- Brazil: a tolling arrangement with Dekel (Manorama Latin America LTDA). It pays a per-tonne processing fee, with no profit share. First commercial batch done; ramp-up expected over 2–4 quarters. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- A ₹460 cr programme over 2–3 years: [Q4 call] [Q1 call] [CARE Mar-26] [IP Q1FY27]
  - **Raipur (forward integration, ~₹330–340 cr):** a third solvent fractionation plant (75,000 TPA), a CBA/ESOS unit (enzymatic CBE, ~75,000 TPA) and a 90,000 TPA refinery. Asset turn targeted above 6x.
  - **Burkina Faso (~₹120–130 cr):** a 90,000 TPA shea processing plant. It should cut seed freight and improve fat yield; payback is guided at ~3 years. Land of ~10 ha is acquired and regulatory approvals are pending.
- Timing: commissioning targeted for Q3 FY28, with full benefit in FY29. [Q1 call]
- Spend: ~₹52 cr by Q4 FY26; ~₹70 cr cumulative by mid-Aug 2026 (~₹20 cr in Q1). FY27 capex is guided at ₹225–250 cr. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Funding: internal accruals plus a ₹500 cr QIP completed in July 2026 at ₹1,470 a share. The company says no further equity is needed. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- Subsidiary funding continues: on 22 Sep 2026 the board approved up to ₹10 cr of share capital each for the Benin, Togo and Ivory Coast subsidiaries. [BSE filing 22-Sep-26]

## Moat evidence
- **Hard-to-copy sourcing** from thousands of forest villages in India and West Africa. CARE calls this "a competitive edge over its competitors". [CARE Mar-26]
- **Customer lock-in:** 3–4 year approval cycles with global majors, and products formulated to each customer's recipe. The company says its prices have been "largely stable" despite commodity volatility. [CARE Mar-26] [Q1 call]
- **Regulation-driven demand:** the EU and India (FSSAI) allow up to 5% CBE in chocolate still sold as "chocolate". High cocoa prices pushed CBE adoption, although the company says cocoa prices bear "no relation" to its CBE prices. [IP Q1FY27] [Q4 call]
- **Operating leverage:** FY26 fixed-asset turnover was 7.4x, and annual cash profit (₹259 cr) exceeded gross block (₹250 cr). The margin moved from 16% (FY24) to 27% (FY26) as CBE went from 10% to 30% of the mix. [IP Q1FY27] [Q4 call]
- Market share is not disclosed.

## Input costs
- Raw materials are ~45–55% of revenue: sal seed (a government MSP of ₹20/kg applies), mango kernel, shea nuts and butter, and palm mid fraction (PMF, imported). Seeds are bought once a season, so the company carries a year of inventory and its cost is locked for the season. [Q1 call] [CARE Mar-26]
- Contracts with customers run 9–12 months and renew on a rolling basis. Cost changes are built into the next contract price with a lag. [Q1 call]
- Freight and containers were the main source of cost pressure in Q1 (other expenses +16%). The ₹120 cr Burkina Faso plant is meant to cut seed freight, since it will ship butter rather than whole seed. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Supply risk: Nigeria imposed a temporary ban on shea-nut exports. The company says sourcing from 22 shea-growing countries limits the impact. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Customs matter and a late disclosure.** [News: ScanX, 10–11 Aug 2026] [Q1 call]
  - A Customs SIIB (Indore) summons dated 23 Jul 2026 concerned PMF imports under the India–UAE CEPA (whether they were eligible for the trade-deal benefit).
  - The company paid ₹20.64 cr voluntarily on 29 Jul 2026 and raised a debit note on the overseas supplier on 10 Aug 2026.
  - It admitted breaching SEBI's 24-hour disclosure rule.
  - Recovery from the supplier is not assured. How the cost was accounted for in Q1 is not disclosed in the material read.
- **Related-party purchases from a promoter entity.** Manorama Africa Limited (Ghana) is 100% owned by Vice-Chairperson Vinita Saraf. [AR FY26] [Q4 call]
  - Purchases from it were ₹191.8 cr in FY26 and ₹229.7 cr in FY25, roughly 20–25% of raw-material purchases in FY26 (~60% previously, per an analyst on the Q4 call).
  - A ₹200 cr omnibus approval for FY27 is sought at the AGM.
  - The company says the purchases are at arm's length.
- **Promoter pay** rose sharply: Ashish Saraf ₹2.85 cr (₹1.80 cr in FY25) and Vinita Saraf ₹1.55 cr (₹0.97 cr). [AR FY26]
- **Disclosure quality:** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
  - The Q4 FY26 results were re-filed after the "wrong file" was uploaded.
  - Press releases have shown only standalone numbers.
  - Per-tonne data is not given.
  - IR answers on guidance were inconsistent: 85–90% utilisation in the deck, 80% on the call.
- **Overseas subsidiaries** are still loss-making (₹13.5 cr in FY26), and capital keeps flowing into them. [Q4 call] [BSE filing 22-Sep-26]
- **Working capital and forex** (~340-day cash cycle; MTM swings), plus regulatory risk: a change in CBE limits, sal-seed MSP or forest-sourcing rules. [CARE Mar-26]
- **Contingent liabilities are small:** claims of ₹0.81 cr, GST ₹0.19 cr, income tax ₹0.35 cr. The auditor gave no qualification or emphasis of matter and reported no key audit matters on the consolidated statements. [AR FY26]
- **Promoter holding** fell from 57.26% to 54.42% in Dec 2024, and was 54.32% at Jun-26 before the July 2026 QIP, which dilutes it further. No promoter pledge was found in the documents read. [Screener]

## My read
The business is clearly improving. Revenue grew fivefold from FY22 to FY26, margins stepped up to ~26–27% as CBE's share rose, and FY26 was the first year of strong operating cash flow. The QIP now funds a ₹460 cr capex that could roughly double fractionation capacity by FY29. The concerns are about governance and disclosure more than economics: ~₹190 cr a year of purchases from a promoter-owned African trader, a late-disclosed ₹20.6 cr customs payment, a re-filed result, and evasive guidance. At ~46x trailing earnings and ~16x book, execution has to be close to perfect. The view improves if the subsidiary losses turn positive, the customs amount is recovered, and the FY28 plants commission on time. It worsens if gross margin stays below 45% or if related-party sourcing rises again.
