# Vintage Coffee & Beverages (VINCOFE, ISIN INE498Q01014)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Consolidated figures (Screener) unless marked. The wholly owned subsidiaries Vintage Coffee Pvt Ltd and Delecto Foods were merged into the parent from 21 Jul 2026 (NCLT order).

## Business and segments
- Hyderabad-based B2B maker of instant coffee (spray-dried and agglomerated) for global brand owners, mostly on a cost-plus basis. It also makes chicory through Delecto (2,000 TPA; revenue ₹40–46 cr a year). [Q1 call]
- Capacity: 6,500 TPA until FY26, then 11,000 TPA after a 4,500 TPA brownfield expansion in Mar 2026 funded from internal accruals. Q1 FY27 production was 2,402 t (full utilisation after 15 days of maintenance) and sales 1,856 t. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Next phase: a 5,500 TPA freeze-dried coffee (FDC) plant in Telangana's food-processing zone. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
  - Capex ₹550 cr, of which ~₹150 cr has been spent.
  - Trials by June 2027 and commercial production from Q2 FY28.
  - A second 5,500 TPA line (~₹370–400 cr) is planned for FY29–30.
- Customers: ~19–20 customers, ~90% of them brand owners and 10% traders. The top 5–6 are 45–50% of revenue. Customer retention is ~98%, and blends are developed in-house for each customer. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Geography (Q1 FY27): West Africa ~30%, Russia/CIS ~22%, Southeast Asia ~20%, Central America ~15%, Europe ~10%, India ~5%. There is little Middle East exposure. [Q1 call]
- Pack mix: 45% bulk and 55% consumer packs in Q1 FY27 (FY26 moved from ~35% to ~53% packs). Packing capacity is 5,000 TPA. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- The brownfield expansion to 11,000 TPA is done (FY26 fixed assets rose from ₹151 cr to ₹302 cr). [Screener] [Q1 call]
- FDC Phase 1: ₹550 cr (₹150 cr spent; ₹25 cr in Q1), funded by the ECB (₹300 cr), equity already raised and internal accruals. Land has been allotted by the Telangana government and advances paid to equipment suppliers. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- FDC Phase 2: ₹370–400 cr, likely through an ECB. Management said "we may have to go for ECB only". [Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹124 cr (Mar-26). Equity was raised repeatedly: [Screener] [AR FY26]
  - Several preferential issues and warrant conversions in FY25.
  - A preferential allotment of 2.0 crore shares in FY26; share capital went from ₹126 cr to ₹146 cr and reserves from ₹177 cr to ₹424 cr.
  - The FY26 AGM (30 Sep 2026) seeks approval for 42 lakh convertible warrants to promoters at ₹164 each (~₹69 cr).
- Operating cash flow (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: -27 → 11 → -36 → -23 → 15. FCF has been negative every year except FY23 (FY26: -₹142 cr). [Screener]
- Working capital: the cash conversion cycle fell from 921 days (FY22) to 178 days (FY26); debtor days are 88. Management guides 120–125 working-capital days. [Screener] [Q1 call]

- Credit rating: none listed on Screener, and none found.

## Moat evidence
- Customer-specific blends with ~98% retention. Management says customers can't easily move a blend to a competitor. [Q1 call]
- The cost-plus pricing model shields margins from coffee-bean prices. Beans are bought back-to-back against orders, with 40–45 days of stock. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- EBITDA/kg of ₹157–175 is above a larger peer's ~₹137/kg, according to an analyst on the Q1 call; management attributes this to pack and geography mix. [Q1 call]
- Scale is small: 11,000 TPA against a ~1 million t global instant-coffee market, and the planned FDC is ~2.2% of the ~250,000 t freeze-dried market. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]

## Input costs
- Green coffee (Robusta, priced off London) is ~80% domestic and 20% imported. Prices are "more or less stable" at USD 3,500–3,800/t, with a healthy Brazil crop against weather risk. [Q1 call]
- Packaging (tins, aluminium, glass) cost more in Q4–Q1 because of the Gulf conflict; the increase has been passed on. [Q4 call]
- Pricing is reset quarterly with customers, so bean-price moves pass through with a lag of up to a quarter. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Promoter pledge and dilution:** [Screener] [AR FY26]
  - Promoters had pledged 26.0% of their holding (per Screener).
  - Promoter holding fell from 47.6% (Mar-24) to 34.65% (Jun-26) as repeated preferential issues went to non-promoters.
  - New promoter warrants are proposed.
- **Use of proceeds:** the AGM seeks to ratify a change in how ₹12.05 cr of preferential-issue money was used (from subsidiary capex to buying FDC land). [AR FY26]
- **Execution and funding:** the ₹550 cr FDC plant is ~2x FY26 net worth, funded by a foreign-currency ECB, and Phase 2 needs another ~₹400 cr. Cash flow has been negative through the growth phase. [Q4 call] [Screener]
- **Customer concentration:** the top 5–6 customers are 45–50% of revenue. The markets carry political and payment risk (West Africa 30%, Russia/CIS 22%). [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Disclosure:** no investor presentation for Q4 FY26 or Q1 FY27 (analysts asked for it). The document Screener labels as the FY26 annual report is actually the FY2024-25 report; the FY26 report was filed separately on 7 Sep 2026. [Q1 call] [AR FY26]
- **Auditor and contingencies:** no key audit matters and no qualification. Contingent liabilities are negligible (income-tax demands of ~₹0.16 cr in total). [AR FY26]

## My read
Vintage has scaled fast: revenue grew 15x from FY22 to FY26 with stable ~18% margins and ~₹157/kg EBITDA. The newly doubled capacity is running full, so FY27 revenue of ~₹850–900 cr looks likely. The next leg depends on a ₹550 cr freeze-dried plant (and possibly a second) funded by foreign-currency debt and repeated equity issues. Cash flow has been negative for years, promoters own only ~35% with part pledged, and customers are concentrated in West Africa and the CIS. The view improves with positive FY27 operating cash flow, on-time FDC trials in mid-2027, and a release of the promoter pledge. It worsens with delays or cost overruns at the FDC plant, or further dilution.
