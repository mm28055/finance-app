# Hindustan Foods (HNDFDS, ISIN INE254N01026)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Consolidated figures (Screener) unless marked. FY25 and Q1 FY26 figures were restated for two group mergers (see Risks).

## Business and segments
- India's largest diversified FMCG contract manufacturer (a "Vanity Case Group company"). It has 42 factories in 12 states and five business units: [IP Q1FY27] [AR FY26]
  - Home and personal care (HPC): 11 plants, ~3 lakh t a year.
  - Food and beverages: 12 plants. It aims to be India's "single largest independent bottler" by end-FY27 (not tied to any one brand).
  - Ice cream: 5 plants, plus cone and stick making.
  - Healthcare/OTC pharma: 2 plants, including the ex-Reckitt Baddi site.
  - Footwear: 12 plants, 72 lakh pairs a year.
- **Two operating models:** [IP Q1FY27]
  - "Dedicated" plants, where one customer takes at least 75% of capacity, on take-or-pay contracts averaging 8+ years: gross block ₹1,123 cr.
  - "Shared" multi-customer plants: gross block ₹622 cr.
  - Q1 FY27 split: revenue 79% dedicated / 21% shared; EBITDA 87% / 13%. The shared side has been dragged down by footwear.
- **Customers:** the company does not name them, but its history shows plants and acquisitions linked to Hindustan Unilever, Reckitt and PepsiCo. Management says it can be "the only manufacturer in the country" for some customer categories. [AR FY26] [Q1 call]
- **Footwear:** ~₹500 cr of FY26 sales. Guided to ₹700–800 cr in FY27, with the order book full from Q2. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Exports:** Dr. Scholl's products go to the UK, EU, Australia, New Zealand and Japan. A US personal-care order is developed but on hold because of tariffs. [Q1 call]

  - Operating profit: 81 → 86 → 95 → 100 → 100.
  - OPM steady at 8–9%.
  - Net profit: 32 → 35 → 39 → 42 → 43.
- Q1 FY27: total income ₹1,207 cr (+18%); EBITDA ₹106 cr (+26%); PAT ₹42.8 cr (+33%, a record). This came despite a ~₹6 cr footwear hit (materials plus a ~30% Haryana minimum-wage increase). [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- Annual revenue (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 2,040 → 2,598 → 2,755 → 3,639 → 4,251. [Screener] [Q4 call]
  - OPM: 6% → 7% → 8% → 8% → 9%.
  - Net profit: 45 → 71 → 93 → 115 → 149.
  - FY26 EBITDA was ₹377 cr (+20%).
- Returns: reported ROCE 14.1%, ROE 14.6%. The company's "adjusted" ROCE for FY26 is 18.9%. It excludes ₹508 cr of underused or not-yet-commissioned assets and uses the price paid for acquisitions rather than book value (Baddi: ~₹220 cr difference). [Screener] [Q4 call]
- Valuation: market cap ₹7,421 cr at ₹612; P/E 45.9; P/B ~6.3; no dividend. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- **FY27 PAT of ₹200–220 cr** (+34–48%), given in Q3/Q4 and reaffirmed on Q1. Q1 PAT was ₹42.8 cr, so the rest of the year needs ~₹52–59 cr a quarter; ice cream is strongest in Q4 and Q1. The CEO said it was "too early" to say whether results would land at the low end. **Kept; back-end loaded.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Newly signed projects:** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
  - Q4: ~₹150 cr signed for FY27.
  - Q1: ₹340 cr signed (₹190 cr more), plus ₹150 cr carried over from FY26, so more than ₹500 cr of capacity to be commercialised in FY27. A pipeline of about ₹1,000 cr was disclosed for the first time.
  - **Raised.**
- **Footwear:** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
  - Q4 said it had broken even in Q2 FY26 and was "PAT positive"; cost pass-through was expected "within the next month".
  - Q1 took a ~₹6 cr hit, with a return to profit promised "in coming months" and H2 capacity 80–90% booked.
  - **Slipped.**
- **Switch to "net" revenue** (recognising only the conversion fee, because of GST duty inversion): Q4 had planned Q1–Q2, then moved it to Q2–Q3 FY27. This will cut reported sales but not profit. **Delayed.** [Q4 call]
- **Financing:** debt-to-equity of about 1:1 at most, and growth without outside equity. The ROCE threshold per project is 18%, and each business unit is targeted at 20% EBITDA growth. **Kept.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex was ~₹500 cr of projects (more than ₹700 cr including acquisitions). Gross block including CWIP was ~₹1,800 cr at Mar-26, and a gross block of ~₹2,150–2,346 cr is expected by Mar-27. [Q4 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- FY27 projects: [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
  - Food and beverages ₹210 cr: Greek yogurt in Goa (₹60 cr, Q3), bottled water and juice in Aurangabad (₹70 cr, Q4), South India water (₹50 cr), a Coimbatore brownfield expansion (₹50 cr), and a greenfield beverage plant (₹50 cr).
  - Ice cream ₹80 cr (Panipat phase 2, Q4). The Panipat phase 1 plant (₹225 cr, ~20,000 t) was commissioned in April 2026.
  - HPC ₹50 cr (Lucknow detergent bar/liquid, Q4), plus Silvassa liquid detergent (₹40 cr, Q2).
  - Healthcare: Ayurveda capacity at Baddi (₹25 cr, Q3).
- Funding is roughly 1:1 debt and internal accruals. [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings (₹ cr): 350 (FY22) → 516 → 727 → 896 → 1,088 (FY26). Net debt to equity was 0.84x at Mar-26 and 0.88x at Jun-26; cash ₹67 cr. [Screener] [IP Q1FY27]
- Operating cash flow is weak relative to profit: [Screener] [Q4 call]
  - OCF (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 36 → 99 → 87 → 118 → 100. CFO/EBITDA was only 40% in FY26.
  - FCF has been negative every year: -104 → -65 → -46 → -118 → -225.
  - Management called FY26 cash flows "less than satisfactory". Causes: the GST duty inversion (food moved to 5% GST while inputs carry higher rates, from Sep 2025) and inventory stocking during the Gulf crisis.
- Inventory days rose from 56 (FY22) to 131 (FY26); the cash conversion cycle went from 5 to 68 days. [Screener]
- No dividends or buybacks; all cash is reinvested. [Screener]
- Rating: India Ratings, latest listed action 24 Oct 2025. The rationale page could not be retrieved as text, so the rating level is not confirmed here.

## Moat evidence
- Scale and breadth: 42 plants, contracts averaging 8+ years, and take-or-pay dedicated plants where "ROCE is protected irrespective of the capacity utilization". [IP Q1FY27]
- Switching costs: customised plants, regulatory approvals (the Baddi site holds US FDA and Russian approvals) and a record of taking over brand-owner plants (Reckitt, HUL-linked). [Q4 call] [AR FY26]
- Pass-through: except for footwear, contracts pass raw-material, packaging and wage increases straight to customers. [Q1 call]
- Pricing power is limited by design: dedicated plants earn a negotiated 16–22% pre-tax ROE. The MD said a 10% PAT margin on a like-for-like basis would be surprising for a contract manufacturer. [Q4 call]

## Input costs
- Most material costs are passed through, so gross margin is not the key metric. EBITDA per unit of capital is. [Q1 call]
- Footwear is the exception. Prices are fixed 6–12 months ahead for each season and ~50% of materials are imported. In Q1 FY27, polymer prices rose 50–60% and freight 4x. Recovery depends on negotiations for the coming season. [Q1 call]
- Labour: the Haryana minimum wage rose ~30% (₹3 cr hit in Q1). Footwear employs ~5,000 people, about 35–40% of Q1 employee cost. [Q1 call]
- The GST inversion is a working-capital cost rather than a margin cost. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Group restructuring (related parties):** [AR FY26]
  - Under an NCLT-approved scheme (order 25 Feb 2026, operative 31 Mar 2026), Vanity Case India Pvt Ltd (the promoter holding company) was amalgamated into HFL, with 46,458,145 new shares allotted to VCIPL's shareholders.
  - The Nashik manufacturing unit of Avalon Cosmetics Pvt Ltd, an "entity under common control", was demerged into HFL for 1,680,939 shares.
  - FY25 figures were restated. Promoter holding moved from 61.31% to 61.84% (Jun-26).
- **Adjusted metrics:** analysts said the company's capital-employed and ROCE slides cannot be reconciled with the reported balance sheet. Management relies on "adjusted" ROCE. [Q4 call]
- **Customer concentration** is not disclosed. Revenue is heavily dedicated to a few large brand owners, and management avoids segment and customer data because it would be a "proxy" for customers. [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Footwear execution:** a two-year turnaround, still loss-making in Q1. [Q1 call]
- **Operational:** a flood at the Silvassa plant in July 2026 (about 10 feet of water; insured; full restart by end-August). A machine from China was stuck on an Iranian ship for 2.5 months. [Q1 call]
- **Leverage creep** and negative FCF: borrowings roughly tripled over FY22–FY26. [Screener]
- **Board changes:** non-executive director Nikhil Vora (Sixth Sense Ventures) resigned on 23 Sep 2026. Chairman Shashi Kalathil moves from independent to non-independent director after completing two independent terms. [BSE filing 23-Sep-26]
- **Auditor:** no qualification or emphasis of matter. The key audit matter was revenue recognition. Contingent liabilities are small: income-tax demand ₹2.78 cr, bank guarantees ₹1.91 cr, letters of credit ₹0.87 cr. [AR FY26]

## What changed between the two calls
- New projects signed rose from ₹150 cr to ₹340 cr (₹500 cr+ including the carry-over), and a ₹1,000 cr pipeline was disclosed.
- The footwear hit was quantified (~₹6 cr in Q1) and the fix pushed to H2. The Q4 "PAT positive" claim was reversed for Q1.
- The Silvassa flood was new; so was the Greek yogurt entry.
- Tone on HPC demand improved ("definite yes, more bullish than 3 months ago").
- The Q1 deck was redesigned with dedicated vs shared splits, after analysts complained about disclosure.

## My read
Profits are compounding steadily: seven straight quarters of year-on-year PAT growth on Screener data and record quarterly profit, with a visible project pipeline and pass-through contracts that protect margins. Against that, the balance sheet absorbs the growth: FCF has been negative every year, debt has tripled since FY22, working-capital days are rising, and returns are presented on an "adjusted" basis that analysts find hard to reconcile. At ~46x earnings the market is already paying for the ₹200–220 cr FY27 PAT target. The view improves if FY27 operating cash flow converts above 70% of EBITDA and footwear turns profitable in H2. A guidance cut or a further build-up of debt without matching cash flow would argue the other way.
