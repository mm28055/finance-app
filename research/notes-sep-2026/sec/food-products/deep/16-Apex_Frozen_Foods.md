# Apex Frozen Foods (APEX, ISIN INE346W01013)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Standalone figures (Screener) unless marked.

## Business and segments
- Kakinada (Andhra Pradesh) processor and exporter of farmed L. vannamei shrimp, with its own SPF (specific-pathogen-free) shrimp-seed hatchery. [IP Q1FY27]
  - Processing capacity 34,240 MTPA in two plants; the Gollaprolu/G. Ragampeta plant is 20,000 MT. Capacity by product: ready-to-cook (RTC) 15,000 MTPA and ready-to-eat (RTE) 10,000 MTPA.
  - Cold storage ~3,500 MT.
- Utilisation is low: ~30% in FY26 (10,286 t sold) and 38% in Q1 FY27. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Customers are food companies, retail chains, club stores and distributors in the US, EU and UK. Customer names and concentration are not disclosed. [IP Q1FY27]
- Geography: [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
  - Q1 FY27: US 70% of shrimp sales (54% a year earlier); EU and UK 25% (39%).
  - FY26: non-US markets were ~52%, the first time non-US sales were the majority; the EU was ~50% of volume. New markets: Japan (started), Russia (approval, end-Q2/Q3), Australia (awaiting customer audit).
- RTE share of volume: 16% in Q1 FY27 (15% a year earlier; 12% of FY26 revenue). It earns at least USD 0.50/kg more than RTC. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- **FY27 volume:** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
  - Q4: "originally envisaged… growth by almost 30%", with a plan of "around 14,000 metric tons".
  - Q1: management said "we have not given any" 14,000 t guidance and now says ~12,000 t (+17%). Q1 was -13% on April labour shortages and shipping disruptions.
  - Medium term: 14,000–15,000 t in FY28–29.
  - **Cut (and disputed).**
- **Margins:** "sustain" FY26 margins (Q4). Q1 was much higher (12.7%). Q1 guidance was "stable", with freight costs doubled and farm-gate prices rising as headwinds. **Beat, with caveats.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **RTE share** of at least 18–20% of FY27 volume (Q1; 16% in Q1). **New.** [Q1 call]
- **Capacity utilisation** of 35–40% or more through FY27. **Kept.** [Q1 call]
- **US tariff refund:** "no clarity" on whether or when. Management says the tariffs were largely borne by customers. **None expected.** [Q1 call]
- **US anti-dumping/countervailing duties:** anti-dumping duty (ADD) currently 3.4% (was 1.35%), with the annual review due in September. The countervailing duty (CVD) of 5.77% has a review expected in December 2026, and Apex hopes for a cut. [Q1 call]
  - In the final results of the 2024–25 ADD review, published 4 Sep 2026, Apex was a non-selected company at 5.53%. [News: Federal Register, 4 Sep 2026]

## Capex and capacity
- No expansion capex is needed: capacity is ~3x current volumes. Maintenance capex runs at ₹2–8 cr a year. The company says the growth constraints are raw-material supply, labour and shipping. [Screener] [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings were cut from ₹107 cr (FY24) to ₹6 cr (FY26); net debt to equity was -0.02x. Cash ₹18.3 cr plus fixed deposits. [Screener] [Q4 call]
- Operating cash flow (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 36 → 121 → 11 → 54 → 96. FCF: 25 → 100 → -1 → 46 → 90. [Screener]
- Inventory days are 105 and the cash conversion cycle is 129 days; export receipts take 70–90 days. About 30–32% of export receivables are hedged. [Screener] [Q4 call]

- Rating: CRISIL (latest action 1 Aug 2025); the rationale could not be retrieved (blocked). The company has little debt.

## Moat evidence
- An integrated hatchery (SPF seed) plus processing; EU approval for both plants. [IP Q1FY27]
- Pricing power is weak: the business is a commodity spread (realisation minus farm-gate cost). Margins moved from 3.1% (FY25) to 12.7% (Q1 FY27) mainly on the rupee and shrimp prices. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- Diversification away from the US (EU ~50% of FY26 volume) and a rising RTE mix are the main levers. The India–UK trade agreement took effect on 15 Jul 2026 and an India–EU agreement is expected around end-2026, though Indian consignments still face 50% testing at EU/UK ports. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Input costs
- Raw material is farmed shrimp bought at the farm gate (~₹327/kg average in FY26; ₹340–348 in Q4–Q1). Supply depends on AP farmers' crops, disease and feed costs. Avanti Feeds reported a spike in feed costs in Q1 FY27, which could squeeze farmer economics. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Ocean freight has "more than doubled" since Q4 FY26 because of the Gulf-war disruption. There were also container shortages. [Q1 call]
- The rupee is a key lever: exports are 100% of sales and only ~30% is hedged. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **US trade exposure:** 70% of Q1 sales went to the US, which carries ADD, CVD and a 10% tariff. Reviews in Sep and Dec 2026 could raise or cut duties, with retroactive true-ups either way. [Q1 call]
- **Guidance credibility:** the 14,000 t figure from the Q4 call was disowned on Q1. Volumes have stagnated at ~10,000–10,500 t for three years. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Concentration and disclosure:** customer concentration is not disclosed. The MD is also the CFO. Calls are handled by the MD and one accounts manager. [Q1 call]
- **Auditor:** no qualification. The key audit matter was the valuation of finished-goods inventory. Disputed dues are small: GST input credits ~₹1.96 cr and customs ~₹0.14 cr. No material related-party transactions beyond KMP pay. [AR FY26]
- Promoter holding is 72.62% with no pledge. [Screener] [AR FY26]

## What changed between the two calls
- Q4 was optimistic: US volumes coming back, ~30% volume growth, 14,000 t.
- Q1 showed volume down 13%, but margin up sharply on a ₹930/kg realisation. Volume guidance was lowered to ~12,000 t.
- US share jumped to 70% as EU shipments slipped on testing and clearance delays.
- Freight costs doubled; farm-gate prices started rising.
- Japan business started; Russia approval is pending.

## My read
Apex has turned from a weak FY25 (OPM 3%) to its best quarter in years (12.7%), and the balance sheet is now debt-free. But the improvement comes mostly from external factors (a weaker rupee, firm US shrimp prices, stable farm-gate costs), and volumes remain stuck at ~30–38% of capacity. The volume guidance was walked back within one quarter. With freight doubling, farm-gate prices rising and US duty reviews due, Q1 looks like a cyclical high rather than a new base. The view improves if volume actually reaches 12,000 t or more with RTE at 20%, and if the December CVD review comes out favourably. It worsens if the rupee strengthens or US duties rise.
