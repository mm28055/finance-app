## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Fractal Analytics

## Executive Summary
Fractal Analytics exists to bridge the gap between frontier AI capabilities and complex enterprise workflows. As Fortune 500 companies rush to integrate generative and agentic AI, they face a "last mile" problem: foundational models lack enterprise context, data security, and domain-specific guardrails. Fractal solves this by building secure, proprietary AI foundations and autonomous agents that optimize pricing, logistics, and customer personalization. 

Fractal operates as a definitive **Category Leader** in the pure-play enterprise AI consulting and platform space. While traditional IT service providers treat AI as a bolt-on capability, Fractal was entirely purpose-built for it. By transitioning clients from clunky legacy software to responsive, agentic AI ecosystems, Fractal is successfully shifting its business mix from traditional analytics consulting to high-margin, IP-led platform revenues.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** Fractal sells enterprise AI transformation. This includes bespoke AI systems architecture, agentic software platforms (like *Cogentiq* for healthcare/CPG and *Asper.ai* for revenue growth), and AI workforce capability building (*Analytics Vidhya*). 
- **Who pays them?** Fortune 500 enterprises, global public sector entities (e.g., BMC public hospitals), and top-tier pharmaceutical and CPG companies. 
- **How do they make money?** The firm is migrating from a traditional time-and-materials services model toward high-leverage, output-based contracts and Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) subscriptions. Their IP-led platforms, such as Asper, operate on a SaaS-like subscription model, while core AI engineering engagements act as sticky, multi-year transformation contracts.

### Segment & Geographic Mix
Fractal is geographically diversified but relies heavily on Western enterprise IT budgets.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
- **The Moat is Durable (High Switching Costs):** Fractal is not merely providing staff augmentation; they are building the ontological and contextual layers of their clients' AI ecosystems. Once an enterprise integrates an agentic platform like *Cogentiq* to manage its core datasets and workflows, ripping and replacing it becomes operationally paralyzing. 
- **Evidence:** Exceptional Net Revenue Retention (NRR) of 117% and a Net Promoter Score (NPS) of 77. Furthermore, 85% of revenue is derived from categorized "Must-Win Clients," and the firm boasts zero churn among its existing client base in the latest quarter.
- **The Bear Case:** The moat relies on the complexity of AI implementation. If frontier model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Databricks) successfully release "out-of-the-box" enterprise solutions that seamlessly integrate with legacy data lakes, Fractal’s specialized middleware and consulting layers could face severe commoditization.

## Growth Vectors
- **Platformization & SaaS Economics:** The next $100M will come from scaling proprietary IP. *Asper.ai* ARR is already growing at nearly 60% YoY, and *Cogentiq* is rapidly landing enterprise transformation deals across CPG and BFSI. This structural shift is expanding gross margins (currently 46%) through software-like operating leverage.
- **Healthcare Domination:** Fractal’s clinical reasoning AI (Vaidya/Cogentiq) is winning massive single-program contracts, including a recent top-5 US pharma deal and municipal pilot programs in India. A successful city-wide public hospital rollout in Mumbai would serve as a highly lucrative global blueprint.
- **Management Execution:** Management is walking the talk on margin expansion. SG&A as a percentage of revenue is consistently shrinking (down to 24.3% in Q1 FY27), driving adjusted EBITDA margins up 189 bps to roughly 17%, even while aggressively expensing R&D (4.5% of revenue). 

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Forensic Red Flags (CFO Departure):** Chief Financial Officer Ashwath Bhat announced his departure in July 2026, just months after the company's IPO and debt repayment. While framed as for "personal reasons" after a 5.5-year tenure, a post-IPO CFO exit requires heightened diligence from the committee to ensure no underlying friction regarding financial reporting or growth expectations.
- **Business Risks:** 
  - *Client Concentration:* Despite deepening accounts, the top 10 clients still account for roughly 52% of total revenue.
  - *Associate Losses:* *Qure.ai* continues to drag on the consolidated bottom line (INR 23 crore loss share in Q1 FY27), despite top-line hyper-growth of 160%.
  - *TMT Volatility:* The 22% YoY decline in the tech vertical highlights vulnerability to sudden CAPEX/OPEX freezes by major tech clients.
- **Thesis Derailers:** A broader enterprise pullback on AI spending. If companies find that initial GenAI pilots fail to deliver ROI, the anticipated multi-trillion-dollar "Software 2.0" super-cycle could stall, leaving Fractal with an overbuilt R&D apparatus.

## Investment Thesis
- **The Bull Case:** Fractal is one of the few pure-play, scale vehicles for investors to capture the enterprise AI super-cycle. Unburdened by legacy IT service debt, the company is debt-free (post-IPO retirement), highly profitable, and expanding margins through operating leverage and IP-led platform sales. With a 117% NRR and strategic partnerships dictating enterprise deal flow, Fractal is positioned to compound earnings exponentially as AI usage shifts from pilot phases to core budgets.
- **The Bear Case:** The company is fundamentally a premium IT services firm masquerading as an AI software vendor. The heavy reliance on a concentrated client base, combined with the alarming 22% drop in the TMT segment and a sudden post-IPO CFO transition, suggests earnings volatility. If frontier AI models abstract away the need for bespoke data architecture, pricing power will collapse. Stay away until the new CFO is seated and TMT segment stabilization is proven in Q2 FY27.
