## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# TAC Security (Tac Infosec)

## Executive Summary
TAC Security exists to democratize enterprise-grade vulnerability management, providing an affordable, AI-native platform alternative to legacy cybersecurity solutions. The company solves the critical problem of fragmented cyber risk assessment by unifying AppSec, compliance, and Web3 security into a single pane of glass. Currently a **Category Challenger** with a disruptive land-and-expand distribution model, TAC is rapidly encroaching on Category Leadership by volume, having aggressively amassed over 10,000 global clients and breaking into the top five vulnerability management firms globally by client count. By deliberately shifting away from manpower-heavy IT services to a highly scalable SaaS architecture, the company has achieved elite unit economics. Propelled by accelerating "Frontier AI" tailwinds, it currently pairs hyperscaler-like top-line growth (+97% YoY in Q1 FY27) with top-decile profitability (48.8% EBITDA, 40.2% PAT), presenting a compelling, albeit high-volume/low-ticket, cybersecurity platform emerging from India to serve the global ecosystem.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** TAC sells automated, AI-driven cybersecurity software and audits. The core product is **ESOF** (Enterprise Security in One Framework) for AppSec and vulnerability management. Adjacent products include **Sokify.ai** (automated compliance) and **CyberScope** (Web3/smart contract auditing). 
- **Who pays them?** The client base spans Fortune 10 enterprises, global tech giants (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, SAP), government infrastructure departments, and high-growth AI startups (including Anthropic and other top global AI firms whose threat surfaces are expanding via GenAI adoption).
- **How do they make money?** The core business is a recurring SaaS model driven by platform subscriptions, cross-selling, and compliance renewals. Legacy IT services and government contracts are intentionally suppressed to protect overall margins.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
- **The Moat is Developing (Scale & Distribution).** Historically, cybersecurity companies spend multi-millions on customer acquisition, burning cash for decades. TAC has built a distribution moat by acquiring 10,000 clients in just two years with near-zero customer acquisition cost. This installed base acts as a proprietary distribution channel for launching new products like Sokify.ai. Furthermore, their integration into Google CASA, MASSA, and serving AI pioneers like Anthropic creates a baseline of regulatory capture and institutional trust. 
- **The Bear Case for the Moat:** The moat is heavily reliant on volume rather than deep enterprise entrenchment. However, management has proven this fragmentation is a feature, not a bug. In Q1 FY27, the top 10 clients contributed just $122,000 combined, with a maximum single ticket of $17,000. While this eliminates concentration risk, the overarching risk remains high churn among a long-tail of micro-clients if they fail to graduate to premium platform tiers.

## Growth Vectors
- **The $100M ARR "2030 Vision" & Near-Term Guidance:** Management is aggressively pivoting from customer acquisition to monetization. Shattering their previous "no guidance" stance, TAC has issued a highly confident ₹100 Cr total income target for FY27, projecting sequential quarterly growth of 10-20%. The ultimate goal remains a $100M ARR by 2030, unlocking a $10,000 Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) across their 10,000-strong base via Sokify and AI module bundling.
- **US Public Sector & Multi-Brand Strategy:** While earlier attempts at a US Nasdaq IPO for CyberScope have taken a back seat, TAC is rapidly operationalizing "Cybersandia." With key local leadership appointed (including a former New Mexico Attorney General as an Independent Director), the subsidiary is positioned to capture US state and local government pipelines.
- **Management Execution:** Management is delivering on credibility and scale. They voluntarily transitioned from half-yearly SME-style reporting to formal quarterly earnings, massively boosting institutional visibility. Operationally, they completely absorbed the expiration of their 3-year tax holiday, actually expanding EBITDA margins to 48.8% in Q1 FY27, proving exceptional operating leverage requiring zero proportional headcount growth.

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Forensic Red Flags (Crypto Treasury Exposure):** TAC’s CyberScope subsidiary accepts payments in cryptocurrency. While FY26 saw negative fair value movements wipe out ₹1.52 crores in "Other Income," Q1 FY27 printed a remarkably clean operational beat with zero crypto noise flagged. Still, historical balance sheet exposure to unregulated digital assets remains a lingering forensic watchpoint.
- **Business Risks (The Execution Treadmill):** The primary risk has shifted from macro headwinds to sheer internal execution risk. To bridge the gap from a ₹100 Cr FY27 target to an ₹830 Cr ($100M) ARR by 2030, TAC must deliver flawless, relentless hyper-growth. Any sales cycle slippages, macroeconomic shocks in the US, or aggressive price undercutting by legacy giants (Tenable, Qualys) could stall momentum. Management must consistently convert their 10,000 active base (invoicing ~1,800 distinct customers per quarter) into higher-tier subscriptions.
- **Regulatory & Execution Derailments:** While highly anticipated US Nasdaq listing plans for CyberScope appear delayed or sidelined, focus has successfully shifted to Cybersandia's operational rollout. The risk now lies in converting recent US public sector roundtables into recognized revenue.

## Investment Thesis
- **The Bull Case:** TAC Security is a rare combination of hyper-growth (+96.8% YoY revenue in Q1 FY27) and elite profitability (48.8% EBITDA, 40.2% PAT). The company is successfully shedding its crypto-volatility baggage and actively monetizing an unprecedented global distribution network of 10,000 clients. Propelled by expanding enterprise attack surfaces in the "Frontier AI" era, TAC is executing flawlessly on cross-selling high-margin compliance (Sokify.ai). If management achieves its highly visible ₹100 Cr FY27 target, the resulting operating leverage will generate exponential cash flow, triggering a major institutional re-rating toward global cybersecurity multiples. 
- **The Bear Case:** The headline "10,000 clients" remains a volume-heavy foundation with a current maximum single ticket size of just $17,000. Scaling from an SME run-rate to a $100M ARR SaaS giant by 2030 requires consecutive quarters of structural ARPU expansion and churn containment that leaves zero room for error. Investors should monitor Q2 and Q3 closely; any failure to meet their explicit +20% / +10% sequential growth targets will severely crack the newly established management credibility and hyper-growth premium.
