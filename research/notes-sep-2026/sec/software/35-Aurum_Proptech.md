## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Aurum Proptech

## Executive Summary
Aurum Proptech has evolved from a disjointed collection of real estate service providers into a cohesive, integrated ecosystem operator. The company addresses the chronic fragmentation of the Indian real estate lifecycle by bridging the gap between property development, distribution, and consumption (rental/living). As of Q1 FY27, Aurum reported its third consecutive profitable quarter, proving its core operations have reached structural breakeven. Furthermore, the company has executed a massive strategic pivot, transforming from a transaction fulfillment platform into a full-stack real estate marketplace following the blockbuster share-swap acquisition of Housing.com.

The company operates a "Land-to-Asset" digital ecosystem. It solves tenant friction through its Rental arm (HelloWorld, NestAway) and accelerates developer monetization through its Distribution arm (PropTiger, Sell.Do, Aurum Analytica, and now Housing.com). With core annual revenues previously tracking toward ₹460 Crores, the integration of Housing.com is expected to accelerate the company’s trajectory toward its ₹1,000 Crores ARR target. However, investors must scrutinize the upcoming turnaround execution: while the core business is now structurally profitable, the consolidated entity is absorbing a massive top-of-funnel consumer engine that currently carries an annualized cash burn of ~₹200 Crores. 

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
Aurum operates a hybrid model blending **SaaS subscriptions**, **Transaction Success Fees**, and **Asset Management income**, now significantly augmented by marketplace discovery.

*   **Rental (B2C & B2B2C):**
    *   **What they sell:** Co-living beds (HelloWorld) and family rental management services (NestAway).
    *   **Revenue Source:** Rental income from tenants and management fees from property owners.
    *   **Model:** Long-term leases with landlords vs. shorter-term tenant contracts (Asset-heavy/hybrid).
*   **Distribution (B2B):**
    *   **What they sell:** Marketplace discovery (Housing.com), qualified leads, CRM software (Sell.Do), and brokerage services.
    *   **Revenue Source:** Real Estate Developers pay for digital advertising, "Annual Operating Plans" (marketing retainers), and success-based brokerage fees (PropTiger).
    *   **Model:** High-margin service and SaaS revenue (Sell.do boasts 11,800+ active licenses with just 7% gross churn). Transaction revenue exhibits H2 seasonality based on performance slabs. 
*   **Capital:**
    *   **What they sell:** Real estate investment technology.
    *   **Status:** Currently negligible and in incubation.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
*   **The Moat (Dramatically Widening):**
    *   **Data Density & Top-of-Funnel Dominance:** Prior to Q1 FY27, Aurum lacked a consumer discovery engine. By acquiring Housing.com (24% market share, 58M monthly active users), they have closed the loop. They now own the proprietary data moat spanning from a user's first Google search, to lead qualification via Aurum Analytica, to final purchase via PropTiger. 
    *   **AI-Native Transformation:** Aurum is embedding AI directly into lead generation, sales workflows, and customer engagement, deliberately aiming to decouple headcount growth from revenue growth.

*   **The Bear Case (Fragility):**
    *   **Turnaround Execution Risk:** While the brokerage and distribution businesses are no longer purely commoditized due to the Housing.com integration, the moat is currently subsidized by massive capital burn. Housing.com brings structural unprofitability (~₹200 Cr annual burn) that must be staunched rapidly. 
    *   **Asset Liability Mismatch:** In the Rental business, Aurum holds long-term lease liabilities while relying on short-term tenant demand, maintaining exposure to macroeconomic shocks in the IT sector.

## Growth Vectors

*   **Organic Scale to ₹1,000 Cr:**
    While management previously outlined a purely organic path to ₹1,000 Cr ARR, the goalposts have moved. The target will now be achieved "much faster" due to the inorganic consolidation of Housing.com (~₹343 Cr FY25 revenue). Growth is now predicated on transitioning this massive portal from a pure advertising/intent model into a full-scale transaction fulfillment engine over the next 4-6 quarters.

*   **Ecosystem Cross-Selling:**
    The immediate growth vector is maximizing "Ecosystem Revenue" by funneling Housing.com's 58 million monthly active users directly into Aurum's existing fulfillment layers. This involves routing tenant intent to HelloWorld/Nestaway and buyer intent to PropTiger/Sell.do, massively expanding the lifetime value (LTV) and reducing Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) across the board.

*   **Regulatory Tailwind:**
    The recent Supreme Court clarification regarding GST on co-living and hostels provides long-awaited regulatory certainty. This is expected to catalyze supply acquisition in the student housing sector, a key growth vertical for the HelloWorld brand.

## Key Risks & Governance

*   **Quality of Earnings (Forensic Note):**
    While the company reported a PBT of ₹2.31 Cr in Q1 FY27, maintaining its profitable streak, headline numbers were heavily supplemented by ₹52.38 Cr in Other Income derived from a ₹112 Cr building sale (classified under discontinued operations). Positively, the core Rental and Distribution segments achieved structural profitability independently. The primary financial risk is now strictly the upcoming consolidated cash drain from Housing.com.

*   **Integration Risk:**
    The acquisition of Housing.com is a high-stakes bet. The portal burns roughly ₹200 Cr annually. While management is leaning heavily on their successful track record of turning around PropTiger and Nestaway (guiding a 4-6 quarter breakeven for Housing.com), failure to execute this playbook flawlessly will drag the newly profitable core business back into the red and rapidly drain the ₹200 Cr war chest promoters are injecting.

*   **Client Concentration:**
    In the Distribution segment, revenue is tied to the cyclicality of real estate developers' launch calendars. While Sell.do serves over 1,200 developers, a broader slowdown in new project launches would immediately impact SaaS licensing and transaction mandates.

## Investment Thesis

### The Bull Case
Aurum Proptech is positioning itself as the definitive "AI-native real estate operating system" in India. The acquisition of Housing.com transforms the thesis: Aurum has effectively captured the top-of-funnel discovery layer, giving them 58M monthly users to feed into their highly profitable transaction (PropTiger/Sell.do) and rental (Nestaway/HelloWorld) fulfillment layers. The core business has reached structural breakeven, proving management's operational discipline. If the team executes their turnaround playbook on Housing.com over the next 4 quarters—realizing cost synergies and cross-selling effectively—Aurum will completely dominate the digital real estate landscape in India with deep consolidated profitability.

### The Bear Case
The strategic pivot to active M&A introduces severe integration and cash-burn risks, accompanied by substantial equity dilution to REA Group. The cultural and operational merging of a massive, cash-burning platform (Housing.com) with a newly profitable core entity is notoriously difficult. If the ₹200 Cr annual burn rate proves resistant to management's efficiency measures, it will force further equity dilution and erode the platform's hard-won profitability. Aurum remains a high-beta play on execution and the broader residential real estate cycle, offering limited downside protection if the turnaround stalls.
