# eMudhra — deep research (Phase A1)

Research date: 27 Sep 2026. ₹ crore (cr) unless stated. DSC = digital signature certificate; PKI = public key infrastructure (the cryptography behind certificates); CLM = certificate lifecycle management; CA = certifying authority.
Transcript check: **the stored "Q1 FY27 transcript" is not eMudhra's — it is Equitas Small Finance Bank's Q1 FY27 call** (AI-generated tijoristack copy), and the stored Q1 analysis and profile are built on it. The real eMudhra Q1 FY27 call (30 Jul 2026) was read from the official BSE filing. The stored Q4 FY26 transcript is the official BSE filing.

## Business and segments
- India's largest licensed certifying authority and the only Indian CA in all major browser trust stores (WebTrust). Three lines of business, Q1 FY27 revenue ₹192.5cr: **Enterprise Solutions** 65% (+50% YoY, incl. Cryptas) — five own platforms: emCA (PKI), SecurePass (identity and access management), CertiNext (CLM), emSigner (e-signature workflows, 500k+ signings a day) and PrivaTrust (data-privacy consent, new); **Trust Services** 15% (−3% YoY) — DSC tokens, e-sign, SSL/TLS certificates; **Services** 20% (+5%) — IT services, mainly in the US. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call official]
- Geography Q1 FY27: international 66% (₹126cr, +34%), India 34% (₹64cr, +21%). FY26: North America 34%, India 36%, Europe 12%, Middle East & Africa 11%, APAC 7%. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Enterprise mix (Q1): cybersecurity 80% / paperless 20%; government & defence 31%, BFSI 25%, enterprises 44%; direct 64% / partners 36%. [IP Q1FY27]
- Acquisitions: Cryptas International (Austria, incl. PrimeSign, an EU qualified trust service provider) — 51% bought 1 Jul 2025 for €5M cash plus an earn-out at 10x 2026 EBITDA (fair value €7.95M) and a put/call on the other 49% in 2028–30 at 10x EBITDA, with a floor of 1x revenue; plus AI Cyberforge (US), Ikon and Two95 (US services). Cryptas revenue ~₹85cr in FY26 at break-even; ₹20cr in Q1 FY27. [AR FY26 acquisition note; Q4 call; Q1 call official]
- ~850 employees in 15 offices, 35+ countries. [IP Q1FY27]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated)
- Annual: revenue FY24 373 → FY25 519 → FY26 702 (+35%); OPM 29% → 24% → 23%; net profit 76 → 87 → 110 (+26%). TTM revenue 745, profit 117. Company-reported FY26 total income ₹713cr, EBITDA ₹165cr (23.2%), PAT ₹110cr. [Screener; Q4 call]
- Quarters (sales / OPM / net profit): Jun-25 147 / 24% / 25; Sep-25 173 / 24% / 26; Dec-25 188 / 22% / 29; Mar-26 193 / 22% / 30; Jun-26 191 / 26% / 32. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27 (company): total income ₹192.5cr (+27.8% YoY, −2.1% QoQ), gross margin 57.3% (53.7%), EBITDA ₹50.4cr (26.2%, +40%), PAT ₹32.0cr (+27.9%); adjusted (ex-ESOP cost and notional interest on the acquisition liability) EBITDA 27.5%, PAT ₹33.9cr. [IP Q1FY27]
- **Organic growth ≈ 15%**: Q1 revenue ex-Cryptas ~₹172cr vs ₹151cr a year earlier (Cryptas joined 1 Jul 2025); chairman says ~13 points of the 28% came from Cryptas. [Q1 call official; IP Q1FY27, derived]
- Returns are modest for software: ROCE 16%, ROE 12.6% (management calls ~14.5–15% ROE sustainable). 5-year sales CAGR 40%, but 3-year profit CAGR only 19%. [Screener; Q1 call official]

## Products: ARR, licence vs subscription, customers, order book
- No ARR, churn or customer-count disclosure. Revenue is a mix of perpetual licences plus AMC (Indian banks, government), recurring subscription/certificate billing (US, SaaS) and one-off DSC token sales. Management: "we still continue to do the recurring billing as well as a license model." [Q4 call]
- Enterprise order book ₹238cr at March 2026 (products only). Management's rule of thumb: next-year enterprise revenue ≈ 2x the order book; for FY27 it guided "2.2x–2.3x" (≈ ₹520–550cr), trimmed because Middle East orders slipped after the regional war. [Q4 call]
- US products grew from ~$6.5–7M to ~$9.5M in FY26; won InCommon, a US federation of ~700 universities, for TLS certificates (now issuing 1,000–1,500 certificates a day). [Q4 call; Q1 call official]
- SecurePass deal sizes ₹4–6cr per large bank or government body, driven by RBI/SEBI authentication mandates; Q1 wins include a large government tax department. [Q1 call official; IP Q1FY27]
- Switching costs: products sit in core bank logins and certificate estates; "replacement is a cumbersome affair". [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (7 May 2026): FY27 organic revenue ~18% ("15% to 18%... most likely 18%"); PAT +25–30% ("27%–28% kind of thing"); enterprise products +25–30%, trust +20%, services flat; US products +25–30%; Cryptas to earn >$1M profit; double PAT in three years (FY27–FY29); "whatever guidance we have given, we have achieved 100%". [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: PAT +27.9% — **on track**; EBITDA margin 26.2% — **beat**; organic revenue ~15% — **below** 18%; trust services −3% — **missed** (+20% guided) because a CCA rule moving DSC tokens to a new security standard (FIPS 140-3) left "no tokens ready for sale in Q1"; services +5% — **better** than flat; Cryptas made first CertiNext and emSigner cross-sales, but the Europe holding company (eMudhra BV) lost ~₹4cr — **not yet**. [Q1 call official; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 restated guidance as "unchanged" but worded it as 18% organic revenue and **25%** PAT growth (Q4 said 25–30%, "27–28%"); EBITDA ~25%, PAT margin 16–16.5%; trust revenue weak again in Q2 until tokens are recertified (~September); UAE trust-service licence expected around end-Q2/early-Q3. [Q1 call official]

## Capex and capacity
- Heavy capitalised spending: fixed assets (incl. intangibles and goodwill) rose from ₹419cr to ₹727cr in FY26; investing outflow ₹198cr vs CFO ₹133cr, so free cash flow was −₹52cr (Screener). Spend covers product development, data centres in the US, Europe, UAE and India, and acquisitions. [Screener; AR FY26]
- Capacity is not a constraint; sales hiring (US senior sellers at $200–300k each) is the swing cost. [Q1 call official]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Unencumbered cash ~₹88–90cr at March 2026 (ICRA) plus ₹46cr investments; borrowings ₹29cr (Cryptas bank loans). Other liabilities jumped from ₹123cr to ₹274cr — mainly the Cryptas earn-out and put-option liability. Not net-debt, but the cash cushion is thin next to the acquisition liability. [ICRA Jun-26; Screener; AR FY26]
- Debtor days ~98–102 (government and export customers pay slowly); ICRA flags "high working capital intensity". Days payable are very high (420) because token and certificate partners are paid late — flattering the cash cycle. [Screener; ICRA Jun-26]
- CFO/operating profit 91% (FY25) and 93% (FY26). Dividend payout ~10% (yield 0.2%). [Screener]
- A FY25 contingent liability of ₹17.8cr for buying back unsold DSC stock from partners (after a July 2024 CCA rule change) was settled in FY26. [AR FY26 note 36]

## Moat evidence
- Regulatory licence (CCA) plus browser-trust-store listing, which few CAs worldwide hold; IDC ranks it No.1 of 42 vendors in India identity and digital trust; emSigner in Gartner's e-signature market guide. [IP Q1FY27]
- Pricing: ICRA says "intense competition limits pricing flexibility to some extent" (DigiCert, Entrust, Sify, Capricorn). Product gross margins 70–75% vs services 18–20%. [ICRA Jun-26; Q1 call official]
- Demand tailwinds with dates: public TLS certificate life cut to 47 days by March 2029 (CA/Browser Forum) and post-quantum migration deadlines 2029–30 in India — both push enterprises to buy CLM. [IP Q1FY27]

## Risks and red flags
- **3i Infotech dispute**: on 4 Feb 2026 3i Infotech alleged criminal conspiracy in the old sale of 3i Consumer Services (now eMudhra) and wrongful redemption of preference shares, claiming >₹128cr plus interest; eMudhra denies it, calls it time-barred, and sent a legal notice (23 Apr 2026). Police took the chairman's statement in July. ICRA calls it "a key monitorable". Not provided for. [AR FY26 note 36(f); Q1 call official; ICRA Jun-26]
- **Related-party optics**: the chairman confirmed that "Capital MXT", which bought ~5% of 3i Infotech, is his "personal investment" — i.e. he now holds a stake in the counter-party suing his company. [Q1 call official]
- **Acquisition quality**: Cryptas (~₹85–100cr revenue) "was not profitable at all"; the put on the remaining 49% has a floor of 1x revenue, which could force a large payment if EBITDA stays low; European entity lost ~₹4cr in Q1 (legal costs). [Q1 call official; AR FY26]
- **Regulatory dependence**: CCA token-standard change cut trust revenue (Q1 and Q2); an earlier CCA rule forced a stock buyback from partners. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- **Auditor**: unmodified opinion; key audit matter on revenue recognition (performance obligations, over-time vs point-in-time). No emphasis of matter found. Contingent liabilities otherwise small (₹3.2cr income tax, ₹0.15cr GST). [AR FY26]
- **Ownership**: DIIs cut from 16.75% (Dec 2025) to 10.55% (Jun 2026). Promoter 54.4%, no pledge noted. [Screener]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone steady and confident on both calls; Q1 added PrivaTrust (DPDP Act privacy) and CBOM/post-quantum tooling as new talking points.
- Numbers: margin rose (26.2% vs 22.4% in Q4) partly because low-margin token sales vanished; revenue slipped 2% QoQ.

## My read
The product side (CLM, identity, e-sign) is growing well and margins improved, with real tailwinds from shorter certificate lives, post-quantum rules and India's privacy law. But organic growth is running nearer 15% than the 18% guided, growth is partly bought (Cryptas) with an open-ended put liability, free cash flow is negative, and the 3i Infotech fight — now with police involvement and the chairman personally holding 3i shares — is an unquantified governance overhang. The price already assumes strong compounding (about 25% a year earnings growth); it needs the doubling-PAT plan to stay on track. Clean resolution of the 3i matter and positive free cash flow would improve the view; another quarter of sub-15% organic growth or a large Cryptas put payment would weaken it.
