# Sagility — deep research (Sep 2026)

Screener code SAGILITY. Price ₹44.7, market cap ₹20,921 cr, P/E 20.4, ROCE 13.4%, ROE 10.6%, dividend yield 0.34% [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].

Transcript check: the stored Q4 FY26 transcript is the official BSE filing. The stored Q1 FY27 transcript is AI-generated (tijoristack). I checked it against the official NSE filing of 28 Jul 2026. Numbers match; only names are garbled (e.g. "Paedric Sarkar" should be Baidik Sarkar of Unifi Capital).

## Business and segments
- **What it does:** Sagility is a US-healthcare-only BPM (business process management) company, carved out of Hinduja Global Solutions (HGS) in 2021. Its owner is EQT, through Sagility B.V.
  - **Payers (insurers):** about 90% of revenue. It handles claims, enrolment, member/provider service, clinical work (utilisation and care management), and payment integrity.
  - **Providers:** about 10%, mainly revenue-cycle management.
  - Payer share was 89.6% and provider share 10.4% in Q1 FY27 [Q1 call official; IP Q1FY27].
- **Revenue and people:**
  - FY26 revenue was $814m (₹7,193 cr), all from US clients.
  - Delivery runs from India (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Mumbai and a new site in Indore), the Philippines, Jamaica and the US.
  - About 65–70% of headcount sits in Bengaluru and Hyderabad [Q4 call; Q1 call].
  - The US onshore share of revenue is rising, mainly because BroadPath delivers onshore; the exact % is not disclosed [Q4 call].
- **Client concentration (very high):**

  | Measure | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Top 3 clients | 68.3% | 66.2% | 59.9% |
  | Top 5 clients | 79.2% | 77.9% | 70.4% |
  | Top 10 clients | — | 90.5% | 83.9% |

  - The largest client groups have an average tenure of 18 years.
  - Sagility serves 7 of the 10 largest US payers.
  - It has nine clients above $20m, eight at $5–20m, 21 at $1–5m, and 71 below $1m [AR FY26 MD&A; IP Q1FY27].
- **Clients and people, Q1 FY27:**
  - 109 client groups (82 at FY26), including 26 added via CareSeed.
  - Headcount 47,307, up 18.5% YoY.
  - Quarterly annualised voluntary attrition 28.6% (Q4 FY26: 38.1%, a seasonal peak) [IP Q1FY27].
- **Deals:**
  - Steady-state ACV (annual contract value) signed: $35.3m in Q1 FY27 and $30.7m in Q4 FY26. Deals come from 18 or more clients each quarter [Q1 call; Q4 call].
  - Pipeline TCV (total contract value) is about $570–575m. The official Q4 transcript prints this as "₹570 million", an evident unit slip [Q4 call].
  - Utilisation is not disclosed.
- **Acquisitions:**
  - BroadPath (Jan 2025): brings Medicare sales and enrolment work, which is seasonal and onshore. It contributed about $13.6m in Q1 [Q1 call].
  - CareSeed (closed 11 Jun 2026): a HEDIS quality-reporting platform (Forecast, Harvest) with $5.1m CY25 revenue at a 31.4% EBITDA margin and 30 small and mid-size payer clients [Q1 call].
- **Seasonality:** revenue from the US Medicare annual enrolment and open-enrolment seasons was about $50m in FY26 (about 6% of revenue), up from about $20m (3%) in FY25. It falls in Q3 and Q4. The H2:H1 revenue split is now about 55:45 [Q4 call; Q1 call].

- **Unit economics:** revenue per head was about $17,000 a year (FY26: $814m over about 47,000 staff). Pricing pressure runs "in the opposite direction". AI, automation and offshoring deflate revenue by about 1–1.5% a year historically, and management expects about 2% in FY27 [Q1 call; Q4 call].

## Guidance history
- **Q4 FY26 guidance for FY27:**
  - Low double-digit organic constant-currency growth.
  - Adjusted EBITDA margin of 24–25%, toward the top of the range if foreign exchange held.
  - Debt fully repaid in FY27.
  - Tax rate 24–25%.
  - About 7–8% of growth was "already factored in" from existing contracts [Q4 call].
- **Earlier record:** FY26 beat its Q3 guidance. Constant-currency growth was 23.6% against 22.5% guided, and margin was 25.3% [Q4 call].
- **Q1 FY27 delivery against that guidance:**
  - Growth: 14.9% organic cc, ahead of the guide. Guidance was **kept** at low double digits, to be narrowed after Q2.
  - Margin: 24.0%. Guidance was **kept** at 24–25% despite a new 120 bps annual hit from minimum-wage increases in Karnataka and Telangana (40 bps landed in Q1; the full-quarter effect from Q2 will be "more than double"). Currency gains (+100 bps) and efficiency offset it.
  - Management no longer says it will be "at the upper end" unless the wage impact turns out lower [Q1 call official].
- **M&A:** acquisitions were promised in both the Investor Day and Q4 remarks. CareSeed closed in June as the first of two planned clinical deals; the second is still in progress [Q1 call].
- **Adjusted EBITDA bridge from Q4 (24.9%) to Q1 (24.0%):** minimum wage −40 bps, salary hikes −50 bps, loss of seasonal operating leverage −100 bps, currency +100 bps or more [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- Q1 fixed-asset additions were ₹74.5 cr, giving free cash flow of ₹241.6 cr. M&A cash paid in Q1 was ₹136 cr, mostly CareSeed plus earnouts [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Capacity is added through new seats and sites. Expansion is being tilted toward other states (Tamil Nadu, Indore) to reduce minimum-wage exposure, a mitigation that will take 12–18 months [Q1 call].
- Earnouts run through FY29 (BroadPath and CareSeed). Intangible amortisation from the acquisitions continues to FY36 [IP Q1FY27].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net debt fell to ₹111 cr at Jun-26, 0.06x adjusted EBITDA. Cash and equivalents were ₹967 cr (Mar-26: ₹904 cr) [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- **Borrowings are from the promoter** (the cash-flow statement line reads "Repayment of promoter borrowings"). They will be fully repaid in FY27 [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call].
- Screener borrowings fell from ₹2,532 cr (FY24) to ₹1,111 cr (FY26), a figure that includes lease liabilities.
- **Days sales outstanding (DSO):** 80 days at Q1 (52 billed plus 28 unbilled), from 87 at Q4. Screener debtor days were 93 in FY26 against 83 in FY25 [Q1 call; Screener].
- **Cash conversion:**
  - Q1 operating cash flow was ₹316 cr, about 70% of EBITDA, dragged down by earnout payments. Q4 was 104.6%.
  - FY26 conversion was lower than FY25 because of higher tax paid, larger unrealised currency gains and FY25 tax refunds not recurring.
  - Screener CFO: FY24 ₹973 cr, FY25 ₹1,214 cr, FY26 ₹1,203 cr [Q1 call; Q4 call; Screener].
- **Payouts:** the dividend is tiny (₹0.10 final for FY26). The cash is being kept for M&A and debt repayment [Q4 call].
- **Rating:** ICRA upgraded Sagility to BBB+ (Stable)/A2, then withdrew the rating at the company's request in Jan 2026. There is no current rating [AR FY26 corporate governance report].

## Moat evidence
- Very long client relationships (18-year average for the largest), and deep embedding in regulated payer workflows where errors carry CMS penalties [AR FY26; Q4 call].
- **Share gains:** Sagility grew about 15% organically against an industry growing 7–8%, as an analyst noted and management did not dispute. Top-5 client revenue grew 16.7% in FY26 [Q4 call; AR FY26].
- Analyst recognition: Avasant "leader" in clinical and care management; NelsonHall, Everest and ISG leader ratings [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Limits of the moat:** pricing power is weak (no price increases; deflation instead), and the business is labour-arbitrage based. Switching costs are high but not absolute: work does migrate, for example one top-5 client moving work from onshore to offshore [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Concentration:** the top 3 clients are 60% of revenue and the top 10 are 84%. Payer mergers or vendor consolidation could hurt badly. The AR lists this as a key risk [AR FY26].
- **Wages and currency:** the minimum-wage shock (120 bps) is being absorbed only because of rupee depreciation. A stronger rupee would expose it. Hedges were taken at lower rates [Q1 call].
- **AI deflation:** revenue drag is rising from 1–1.5% toward 2%+, and management expects it to "gradually inch up" [Q4 call].
- **Legal exposure — Synergy litigation:**
  - A vendor claims $115.9m (₹1,094 cr) on unjust-enrichment grounds, or $53.2m on lost profits, over pre-acquisition commissions.
  - The sellers (HGS and affiliates) kept this liability and indemnify Sagility.
  - The risk to Sagility is small unless the indemnity fails [AR FY26 contingent liabilities].
- **Tax and goodwill:**
  - New income-tax/transfer-pricing demands of ₹92.6 cr (FY22–FY23) were not provided for; the prior-year figure was nil. Tax positions are a key audit matter [AR FY26].
  - Group goodwill is about ₹6,039 cr against equity of about ₹9,660 cr. Impairment would hit hard if growth faltered [AR FY26].
- **Management exits:** Group CFO S.D. Srinivasan resigned (effective 15 Nov 2025) and the Chief Growth Officer resigned (28 Nov 2025). A new CFO, Srinivas Mattapalli, joined on 14 Feb 2026 [AR FY26].
- **Promoter selling:**
  - Sagility B.V. (EQT) cut its stake from 82.39% to 50.95% during FY26. This included an offer for sale of 70.3 cr shares on 27–28 May 2025 to meet minimum public shareholding rules.
  - Further selling is likely, and is an overhang on the stock [AR FY26; Screener].
- **Dilution:** a new ESOP pool of about 3.3% of equity is coming [Q4 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- **Q4 call:** celebratory. FY26 beat guidance, with a stronger seasonal half and a rebrand. Margin was framed as 24–25% with upside if currency held.
- **Q1 call:** the new wage-shock issue arrived (120 bps). Margin upside is now conditional on the wage impact, and the "upper end" talk softened.
- CareSeed and mid-market cross-selling became the new story.
- Management was more candid that large managed-service deals take 3–6 months to negotiate.
- It started quantifying AI deflation (about 2%, rising).

## My read
The operating business is improving:
- 15% organic constant-currency growth against an industry at 7–8%.
- Stable 24–25% adjusted margins.
- Debt almost gone.
- Concentration slowly falling (top-3 from 72% in FY23 to 60%).

The quality of earnings needs care. Reported PAT runs about 20% below adjusted, and FY26–FY27 margins lean on a weak rupee. At about 20x reported trailing earnings, the price needs only about 10–12% EPS growth. That looks achievable if growth holds at low double digits and amortisation falls away. The main swing factors are a rupee rebound, the loss of a top-3 client, or faster AI-led deflation. A second accretive deal and a clean Q2 margin (at least 24% with the full wage hit) would strengthen the case.
