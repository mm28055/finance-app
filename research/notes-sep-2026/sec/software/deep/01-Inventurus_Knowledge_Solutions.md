# Inventurus Knowledge Solutions (IKS Health) — deep research (Sep 2026)

Screener code IKS. Price ₹1,816, market cap ₹31,171 cr, ROCE 31.5%, ROE 31.4%, no dividend [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].
Transcript check: the stored Q4 FY26 transcript is the official BSE filing. The stored Q1 FY27 transcript is AI-generated (tijoristack); I checked it against the official company transcript (ikshealth.com/ir/2027/q1/transcript-q1.pdf). The key numbers match, but a few details differ (see Stored-data errors).

## Business and segments
- IKS sells a "care enablement" platform to US healthcare providers. It takes over admin and clinical "chore" work for them: revenue-cycle management (RCM: billing and collections), medical coding, clinical documentation (human-assisted and AI scribes such as Scribble Now/Select), prior authorisation and patient access (MyCare Hub). It reports one segment and has 100% US exposure. The AR notes a "healthcare sector and geographic concentration" risk because the entire business is in the US [AR FY26 p.~279; Q4 call].
- Clients: about 600 provider organisations, of which about 450 are large enterprises. Around 90% of revenue is repeat business. Management cut the client count from 900+ (after the AQuity deal) to about 600 on purpose, dropping small clients [Q4 call].
- Pricing is mostly outcome-based: a percentage of the client's collections or revenue, not billed per FTE. It also runs "NEVA" (net economic value add) risk-sharing deals; Palomar Health produced $3m of NEVA earnings in calendar 2025 [Q4 call].
- TAM is $260bn+ of US provider admin spend, growing about 8% a year. The outsourced portion is about $35bn, growing about 12% a year. Management's yardstick: growth above 12% in constant currency (cc) means IKS is gaining share [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **TruBridge (closed 9 Jul 2026, after Q1):** a US EHR (electronic health record) and RCM vendor for rural and community hospitals, with its EHR in about 700 of roughly 2,200 rural hospitals. Management gave its share as "30%-plus" on the official Q1 call and "nearly 35%" on the Q4 call. Enterprise value was up to $565m [AR FY26 Note 47; IKS press release Jul-26]; management said it bought "$68 million of EBITDA for about $550 million" [Q1 call official].
- Post-deal scale: legacy IKS runs at about $97m a quarter ($388m a year). TruBridge was reset to about $75m a quarter ($300m a year). Combined that is about $688m of revenue, 16,000+ staff and close to 2,000 clients [Q1 call].
- People (legacy IKS, June 2026): 12,889 staff, including 1,902 clinically trained and 565 in technology. A year earlier it had 12,300 [IP Q1FY27 via Investing.com; Q1 call].
- IT-services metrics: IKS does not disclose TCV (total contract value), utilisation or attrition by quarter. The top-10 clients' average tenure is 5.76 years and the top-5's is 7.41 years [IP Q1FY27]. Top-10 clients were 37% of FY26 revenue but 43–48% on a quarterly basis. The CFO told analysts to look at the annual figure [Q4 call]. Q4 FY26 top-10 revenue was ₹452 cr of ₹857 cr, about 53% [Q4 call]. That is high concentration.

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly revenue went ₹740 (Jun-25) → 781 → 815 → 858 → 894 (Jun-26). Operating profit went 238 → 270 → 279 → 295 → 290. OPM (operating profit margin) went 32% → 35% → 34% → 34% → 32%. Net profit went 152 → 181 → 183 → 206 → 194 [Screener].
- Q1 FY27: revenue +21% YoY (+12% cc), PAT +28%. Reported EBITDA was ₹294 cr (33%). Stripping out about ₹20 cr of TruBridge deal costs gives ₹314 cr, or about 35%. Forex was neutral in Q1 against a ₹23 cr net forex gain in Q4. The tax rate rose to 22.4% [Q1 call official; IP Q1FY27].
- Annual revenue went ₹1,818 cr (FY24) → 2,664 (FY25) → 3,194 (FY26), with TTM at 3,347. OPM went 29% → 29% → 34%. Net profit went 370 → 486 → 722. FY26 revenue grew 20%, EBITDA 38% and PAT 48% [Screener; Q4 call].
- Unit economics: FY26 EBITDA per employee was ₹8.8 lakh. In Q1, revenue grew 12% cc while headcount grew only 4.8% (12,300 → 12,889). This shows revenue is decoupling from headcount [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Pro-forma: TruBridge brings about $68m of EBITDA (roughly ₹650 cr) at about a 22–23% margin. The combined margin falls to 26–27% at first, and management aims to rebuild it to the early-to-mid 30s in "the next couple of years" [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- IKS gives no formal guidance. Its "aspiration" is 12%+ cc growth for the legacy $388m book. That was restated on both calls, and management said it would not guess TruBridge growth for 2–3 quarters [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **True North:** at least ₹3,000 cr EBITDA by FY30, from about ₹1,000 cr LTM Dec-25, with net debt back to about zero. It was first set out on the Q4 call and **kept** on Q1 as "at least ₹3,000 cr", with no equity dilution beyond ESOPs and no further large acquisitions assumed. The presentation shows FY30 net debt of about ₹300 cr and roughly a 30% EBITDA CAGR [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Q4 promise vs Q1 delivery:
  - "Some additional transaction expenses in Q1": delivered, at ₹20 cr (Q4 had ₹25 cr) [Q4 call; Q1 call].
  - FY27 legacy tax rate of about 22%: Q1 came in at 22.4% [Q4 call; Q1 call].
  - TruBridge EBITDA of about $68m: confirmed [Q1 call].
  - TruBridge revenue: **cut** from $340m to $300m a year. Of the $10m-a-quarter gap, $2–3m is stricter revenue recognition (TruBridge had booked estimated RCM collections), about $8m comes from exiting two loss-making services (small-hospital IT management and "early-out" collections), and $2–3m is planned customer discounts [Q1 call official].
  - Cross-sell: the Q4 call cited Holyoke (full platform) and a top-5 health system. Q1 added Advocate Health (a top-5 US system with $35bn+ revenue) expanding in RCM and coding, a large California system moving to Epic, a national musculoskeletal group and StrideCare [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Margin: Q4 adjusted EBITDA was about 35%, and Q1 held at 35% excluding deal costs. Q2 FY27 will carry "similar range" integration costs, and from Q2 it will also include a partial TruBridge quarter [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- IKS is capital-light. FY26 CFO was ₹674 cr on Screener; the company's own figure is ₹863 cr, which it describes as OCF of 79% of EBITDA. Company-reported FCF was ₹612 cr against PAT of ₹721 cr [Screener; Q4 call].
- Capex goes mainly into tech hires and small AI acqui-hires: ThinkDTM, and ARAI for about $1.2m upfront (knowledge graphs for "glass-box" explainable AI). Management plans to build its own small language models (SLMs) on TruBridge's patient data to cut dependence on costly commercial large language models (LLMs) [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- TruBridge's EHR must move from COBOL to PostgreSQL in modules. Management calls it an 18–24 month journey before full "system of action + system of record" integration [Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net debt fell from ₹563 cr (Mar-25) to ₹252 cr (Mar-26), and stood at ₹265 cr at Jun-26. Q1 cash flow was hit by a one-time NEVA payment to a client [AR FY26 MD&A; IP Q1FY27; Q1 call official]. Screener shows gross borrowings of ₹753 cr (Mar-26), including leases.
- The TruBridge deal was debt-funded; the stored analysis cites about $600m of new debt, which I could not verify in a document I opened. Management expected about 3x EBITDA leverage at close [Q4 call]. CARE (A+/Stable, June 2026) expects net debt/PBILDT (operating profit) below 2x from FY28 [CARE Jun-26 via InvestyWise].
- Security: the existing term loan is secured by a pledge of 100% of the shares of the US subsidiary IKS Inc [AR FY26 borrowings note]. This is a lender pledge, not a promoter pledge.
- Debtor days went 73 (FY24) → 73 (FY25) → 65 (FY26). Working-capital days were −31 [Screener]. IKS pays no dividend or buyback, since cash goes to deleveraging.
- CARE figures: FY26 gearing 0.27x and interest cover 14.5x [CARE Jun-26].

## Moat evidence
- Rankings: #1 in AI-driven RCM (Black Book), near #1 in KLAS ambulatory RCM, and products listed on Epic Connection Hub [Q4 call].
- Stickiness: top-10 tenure is about 6 years, top-5 about 7 years, and repeat revenue is 85–90%. A recent five-year lock-in deal was signed "at a very healthy price" [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- Outcome pricing keeps productivity gains with IKS rather than passing them to clients. Management admits price deflation in easily automated features such as the ambient scribe [Q4 call].
- Cost position: 35% adjusted margin against TruBridge's 22–23%. The AQuity playbook (move work offshore, add tech) lifted group margin from 29% to 34% in two years [Screener; Q1 call].
- Data: TruBridge's patient base is a training corpus for proprietary models. The official Q1 call says "5 million-plus patients", while the Q4 call said "15 million plus" [Q4 call; Q1 call official].

## Risks and red flags
- **Integration and leverage:** TruBridge is about as big as IKS by revenue and is debt-funded. Its revenue was restated down by 12% within a month of closing. Its growth rate is still unknown to management [Q1 call].
- **Concentration:** top-10 clients are 37% of annual revenue and top-5 are high too. Everything is in US healthcare, so the business is exposed to Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement policy [Q4 call; AR FY26].
- **Competition and AI:** about 30 point-solution vendors are trying to become platforms, including Abridge, in which IKS holds a stake. Pricing pressure is admitted. EHR vendors such as Epic are adding native AI [Q1 call].
- **Accounting items:** the goodwill impairment test on the AQuity CGU (cash-generating unit) is a key audit matter, and revenue recognition is another. The auditor, Price Waterhouse, gave a clean opinion [AR FY26 auditor's report].
  - Contingent liabilities are small: tax/transfer-pricing disputes of ₹4.9 cr, plus a FY22 transfer-pricing demand of ₹16.1 cr received after year-end [AR FY26 Note 33].
  - AQuity India has EPFO provident-fund demands, with a ₹12.6 cr provision [AR FY26 Note 45].
  - A contingent consideration of about ₹41.7 cr from business combinations is still on the books [AR FY26 Note 19].
- ESOP costs are rising and management declines to guide on them. An associate (Western Washington MSO) loses money, about ₹5 cr in Q4 [Q4 call].
- Ownership: promoter holding is 63.7%, FII 8.0% and DII 7.5% [Screener].

## What changed between the two calls
- Q4 (May): tone was celebratory (FY26 PAT +48%). The deal was not yet closed and the focus was AI product launches (Scribble Select, MyCare Hub, ARAI).
- Q1 (Aug): the deal had closed and the tone was more sober. TruBridge's revenue base was cut by $40m, margins will dip to 26–27%, and there was no TruBridge growth view. Q1 cc growth of 12% was one of the slowest quarters since listing, as an analyst pointed out. The ₹3,000 cr EBITDA target was reaffirmed and tightened to "at least". New issues: the Abridge competitive overlap, pricing pressure, and the NEVA cash payment.

## Best management quotes
- "We will aspire to continue to grow our $388 million book of business at 12%." — Sachin Gupta [Q1 call]
- "With increasing competitive intensity, there is always going to be pricing pressure." — Sachin Gupta [Q1 call]
- "I don't anticipate more transformative acquisitions anytime soon." — Sachin Gupta [Q4 call]

## My read
The legacy business is very good. It grew 12–13% cc with 35% margins, 5% headcount growth and 80%+ cash conversion. The ₹3,000 cr EBITDA target implies roughly 30% EBITDA growth a year to FY30. That now rests mostly on fixing TruBridge's margin (22% to low-30s) and cross-selling into its 700 hospitals. The early $40m revenue reset is a warning that the target is not yet proven. At about 41x trailing earnings, the price needs the True North path, roughly 25–30% EPS growth, to hold. The view weakens if legacy cc growth falls below 12%, if TruBridge EBITDA slips below the $68m run-rate, or if net debt/EBITDA is not falling by FY28.
