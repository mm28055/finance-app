# Mastek — deep research (Phase A1)

Research date: 27 Sep 2026. ₹ crore (cr) unless stated; $ = US dollars; "cc" = constant currency; "12-month backlog" = orders expected to turn into revenue within 12 months.
Transcript check: the stored Q1 FY27 transcript is an AI-generated (Tijori) version; it was checked against the official BSE transcript (filed 28 Jul 2026). The stored Q4 FY26 transcript is the official BSE filing.

## Business and segments
- Mid-sized IT services firm: digital & application engineering (50.0% of Q1 FY27 revenue), Oracle Cloud & enterprise apps via the former Evosys (27.2%), data, automation & AI (13.4%), digital commerce/Salesforce via MST Solutions (9.3%). Now pitches itself as an "AI transformation company in select verticals". [IP Q1FY27]
- Geography Q1 FY27: UK & Europe 67.3% (63.8% a year ago), US 21.7% (23.1%), AMEA (Middle East, SE Asia, India, Australia) 11.0% (13.1%). The mix has swung back towards the UK as the US and Middle East shrank. [IP Q1FY27]
- FY26 segment revenue / segment profit: UK & Europe ₹2,411cr / ₹476cr (19.7%); North America ₹829cr / ₹51cr (6.1%); AMEA ₹459cr / ₹23cr (5.0%). FY25: UK ₹1,981cr (19.8%), NA ₹933cr (8.9%), AMEA ₹542cr (6.4%). The UK earns almost all the profit; NA revenue fell 11% in FY26. [AR FY26, segment note]
- Industry Q1 FY27: Government & Education 42.9%, Health & Life Sciences 19.3% (25.0% a year ago), Financial Services 15.0% (11.9%), Manufacturing & Tech 12.5%, Retail 10.3%. Private sector is ~40% of global revenue and ~30% of UK revenue ("circa"). [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Contract type: fixed price 43.1% (39.7% a year ago), time & material 56.9%; NA is 50–55% fixed-bid. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- People: 4,897 staff (3,283 offshore, 1,614 onsite), +167 in Q1 of which UK +125; LTM attrition 16.4% (17.4% Q4, 19.5% a year ago); utilisation net of leave 83.9% (85.7% Q4, 87.2% a year ago); subcontractor cost 18.5% of revenue. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Revenue per employee ≈ $104.8M × 4 ÷ 4,897 ≈ $86k a year; management said revenue per resource rose 12%+ in FY26 as headcount fell. [Q1 call, derived; Q4 call]

- Quarters (sales / operating profit / OPM / net profit): Jun-25 915/137/15%/92; Sep-25 940/146/15%/97; Dec-25 906/152/17%/108; Mar-26 938/151/16%/106; Jun-26 985/151/15%/106. [Screener]
- In dollars, growth is weak: FY26 $421.2M (+3.1%); Q1 FY27 $104.8M, +1.2% QoQ (+1.8% cc) but −2.4% YoY. Rupee growth (+7.7% YoY) is mostly the weaker rupee against the pound. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 FY27: revenue ₹985.3cr, operating EBITDA ₹151.4cr (15.4% vs 16.1% Q4), PAT ₹105.9cr (10.6% of income), diluted EPS ₹33.9 (+15% YoY). [IP Q1FY27]
- Margin bridge Q4→Q1: Middle East bench costs and a doubtful-debt provision on delayed collections/unbilled work, a mix change and UK regulatory costs; partly offset by cost savings and a currency tailwind. No rupee amount given for the provision. [Q1 call]
- FY26 exceptional charge ₹30.1cr (labour-code gratuity/leave true-up: ₹6.4cr Q3 + ₹23.7cr Q4); Q3 EBITDA restated from 16.1% to 16.8% as a result. [Q4 call]
- Returns: ROCE 17.7%, ROE 15.3% (Screener); these are held down by ₹1,780cr of goodwill. [Screener; AR FY26]

## Deal wins / TCV / clients
- 12-month backlog $310.1M (₹2,935cr) at Jun-26: +3.2% QoQ, +13.3% YoY in $, +25% in ₹. Total order backlog by region: UK & Europe $360.5M (flat QoQ), US $113.5M (+29.8% QoQ, +23.5% YoY), AMEA $54.1M (−6% QoQ, −16% YoY). [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Analyst point (HDFC Securities): excluding the single $25M US deal, backlog was about flat QoQ; management did not dispute this, only said the large-deal pipeline is bigger than before. [Q1 call]
- Largest win: a 5-year, $25M Salesforce Agentforce deal with a US federal security agency; ramp-up expected in H2 FY27. Other wins: an English council Oracle Cloud ERP, four programmes in NHS England's Data Directorate, a 5-year KSA hospital-group Oracle programme, a 5-year KSA transport managed-services deal, a Saudi investment bank. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- AI: 43 AI deals in Q1 with AI order book $64.0M (Q4: 27 deals, $27.5M; Q3: $16.9M); ~45% of new order book came from AI deals. Q4 call noted average AI deal size was then only ~$1M. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Client concentration rising, not falling: top-5 32.0% of revenue (33.9% Q4, 34.7% a year ago), top-10 44.7%. Top-5 revenue fell 4% QoQ and 12% YoY because an NHS England project ended before its replacement started. Clients billing >$1M: 69 (75 a year ago); active clients 309 (326 in Q4); ~400 "active customers" in total. Top 30–40 clients give 65–70% of revenue. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Pricing: renewal discounts "generally 15%", 20–25% in one or two cases; new deals priced aggressively. [Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (20 Apr 2026): no numeric revenue guidance; FY27 "will be a better performance than the previous year"; EBITDA to stay at "16%-16.1%"; tax rate 24.5–24.7%; FCA deal "fully ramped up by the end of Q1"; ATLAS and Biometric renewals to ramp "between Q1 and Q2"; North America to show "positive revenue growth as we step into FY27"; DSO to stay near 73 days. [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: EBITDA 15.4% — **missed** the 16% anchor; FCA still ramping into Q2 (**slipped**); ATLAS/HADES are renewals with no ramp (**no uplift**); NA +2% cc QoQ but management now says it becomes a growth engine only "by H2" (**pushed out**); DSO 75 (**slightly missed**); effective tax ~23.5% (in line). [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 FY27 call outlook: FY27 better than FY26 kept; no full-year margin guidance; Q2 hit by wage increments plus a new ESOP cost of $0.4–0.5M a quarter (~0.4–0.5% of revenue) from Q2; aims to offset "a significant portion" through cost savings. Middle East: at best "flat" if won projects ramp. [Q1 call]
- Longer target: North America margin to reach "mid-teens" once revenue reaches $28–30M a quarter (currently ~$23M a quarter). [Q1 call, derived from 21.7% × $104.8M]

## Capex and capacity
- Asset-light: FY26 fixed assets ₹1,938cr are mostly goodwill (₹1,780cr); capital commitments only ₹1.1cr. [Screener; AR FY26]
- Capital use is M&A: CEO said M&A will target "domain capabilities" in core verticals plus small AI start-up stakes. ICRA flags debt-funded acquisitions as a key monitorable. [Q4 call; ICRA Mar-26]
- Goodwill by unit: North America ₹1,333cr, UK & Europe ₹360cr, AMEA ₹88cr. The discount rate used for the NA impairment test was raised to 15% (11.5% in FY25). NA goodwill is 1.6× NA's FY26 revenue and NA earned only ₹51cr segment profit — the auditor made goodwill impairment a key audit matter. ₹114cr of Taistech (US) goodwill was already written off in FY25. [AR FY26 note 3(c); AR FY26 auditor's report]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Cash and investments ₹945cr at Jun-26 (₹938cr Mar-26, ₹622cr Mar-25); borrowings ₹438cr at Mar-26 (₹583cr Mar-25), mostly acquisition loans; net cash ≈ ₹500cr. Q1 repaid ₹22.8cr of debt and paid ₹107cr variable pay; operating cash in Q1 only ~₹27cr. [IP Q1FY27; Screener; Q1 call]
- ICRA: debt $16.5M (~₹150cr) due in FY27; debt/OPBITDA 1.0×, interest cover 16.3× (H1 FY26). [ICRA Mar-26]
- Cash flow from operations: FY24 ₹420cr, FY25 ₹396cr, FY26 ₹542cr (+35%) — about 1.3× net profit in FY26. [Screener; Q4 call]
- DSO 75 days (73 Q4, 82 a year ago); Screener debtor days 57 (FY26). $2.5M of collections slipped by 2–3 days into July. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; Screener]
- Dividend FY26 ₹24/share (₹8 interim + ₹16 final), payout ~18%. [Q4 call; Screener]
- Hedges: £22.6M at ₹120.3 and $7.6M at ₹91.8 for the next 12 months. [IP Q1FY27]

## Moat evidence
- Three decades serving the UK public sector (Home Office, NHS, local government, FCA); public sector grew 7.9% QoQ in Q1; UK frameworks give 3–5 year visibility. [Q1 call; Q4 call; ICRA Mar-26]
- UK & Europe earns ~20% segment margin while NA and AMEA earn 5–6% — the moat is local, not global. [AR FY26]
- Oracle Cloud depth: Everest "Major Contender and Star Performer" (Oracle Cloud Apps 2026), Gartner market guide listing; 200+ healthcare providers served; "123 entities live in 12 months". These are recognitions, not share data. [IP Q1FY27]
- Switching costs are being tested: the CEO said incumbents are "challeng[ing] us on our turf" and renewals come at 10–15% discounts. [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- UK government concentration: ~43% of revenue from Government & Education; a new UK political leadership is reviewing priorities and budgets. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; ICRA Mar-26]
- Goodwill risk: ₹1,333cr on North America, a unit making ~6% margins with falling revenue; a write-down would hit book value (₹965/share) though not cash. [AR FY26; Screener]
- Middle East: bench costs, delayed collections and a doubtful-debt provision in Q1; AMEA backlog down 16% YoY. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Tax: FY23 assessment added ₹123.5cr to income (₹91cr transfer pricing, ₹32.5cr domestic), tax effect ~₹31cr; appeal filed 26 Jun 2026. Contingent income-tax demands rose to ₹36.7cr at Mar-26 (₹5.6cr Mar-25) plus ₹9.4cr sales tax and ₹7.1cr employment claims. [Q1 call; AR FY26 note 34]
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; emphasis of matter only on restating standalone accounts for the merger of Mastek Enterprise Solutions (common-control, no consolidated impact). Key audit matters: revenue recognition on fixed-price contracts and goodwill impairment. [AR FY26]
- Ownership overhang: Mint (Feb 2026) reported Zensar in talks to buy an 18–20% promoter stake for $200–400M, with promoters seeking nearly double the then market value; Persistent and ChrysCapital reportedly looked and walked away. No deal announced. The CEO (Evosys founder) holds 5.48% as a non-promoter. [News: Mint via M&A Critique, Feb 2026; AR FY26]
- Promoters 35.77%, no pledge shown. FIIs fell from 11.2% (Sep-25) to 7.5% (Jun-26); DIIs rose from 11.5% to 19.9%. [Screener]
- AI deflation: management admits AI lets clients do the same work with less effort, driving discounts; headcount to grow slower than revenue. [Q4 call]

## My read
Mastek is a good UK public-sector business (≈20% margin, sticky, growing) attached to a North America unit that has shrunk, earns ~6% and carries ₹1,333cr of goodwill, plus a Middle East unit now hit by the West Asia crisis. Dollar revenue is flat to down year-on-year and the 16% margin anchor has already slipped, so the rupee EPS growth (~15%) partly reflects currency and lower interest. At a low earnings multiple with ~₹500cr net cash, the price needs only low single-digit dollar growth and stable ~15% margins. The view improves if the $25M US deal ramps in H2 and NA reaches $28–30M a quarter; it worsens on a UK spending review cut, a North America goodwill write-down, or margins falling below 15% after wage hikes and ESOPs.
