# Newgen Software — deep research (Phase A1)

Research date: 27 Sep 2026. ₹ crore (cr) unless stated. Annuity = SaaS/subscription + ATS/AMC (annual licence maintenance fees) + support. ATS = annual technical support.
Transcript check: the stored Q1 FY27 transcript is the official BSE filing. The stored Q4 FY26 transcript is an AI-generated copy (tijoristack); it was checked against the official BSE transcript (call of 30 Apr 2026) and differs in places (see Stored-data errors).

## Business and segments
- Indian enterprise software product company. NewgenONE platform: content management (ECM, "OmniDocs"), process automation/low-code (BPM), customer communications (CCM), document AI and an "Agent Studio"; sold as vertical products — loan origination and collections, trade finance, account opening, insurance policy administration, healthcare payer tools, government records. ~500 active customers in ~79 countries. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- Revenue mix Q1 FY27: support 30%, ATS/AMC 24%, SaaS/subscription 17%, sale of licences 16%, implementation & other services 13% — annuity 71% (₹254cr, +14% YoY). FY26 annuity ₹968cr = 62% (56% in FY25): SaaS ~12%, ATS/AMC ~21%, support ~28%. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call official]
- **Licence vs subscription**: India and Middle East buy perpetual licences plus ATS (lumpy, recognised upfront); US, UK and Australia buy subscriptions/SaaS (ratable). Subscription revenue FY26 ₹525cr (+24%); SaaS +36% in FY26 and +40% in Q1 FY27 (~₹60cr a quarter). Indian licence revenue fell 20–30% in FY26. [Q4 call official; Q1 call]
- Geography Q1 FY27: EMEA ₹114cr (32%, +10%), India ₹96cr (27%, roughly flat by derivation), USA ₹92cr (25%, +27%), APAC ₹56cr (16%, +12%). FY26: EMEA 31%, India 29%, USA 23%, APAC 17%; exports 61% of revenue. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Sep-26]
- Verticals Q1 FY27: banking & financial services ₹225cr (63%, +5%), insurance & healthcare ₹79cr (22%, +58%), government 6%, others 9%. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- People: ~4,200 staff at Q1 FY27, flat on FY26 and down from ~4,500–4,600 in FY24–FY25 (FY26 headcount −6–7% organically while hiring ~400 campus recruits). R&D ~9% of revenue, sales & marketing ~26% in Q1 (22% in FY26). [Q1 call; Q4 call official]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated)
- Annual: revenue FY24 1,244 → FY25 1,487 → FY26 1,574 (+6%); OPM 23% → 25% → 26%; net profit 252 → 315 → 301 (FY26 after ₹43.4cr exceptional items: Labour Codes ₹30.1cr and a Qatar legal claim ₹13.4cr; ex-exceptional PAT ₹334cr, +6%). TTM revenue 1,610, profit 314. [Screener; AR FY26; IP Q1FY27]
- Quarters (sales / OPM / net profit): Jun-25 321 / 14% / 50; Sep-25 401 / 26% / 82; Dec-25 400 / 27% / 63 (after exceptional); Mar-26 453 / 34% / 106; Jun-26 357 / 16% / 63. Strong Q4 seasonality (March licence closures). [Screener]
- Q1 FY27: revenue +11.2% YoY, EBITDA ₹56cr (15.7% vs 14.0%), PAT ₹63cr (+26%). **Other income ₹36cr was ~44% of PBT** (treasury and mark-to-market gains) — PAT growth leans on it. [IP Q1FY27; Screener; Q1 call]
- 5-year revenue CAGR 19%, profit CAGR 21%; 3-year sales CAGR 17%, but TTM only 8%. ROCE 25%, ROE 19.6%. [Screener]
- Unit economics: revenue per employee ≈ ₹37 lakh a year (FY26 revenue ÷ ~4,300 staff); customers billing over ₹5cr rose 87 → 101 in FY26. [Q4 call official, derived]

## ARR, bookings, customers and churn
- No ARR, RPO or churn figures are disclosed. An analyst asked for RPO (remaining performance obligation); management said subscription is still only "12%, 13%" of revenue and it may disclose "in future course". Deferred revenue rose from ~₹220cr to ~₹300cr in Q4 FY26 (US/UK/Australia subscriptions and large ATS renewals). [Q1 call; Q4 call official]
- Order book FY26 +13% YoY; bookings in Q1 "growing like double digits" (no number). New logos: 47 in FY26; 10 in Q1 FY27 (below the usual 12–13; management says deal sizes are larger). [Q4 call official; Q1 call]
- Q1 wins: Kuwait insurance policy-administration system ₹26.7cr; Philippines retail loan origination ₹16.2cr; Annapurna Finance AI loan origination/collections ₹15.6cr; UK ECM ₹14.5cr. Sept 2026: Qatar regulatory-reporting platform $1.79M (₹17.1cr). [IP Q1FY27; BSE filing 21 Sep 2026]
- Concentration is low: top-10 customers ~20% of revenue. [CRISIL Sep-26]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (30 Apr 2026): no FY27 revenue number ("give us one more quarter"); net margin ~21% to be held, with expansion only if growth reaches the "higher teens"; Middle East disruption to be worked through "in Q1 and Q2"; receivables to be cut "aggressively"; India "worst is over"; AI deflation of 2–3% expected on implementation/support streams (~40% of revenue). [Q4 call official]
- Q1 FY27 (16 Jul 2026) delivery: revenue +11% — **ahead** of the cautious tone; annuity +14%, SaaS +40% — **met**; implementation revenue fell ~23% (≈₹12cr short) on delayed project starts in EMEA and India — **missed**, promised catch-up in Q2–Q3; India ~flat — **not yet** "growth"; debtor days 145 vs 164 in Q4 but 123 a year ago — **partly met**. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- New statements: "hopeful that we will maintain a double-digit growth" (not formal guidance); margins — CFO said the company "usually" makes 23–25% EBITDA for the year and "will surely maintain this 20% kind of number" (unclear whether EBITDA or net). [Q1 call]
- Independent check: CRISIL (Sep 2026) projects FY27 revenue of only ₹1,600–1,700cr, i.e. ~2–8% growth — below management's hope. [CRISIL Sep-26]

## Capex and capacity
- Asset-light: fixed assets ₹239cr (own offices), depreciation ~₹37cr a year; FY26 free cash flow ₹223cr on CFO ₹232cr. No capacity constraint — the constraint is sales closures and implementation staffing. [Screener]
- Acquisitions "deliberating for last couple of years" without a suitable target. [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Cash and liquid investments ₹1,115cr (₹412cr cash + ₹703cr liquid funds) at March 2026 ≈ 16% of the ₹6,916cr market value; borrowings ₹39cr (leases); debt/equity 0.00x. [CRISIL Sep-26; Screener]
- **Receivables are the weak spot**: debtor days 130 → 137 → 164 (FY24–FY26); Q1 FY27 145 days vs 123 a year earlier. CRISIL's downgrade trigger is net receivables "above 140 days" — Q1 is above it. CRISIL puts the gross current-asset cycle at 396 days. Management blames slow Middle East collections after regional conflict, and says ECL provisioning covers risk. [Screener; IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Sep-26; Q4 call official]
- Cash conversion: CFO/operating profit 117% → 76% → 86% (FY24–FY26). The official Q4 transcript says CFO "INR302 crores", but the presentation and Screener show ₹232cr (₹2,318M) — use ₹232cr. [Screener; IP Q1FY27]
- Dividend ₹6/share for FY26 (payout 28%). Analysts asked for a buyback on both calls; management "given those input to Board". [Q4 call official; Q1 call; Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Switching costs: loan origination, trade finance and records systems sit inside bank workflows; ATS/support grows even when licence sales fall (annuity rose from 56% to 62% of revenue in one year). [Q4 call official]
- Analyst recognition: Forrester Wave "Leader" in Content Platforms (Q1 2025); Gartner "Niche Player" in Business Orchestration & Automation; multiple Gartner market-guide listings. 67 patent filings, 25 granted. [IP Q1FY27]
- Wallet share: ₹5cr+ customers 87 → 101 in FY26; US revenue +17% in FY26 and +27% in Q1 FY27, showing it can win in mature markets against Pega/Appian-type rivals. [Q4 call official; Q1 call]
- Pricing: management says there is "no such demand of any effort or cost efficiencies from our customers" on support, but AI pricing models are "work in progress". [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Receivables and Middle East exposure**: 145–164 debtor days; EMEA is the biggest region; CRISIL's downgrade trigger already crossed on Q1 numbers. [CRISIL Sep-26; IP Q1FY27]
- **Implementation delivery risk**: a Qatar bank sued over "non delivery of contracted deliverables"; Newgen lost at the Court of Cassation and booked ₹13.4cr (refund $1.37M plus costs) as an exceptional item in FY26. [AR FY26 note 32A]
- **Leadership change**: CEO Virender Jeet resigned in June 2026 ("personal and professional reasons"); COO Tarun Nandwani (33 years at Newgen) became CEO and Pramod Kumar Chief Growth Officer from 1 Aug 2026. [Q1 call; News: Business Standard, 10 Jun 2026]
- **Earnings quality**: Q1 profit growth helped by ₹36cr other income; licence revenue lumpy; no ARR/RPO disclosure. [Screener; Q1 call]
- **Promoter family**: promoter holding 53.45%; CMD Diwakar Nigam paid 79x median salary, wife (whole-time director) 27x; daughter joined the board as non-executive director in Nov 2025. Contingent liabilities tiny (income-tax demands ₹3.1cr). Auditor: unmodified opinion; key audit matter on percentage-of-completion revenue for fixed-price implementation contracts; no emphasis of matter found. [AR FY26]
- **Ownership drift**: FIIs cut from 20.3% (Dec 2024) to 13.8% (Jun 2026); stock −53% from its 52-week high. [Screener]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone: Q4 was defensive (Middle East war, "AI uncertainty" slowing large Indian bank deals, refused to give a number); Q1 upbeat ("AI demand is coming with all RFPs"), double-digit growth "hoped", but new CEO speaking.
- Numbers: SaaS acceleration continued (+36% FY26 → +40% Q1); licence had a "bumper" Q1; implementation fell sharply; debtor days eased from 164 to 145.
- New issues: CEO exit; implementation slippage; margin language shifted from "21% net margin" to a muddled "20% kind of number".

## Best management quotes
- "In this kind of environment, projecting a number for next year may not be advisable." — Virender Jeet, CEO [Q4 call official]
- "We are hopeful that we will maintain a double-digit growth." — management [Q1 call]
- "DSO is a challenge we recognize." — Tarun Nandwani, COO/CEO-designate [Q1 call]

## My read
The product franchise is intact — annuity revenue keeps compounding, SaaS is growing ~40% and US growth is strong — but the lumpy India/Middle East licence business and the receivables problem keep the company's growth near high single digits and tie up cash. The stock at about half its peak prices in modest growth; it needs roughly low-teens earnings growth, which requires India licences to recover and implementation to catch up in Q2–Q3. Debtor days back below 130, a visible ARR/RPO disclosure, or a buyback would improve the view; another quarter of slipping implementation, receivables above 150 days, or a CRISIL outlook change would weaken it.
