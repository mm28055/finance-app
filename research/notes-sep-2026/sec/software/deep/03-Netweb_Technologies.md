# Netweb Technologies — deep research (Sep 2026)

Screener code NETWEB (standalone; consolidated page is empty). Price ₹4,545, market cap ₹27,019 cr, P/E 104, ROCE 37.5%, ROE 32.8% [Screener, 25–27 Sep 2026].
Transcript check: the stored Q4 FY26 transcript is the official BSE filing. The stored Q1 FY27 transcript is AI-generated (tijoristack). I checked it against the official BSE filing dated 1 Aug 2026. Numbers mostly match; differences are listed under Stored-data errors.

## Business and segments
- Netweb designs and makes "high-end computing systems" under the Tyrone brand at Faridabad. It has three pillars that make up more than 95% of revenue [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]:
  - **AI systems**: GPU-dense servers, liquid cooling and orchestration software.
  - **HPC**: supercomputers for the National Supercomputing Mission (NSM), ISRO, IITs and C-DAC.
  - **Private cloud/HCI**: hyper-converged data-centre stacks.
- **Q1 FY27 segment revenue:**
  - AI systems ₹510.6 cr (62.3% of revenue, +484% YoY).
  - Private cloud ₹135.3 cr (16.5%).
  - HPC ₹125.3 cr (15.3%).
  - Rest: storage, data-centre servers and software/services [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call official].
- **FY26:** revenue ₹2,184 cr; AI was 43.4% of revenue (+460% YoY) [Q4 call].
- **Customers:** about 50% government/PSU/research and 50% enterprise, including TCS, Infosys, Yotta and ISRO [Q4 call; CRISIL Jun-26].
  - Top-5 customers are 65–70% of revenue [CRISIL Jun-26].
  - Three customers each contributed 10% or more of FY26 revenue (two in FY25) [AR FY26 Note on major customers].
  - This is very high concentration.
- **Geography:** almost all domestic. Exports were ₹30.5 cr in FY26 (1.4%), down from ₹62 cr in FY25. Management has stopped chasing exports [AR FY26 revenue note; Q1 call].
- **Suppliers:** the top 3 (Nvidia, AMD, Intel, Samsung and similar) are 65–70% of purchases, mostly sourced from Taiwan. Only about 30% of procurement is from India [CRISIL Jun-26; AR FY26 BRSR].
- **Capacity:** "capability-driven, not capacity-driven". Utilisation is about 65–70%. Current facilities can support ₹3,000 cr+ of revenue. A new 15,000 sq ft line for liquid-cooled racks (150 kW+/rack) was commissioned in Q4 [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- R&D team of about 125 people. R&D is 2–3% of revenue and fully expensed [Q1 call; CRISIL].

## Guidance history
- **Q4 FY26 call (4 May) promised:**
  - 35–40% revenue growth for "the next couple of years". This was ambiguous: the CFO called it "enterprise-level", but IR advisor Sanjeev Sancheti said it excluded the strategic order.
  - 13–14% EBITDA margin.
  - No significant capex; routine ₹20–25 cr.
  - Cash conversion cycle of 90–110 days.
  - The ₹1,600 cr strategic AI order to be executed "within the next 3 quarters".
  - Exports steady at about 5% [Q4 call].
- **Q1 FY27 delivered:**
  - Revenue +172% YoY: far ahead of guidance.
  - Margin 14.7%: above the range.
  - About ₹430 cr of the ₹1,600 cr strategic order executed. Q1 needed roughly ₹530 cr a quarter to finish in 3 quarters, so this was slightly slower. **Mostly met.**
  - Cash conversion cycle 96 days: within range.
  - Management now says "strategic is the new normal", stopped giving a separate growth number, and restated the 13–14% margin. The revenue growth guidance was **dropped**, not raised [Q1 call official].
- **Order book:** firm ₹2,507 cr plus L1 (lowest bidder, not yet contracted) of ₹848 cr, about ₹3,355 cr in total. At Q4 it was ₹2,100 cr + ₹300–350 cr L1 [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call].
  - Pipeline is "upwards of ₹10,000 cr" and now includes strategic deals. Last quarter's ₹4,400 cr excluded them, so the two are not comparable.
  - Pipeline converts about 60% over 18–24 months [Q1 call official].
- **Execution time:** 16–20 weeks. The Q4 call had already said "10 to 20 weeks, 18–20 for organic", so this is not a new slip [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- Capex is small: ₹20–25 cr a year of routine spend and no major capex planned. FY26 investing outflow of ₹216 cr on Screener was mostly deposits/treasury [Q4 call; Screener].
- Growth needs **working capital**, not plant. The board took an enabling resolution to raise up to ₹1,200 cr (debt, equity or both), valid for 12 months, "largely for working capital, no M&A". Nothing had been raised as of the Q1 call [Q1 call official].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net cash was ₹124 cr at Mar-26 (restated; the Q4 call said ₹83 cr). This turned into net debt of ₹200 cr at Jun-26. Free cash fell from about ₹407 cr to about ₹73 cr, and borrowings were about ₹263 cr [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call].
- Net debt/equity 0.25x; net debt/EBITDA 0.41x [IP Q1FY27].
- Working-capital days (company definitions):
  - Receivable days went 87 (Mar-25) → 86 (Mar-26) → 78 (Jun-26).
  - Inventory days went 53 → 86 → 110.
  - Payable days went 67 → 88 → 92.
  - Cash conversion cycle went 73 → 84 → 96 days [IP Q1FY27].
- On Screener's year-end definitions, FY26 inventory days were 170 and the cash conversion cycle 121. CRISIL puts gross current assets at 268 days [Screener; CRISIL Jun-26].
- Inventory build-up is "a hedge, not a risk", made deliberately against memory/HBM shortages [Q1 call].
- **Cash flow:** FY26 CFO was ₹790 cr on Screener, but the company said ₹171.5 cr. Screener's figure is likely lifted by supplier credit and advances at year-end. The gap is unexplained, so treat FY26 cash flow as unclear. FY25 CFO was −₹13 cr [Screener; Q4 call].
- Bank lines were raised from ₹700 cr to ₹2,420 cr, of which ₹2,040 cr is working capital. Average fund-based utilisation was only 24%. There is no term debt. CRISIL rates Netweb A+/Stable/A1 (upgraded in 2025, reaffirmed Jun-26) [CRISIL Jun-26].
- Dividend: ₹3/share for FY26 (yield 0.07%) [Q4 call; Screener].

## Moat evidence
- It is one of the few Indian-origin OEMs with in-house motherboard, chassis and firmware design. It built Kabru, PARAM Yuva II and 300+ HPC systems. This qualifies it for "design and make in India" rules in NSM 2.0 and sovereign-AI tenders [CRISIL; Q1 call].
- Early access to Nvidia, AMD and Intel roadmaps, 9–12 months ahead, and readiness for new GPU platforms such as B300 and Vera Rubin [CRISIL; Q4 call].
- Share: management claims it is "shipping the highest number of large GPU deals" in India, but no market-share figure is given. The Lattice report in the presentation sizes India's AI market at ₹323 bn in FY26 growing to ₹1,143 bn by FY30 (37% CAGR), HPC at ₹71 bn → ₹112 bn (12%), and private cloud/HCI at ₹457 bn → ₹919 bn (19%) [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- Pricing: margins held at 13–15% while memory prices rose, with pass-through on new bids [Q1 call]. This is not strong pricing power, since margins stay low in absolute terms.
- Weak points: low switching cost against global OEMs (Dell, HPE, Supermicro), and heavy reliance on a few GPU suppliers.

## Risks and red flags
- **Concentration:** top-5 customers are 65–70% of revenue, three customers are each above 10%, and top-3 suppliers are 65–70% of purchases. The business depends on IndiaAI Mission spending and a handful of neo-clouds such as Yotta [CRISIL; AR FY26].
- **Lumpiness and working capital:** 30–40% of annual revenue lands in Q4. Inventory days jumped to 110. The company swung from net cash to net debt in one quarter and may need to raise fresh capital [CRISIL; IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- **Promoter selling:** promoters sold about 4% in February 2026, citing liquidity, and promised no further sale for 12 months. Promoter holding went from 75.04% (Mar-24) to 66.98% (Jun-26) [Q4 call; Screener].
- **Accounting:** inventory valuation changed from FIFO to moving weighted average from 1 Apr 2025, with prior periods restated (said to be immaterial) [AR FY26 Note 53].
  - The FY26 CFO figure is inconsistent between Screener (₹790 cr) and management (₹171.5 cr).
  - Revenue recognition on multi-element contracts is a key audit matter. No qualification or emphasis of matter was found [AR FY26 auditor's report].
  - Contingent liabilities are mainly bank guarantees of ₹136 cr, plus about ₹1 cr of tax [AR FY26].
- **Disclosure:** the pipeline definition changed in Q1 (strategic deals merged in), so like-for-like comparison is impossible. The official transcript also misprints the pipeline as "INR10,401 million".
- **Valuation risk:** the stock trades at about 104x trailing earnings on a low-margin hardware model.

## What changed between the two calls
- The Q4 tone was confident but defensive. It separated the ₹1,600 cr strategic order from the "organic" business, gave 35–40% growth guidance and stressed zero net debt.
- By Q1, revenue and margins were at records. But net debt appeared, inventory rose from 86 to 110 days, and a ₹1,200 cr enabling resolution had been taken.
- Management merged strategic into normal business and stopped quantifying growth. It added physical AI and quantum simulators as R&D bets.
- Management was curt with questions about capital raising, e.g. "I don't know where you got this news for the QIP".

## Best management quotes
- "The inventory-led approach has actually acting as a hedge, not a risk." — Ankit Singhal, CFO [Q1 call]
- "There is no strategic order now. All orders are normal orders actually. The strategic is the new normal." — management [Q1 call]

## My read
Demand is real. The order book plus L1 of about ₹3,355 cr exceeds FY26 revenue, and India's AI build-out is policy-backed. Growth is therefore likely to stay very high in FY27, with TTM revenue probably above ₹3,000 cr. But this is a 13–15% margin integrator whose growth now eats cash and may need equity. It depends on a few customers and a few GPU suppliers. At about 104x trailing earnings, the price needs EPS to compound at roughly 40%+ for several years. That depends on sovereign-AI orders repeating after the current wave. I would get more comfortable if Q2/Q3 show inventory days back under 90 and positive operating cash flow without a big raise. A QIP at these levels, or a quarter with no new large orders, would be the warning signs.
