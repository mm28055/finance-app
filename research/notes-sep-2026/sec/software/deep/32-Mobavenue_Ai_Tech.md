# Mobavenue AI Tech (formerly Lucent Industries) — deep research (Phase A1)

Research date: 27 Sep 2026. ₹ crore (cr) unless stated. BSE-only listing (scrip 539682). Both stored transcripts are the company's official BSE filings (checked) — no transcript errors found. FY25 figures are restated under common-control accounting from 4 Sep 2024, so FY25 is a part-year and not comparable.

## Business and segments
- Programmatic ad-tech platform (a "DSP", demand-side platform that bids for ad slots in real time) sold on an outcome basis: advertisers pay per install, purchase, lead or re-engagement delivered, not per impression. Products grouped as "A3": Awareness (PrsmX — mobile, CTV, OTT, DOOH), Acquisition (SurgeX Android, PiiX Apple Search Ads launched Q1 FY27, DiscoverX/AmplifyX OEM), Activation (ResurgeX retargeting). [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- 155+ active brands in 12 countries; 200+ employees; offices in Mumbai, Delhi, Gurgaon, Bengaluru, London, New York, Singapore. Top verticals: quick commerce, fintech, BFSI, retail, travel — top 5 sectors are 70–80% of revenue. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Channel mix Q1 FY27: direct advertisers 65.2% (73.9% in FY26), agencies/resellers 34.8%. Geography: India 79.3%, international 20.7% (11.5% in FY26). The jump came from "a global client onboarded through our reseller/agency channel" — a ₹10.02cr order won by the UK subsidiary (announced 18 Jun 2026). [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.7, p.11; News: Sahi, 18 Jun 2026]
- Operating KPIs: Q1 FY27 14.16 mn verified outcomes (+57% YoY) at ₹49.94 per outcome (+11% YoY; ₹48.44 in Q4, ₹44.99 in Q1 FY26); FY26 42.72 mn outcomes. 1.3 bn signals/day, ~2.6 bn devices reached, bid decisions in <15 ms. [IP Q1FY27 p.10; Q4 call]
- Corporate history: Lucent Industries (education, then goods trading shell) was taken over via open offer by Mobavenue's promoters (Sep 2024); the listed company then bought 100% of Mobavenue Media Pvt Ltd (MMPL) from the same promoters on 3 Sep 2025 for ₹59.68cr; renamed Mobavenue AI Tech; board approved merging MMPL into the listed company on 15 Sep 2026 (fast-track, sec. 233). [AR FY26 note 35/46; BSE board outcome 15 Sep 2026]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated)
- Revenue FY25 87 (part-year, restated) → FY26 218; OPM 17% → 21%; net profit 10 → 29. TTM revenue 245, TTM PAT 35. [Screener]
- Quarters (sales / OPM / net profit): Jun-25 46 / 19% / 6; Sep-25 54 / 20% / 7; Dec-25 55 / 22% / 8; Mar-26 63 / 21% / 8; Jun-26 73 / 21% / 12. Revenue, EBITDA and PAT rose every quarter. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27 (company): revenue ₹72.8cr (+56.9% YoY, +16.3% QoQ); EBITDA ₹15.4cr (21.2%, +77%); PAT ₹11.7cr (16.1%, +95%) — PAT helped by other income of ₹2.2cr (interest on the unspent ₹50cr raise) vs ₹0.4cr in Q4. [IP Q1FY27 p.12]
- Cost structure Q1 FY27: supply & data cost ₹44.0cr (60.4% of revenue; 62.5% a year ago) — so gross margin ~40%; employee cost ₹8.4cr (₹4.4cr a year ago); other expenses ₹5.0cr. FY26: data cost ₹131.6cr (60%), employees ₹25.9cr, other ₹15.6cr, EBITDA ₹45.4cr. [IP Q1FY27 p.12; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Unit economics: revenue per outcome ~₹50; after ~60% data/supply cost, gross profit ~₹20 per outcome. [IP Q1FY27; derived]
- Returns: ROCE 72%, ROE 58% on a small equity base (book value ₹11.7/share; net worth ₹90.6cr). [Screener; AR FY26]

## Guidance history
- Stated framework, unchanged across both calls: "Rule of 50" — revenue growth above 30% a year with EBITDA margin 20%+, "not a one-year target" but the long-run shape; FY27 outlook "directional rather than formal guidance". No numeric FY27 revenue target. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Q4 promises vs Q1 delivery: sequential momentum (**met**: +16% QoQ); margin ≥20% (**met**: 21.2%); deepen UK/LATAM and add markets (**met**: US and Singapore offices, Philippines PrsmX launch); new purpose-built platforms (**met**: PiiX); M&A "at the right price" (**not yet** — ₹50cr raise still unspent). Direct-client share **fell** from 73.9% to 65.2%, contrary to the Q4 emphasis on direct relationships; CFO says it will revert "for the long term". [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Longer term: "Mission 2030" — a global AI-native platform; 12–18 months for the Neural Engine to make more optimisation decisions autonomously and for PiiX/international to become meaningful. PiiX revenue is "negligible" today. [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Asset-light: fixed assets ₹7cr, CWIP ₹9cr at Mar-26. Capex is capitalised software: auditor flags ₹11.0cr of internally generated intangibles recognised in FY26, consisting of "salary cost, director remuneration, etc." — about a quarter of FY26 EBITDA. Intangibles under development ₹9.2cr at Mar-26. [AR FY26 auditor's report KAM; MD&A; Screener]
- FY26 FCF ₹22cr on CFO ₹35cr (CFO/EBITDA 93%). [Screener]
- Use of ₹50cr preferential raise (4,59,558 shares at ₹1,088 pre-split ≈ ₹218 post-split, to non-promoters, Feb 2026): "entire funds are unutilised" at Mar-26, held in fixed deposits; mainly for acquisitions, plus technology and general purposes. The board had approved up to 9,19,117 shares (~₹100cr); only half was allotted. [AR FY26 note 18, corporate governance report; Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Mar-26: cash ₹39.9cr + current investments ₹30.8cr; bank borrowings ₹8.5cr (cash credit, personally guaranteed by all directors). Net cash ~₹62cr before the deferred consideration still owed to promoters (~₹45.7cr, see Risks) — ~₹16cr after it. [AR FY26 MD&A; note 41]
- Receivables: trade receivables ₹59.3cr; trade receivables plus contract assets (unbilled) ₹87.3cr = ~146 days of FY26 revenue. Screener debtor days 99 (193 in part-year FY25). Working capital is funded by supplier payables (other liabilities ₹102cr). The auditor made receivable recoverability a key audit matter. [AR FY26 KAM; Screener]
- Currency: international 20.7% of revenue is USD/GBP-billed while supply costs are partly dollar-linked; CFO calls revenue "very well balanced between INR and USD" as a natural hedge — overstated given 79% India revenue. [Q1 call]
- Dividend yield 0.1%; promoters voluntarily forgo dividends. No buyback. No credit rating found. [Screener; AR FY26 note 41]

## Moat evidence
- Pricing: revenue per outcome up 11% YoY while outcomes grew 57% — evidence the models are finding higher-value users rather than cutting price. [IP Q1FY27 p.10]
- Retention: ~80% of revenue from retained customers; top-10 clients ~25% of revenue, top-50 ~60–65%; internal cap of 6–7% per client (quick-commerce clients may exceed it). [Q4 call]
- Full-stack ownership: GMP360 stack "100% proprietary" with no revenue-sharing licences; neural models retrained hourly; 50 TB trained in ~1 hour vs 10–12 hours earlier. These are management claims without third-party proof. [Q4 call]
- Awards: gold at ad:tech India, AdTech Solution of the Year and similar trade awards. Industry recognition, not market share. [Q4 call]
- Limits: clients do not give exclusivity ("we do compete with some of the other platforms"); competitors named are AppLovin, Unity, The Trade Desk, Affle and InMobi — all far larger. A ~40% gross margin is thin for a "platform". [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Related-party purchase of the business**: the listed company bought MMPL from its three promoters (Kunal Kothari, Tejas Rathod, Prachi Arora; ₹19.89cr each, ₹59.68cr total), payable over two years; ₹45.7cr (present value) still outstanding at Mar-26. Minority shareholders are funding a cash payout to promoters, while promoters hold 65.6%. [AR FY26 notes 35, 41]
- **Loans to promoter-linked companies**: ₹2.46cr to PD Avenue Marketing and ₹4.0cr to Quid Innovation outstanding at Mar-26 (with interest); both are also customers and suppliers (FY26 supply/data cost ₹4.2cr and ₹1.8cr; sales ₹0.8cr and ₹2.3cr; receivable from Quid ₹3.6cr). [AR FY26 note 41]
- **Capitalised salaries incl. director pay** (₹11cr in FY26) flatter EBITDA; auditor made it a key audit matter. [AR FY26 KAM]
- **Emphasis of matter**: capital remittance to the UK holding company and a step-down subsidiary in **Russia** (Surge Company LLC, set up Apr 2024) still "in process" at Mar-26 — a foreign-exchange compliance gap flagged two years running; the Russian entity also appears in related-party sales. Three foreign subsidiaries' accounts were unaudited. [AR FY26 auditor's report]
- **Concentration**: international share doubled on one reseller-channel global client (₹10cr order); top-5 sectors 70–80% of revenue, heavy in quick commerce/fintech where ad budgets swing. [Q1 call; News: Sahi, Jun 2026]
- **Platform dependence**: Apple/Google privacy changes, walled gardens and supply prices drive the 60% data cost line; management names regulation as the top risk. [Q1 call]
- **Governance depth**: CFO changed on 1 Apr 2026 (co-founder Tejas Rathod moved to CTO; Vijay Basantani appointed); IR advisor changed between calls (Adfactors → Valorem); thin float (4,323 shareholders at Jun-26, 34% public). [AR FY26; Screener]

## What changed between the two calls
- Q4 call stressed direct-client quality (73.9%) and UK/LATAM; Q1 pivoted to US entry (Times Square brand launch), agency/reseller growth and the "Neural Engine" product. Direct share fell 8.7 points in one quarter.
- Numbers improved: revenue growth accelerated from 42% (Q4) to 57% YoY; PAT margin 13.5% → 16.1%; revenue per outcome ₹48.44 → ₹49.94.
- New disclosures on the Q1 call: sector concentration (top-5 sectors 70–80%), named global competitors, currency risk. Not repeated: top-10/top-50 client shares (given only on Q4 call).
- Tone: from "AI-powered" to "AI-native"; heavier on branding and product launches, lighter on numbers (no FY27 target, no cash-flow figure on either call).

## My read
The operating numbers are genuinely strong — five straight quarters of sequential growth, a steady 21% margin, rising price per outcome and positive free cash flow — and Q1 FY27 beat the "Rule of 50" comfortably. But the structure deserves caution: the listed shell paid its own promoters ₹60cr for the operating business, lends small sums to promoter-linked firms that are also clients and suppliers, capitalises part of its salary bill (including directors') and carried receivables plus unbilled revenue of ~146 days. At a price that needs roughly 30%+ growth for years, the stock depends on the international push working beyond one large reseller client. What would improve the view: the deferred consideration paid off cleanly, promoter-entity loans and trades wound down, direct share recovering while international keeps growing, and unbilled revenue falling. What would hurt it: a slowdown in quick-commerce ad budgets, a jump in data costs, or the ₹50cr raise used for another related-party deal.
