# Tata Technologies — deep research (Sep 2026)

Screener code TATATECH. Price ₹703, market cap ₹28,547 cr, ROCE 20.9%, ROE 16.3%, dividend yield 1.2% [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].
Transcript check: both stored transcripts (Q4 FY26 of 4 May 2026 and Q1 FY27 of 17 Jul 2026) are the official BSE filings, with cover letters from the company secretary. They are not AI-generated.

## Business and segments
- Tata Technologies is an engineering R&D (ER&D) services firm for automotive (its largest vertical), aerospace and industrial heavy machinery (IHM) [AR FY26; Q1 call].
- It has two segments:
  - **Services**: product engineering, embedded software/SDV (software-defined vehicle), digital manufacturing. ₹4,256 cr in FY26, 77% of revenue.
  - **Technology Solutions**: resale of PLM (product lifecycle management) software, plus an education business. ₹1,249 cr in FY26, 23% [AR FY26 Note 23].
- Q1 FY27 revenue was $175.4m. Services was $136.6m (+4.3% QoQ, +24.4% YoY cc) and Technology Solutions $38.8m (+4.2% QoQ, +27.9% YoY cc) [Q1 call].
- Verticals, Q1 FY27:
  - Automotive non-anchor clients: $43.9m, +56.3% YoY.
  - Aerospace: $10.2m, +38.1% YoY, run-rate above $40m a year (Airbus, North American engine makers, Air India MRO).
  - IHM: about $15m [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- **Anchor (Tata Motors/JLR) concentration:**
  - Anchor clients were 48.9% of Services revenue in Q1, down 150 bps QoQ [Q1 call].
  - JLR alone billed ₹1,337 cr in FY26, about 24% of group revenue. Tata Motors (CV) added ₹342 cr [AR FY26 related-party note].
  - Top 20 clients are about 88% of the business [Q4 call]. The AR lists top-5 concentration as a key risk [AR FY26 MD&A].
- **Geography (by customer site), FY26:** India ₹2,227 cr (40%), UK ₹1,480 cr (27%), North America ₹1,277 cr (23%), rest of Europe ₹405 cr (7%), rest of world ₹116 cr (2%) [AR FY26 Note 23]. US exposure is therefore about a quarter. Europe was $67.9m in Q1 (+10.1% QoQ), helped by ES-Tec [Q1 call].
- **ES-Tec** (Wolfsburg, Germany; a Volkswagen-centred engineering firm) was bought on 27 Nov 2025.
  - Price: valuation up to €75m; present value of consideration €64.7m (₹671 cr).
  - The deal created ₹365 cr of customer intangibles and ₹407 cr of goodwill [AR FY26].
  - It adds about $9m of revenue a quarter [Q4 call].
- **BMW TechWorks JV** (not consolidated) has 2,000+ engineers. Q1 profit share was ₹9.5 cr (+43.5% QoQ); total contribution including deferred income was ₹17.8 cr [Q1 call].
- **People:** 12,579 staff at Q1-end (−67 QoQ). TTM voluntary attrition was 16.0% (Q4: 16.2%) [Q1 call]. Utilisation is not disclosed.

## Guidance history
- **Q3 → Q4:** Q4 was guided to more than 10% QoQ revenue growth and above 16% margin. It delivered 12.4% cc and 16.0%: **met** [Q4 call].
- **Q4 promises for FY27** [Q4 call]:
  - Double-digit organic cc revenue growth, excluding ES-Tec.
  - Exit FY27 with a margin run-rate above 18%.
  - At least two more Full Vehicle Programs (FVPs) to close in 8–12 weeks.
  - Double-digit growth at both anchor and non-anchor clients.
- **Q1 delivery:**
  - Revenue: +4.3% QoQ cc. Growth guidance was **kept and strengthened** to "strong double-digit", and the CEO now expects H2 growth to be "much greater" than H1 [Q1 call].
  - Margin: +10 bps only. Asked directly about the 18% exit, the CFO said "rather than focusing on any specific margin milestone", promising only QoQ expansion including Q2, despite wage hikes and German restructuring. The 18% target was **softened**, not restated [Q1 call].
  - Deals: a $100m, 5-year Tenneco deal (mostly new business; ramps from Q2), a North American industrial-equipment deal, a range-extender vehicle programme with a global OEM, and an off-highway OEM win. The Japanese FVP had already been announced in April, not in Q1. Management said more deals had closed but did not name them. Delivery on "two more FVPs" is therefore **unclear/partly met** [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Longer term:** a "North Star" of $1bn revenue in 2–3 years, including one or two acquisitions. FY26 revenue was about $650m [Q4 call]. The aerospace target is $100m in about 3 years, from a ~40% historical CAGR [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- The business is asset-light. FY26 CFI of −₹776 cr was mainly ES-Tec plus treasury investments [Screener; AR FY26].
- Capacity is being added through hiring for the Tenneco and FVP ramp-ups ("we will certainly be bringing in additional headcount"). Headcount had been held flat through the downturn to protect delivery capacity [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- AI: the chromosome.ai platform, and 50%+ of engineers are "AI-ready" [Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net cash was ₹1,188 cr at Mar-26 (Q3: ₹524 cr) and ₹880 cr at Jun-26 [Q4 call; Q1 call]. Q1 fell partly on the dividend. Management did not give the full Q1 bridge.
- Borrowings on Screener rose from ₹237 cr to ₹938 cr (Mar-26), including leases. The main item is a ₹569 cr unsecured bank term loan for ES-Tec at SOFR (4.20%) + 1.30%, repayable in 8 half-yearly instalments from May 2027 [AR FY26 borrowings note].
- DSO (days sales outstanding) was 97 days in Q1 (billed 65, unbilled 32), against 95 in Q4 and 111 in Q3. Screener debtor days were 79 in FY26 [Q1 call; Screener].
- Cash conversion: FY26 FCF was ₹742 cr, 87% of EBITDA [Q4 call]; Screener CFO was ₹776 cr.
- Dividend: FY26 total ₹11.70/share (final ₹8.35 + special ₹3.35), the same as FY25. Payout was 87% of FY26 profit [Q4 call; Screener].

## Moat evidence
- A long record of turnkey full-vehicle work, including VinFast and NIO. Management claims 18–24 month vehicle development against 36–48 months at Western OEMs [Q1 call].
- A first full-vehicle programme with a Japanese OEM with which it had no prior relationship [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Strategic partner status: BMW JV (2,000+ engineers), Volvo's consolidated supplier base, Airbus strategic supplier list, JLR's "Visionary Supplier Award" (June 2026) [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Switching costs: multi-year PLM operations deals, such as ownership of a European luxury OEM's enterprise PLM operations (April 2026), and the 5-year Tenneco programme [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Diversification is working: non-anchor auto +56% YoY, and the largest-customer share fell 340 bps between Q1 and Q4 FY26 [Q1 call; AR FY26].
- Cost position: margins are below peers (16% against a pre-FY26 18%). The margin moat is not yet evident.

## Risks and red flags
- **Related-party dependence:** JLR is a Tata Motors PV subsidiary and a promoter-group company. The AGM sought approval for related-party transactions with JLR of up to ₹1,750 cr in FY27, plus other RPT approvals of ₹1,541 cr and ₹652.5 cr [AR FY26 AGM notice]. Pricing is certified at arm's length. The JLR cyber-attack in FY26 showed how a single client event can hit a quarter.
- **Fixed-price execution risk:** percentage-of-completion revenue on fixed-price contracts is a key audit matter. As FVPs and turnkey deals grow, estimate risk grows [AR FY26 auditor's report]. ES-Tec's purchase-price allocation and contingent consideration were also a key audit matter. The auditor's opinion was clean, with no emphasis of matter found [AR FY26].
- **Europe/Germany:** VW and other German OEMs are restructuring. The CFO flagged "temporary headwinds within parts of our Germany business" [Q1 call]. ES-Tec's goodwill of ₹407 cr depends on VW.
- **Margin delivery:** mobilisation costs, Q2 wage hikes and a weaker Technology Solutions mix. The 18% exit is no longer restated [Q1 call].
- Contingent liabilities are small, about ₹41 cr (bonus, income tax, GST, service tax) [AR FY26 Note 32].
- Promoter holding is 55.2% with no pledge shown; FII 6.3% [Screener]. Tata Motors had sold shares at the 2023 IPO. No new selling was seen in this research.

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "inflection point" (Q4) to "breakout year" (Q1). Analysts called it "infectious optimism".
- Growth confidence went up, from double-digit to "strong double-digit" with H2 acceleration.
- Margin commitment went down, from ">18% exit" to "QoQ expansion".
- The Middle East commodity risk, raised in Q4, was not mentioned in Q1. German restructuring was new in Q1.
- IR head Vijay Lohia (Q4) was replaced by Prateek Rampuria (Q1) as call host. It is not clear whether this is a management exit.

## My read
The business is improving after a flat FY24–FY26. Two quarters of real sequential growth, a deal pipeline with named large wins (Tenneco $100m, a Japanese FVP) and falling anchor concentration support the recovery story. Profit, however, is still below FY24 (TTM ₹557 cr against ₹679 cr). The margin goal has been quietly softened while turnkey deals add execution risk. At about 44x trailing earnings, the price needs FY27–FY28 EPS growth of 25%+, i.e. revenue growth in the mid-teens plus margins back to 18%. I would turn more positive if Q2/Q3 show margins of 17%+ with continued 4–5% QoQ cc growth. I would turn negative if margins stall at 16% or JLR/German demand weakens.
