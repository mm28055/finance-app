# Indian listed software & IT services — sector research (Phase A2)

Research date: 27 Sep 2026. $ = US dollar; ₹ crore (cr). Sources tagged in brackets; company facts tagged [deep: NN] come from the company deep-research files in this folder (each built from official filings). Where only a search summary could be read (site blocked), the tag says so.

## 1. Market size and structure
- NASSCOM Strategic Review (Feb 2026): India tech industry revenue **$315 bn in FY26, +6.1%** (FY25 $297 bn). Split: IT services $149 bn, ER&D $63 bn, BPM $59 bn, software products $23 bn, hardware $21 bn. Exports $246 bn (~78%). [NASSCOM SR 2026 via Kotak Neo news, Feb 2026]
- Headcount ~5.95–6.0 mn, **+2.3% (+135,000)** — revenue grew almost 3x faster than people, the clearest sign of AI-led productivity. AI revenue $10–12 bn in FY26; 2 mn+ staff upskilled in AI. [NASSCOM SR 2026]
- GCCs (captive centres of foreign firms): **2,117 GCCs, 3,728 units, $98.4 bn revenue, ~2.36 mn staff** in FY26; count up 32% since FY21; 506 of the Forbes Global 2000 now run India centres; ~half of new GCCs since FY21 were set up AI-first. [Zinnov–NASSCOM India GCC Landscape 2026 via search summary / PR Newswire]
- Listed-company structure (our 44): a small group of profitable mid-tier services exporters; five ER&D firms; four BPM/healthcare-operations firms; ~16 product/platform companies (banking software, SaaS, govtech, fintech); and a fast-growing AI-hardware/cloud and messaging group. Large caps (TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Wipro) set pricing and are the benchmark but are not in the list.

## 2. FY27 demand picture
- **NASSCOM:** FY27 tech-spending growth "range-bound between 5 and 7%", with FY27 the year AI spending "matures" from pilots to re-engineering. [NASSCOM SR 2026 via search summary]
- **Gartner (27 Jul 2026):** world IT spending **$6.37 tn in 2026, +14.2%** — but almost all the acceleration is AI hardware: data-centre systems +62.5% ($822 bn), software +15.5%, IaaS +29.3%, while **IT services grow only +5.3% ($1.57 tn; +3.8% in 2025)**. [Gartner via TelecomTV, Jul 2026]
- **ISG Index Q2 2026 (Jul 2026):** combined ACV $42.4 bn (+43%, record) but driven by cloud/IaaS ($25.8 bn, +78%). **Managed services ACV $10.9 bn, +2.7%**; IT outsourcing $7.7 bn (**−3%**); BPO $2.3 bn (+34%); **ER&D $0.93 bn (−6%)**; new-scope awards a record $8.2 bn (+14%); mega-deals (≥$100 mn ACV) 7 vs 8, ACV −27%. ISG cut nothing but forecasts **2026 managed services +2.1%, XaaS +30%**. ISG's AI chief: "traditional labor-intensive work is increasingly displaced by LLMs with pricing deflation emerging." [ISG Index Q2 2026 via StockTitan]
- **Q1 FY27 results (Apr–Jun 2026):** large caps near zero growth — TCS +0.4% QoQ cc, Infosys +1.0% (FY27 guide 1.5–3.0% cc, EBIT 20–22%), HCLTech −0.5%, Wipro −1.2%. Mid-tiers outgrew them (Coforge, Persistent, Mphasis mid-to-high teens YoY; partly acquisitions); TCV: TCS $9.5 bn, Infosys $3.6 bn, HCLTech $2.4 bn. Brokers describe wins as "vendor consolidation rather than … discretionary spend". [Upstox Q1 FY27 review; Infosys 6-K Jul 2026 via search summary; Business Standard headline 19 Aug 2026]
- Our mid-tier sample confirms the split: Zensar BFS +14.7% YoY cc but TMT −28%; Birlasoft manufacturing weak "for one more quarter"; Expleo cc growth flat; Digitide revenue −3% QoQ. [deep: 12, 16, 39, 41]

## 3. Client-industry spending (US/Europe)
- **BFSI** is the strongest vertical: banks funding AI and core-modernisation; Zensar BFS rose to 48.8% of revenue; Sonata/Birlasoft/Mastek all cite BFSI wins, typically **18–24-month tenures** and cost-take-out framing. [deep: 12, 15, 16, 21]
- **Tech/Hi-tech and communications** are weak (Zensar TMT −28% YoY; R Systems' tech-platform clients 39% of revenue, down from 42%). [deep: 12, 30]
- **Manufacturing/industrial** is split: US industrial, agri, power and data-centre equipment makers are at record profits and outsourcing more engineering (Onward's ₹33cr power-management ODC; Cyient T&M +14.8%), while European discrete manufacturing is soft. [deep: 44, 11]
- **Healthcare payers/providers**: steady outsourcing; Sagility grew ~15% organic vs an industry at 7–8%; IKS legacy 12–13% cc with ~35% margins. [deep: 05, 01]

## 4. GenAI: pricing, headcount, deal sizes
- **Deflation estimate:** Kotak Institutional Equities (25 Aug 2026) — AI productivity could cause **3–3.5% annual revenue deflation** in Indian IT services through FY28; new AI demand not enough to offset before **FY29** ("crossover"). JPMorgan: large caps unlikely to return to 7–8% growth, expects **3–4%**, "L-shaped" recovery possibly beyond FY29. Motilal Oswal: fast deflation could cut large-cap EPS ~10%, but industry growth could reaccelerate to **8–9% in FY28** as AI modernisation scales. [ANI/Kotak via search summary; Business Standard/Motilal via search summary]
- **Headcount:** Q1 FY27 net adds — TCS +9,279 (593,798; attrition 13.6%), Wipro +888 (attrition 13.9%), Infosys −532 (13.0%), HCLTech −3,292 (12.7%). Industry headcount +2.3% vs revenue +6.1%. Attrition is low (12–15%), so wage inflation is contained. [Zee Business/StartupFeed via search summary; NASSCOM SR 2026]
- **Who is hurt most:** application maintenance, testing and voice/back-office BPM (hours-based, repeatable work). Examples: Expleo says AI shrinks testing ticket sizes; Digitide expects headcount to fall and is walking away from low-margin BPM; Onward's MD says clients "have already removed" AI-addressable work from outsourcing budgets. [deep: 39, 41, 44]
- **Who benefits:** data/AI engineering (Fractal, LatentView), cloud/data-centre build-out (E2E, Netweb, Black Box), and platforms that own a system of record (Unicommerce, Nucleus, Intellect). [deep: 09, 20, 08, 03, 07, 42, 34, 14]
- **Deal sizes/tenure:** fewer mega-deals (ISG: 7 in Q2, ACV −27%); more mid-size, new-scope, 1.5–3-year deals; renewals repriced with productivity give-backs. [ISG Q2 2026; deep: 15, 16]

## 5. GCC competition
- GCCs ($98.4 bn revenue) now take work that used to be outsourced; many are AI-first. For mid-tiers this is both a threat (in-sourcing) and a channel (build-operate-transfer, staffing GCC projects — e.g. Onward bills ~13% of revenue in India to global clients' Indian centres). [Zinnov–NASSCOM 2026; deep: 44]

## 6. US policy: H-1B fee and outsourcing tax
- **H-1B $100,000 fee** (Sep 2025 proclamation): struck down by US District Judge Leo Sorokin (Boston) as an unlawful tax not approved by Congress; the First Circuit refused to pause the ruling (24 Jul 2026); appeal continues. DHS then **proposed a $103,265 per-petition fee by rulemaking (late Aug 2026, 30-day comment period)** — legal challenges expected. Wage-based lottery bills (American White-Collar Worker Jobs Act; End H-1B Visa Abuse Act) pending. [Investing.com/Trade Brains; Whalesbook, Aug–Sep 2026 via search summaries]
- **HIRE Act (S.2976, Sen. Moreno, Oct 2025):** 25% excise tax on "outsourcing payments" plus loss of deduction — raises effective cost of $100 of offshore services from ~$79 to ~$125. Status: **stalled in Senate Finance Committee** (no new co-sponsors as of Feb 2026). A tail risk, not a base case. [RSM; congress.gov; Penbrothers via search summary]
- Mid-tiers and ER&D firms with mostly offshore delivery and local US hiring are less exposed than large caps; on-site-heavy staffing models are most exposed.

## 7. Rupee
- Rupee **₹95.80/$ on 25 Sep 2026** (record low ~₹96.8 earlier in 2026), weakened by a Middle East conflict and Brent above $100; average ₹94.71/$ in Q1 FY27 vs ₹85.57 a year earlier (~11% depreciation). [Outlook Business/HDFC Sky, 25 Sep 2026; deep: 11]
- Effect: a large one-off lift to rupee revenue and margins (street estimate: +40–320 bps EBIT YoY for the top six in Q4 FY26). Much of our sample's "double-digit" rupee growth is currency: e.g. Onward, Cyient and Expleo growth is far lower in constant currency. Risk: clients ask for price give-backs; the tailwind reverses if the rupee recovers. [Search summary of street estimates; deep: 11, 39, 44]

## 8. Margins, utilisation, attrition
- Margin tiers (EBIT/OPM, latest): large caps 16–24% EBIT; mid-tier services 10–20% OPM (Zensar, Sonata, Birlasoft mid-teens; Digitide 10%); ER&D 12–20% (KPIT below its May guide; Onward 12%); BPM/RCM 24–35% (Sagility, IKS); banking products and SaaS 20–35% (Nucleus fell to 4% in Q1); CPaaS 10–17% (low gross margin, telco/Meta set prices); AI cloud 75% EBITDA at E2E on scarce GPUs. [deep files; Upstox review]
- Labour codes: India's new labour codes and April 2026 state minimum-wage revisions raised staff cost for BPM (Digitide ~₹10cr a quarter) and one-off past-service charges across the sector. [deep: 41, 34]
- Attrition 12–15% at most firms (Onward 14.85%; exceptions like Expleo 25%). Utilisation is being managed through lower fresher intake rather than layoffs. [deep: 44, 39; search summary]

## 9. ER&D (engineering services)
- ISG: ER&D ACV −6% in Q2 2026. Global auto OEM EBITDA margins fell from ~11% (late 2024) to <8% (late 2025); OEMs are narrowing software-defined-vehicle programmes to ADAS and connected services. [ISG Q2 2026; Everest/industry summaries via search]
- Company evidence: **KPIT** — FY27 "a lost year", USD revenue flat-to-down, margins 3–4 points below guidance, European OEM cuts; **Tata Technologies** — recovering (+24% YoY cc services) on new non-JLR wins; **Cyient** — core DET flat in cc for five quarters; **Onward** — industrial/power demand strong, auto weak. ER&D growth is now driven by **industrial, power/data-centre equipment and aerospace**, not European autos. [deep: 06, 02, 11, 44]

## 10. CPaaS and the WhatsApp shift
- SMS still ~60% of Tanla's revenue (68% in FY25); WhatsApp/RCS ("OTT") ~31–32% and growing, but Meta is shifting traffic from promotional to utility messages (~10x cheaper), lowering realisation; CARE: margins "constrained due to high dependence on telecom operators and OTT vendors". Enterprise CPaaS gross margin ~20%. [deep: 18 (CARE Apr-26, Q4 call)]
- Route Mobile: revenue back to ~10% volume growth, gross margin slipping, repeated vendor-prepayment write-offs (₹136cr in FY26). Volume growth is healthy (bank OTPs, UPI, e-commerce), pricing is not. [deep: 28]

## 11. AI data centres and GPU cloud (IndiaAI)
- IndiaAI Mission (₹10,372cr outlay): 38,000+ GPUs empanelled from private operators (Yotta, E2E, Jio, Tata, CtrlS, NxtGen) at subsidised ~₹65–150 per GPU-hour (reports differ); government target 100,000 GPUs by Dec 2026. [IndiaAI coverage via search summaries; Business Standard headline Jun 2026]
- Beneficiaries: **E2E** (MRR ₹37cr → ₹72cr in one quarter; 75% EBITDA; debt-funded), **Netweb** (order book + L1 ~₹3,355cr > FY26 revenue; 13–15% margins; working-capital heavy), **Black Box** (hyperscaler data-centre cabling/integration). Risks: GPU price deflation, customer concentration (Netweb top-5 = 65–70%), capital intensity. [deep: 08, 03, 07]

## 12. BPM and healthcare RCM
- ISG: BPO ACV +34% in Q2 2026 — the one outsourcing segment growing fast, but mostly re-engineered, AI-assisted contracts. [ISG Q2 2026]
- US healthcare: Sagility (payers, 7 of top-10 US payers) growing ~2x the industry; IKS buying scale (TruBridge, $565 mn EV) to reach rural hospitals; Digitide entering provider RCM (~$25 mn). India BPM (Digitide) is the weakest part — wage codes, AI, low pricing power. [deep: 05, 01, 41]

## 13. Competitive structure and where profit sits
- Profit pools: (1) **healthcare operations** (IKS/Sagility, 25–35% margins, long contracts); (2) **IP/platform owners** (banking software, vertical SaaS; 20–35% margins when growing, but lumpy licences); (3) **AI infrastructure** (scarcity rents today); (4) **mid-tier services** (10–20%, pricing under AI pressure, rupee-flattered); (5) **CPaaS and adtech** (volume businesses at 10–20% margins, input prices set by telcos/Meta/Google).
- Scale matters less than niche: mid-tiers winning vendor-consolidation deals are those with a clear vertical (BFSI, healthcare) or AI/data capability.

## 14. Cycle position
- Services: **late-trough / early transition** — 2025–26 was a pricing reset from GenAI; managed-services bookings flat; ER&D in a European-auto downturn. Infrastructure: **early boom** (Gartner data-centre +62.5%). Currency: peak tailwind. Policy: elevated but unresolved risk (H-1B fee, HIRE Act).

## Suggested buckets (44 companies)
1. **IT services & data/AI analytics (12)** — Fractal Analytics, Zensar Technologies, Sonata Software, Birlasoft, Latent View Analytics, Mastek, Happiest Minds, R Systems International, Saksoft, Xtranet Technologies, InfoBeans Technologies, Expleo Solutions. Rationale: people-based, hours-billed export services — same drivers (US/Europe BFSI and tech budgets, GenAI deflation, rupee, attrition).
2. **Engineering R&D services (5)** — Tata Technologies, KPIT Technologies, Cyient, Ceinsys Tech, Onward Technologies. Rationale: product/plant engineering for OEMs — driven by auto (Europe) and industrial/aerospace/power R&D cycles, not IT budgets.
3. **BPM & healthcare operations (4)** — Inventurus Knowledge Solutions, Sagility, Datamatics Global Services, Digitide Solutions. Rationale: process outsourcing (US healthcare RCM/payer work, collections, CX) — labour-intensive, long contracts, most directly exposed to AI automation and Indian wage codes.
4. **Software products, SaaS & fintech/govtech platforms (16)** — Amagi Media Labs, RateGain, Intellect Design Arena, Newgen Software, eMudhra, CE Info Systems (MapmyIndia), Capillary Technologies, Network People Services, Aurionpro Solutions, Protean eGov, Zaggle Prepaid, Nucleus Software, Aurum Proptech, IZMO, Unicommerce eSolutions, TAC Infosec. Rationale: own IP and licence/subscription/transaction revenue — judged on ARR, retention and lumpy deal wins rather than headcount (could be split into banking/fintech/govtech vs vertical SaaS if a sixth tab is allowed).
5. **Messaging, adtech & AI infrastructure (7)** — Netweb Technologies, Affle 3i, Black Box, E2E Networks, Tanla Platforms, Route Mobile, Mobavenue AI Tech. Rationale: volume/capacity businesses — CPaaS and adtech ride message/ad volumes with input prices set by telcos, Meta and Google; Netweb/E2E/Black Box ride the AI data-centre and IndiaAI GPU build-out.
