# TAC Infosec (TAC Security) — deep research (Phase A1)

Research date: 27 Sep 2026. ₹ crore (cr) unless stated. NSE Emerge SME company (half-yearly filer until FY26; voluntary quarterly results from Q1 FY27). **Both stored transcripts are AI-generated (tijoristack).** The "General 29 Jul 2026" one is the Safehouse acquisition media meet, not an earnings call — checked against the official NSE transcript (filed 31 Jul 2026). For the 15 Jul 2026 Q1 FY27 call I could not find an official exchange transcript (only a YouTube recording is referenced); its numbers are cross-checked against Screener, the company's press releases and news (Tribune/ANI, 17 Jul 2026). The previous official results meeting is the FY26 investor meet of 20 May 2026 (official NSE transcript + presentation), used below as the "Q4 call". No credit rating (virtually no debt).

## Business and segments
- Vulnerability-management and application-security SaaS (**ESOF**), SOC 2/ISO compliance automation (**Socify.ai**, launched Sep 2025), Web3/smart-contract audits (**CyberScope**, Greek subsidiary Cyberscope I.K.E., acquired Feb 2024) and small Indian government services. US public-sector arm **CyberSandia** (New Mexico) started operations in Q1 FY27. Products are Google CASA/app-store security assessments at low ticket sizes; company says it is "60–70% less expensive than peer companies". [Q1 call; May-26 investor meet; AR FY26]
- Q1 FY27 revenue split (company): ESOF ₹15.1cr (76%), Socify.ai ₹3.8cr (19%), services ₹0.9cr (small, deliberately shrunk). Over 70% of revenue from North America, Europe and UK. [Q1 call; May-26 meet]
- Clients: "10,000+" cumulative across TAC and CyberScope in 100+ countries, but only ~1,800 were invoiced in Q1 FY27; top-10 clients together only ~$122,000 in the quarter, largest ~$17,000 — i.e. average billed ticket ~₹1 lakh a quarter. Client additions: 1,750 in Q1 FY27, 1,600 in Jul–Aug 2026; Socify.ai reached 300 customers by Sep. [Q1 call; NSE press releases 7 & 14 Sep 2026]
- Promoter Trishneet Arora (Founder, Chairman, CEO) is the largest shareholder; promoters 56.96% (Oct 2025). Total equity raised to date ~₹30cr (IPO 2024). [Screener; May-26 meet]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated)
- Revenue FY24 12 → FY25 30 → FY26 57 (+88%); OPM 57% → 50% → 54%; PAT 6 → 15 → 26. ROCE 38%, ROE 32.5%. [Screener]
- FY26 halves: H1 ₹29.5cr included ~₹9.1cr from two one-off partner-led Web3 orders (~$1 mn); underlying H1 ~₹20.4cr; H2 ₹27.8cr (+53% vs H2 FY25 ₹18.1cr). Crypto fair-value loss ₹1.52cr made FY26 other income negative. [May-26 meet & IP]
- Quarters on Screener (sales / OPM / PAT): Jun-25 9.8 / 46.6% / 3.4; Mar-26 15.5 / 44.8% / 6.0; Jun-26 19.8 / 48.2% / 8.1. Q1 FY27 revenue +103% YoY and +27% QoQ on Screener; company's "total income ₹20cr, +97% YoY, +33% QoQ" includes other income. Q1 was the first quarter after the three-year tax holiday ended (Mar 2026), yet PAT margin rose to ~40%. [Screener; Q1 call; News: Tribune/ANI, 17 Jul 2026]
- Unit economics: billed revenue per invoiced client ~₹1.1 lakh a quarter; Socify.ai >$4,000 per customer; 2030 target $10,000 per customer across 10,000 clients ($100 mn ARR). [Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- May-26 FY26 meet: "I beg to not give any 27 guidance. Our results will speak." Only the 2030 "Bold Vision" ($100 mn ARR, 40% EBITDA benchmark) stood; CyberScope's $5 mn Nasdaq IPO stuck after a new Nasdaq small-IPO rule; CyberSandia "behind the timeline". [May-26 meet]
- Q1 FY27 (15 Jul): beat its internal Q1 target (₹18cr) with ₹20cr total income; **new** FY27 guidance ~₹100cr total income, built as Q2 ₹24cr (+20% QoQ), Q3 +10%, Q4 +20%; EBITDA margin above the 40% benchmark. Reaffirmed in the 7 Sep 2026 press release ("FY27 revenue guidance of approximately 100 crore"). [Q1 call; News: Tribune, 17 Jul 2026; NSE PR 7 Sep 2026]
- Track record: said "January better than December…" in FY26 and delivered H2 growth, but H2 FY26 disappointed investors after the partner-inflated H1; US listing of CyberScope and CyberSandia revenue both slipped. [May-26 meet]
- Implied Q2–Q4 needs ₹80cr in nine months vs ₹57cr for all of FY26 — about 75% growth for the year.

## Capex and capacity
- Asset-light but capitalises development: intangible assets and intangibles under development grew; FY26 cash spent on intangibles ₹4.4cr (₹5.4cr FY25) and ₹6.2cr on subsidiaries; part of staff cost is capitalised to intangibles (note 20). Standalone key audit matter: capitalisation of product-development cost (product intangible ₹4.8cr at Mar-26). [AR FY26 cash-flow statement, notes 12, 20, auditor's report]
- Acquisitions: Safehouse Technologies (Israel; consumer VPN/privacy app, "10 lakhs+ downloads") via binding term sheet on 24 Jul 2026 for only **₹1cr** in cash plus TAC shares; TAC also proposes to fund a new entity "TAC Safehouse Limited" (amount undisclosed) and plans embedded consumer cyber insurance. [NSE filing 24 Jul 2026; official 29 Jul transcript]
- Seven-country footprint (R&D and customer success in Canada; staff in Greece, Romania, Denmark, UK); new EVP HR hired from ArmorCode/Zscaler (Sep 2026). [NSE PR 14 Sep 2026]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Mar-26: cash and bank ~₹22.9cr; borrowings ~₹1cr; equity ₹83cr. An inter-corporate deposit of ₹5cr was lent to Goenka Business & Finance Ltd (28 May 2025, 12%, ₹4.7cr outstanding). [AR FY26 notes 13–15; Screener]
- **Cash conversion is weak:** FY26 CFO ₹9.5cr (company) / ₹12cr (Screener) against PAT ₹26.4cr; trade receivables rose ~₹10.6cr to ~₹22.8cr; debtor days 145 (119 in FY25, and far above a card-paid SaaS model). This contradicts the CEO's May claim that "more than 80%" of FY26 revenue had been collected. [AR FY26 cash-flow statement; Screener; May-26 meet]
- No dividend; bonus issue in FY26 (equity doubled to ₹21cr). CEO: no fund-raise "until we reach a fair enterprise value". [Screener; Q1 call]

## Moat evidence
- Distribution: client count rose from ~100 (2013–2024) to 10,000+ in two years; big names (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Anthropic, SAP) buy low-ticket assessments, often via Google's CASA program. Accreditations: CREST, ISO 27001. [May-26 meet; PRs]
- Margins (48–54% EBITDA) are exceptional for security software, but the low average ticket and "assessment-based" billing (clients pay quarterly/annually as assessments fall due) look more like a high-volume testing service than deep enterprise lock-in. No disclosed retention, NRR or ARR metric. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Governance (secretarial audit, AR FY26):** loans to CEO Trishneet Arora (peak ₹1.28cr) and whole-time director Charanjit Singh "in violation of Section 179, 185 and 186" (repaid by year-end); related-party transactions without omnibus approval and above approved limits (later ratified); RPT figures reported to NSE did not match audited figures; independent directors paid ₹1.7 lakh each without shareholder approval; only three audit-committee meetings (four required); insider-trading database control lapses; NSE fines for late board-meeting intimations. [AR FY26 secretarial audit report]
- **Related parties:** the CEO's relative Shivani Arora received ESOP shares with fair value ₹7.1cr above the exercise price in FY26; CEO waived ₹1.5cr of approved pay while taking a company loan. [AR FY26 note 33]
- Small audit firm (Maharishi & Co., Jamnagar); unmodified opinion; KAMs on consolidation and capitalised development cost. [AR FY26]
- Crypto exposure at CyberScope (payments taken in crypto; ₹1.52cr fair-value loss in FY26). [May-26 IP]
- Guidance risk: ₹100cr FY27 needs a sharp H2 step-up; Q3 is seasonally weak. Promotional communication style (daily press releases, "fastest-growing cybersecurity company globally"). [Q1 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- From refusing FY27 guidance (May) to a precise ₹100cr target with quarterly steps (July); from defensive (H2 "not a revenue miss", crypto slump, US shutdown) to AI-boom optimism.
- CyberScope Nasdaq IPO disappeared from the narrative; B2C (Safehouse) and cyber insurance were added two weeks after the Q1 call.
- Reporting moved to quarterly; main-board migration planned "in a couple of quarters".

## My read
Reported growth and margins are genuinely strong (revenue doubling, ~48% EBITDA) and client additions are accelerating, but the quality signals are poor: cash flow is less than half of profit, receivables stretch to ~145 days, and the annual report records loans to directors in breach of the Companies Act, RPT lapses and a large ESOP benefit to the CEO's relative. At ~₹940cr market cap the price needs the ₹100cr FY27 guidance met and cash conversion to catch up with profit. Clean Q2 (₹24cr), falling debtor days and a main-board migration with a larger auditor would improve the view; a Q2 miss or more related-party surprises would break it.
