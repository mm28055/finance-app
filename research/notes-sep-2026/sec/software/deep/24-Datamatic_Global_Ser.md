# Datamatics Global Services — deep research (Phase A1)

Research date: 27 Sep 2026. ₹ crore (cr) unless stated. BPM = business process management (outsourced back-office work); EBIT margins below are the company's segment EBIT, not EBITDA.
Transcript check: BOTH stored transcripts (Q4 FY26, 22 May 2026; Q1 FY27, 6 Aug 2026) are AI-generated (Tijori). Both were checked against the official transcripts (Q1 filed on BSE 12 Aug 2026; Q4 from the company website).

## Business and segments
- Mid-sized IT + BPM firm, promoter-run by the Kanodia family (66.33%). Three segments, Q1 FY27: Digital Operations ₹296.8cr (58%, +16.1% YoY, EBIT 19.3%) — publishing content (Lumina Datamatics + TNQTech), finance & accounting, document processing; Digital Technologies ₹153.1cr (30%, +6.1%, EBIT 8.9%) — apps, cloud, Salesforce (Dextara), AI platforms, automated fare collection; Digital Experiences ₹64.0cr (12%, −5.3%, EBIT 11.7%) — contact centres in India, Philippines, US. [Q1 call]
- FY26 segments: Operations ₹1,101.4cr (+40.2%, EBIT 18.7%, lifted by TNQ); Technologies ₹626.5cr (−4.5%, EBIT 9.7%); Experiences ₹259.2cr (−8%, EBIT 7.9%). [Q4 call]
- Verticals Q1 FY27: Education & Publishing 29%, Technology & Consulting 20%, BFSI 19%, Manufacturing & Logistics 11%, Retail 9%, Not-for-profit & Government 8%, others 4%. [Q1 call]
- Geography FY26: US 54%, UK & Europe 22%, India 16%, rest 8%. [Q4 call]
- Products: TruBot (RPA), TruCap+ (document AI), TruBI, TruAI (underwriting, credit analysis), FINATO (finance automation), KAiBRE/KAiSDLC (legacy code modernisation), TruFare (metro/rail fare collection). Lumina serves 9 of the 10 largest publishers; top-3 global digital-content outsourcer (company claim). [AR FY26; IP Q4FY26; Q1 call]
- People: 15,744 employees (group, Mar-26) vs 15,660; FY26 attrition 16.2% (ICRA cites 11.5% for 9M FY26 on a different basis). Revenue per employee ≈ ₹12.6 lakh a year (~$14–15k) — typical of a BPM-heavy mix. [AR FY26; IP Q4FY26; ICRA Mar-26; derived]
- Utilisation not disclosed.

- Q1 FY27: revenue ₹513.9cr (+9.9% YoY, −1% QoQ); EBITDA ₹101.1cr (19.7%, +343 bps YoY; includes April wage hikes); EBIT ₹78.4cr (15.3%); PAT after minority ₹72.3cr (+43.5%). [Q1 call]
- FY26 exceptional items ₹65cr: labour-code charge ₹24.0cr (₹40.3cr in Q3, reversed ₹16.2cr in Q4) and ₹40.9cr fair-value increase in contingent consideration for acquisitions (mostly TNQ's remaining 20%, which outperformed). Adjusted FY26 PAT ₹253cr (12.4%). [IP Q4FY26; Q4 call]

## Deal wins / clients (IT-services lens)
- No order book or TCV disclosed; asked on the Q4 call, the CEO said he would have the number "for the next call" — it was not given on the Q1 call. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Wins cited: SBI Life (TruAI underwriting, agentic AI), a US pet-care/veterinary firm (AI voice agent), a US consumer-products company (KAiBRE/KAiSDLC legacy modernisation), a US logistics firm (Salesforce CRM via Dextara). ~60% of FY27 deals won so far are "AI-led or largely AI-driven". [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Deal shape: AI projects run 3–9 months rather than 3–5 year annuity contracts; management says deal sizes are rising (Dextara deals moved from $50–70k to $0.5–1M). [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Client concentration rising: top-5 / top-10 / top-20 = 30% / 41% / 54% in Q1 FY27 vs 26% / 39% / 52% in Q4 FY26. 300+ "significant" customers; 5 new clients added in Q4. [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q4FY26]
- Consistency flag: on the Q4 call the CMO said TruAI underwriting had "already signed up three or four" customers; on the Q1 call the CEO said "We've got the first customer" booking revenue. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## AI impact, currency, US exposure
- AI R&D of ₹40–50cr a year is expensed (not capitalised) inside Digital Technologies, depressing its margin; to stay at this level in FY27. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Management names two AI risks: clients automating in-house instead of outsourcing, and clients moving work to their own captive centres (GCCs) — the latter already hit Digital Experiences in FY26. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- US 54% of revenue; ICRA puts US+Europe at over 76% and flags the proposed US HIRE Act and H-1B fee rises (limited impact given offshore delivery). Currency hedged through forward contracts. [Q4 call; ICRA Mar-26; AR FY26]

[Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: revenue +9.9% YoY — **ahead** of ~8% (but flat-to-down QoQ); EBITDA 19.7% — **in line**; Technologies +6.1% — **delivering**; Experiences still −5.3% but EBIT margin recovered to 11.7% from 4.4% — **mixed**; order book not provided — **missed**; FINATO agentic sign-ups not mentioned — **unclear**. [Q1 call]
- Q1 call outlook: growth guidance **kept** at high single digits; margin now "roughly 0.5% improvement … closer to 20%" — the **low end** of the earlier 50–100 bps; new target of ~₹3,000cr revenue in "about a 3-year window" from FY27 including acquisitions (≈15% a year from ~₹2,000cr, so it needs M&A); EBITDA 19–20% to be maintained. [Q1 call]

- Borrowings ₹221cr at Mar-26 (Screener), including a ₹150cr TNQ acquisition loan at 3-month T-bill + 265 bps (effective 9.13%), secured by charges on assets and a pledge of TNQ shares. [Screener; AR FY26 note 20.1]

## Moat evidence
- Niche depth rather than scale: 9 of top-10 global publishers; 3 of top-5 Fortune 500 retailers for digital content; analyst rankings in finance transformation (company claims). [IP Q4FY26]
- Own products (8, per AR) give some IP-led revenue, but no product revenue split is disclosed. [AR FY26]
- Weak spots: Digital Experiences lost work to client captives; Digital Technologies lost revenue as projects ended (−4.5% in FY26). [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- Growth is modest organically (~8%) and the ₹3,000cr target depends on acquisitions; the company overpaid versus plan on TNQ's earn-out (₹40.9cr fair-value charge). [Q1 call; IP Q4FY26]
- Client concentration rising (top-5 at 30%). [Q1 call]
- Accounting judgements: key audit matters on fixed-price revenue (percentage-of-completion, rising unbilled revenue) and goodwill of ₹611cr. [AR FY26 auditor's report]
- A subsidiary with negative net worth (~₹26cr, per an analyst on the Q4 call); management expects recovery in ~2 years. Not named on the call. [Q4 call]
- Contingent liabilities small: income tax ₹11.0cr, GST ₹0.6cr; bank guarantees ₹54.0cr. [AR FY26]
- Governance: promoter family holds 66.33% and runs both the parent and Lumina/TNQ (Sameer Kanodia is CEO of Lumina and TNQ); related-party dealings stated as ordinary-course, none material. Institutional ownership is tiny (FII 0.5%, DII 0.1%). [AR FY26; Screener]
- AI: shorter projects reduce visibility; in-house automation and GCCs could shrink outsourcing budgets. [Q1 call]

## My read
Datamatics has quietly turned into a steadier, higher-margin business: FY26 EBITDA margin hit a record 18.7% and Q1 FY27 held ~20% even after wage hikes, helped by the high-margin TNQ publishing unit and by cost control. The core problem is growth — organic revenue is guided at only ~8%, Digital Technologies and Experiences shrank in FY26, and AI is shortening project length while pushing clients towards in-house captives. At the current price the market asks only for high-single-digit earnings growth, which the margin gains and ~₹500cr post-payout cash make achievable; upside needs AI platforms (TruAI, KAiBRE) to become visible revenue or a sensible acquisition. Watch for a disclosed order book, Digital Experiences returning to growth, and whether top-5 concentration keeps rising.
