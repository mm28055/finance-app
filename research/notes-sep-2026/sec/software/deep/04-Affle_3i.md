# Affle 3i — deep research (Sep 2026)

Screener code AFFLE. Price ₹1,578, market cap ₹22,236 cr, P/E 46.5, ROCE 16.8%, ROE 13.8%, no dividend [Screener, 25 Sep 2026].

Transcript check: the stored Q4 FY26 transcript is the official BSE filing. The stored Q1 FY27 transcript is AI-generated (tijoristack). I checked it against the official BSE filing of 17 Aug 2026. The numbers match closely; minor differences are listed under Stored-data errors.

## Business and segments
- **What Affle does:** it is an ad-tech platform that sells "converted users" on a CPCU basis (cost per converted user: the advertiser pays only when someone installs, registers, buys or deposits).
  - Q4 FY26: 120.3m conversions at an average CPCU of ₹60.0, giving ₹721 cr of CPCU revenue out of ₹724 cr total. CPCU is about 99% of revenue [Q4 call].
  - Affle buys ad inventory and data from publishers, SDKs, OEMs and exchanges. It earns the spread, so inventory and data cost is its main expense.
- **Advertiser verticals ("E/F/G/H"):** e-commerce, fintech, gaming/entertainment (including real-money gaming, RMG), and healthcare/hospitality.
  - In India, the ranking in Q1 FY27 was E > F > H > G. RMG is still recovering after India's 2025 ban [Q1 call official].
- **Geography, Q1 FY27:**
  - India and emerging markets: 72.2% of revenue, +20.2% YoY.
  - Developed markets: 27.8%, +20.7% YoY. An analyst estimated about 11–12% excluding currency, and management disputed that framing [Q1 call official].
  - Only about 20% of the business is US dollar-to-dollar. In the rest, CPCU rates are reset to local-currency ROI targets (the advertiser's required return) [Q1 call].
- **Channels:**
  - About 75% of revenue comes directly from advertisers; 79% in Q1 against 74% in FY26. The rest comes via ad agencies [Q4 call; Q1 call].
  - More than 90% of focus is on in-app mobile and CTV (connected TV); a small part is digital out-of-home [Q4 call].
- **IT-services-style metrics:**
  - Headcount is not disclosed on the calls. Employee cost rose 7.8% YoY in Q1 [Q1 call].
  - There is no order book or TCV (total contract value). Revenue runs on annual advertiser budgets ("Diwali to Diwali") [Q4 call].
- **Assets and IP:**
  - AdColony (a US mobile video ad network) was bought in 2026 for $4.7m (₹44.4 cr) — assets only, not the company [Q1 results filing via Multibagg].
  - 18 patents granted and 21 filed, covering about 300 claims, including "human vs non-human" traffic filtering [Q4 call].

- **Q1 FY27:**
  - Revenue +20.4% YoY and +3.1% QoQ. This was the 14th straight quarter of sequential growth.
  - EBITDA ₹167.6 cr (22.4%).
  - PAT ₹128 cr (+21.7%).
  - Inventory and data cost was 63.2% of revenue, the same as Q4 at 63.3% [Q1 call official].
- **Annual:**
  - Revenue went ₹1,843 cr (FY24) → ₹2,266 cr (FY25) → ₹2,709 cr (FY26); TTM ₹2,836 cr.
  - OPM went 20% → 21% → 23%.
  - Net profit went ₹297 cr → ₹382 cr → ₹455 cr.
  - Five-year growth has been about 20%. FY26 was +19.5% revenue, +26.3% EBITDA and +19.1% PAT [Screener; Q4 call].
- **Unit economics:**
  - Gross margin is revenue minus inventory and data cost. It fell from about 39% to about 36.5% over 7–8 quarters and is now about 36.8% [Q4 call; Q1 call].
  - CPCU rate is "above ₹60". Management says the gross-margin fall is deliberate spend on premium users, CTV and developed markets, plus currency pass-through [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- **Standing guidance:**
  - About 20% organic revenue CAGR over the medium term.
  - EBITDA margin moving to 23–25% "over a period of time".
  - "10x in the decade", ideally in 5 years, which needs M&A.
  - $1bn revenue "in the next few years" [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Q4 promises and what Q1 delivered:**
  - 20%+ growth: **met**, at 20.4%.
  - Gross margin: expected to take "about a year" of investment, then a recovery in the second year. Q1 was flat at 63.2% cost, so it is **on track, not yet improving**.
  - M&A: in May, "a realistic chance... within this calendar year". In August, "close by early 2027" after third-party diligence. The M&A timing **slipped by about one quarter** [Q4 call; Q1 call official].
  - Cash conversion: FY26 operating cash flow was ₹502 cr, 110% of PAT. Q1 OCF/PAT fell to about 41%, which management blamed on collections pulled forward into March. It guided 80–85% by Q3 [Q1 call official].
- **Internal target:** business plans are "pegged at 25%". Management says "95% of revenue grew >25%" once RMG and some fintech are excluded. This is an adjusted figure the company chose itself [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- The company capitalises a lot of development cost:
  - FY26: ₹165 cr of intangible assets capitalised, and ₹174 cr added to intangibles under development [AR FY26 auditor's KAM, from component auditor EY].
  - Depreciation and amortisation runs about ₹33–34 cr a quarter.
  - Screener shows fixed assets up from ₹1,221 cr to ₹1,394 cr and CWIP from ₹111 cr to ₹131 cr, with FY26 CFI of −₹299 cr.
  - So a meaningful share of product spend bypasses the P&L. EBITDA is flattered compared with peers that expense this cost.
- **Capital raise for M&A:**
  - Promoter Affle Holdings is buying about 7.4m warrants at ₹1,487 each, about ₹1,100 cr in total, with 25% paid upfront. ₹275.1 cr was received in Q1 [Q4 call; Q1 results via Multibagg].
  - Ticket size was not given. More than 10 targets were screened and about 4 shortlisted, "small, mid-sized and larger" [Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- **Debt:** borrowings are ₹15 cr (Mar-26), down from ₹183 cr in Mar-24. The company is effectively net cash, before the ₹275 cr of warrant money [Screener].
- **Working capital:**
  - Debtor days: 63 → 48 → 52 (FY24–FY26).
  - Working-capital days jumped from 25 to 82 in FY26, which Screener flags as a con.
  - FY26 CFO was ₹502 cr, against ₹455 cr PAT [Screener].
- **Payouts:** no dividend and no buyback; cash is held for M&A.
- **Other income:** ₹25 cr in Q1; FY26 other income fell 17% on forex [Q4 call; Screener].

## Moat evidence
- **Business model:**
  - The CPCU model plus direct integration with advertisers' first-party data (100% of the business, even through agencies) is harder to copy than CPM/CPC sellers. Management names Trade Desk as an example of that kind of seller [Q4 call].
  - Affle claims 4bn+ reachable devices [Q4 call].
- **Pricing power:**
  - The CPCU rate has risen over 10 years.
  - EBITDA margin has held at 22–23% even while gross margin was given back. That points to operating leverage rather than pricing strength [Q4 call; Screener].
- **Track record and recognition:** 14 straight quarters of QoQ growth. It is ranked among top ROI partners in the Singular ROI Index 2026, and won CTV awards [Q1 call].
- **Weak points:**
  - Affle does not own the inventory it sells.
  - Walled gardens (Google, Meta) and AI-driven ad tools are strengthening.
  - The gross-margin decline suggests the spread is under pressure in developed markets.

## Risks and red flags
- **Bobble AI (24.07% stake, ₹135.8 cr):**
  - It has been classified as "held for sale" since May 2024. That is more than two years, while Ind AS 105 normally expects a sale within 12 months.
  - Bobble is now in insolvency (NCLAT appeal pending).
  - Affle has alleged mismanagement, and says it won Singapore arbitration (SIAC) and High Court orders for inspection rights that Bobble's management ignored.
  - No impairment has been booked; "impairment would be tested once the appeal is decided". This is roughly 28% of one year's PAT at risk [AR FY26 notes; Q1 call official].
- **Capitalised R&D:** about ₹165–174 cr a year (see Capex). EY flagged it as a key audit matter [AR FY26].
- **Promoter dilution and related-party dealings:**
  - Promoter holding fell from 59.9% (Sep-23) to 54.9% (Jun-26) [Screener].
  - The ₹1,100 cr warrant issue goes to the same Singapore promoter entity. Management declined to say how Affle Holdings is funding it ("this is a call for Affle 3i, not Affle Holdings") [Q4 call].
  - There are small related-party transactions with Affle Holdings, AGPL and AXPL [AR FY26 Note 32].
- **M&A risk:**
  - The timeline has already slipped. The target is undisclosed. Past acquisitions (2020–23) were "barely breaking even" when bought and took about 3 years to convert to CPCU [Q4 call].
- **Concentration and regulation:**
  - Exposure to RMG and fintech rule changes in India.
  - Currency risk on developed-market ROI pricing.
  - Customer concentration is not disclosed.
- **Contingent liabilities:** small. The largest is an AY2017-18 income tax demand of ₹6.5 cr. Capital commitments are ₹28.7 cr [AR FY26 Note 30].
- **Audit:** Walker Chandiok (Grant Thornton India) audits the group, with a clean opinion. Key audit matters are revenue/receivables and capitalised intangibles [AR FY26].

## What changed between the two calls
- **Q4 call:** focused on the warrant issue, gross-margin questions and M&A "within this calendar year".
- **Q1 call:**
  - The AdColony asset buy took centre stage: 100,000 apps and 500m developed-market devices targeted "this year".
  - M&A moved to "early 2027".
  - OCF/PAT dropped to about 41%.
  - Bobble insolvency surfaced as a new issue.
  - A new "AI CIDs" story was added (autonomous connected devices: smart TVs, cars, retail screens).
- **Tone:** equally bullish, but more defensive on cash conversion and on Bobble.

## My read
The core business is steady rather than accelerating:
- About 20% growth every quarter.
- A 22–23% EBITDA margin that has not moved to the promised 23–25%.
- Gross margin still down about 250 bps from its peak.

Earnings quality is weaker than the headline, for three reasons:
- Capitalised development roughly matches annual D&A.
- Q1 cash conversion was 41%.
- Bobble's ₹136 cr sits unimpaired.

At about 46x trailing earnings, the price needs about 20% EPS growth sustained for a decade. That requires the promised M&A to add growth without diluting margins, which is unproven.

**What would change my view:**
- Positive: OCF/PAT back above 80% by Q3, and gross margin turning up in H2 FY27.
- Negative: a large, low-margin acquisition, or a Bobble write-off paired with another delay.
