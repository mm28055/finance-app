## Business and segments
- **What it sells:** an Indian "neocloud", also called an AI factory. It rents NVIDIA GPU capacity (H100, H200, B200; L40S and L4 via JarvisLabs.ai), plus CPU cloud, storage and its TIR software platform for training, fine-tuning and inference. Customers are AI-native start-ups, SaaS companies, enterprises, research bodies and India AI Mission allottees. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q1 call]
- **Capacity:**
  - About 5,100 GPUs were live at Q1 FY27 end, including the first 1,024 B200s, which went live mid-May 2026.
  - Another 1,024 B200s are due "in the next couple of months".
  - Plans cover B300, GB300 and Vera Rubin.
  - At Q4 FY26 the base was about 3,900 capacity units across CPU, GPU and storage. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- **Utilisation:**
  - About 80%, and "certainly less than 85%", in March 2026 across all infrastructure.
  - "Quite a maximal utilization" on the new B200s in Q1, and the existing fleet was "pretty much at maximum level". [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Revenue mix:** GPUs are to reach 85–90% of revenue "over coming quarters". CPU is "not a very major volume". [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Customer and geographic mix:**
  - At Q4, Tarun Dua said government/India AI Mission business "has not exceeded more than say 35%-40%" and international was "35% to 37%".
  - On the Q1 call he gave India AI at "about 20%, 21%" and international at "closer to 37%", with the rest domestic.
  - **US / overseas revenue share is about 37%**, mostly through JarvisLabs' self-serve platform. A Delaware subsidiary was set up after the year-end to sell abroad. [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- **Structure:** SovCloud Technologies Ltd was incorporated on 17 Jun 2026 as a wholly-owned subsidiary for large GPU clusters and "enabling funding arrangement[s]". L&T owns 18.45% (up from 14.92%). It is both an investor and an arm's-length partner: E2E uses L&T data-centre space, L&T uses E2E cloud, and an MoU covers monetising L&T-built GPUs. [AR FY26; Q1 call]
- **Headcount:** 207 at 31 Mar 2026, which makes it very asset-heavy and people-light. [AR FY26 MD&A]

## Guidance history
- **Q3 FY26 (stored, AI version):** March-2026 MRR guidance of ₹35–40 cr, and a long-term EBITDA margin aim of about 70%. [Stored Q3 transcript/analysis]
- **Q4 FY26 (20 Apr 2026):**
  - The March MRR target was met at ₹37.4 cr.
  - Management **declined FY27 MRR guidance**: "it would not do justice to do a MRR guidance for one year in the future".
  - B200 cluster 1 promised "before mid of May", and a second 1,024 "in a couple of months".
  - A "6,000 GPUs by FY27, at least minimum" figure was raised by an analyst and not denied. [Q4 call]
- **Q1 FY27 (22 Jul 2026):**
  - B200 went live on time (mid-May).
  - The 75.2% margin already beats the ~70% long-term aim; management called it "quite sustainable over the medium term".
  - Still no MRR guidance: "we always advise everyone to look at what we have done in the past".
  - The second B200 lot slipped from "a couple of months" (April) to "next couple of months" (July).
  - **Verdict: delivered and beaten on margin and MRR; guidance withheld.** [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- **FY26 capex:** investing outflow was ₹429 cr (FY25: ₹983 cr). Fixed assets rose from ₹389 cr to ₹1,036 cr, with CWIP (work in progress) of ₹533 cr at Mar-26. Capital commitments fell from ₹904 cr (Mar-25) to ₹19 cr (Mar-26). [Screener; AR FY26 note 37]
- **Q1 FY27 capex:** reported at about ₹870 cr (search summary of the presentation; not verified from the filing). This is consistent with the debt rising to about ₹450 cr ("the loan… broadly INR 450 crores, which with the other lot coming… would be increasing"). Management refused to quantify peak debt. [Q1 call; search result: earningspulse, Jul 2026 — unverified]
- **Funding raised:**
  - Preferential issues in 2024 (₹405.7 cr in Sep-24 and ₹1,079.3 cr in Dec-24, the latter mainly L&T).
  - A QIP of ₹107 cr in Feb-26.
  - At 31 Mar 2026, ₹50.2 cr and ₹77.1 cr of the 2024 issues remained unused. CARE is the monitoring agency.

- **Payback:** the stated useful life is 6 years for computer equipment. Management argues each GPU generation earns for "6–8 years" or "7, 8 years". At 75% EBITDA margins the payback on the ₹1,500-cr gross block is roughly 3–4 years if utilisation holds. [AR FY26 notes; Q1 call; Q4 call]

- **Contingent liabilities:** none. [AR FY26 note 37]

## Moat evidence
- **Hardware access:** Tarun Dua says "we are the haves… where capacity is running out everywhere". Being early to B200s in India, and an India AI Mission supplier, is an access advantage while supply is tight. [Q4 call]
- **Software:** the TIR platform and the JarvisLabs acquisition support self-serve orchestration of large clusters. Management argues sovereign, open-weight deployments favour an Indian AI factory over API-only frontier models. [Q1 call; AR FY26]
- **Pricing power (near term):** it pushed through CPU and GPU price rises. Customers chose to prepay and sign 6–24-month contracts to lock prices. Management says demand for "1, 2 to 3 years" contracts is rising. [Q1 call]
- **Limits:** "It's practically… almost first come first served today", in Dua's words, and the fleet is small (about 5,000 GPUs against hyperscalers' hundreds of thousands). The moat is timing and scarcity, not a lasting cost edge. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Disclosure gaps:** management refuses to split revenue by customer type, training versus inference, contract length or GPU generation ("let us not do this today"), and gives no MRR guidance and no peak-debt figure. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- **Commodity cycle:** Dua himself expects "phases where there is shortage of demand". Profit depends on GPU rental rates holding over a 6-year depreciation life while newer chips arrive every 12–18 months. [Q1 call]
- **Leverage turning:** debt rose from about ₹103 cr to about ₹450 cr in one quarter, and more is coming for the next B200 lot and SovCloud "funding arrangements". [AR FY26; Q1 call]
- **Related parties:** L&T (18.45% holder) is a partner, supplier and customer at once. There was ₹3.7 cr of unbilled revenue due from L&T at Mar-26. Promoter holding fell from 59.71% (Sep-23) to 39.45% through dilution. [AR FY26 note 36; Screener]
- **Concentration:** government/India AI business was up to 35–40% at Q4 and about 20% by Q1. The India AI allottees did not scale up as hoped ("we have not insisted on them having to increase their scale"). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Operations:** the stored Q3 analysis records a Mumbai service outage in Dec 2025 (from the AI transcript; not checked against the official Q3 text).
- **Auditor:** unmodified opinion, with the key audit matter on revenue recognition. No contingent liabilities. [AR FY26 auditor's report]

## My read
Execution in the last two quarters has been outstanding. MRR rose about 34% in Q4 (₹28 cr to ₹37.4 cr) and then nearly doubled in Q1 (to ₹71.8 cr), the B200s were fully used at launch, and the business turned profitable at a 75% EBITDA margin. But this is a scarcity-rent business in a hardware cycle, now funded partly by debt, with very thin disclosure. Even on the June exit MRR (about ₹860 cr annualised) the market value is about 15 times revenue, so the price needs several more capacity doublings at today's rental rates. It also needs the 6–8-year GPU life to hold. My view would improve with disclosed contract tenure and a customer mix showing multi-year contracted revenue. It would worsen if GPU spot prices fall, utilisation drops after new capacity arrives, or net debt keeps climbing without MRR following.
