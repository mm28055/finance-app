# Capillary Technologies — deep research (Phase A1)

Research date: 27 Sep 2026. ₹ crore (cr) unless stated. ARR = annual recurring revenue (run-rate of contracted subscription revenue); NRR = net revenue retention (this year's revenue from last year's customers ÷ last year's); ACV = annual value of newly signed contracts; "organic" = customers already on Capillary's own platform; "inorganic" = customers of acquired firms not yet moved over.
Transcript check: both stored transcripts (Q4 FY26 call and Q1 FY27 call, 4 Aug 2026) are the official BSE filings.

## Business and segments
- Loyalty-programme software (points, tiers, rewards ledgers) sold to large consumer brands on 3–7 year subscriptions; 465 brands, 25 Fortune 500 clients, 2.7 billion consumer profiles, 53 countries, ~761 staff. Listed 21 Nov 2025 (IPO at ₹549–577). [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- Product mix: Loyalty+ >90% of revenue (priced per member or per transaction, like a bank ledger); Engage+ (campaigns) ~5%; Rewards+ ~5–7%; aiRA AI agents ~$2–2.5M ARR (under 3% of ARR), priced on usage/outcomes. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Revenue type Q1 FY27: subscription ₹242.5cr (94.5% of revenue, +50% YoY), installation ₹12.2cr, campaign services ₹1.9cr. [IP Q1FY27]
- Geography: US ~60%, Europe ~15%, Asia ~25% (Q1 FY27, per CEO). FY26 by customer location: USA ₹408cr (56%), UK ₹113cr (15%), others ₹213cr (29%). [Q1 call; AR FY26 note 36]
- Two engines: organic (Q1 FY27 organic revenue ₹155.1cr, +17% YoY, ~23% EBITDA) and acquired books — Persuade, Brierley, Rewards+ (all but two customers migrated), Kognitiv (May 2025, ~₹75–80cr revenue, 16–17 customers), SessionM from Mastercard (1 May 2026, ~$32M ARR, ~45 customers). [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Customers: top-5 revenue ₹86.7cr in Q1 FY27 vs ₹72.7cr a year earlier — about 34% of revenue vs 40% (derived). One large US healthcare/pharmacy customer won in FY26 did not grow in Q1 and cut NRR by 5 pts. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]

## Key numbers
- Screener (consolidated): revenue FY24 ₹525cr → FY25 ₹598cr → FY26 ₹735cr (+23%); OPM −2% → 11% → 13%; net profit −₹57cr → ₹13cr → ₹52cr (FY26 includes ₹25cr Kognitiv indemnity). TTM revenue ₹811cr, profit ₹42cr. [Screener]
- Quarters (sales / operating profit / OPM / net profit): Jun-25 180/18/10%/0.8; Sep-25 179/19/10%/0.3; Dec-25 184/25/14%/8; Mar-26 191/31/16%/43; Jun-26 257/40/16%/−9.6. Screener's OPM is ~1.5 pts below the company's "adjusted EBITDA" (which adds back ESOP cost and strips finance income). [Screener; IP Q1FY27]
- Company metrics Q1 FY27: revenue ₹256.6cr (+43%; ~6 pts from currency, ~2 months of SessionM); adjusted EBITDA ₹44.0cr (17.2% vs 10.5%, +132%); reported PAT −₹9.6cr; "normalised" PAT ₹25.4cr after adding back the ₹33.4cr fraud loss and a ₹1.6cr one-time deferred tax charge. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- FY26: revenue ₹734.6cr (+23%), adjusted EBITDA ₹106.9cr (14.6%), PAT ₹52.4cr; normalised PAT ~₹32cr. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- ARR ₹1,026.6cr at Jun-26 vs ₹719.6cr a year earlier (+43% per presentation; the CFO said "about 34%" on the call — the presentation figure is arithmetically right); ARR ₹765cr at Mar-26 (excl. SessionM). [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- NRR (TTM Jun-26): organic 111% (116% excluding the one healthcare client), inorganic 120% (flattered by a small Kognitiv base), blended 113%. FY26: organic 114%, inorganic 94%, blended 110%. NRR mix: ~4–5 pts price escalators and usage overages, ~10 pts upsell/new brands, ~5% churn. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- New ACV (TTM): ₹92.6cr, +75% YoY excluding the one large healthcare contract; FY26 full-year new ACV ₹121cr, "similar to last year". Win rate "upwards of 35%". [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Gross margin: subscription GM 66% overall; organic >75%; acquired books ~30% until migrated, then 65–75%. SessionM server costs were ~50% of revenue vs Capillary's 7–10%. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Unit economics: revenue per employee ≈ ₹256.6cr × 4 ÷ 761 ≈ ₹1.35cr a year; headcount rose only 1% in FY26 against 23% revenue growth. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call; derived]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (May 2026): "revenues will cross INR 1,000–1,050 crores in FY27"; EBITDA % may rise only ~1–1.5 pts because SessionM earns ~zero in year one; SessionM break-even in year 1, "significant margins" in year 3; long-run 25–30% EBITDA; organic NRR target ~115%; ~₹70–80cr of brand-new customer revenue a year (~10% growth). [Q4 call]
- Before Q1, the company had put a figure on this: the Q1 presentation calls ₹1,065cr revenue and ₹172cr adjusted EBITDA (≈16.2%) "our earlier FY27 guidance". [IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: revenue ₹256.6cr with only 2 months of SessionM; adjusted EBITDA 17.2% — **ahead** of the ~16% implied; SessionM already break-even, generating ₹5–6cr free cash in two months — **ahead**; Kognitiv first AI-led migration set for 1 Sep 2026, all by Sep 2027 — **on track** (not yet proven); NRR 111% organic — **slightly below** the 115% plan (one client). [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 call: "We will definitely beat our INR1,065 crores and revenue and INR172 crores number", but guidance **kept**, not raised. New ACV for FY27 to be "at least 30%–40%" above FY26. Organic growth ~17% for FY27 (23% with ~6 pts currency). aiRA aim: 5–10% of revenue this year. [Q1 call]
- ESOP cost ₹12–15cr a year (1–2% of revenue). [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Asset-light cloud software run on four co-location data centres; the real capital use is M&A and capitalised development. [Q1 call]
- Capitalised software development ₹36.6cr in FY26 (~5% of revenue) — this cost bypasses EBITDA and is amortised later; the auditor made it a key audit matter. Capex ₹39.4cr. D&A ₹75cr in FY26 (₹20cr in Q1 FY27), mostly acquired-intangible amortisation. [AR FY26 auditor's report; AR FY26 MD&A; Screener]
- M&A economics: typically pays 0.3–1.5× revenue, keeps ~70% of acquired revenue after migration discounts and churn, reaches ~45% contribution margin, 4–5 year cash payback. Kognitiv cost ₹147cr net of the ₹25cr churn indemnity; SessionM $20M enterprise value but only ₹16.9cr cash after debt-like adjustments (~0.07× ARR). CustomerGlu (Experiences+) "a few 100K" dollars. [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- Management says migrations of Kognitiv and SessionM could add ₹100–130cr of EBITDA over time on ~₹390cr of acquired revenue; SessionM path: break-even → ~15% in a year → 35–40% after another year; $6–8M server savings over 3–4 quarters. [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Cash and equivalents ~₹483cr at Mar-26 (incl. ₹331cr net IPO money); unutilised IPO proceeds ₹323cr; borrowings ₹45cr (debt/equity 0.04). Half of cash held in USD, a natural hedge. [AR FY26; Q1 call]
- Cash from operations FY26 ₹150cr vs adjusted EBITDA ₹107cr (customers pay upfront); FY25 was −₹46cr. Management quotes free cash flow ~200% of PAT and cash return on invested capital 22–23%. [Screener; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Debtor days 90 (FY26) vs 98; working-capital days −10. [Screener]
- Reported ROCE only ~3–5% because of ₹310cr goodwill, ₹139cr acquired intangibles and IPO cash. No dividend. [Screener; IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- Accumulated tax losses keep the tax line near zero or negative for now. [Q4 call]

## Moat evidence
- Forrester Wave Q4 2025: leader, highest scores for current offering and strategy, 5/5 on 22 of 27 criteria. [IP Q1FY27]
- Switching costs: a loyalty platform sits inside ~9 integration points per customer and is audited like a ledger; uptime 99.997% in Q1. Churn ~5% a year. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Market structure: "far less than 10%" of loyalty spend sits with software firms; the rest with agencies (Epsilon ~$2B, Merkle ~$0.5B). ~90 loyalty companies have $10–100M revenue — a long list of buyable targets. [Q1 call]
- Pricing power: contracts carry inflation escalators (3–4% in the West, 7–8% in high-inflation markets) plus usage overages. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Cyber fraud:** between 24 Jun and 2 Jul 2026 an executive at SessionM's Czech subsidiary — acquired only seven weeks earlier — wired EUR 3.05M (₹33.4cr) to fraudsters impersonating the Group CEO. ₹4.7cr frozen in Singapore; Hong Kong recovery 6–8 months; insurance claim 5–6 months; KPMG forensic review under way. Shows weak payment controls in acquired entities. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- **Accounting judgement:** key audit matters on capitalisation of internal development (₹36.6cr in FY26), goodwill impairment (₹310cr) and Kognitiv purchase-price allocation; heavy use of "adjusted EBITDA" and "normalised PAT" (FY26 PAT included ₹25cr indemnity income, Q1 FY27 excludes a ₹33cr loss). Auditor noted it could not confirm a database-level audit trail at a third-party accounting software provider. [AR FY26 auditor's report; IP Q1FY27]
- **M&A dependence:** organic growth ~17% (incl. currency) vs headline 43%; acquired books keep only ~70% of revenue through migration. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- **Concentration:** top-5 ~34% of revenue; one large healthcare customer is flat. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- **Ownership:** promoter is Capillary Technologies International Pte (Singapore holding, 49.70% at Mar-26); promoter group 51.45% at Jun-26 (52.23% at Mar-26). Venture holders (Avataar funds/Trudy, Ronal Holdings, Filter Capital, Schroders PE) are top shareholders — future sales possible. [AR FY26; Screener]
- **AI risk:** management argues AI helps (system of record), but aiRA is still tiny (~$2.5M ARR, <10 paying customers of 150 key accounts). [Q1 call]

## My read
Capillary is a genuine category leader with sticky ledger-like software, ~75% organic gross margins, cash-generative upfront billing, and an M&A engine that has so far bought customers cheaply (SessionM at ~0.07× ARR). The business is improving: adjusted EBITDA margin has gone from ~11% to 17% in a year and FY27 guidance of ₹1,065cr / ₹172cr looks conservative after Q1. But organic growth is only ~15–17% (incl. currency), profits lean on adjustments, and the Czech fraud shows controls lag acquisitions. At a very high reported earnings multiple the price needs roughly 25%+ yearly earnings growth for many years — plausible only if migrations lift margins toward 25%. The view improves if Kognitiv migrations deliver ₹15–20cr extra EBITDA by FY28 and organic NRR returns to 115%; it worsens if another control lapse, a large-client loss or a goodwill write-down appears.
