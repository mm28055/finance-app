## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Yatra Online

## Executive Summary
Yatra Online exists to digitize and streamline the highly fragmented logistics of Indian corporate and consumer travel. For enterprise customers, Yatra solves the friction of offline travel procurement by providing an AI-driven, end-to-end platform that enforces policy compliance, manages expenses, and guarantees cost savings. For B2C and affiliate partners, it provides deep, API-led access to one of India's largest domestic hotel and airfare inventories. 

While Yatra operates as a **Challenger** in the fiercely competitive B2C online travel agency (OTA) market, it has definitively evolved into a **Category Leader** in India’s B2B and enterprise travel sector. By pivoting from aggressive B2C customer acquisition to highly retentive, SaaS-like corporate travel management, Yatra decoupled its fate from cash-burning consumer discount wars to achieve peak profitability in FY26. Entering FY27, the company is navigating a heavily margin-compressed "building through the turbulence" phase, absorbing upfront investments to expand its MSME and international footprint while waiting for geopolitical headwinds to subside.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
*   **What do they sell?** 
    *   *Corporate (B2B/B2E):* Comprehensive travel management, MICE (Meetings, Incentives, Conferences, and Exhibitions) packages, and SaaS-based expense management software (Recap).
    *   *Consumer (B2C) & Affiliates:* Air ticketing, domestic/international hotel bookings, and API-led inventory distribution.
*   **Who pays them?** Large-cap Indian enterprises, mid-market SMEs, retail consumers, and a growing network of B2B travel agents/affiliates.
*   **How do they make money?** Yatra generates Revenue Less Service Cost (RLSC or gross margin) via transaction take-rates/commissions on gross booking value (TTV). They also capture spread margins on MICE events and recurring subscription fees from their integrated expense management tools.

The B2B/B2C split historically averages 65/35 in favor of corporate, though it has slightly moderated to the mid-60s due to recent MICE weakness.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
*   **Durable Moat in B2B (High Switching Costs):** Yatra’s enterprise moat remains formidable. By integrating Agentic AI (for policy compliance and anomaly detection) and its Recap expense platform directly into corporate ERP systems, Yatra generates an exceptional client retention rate consistently north of 97%.
*   **Fleeting Moat in B2C (Commoditized):** The retail flight booking space remains transactional and discount-driven. Yatra mitigates this by focusing on profitable unit economics rather than sheer volume, leveraging technology to upsell add-ons rather than competing purely on base fares. 
*   **The Bear Case on Moat:** Supplier consolidation and structural macro vulnerability. The Indian aviation market is an effective duopoly, giving airlines leverage over commissions. Furthermore, Yatra's high-margin segments are highly exposed to discretionary corporate budgets, which quickly contract during geopolitical instability. 

## Growth Vectors
*   **The Mid-Market Corporate Whitespace:** With Indian enterprise travel online penetration still under 25%, the MSME market is a massive untapped vector. Yatra deployed "TravelPro," a dedicated down-market offering that is rapidly accelerating pipeline velocity. In Q1 FY27, TravelPro accounted for 30 of the 53 new corporate logos won (capturing ~INR 80 Cr in Annual Billable Value).
*   **Middle East Expansion (Kanoo JV):** Yatra is globalizing its corporate tech stack via a joint venture with Kanoo Travel. While front-loaded hiring and GCP cloud migration costs dragged near-term P&L, it establishes a SaaS-like revenue stream in a lucrative adjacent geography.
*   **SaaS Cross-Selling:** The rollout of the Recap expense management solution serves as a wedge to acquire new accounts and expand wallet share, adding 20 new customers shortly after launch.
*   **Management Execution:** Execution has recently stumbled compared to FY26. Facing macro volatility, management formally withheld numeric FY27 guidance and reported a 10.4% YoY drop in Q1 revenue with a 39.4% YoY plunge in Adjusted EBITDA. Crucial catalysts have also stalled: the corporate credit card rollout is delayed over MDR fee disputes with banks, and the highly anticipated US holding company restructuring remains stuck in legal limbo. 

## Key Risks & Governance
*   **High Sensitivity to Geopolitical & Operational Shocks:** The business is highly leveraged to macro stability. Escalating Middle East conflicts decimated Q1 FY27 performance, costing the company ~INR 30 Cr in lost high-margin MICE revenue and wiping out PAT (a negligible INR 0.34 Cr).
*   **Margin Compression from Fixed Costs:** Management has absorbed elevated hiring and technology setup costs ahead of revenue realization (particularly for the Kanoo JV), significantly compressing EBITDA margins during a period of top-line weakness. 
*   **Currency and Inflation Risks:** Sustained international airfare inflation (up 20-30%) acts as a structural deterrent to outbound travel, squeezing fixed-price MICE margins as input costs rise unexpectedly.
*   **Governance / Forensic Check:** While operating metrics are reported transparently, management has become noticeably evasive regarding the structural collapse of the US holding company, citing multi-jurisdictional complexities and frustrating institutional investors seeking liquidity. However, the balance sheet remains healthy, carrying INR 197.6 Cr in cash and equivalents against minimal debt.

## Investment Thesis
*   **The Bull Case:** Yatra is a mispriced proxy for the structural formalization of the Indian economy. As infrastructure improves and GCCs rapidly scale across India, corporate mobility will boom. Yatra is harvesting this with surgical precision—capturing market share in Air and expanding its B2B moat into the MSME space via TravelPro. While Q1 was rough, early Q2 data already shows a 50% sequential bounce-back in MICE travel. If the Kanoo JV scales successfully and margins breach the 20% threshold by FY28, the stock is poised for a significant re-rating upon eventual US holding company simplification.
*   **The Bear Case:** The travel industry remains inherently fragile, and Yatra is currently bearing the brunt of it. Sustained geopolitical tension and elevated international airfares may structurally impair the lucrative MICE business longer than anticipated. If MSME client acquisition through TravelPro fails to offset these lost margins, management's 30% Adjusted EBITDA CAGR target will prove impossible. Stay away if you believe corporate travel budgets face secular tightening, or if you view the opaque, delayed US holding structure as a permanent trap for institutional liquidity.
