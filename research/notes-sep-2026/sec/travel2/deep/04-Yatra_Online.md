# Yatra Online Ltd (NSE: YATRA) — deep research, Sep 2026

Price ₹104, market cap ₹1,629 cr, P/E 47.3, ROCE 7.2%, ROE 6.2%, no dividend, 52-week range ₹89–202 (down 49% from high) [Screener, 27-Sep-26]. Promoter: Yatra Online Inc. (Nasdaq-listed) via THCL Travel Holding Cyprus, 62.66% [Screener, Jun-26; AR FY26 note 36].

## Business and segments
- India's largest managed corporate-travel company plus a consumer online travel agency. 1,300+ large and mid-size corporate clients, ~60,000 SME clients, ~55,000 travel agents; corporate retention ~97% [IP Q1FY27 p.3].
- Revenue is booked partly gross (MICE packages) and partly net, so the company tracks "revenue less service cost" (RLSC, its gross margin). FY26 RLSC split: air ₹244.9 cr (51%), hotels and packages ₹153.4 cr (32%), other ~17% [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 p.13].
- Mix: B2B/corporate about mid-60s % of business (down from late 60s); international under 30% of business (was late 30s–40%) [Q1 call].
- Q1 FY27 operating data: gross bookings ₹2,100.7 cr (+17%); air bookings ₹1,657.9 cr (+18%), air passengers 12.64 lakh (+4.8%), air margin 4.2% (4.6%); hotels and packages bookings ₹387.6 cr (+13%), room nights 5.48 lakh (+30%), margin 9.95% (9.05%); total transactions +12% [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- MICE (group incentive travel): Q1 revenue about ₹30 cr lower YoY; gross-margin hit about ₹6 cr; management says MICE is 20–25% of profit in a normal year, at 40%+ operating margin [Q1 call].
- New: TravelPro (SME product) — 30+ of 53 new corporate logos in Q1, about ₹80 cr of annual billable value; Recap expense software — 20 customers; seven-year partnership with Kanoo Travel to run corporate travel tech in the Middle East, revenue from July 2026 [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.9].

## Key numbers (Screener consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly revenue: Q1 FY26 209.8, Q2 350.9, Q3 256.8, Q4 189.0, Q1 FY27 187.9. Operating profit: 23.1, 23.9, 22.5, 10.9, 12.4 (OPM 11.0%, 6.8%, 8.8%, 5.8%, 6.6%). Net profit: 16.0, 14.3, 8.3, 8.2, 0.34 [Screener].
- Company Q1 FY27: revenue ₹187.9 cr (−10.4%), RLSC ₹122.7 cr (+6.1%), adjusted EBITDA ₹15.1 cr (−39.4%; ₹24.9 cr a year ago), EBITDA after ESOP ₹13.2 cr (−46%), PAT ₹0.3 cr (−98%). Operating expenses rose to ₹108.4 cr from ₹91.8 cr (+18%) on Kanoo, TravelPro and tech hiring [IP Q1FY27 p.14].
- Annual revenue: FY24 422, FY25 791, FY26 1,007 (+27%). Operating profit: 15, 44, 80. Net profit: −5, 37, 47 [Screener]. FY26 RLSC ₹482.4 cr (+24.5%), adjusted EBITDA ₹91.7 cr (+37.5%), PAT ₹46.8 cr (+28%) [Q4 call].
- Q4 FY26 was already weak: revenue −14%, adjusted EBITDA −34%, EBITDA and PAT −46% YoY [Q4 call, official].
- Unit economics: air gross take ~7–7.7%, net air margin 4–4.2% after discounts (up from 2.7% in FY24); hotels net ~9–10%. Each new corporate customer adds "almost a 50% net contribution margin" [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.13].

## Guidance history
- **Medium-term (both calls):** RLSC CAGR 20% and adjusted EBITDA CAGR 30% over 2–3 years — **kept** [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **FY27:** no guidance on either call; "hopefully come out with guidance next quarter" [Q1 call].
- Q4 said Q1 "will likely remain a bit muted" and H2 FY27 "materially stronger than the first half" — Q1 was indeed weak (adjusted EBITDA −39%), so the H2 promise is now the whole case [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Margin: Q1 said adjusted EBITDA/RLSC should be "quite close to the 20% mark" in H2 (Q1: 12.3%; FY26: 19%), with 30% a mid-term aspiration [Q1 call].
- MICE: Q4 said Q1 run-rate 20% above Q4; Q1 said Q2 MICE bookings ~50% above Q1 — **recovering** but from a low base [calls].
- Air margins: expected to recover in H2 once Middle Eastern airlines finalise productivity-linked bonuses (PLBs) [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.9].
- **Corporate card to cut receivables:** Q4 "product ready for the market within a quarter or two"; Q1 still negotiating with banks over the 165-basis-point merchant fee — **delayed** [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Holding-company collapse** (merging the Nasdaq parent into the Indian company): "key priority" on both calls; no timeline — **no progress disclosed** [calls].

## Capex and capacity
- Capex is modest: Screener investing outflow ₹43 cr in FY26; spend is mainly technology (Google Cloud migration, AI tools) and people for TravelPro and Kanoo [Screener; Q1 call].
- Kanoo set-up costs hit Q1 with no revenue; people costs "will remain at these levels", contribution positive from Q2 [Q1 call].
- Payback: not disclosed. Management targets ROCE in the "high teens" within 3–4 years vs ~6–7% now [Q4 call; Screener].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Cash and term deposits ₹197.7 cr at 30-Jun-26 (₹223 cr at Mar-26); borrowings ₹103 cr at Mar-26 [Q1 call; Q4 call; Screener].
- Working capital is the weak spot: receivable days 194 in FY26 (251 in FY25, 389 in FY24) because corporates pay on credit; working-capital days 146 [Screener].
- Operating cash flow turned positive at ₹76 cr in FY26 after four negative years; FCF ₹31 cr [Screener].
- Goodwill ₹52.7 cr reallocated to air and hotel units after an internal merger — a key audit matter [AR FY26 auditor's report].
- ESOP cost (₹1.9 cr in Q1) is borne through parent options and is "non dilutive to YOL shareholding" [IP Q1FY27 p.14].

## Moat evidence
- Corporate: "India's largest managed corporate travel services provider"; retention ~97%; 71% of top-100 customers have been with Yatra over 5 years; 163 new corporate clients in FY26 with ₹956.8 cr annual billable value; 53 in Q1 FY27 with ₹222.3 cr [IP Q1FY27 p.3, p.8; Q4 call].
- Air passenger growth ~2x industry in Q4 (9.6%) and Q1 (4.8%) [calls].
- Online penetration of Indian corporate travel only ~22–23% (Videc report cited) — large headroom [IP Q1FY27 p.4].
- Weaknesses: consumer business is third or fourth behind MakeMyTrip and others; airline commissions are set by a near-duopoly; MICE is lumpy and exposed to international shocks.

## Risks and red flags
- **SEBI query on IPO money (auditor's emphasis of matter):** ₹339.1 cr of deposits/advances to airlines and hotels up to June 2024 were counted as use of IPO proceeds under "customer acquisition and retention, technology and other organic growth". NSE and SEBI queried this; the company replied (last on 26-Dec-25) relying on legal opinions; no further SEBI communication yet. Auditor MSKA & Associates flagged it in both standalone and consolidated reports [AR FY26 note 46/47; auditor's reports].
- **Contingent liabilities ₹131 cr** at Mar-26 (₹115 cr): income-tax demands ₹83.6 cr (including new notices of ₹30.8 cr on subsidiaries and a ₹52.8 cr FY22 demand under appeal), GST notices on input credit ₹10.9 cr, customer claims ₹13.2 cr, bank guarantees ₹22.5 cr — about 16% of net worth [AR FY26 consolidated note].
- **Holding structure:** the Nasdaq parent sold ~1.8% of Yatra India in Feb-26 to fund legal costs of the proposed collapse; promoter stake fell from 64.46% to 62.66%. Timeline unknown; an analyst questioned whether foreign holders have an incentive to fold into India [Screener; Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Profit fragility:** PAT ₹0.3 cr in Q1; fixed costs rising ahead of Kanoo/TravelPro revenue; MICE and Middle East transit exposure (Middle East under 20% as destination, 30%+ of international traffic transits it) [Q1 call].
- Receivables at 194 days tie up capital; the corporate card fix is delayed [Screener; Q1 call].
- No credit rating found.

## What changed between the two calls
- May: "record year" framing, FY26 cash flow ₹76 cr highlighted, Q1 expected muted. August: "investing through the cycle" — Kanoo, TravelPro, Recap costs hit before revenue; cash flow not discussed.
- Q1 opex jumped 18% while revenue fell 10%, turning a MICE shock into near-zero profit.
- New talking point: international share now below 30%; B2C and travel agents gained share.
- Corporate card and holding-company collapse both slipped further.

- Q4 FY26 "yoy=27" is wrong: revenue fell 14% (₹189.0 cr vs ₹219.0 cr).
- "ob" values (227.3, 270.9) are not an order book; they are quarterly new corporate wins' annual billable value — and Q1 should be ₹222.3 cr, not 227.3.
- EBITDA fields are company adjusted EBITDA (before ESOP): Q1 15.1 vs Screener operating profit 12.4; Q4 16.6 vs 10.9; Q3 24.7 vs 22.5; Q2 25.5 vs 23.9. The stored "mgn" is adjusted EBITDA/revenue; the company's own margin is on RLSC (Q1 12.3%).
- Profile omits the SEBI IPO-proceeds query and the parent's February stake sale; "evasive … structural collapse" wording overstates — management said work is ongoing across four jurisdictions.

## My read
The corporate-travel franchise is real (97% retention, steady wins, rising hotel mix), but the listed company earns thin margins, carries 194 days of receivables, and has just had two quarters of sharply lower profit from MICE and Middle East disruption while adding fixed costs. At about ₹1,630 cr market cap, the price assumes FY26-level profit returns and then grows; if adjusted EBITDA gets back to ~20% of RLSC in H2 and the 30% EBITDA CAGR resumes, the stock is reasonable, otherwise it is expensive on current earnings. What would change the view: a clean close of the SEBI IPO query and the holding-company collapse (up), or another weak H2 and rising receivables (down).
