# BLS International Services (NSE: BLS) — deep research, Sep 2026

Price ₹224, market cap ₹9,234 cr, P/E 13.1, ROCE 29.8%, ROE 32.1%, dividend yield 1.11%, 52-week range ₹218–359 [Screener, 27-Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- Two segments. **Visa & Consular** (visa, passport, attestation, citizenship/residency advisory via Citizenship Invest, plus two small UK hotels) and **Digital Services** (housed in listed 51%-owned subsidiary BLS E-Services: banking correspondent (BC) outlets, loan distribution via Aadifidelis, e-governance, assisted e-services) [IP Q1FY27 p.5, p.38].
- Who pays: the visa applicant pays a service fee plus optional add-ons (courier, lounges, SMS, form filling); governments award exclusive contracts, typically 5–10 years, by tender [IP Q1FY27 p.8, p.10]. Stored profile says 3–5 years; the company says 5–10.
- Q1 FY27 split: Visa & Consular revenue ₹560 cr (63%), EBITDA ₹226 cr, margin 40.3%; Digital revenue ₹330 cr (37%), EBITDA ₹27 cr, margin 8.2%. Visa earns about 90% of group EBITDA [Q1 call].
- FY26 split: Visa ₹1,840 cr revenue, ₹738 cr EBITDA (40.1%); Digital ₹1,158 cr revenue (FY25 ₹540 cr), EBITDA ₹81 cr (7.0%) [Q4 call; AR FY26 note 37].
- Scale: 100+ countries, 46 client governments, 44.1 lakh visa applications in FY26 (37.5 lakh FY25), 11.3 lakh in Q1 FY27 (flat YoY). BLS claims about 17% of the global outsourced visa market by value and about 10% by volume, excluding the USA, and "top 2" globally (VFS Global is the leader) [IP Q1FY27 p.10, p.33].
- Unit economics: net revenue per application (revenue less cost of services, ex-citizenship and hotels) ₹3,521 in Q1 FY27 vs ₹3,167 (+11%); FY26 ₹3,302 vs ₹2,903 (+14%); FY23 ₹1,613. Visa net revenue (ex citizenship/hotels) grew only 10.7% in Q1 (₹398 cr vs ₹360 cr) [IP Q1FY27 p.22, p.27].
- Q1 revenue from acquired units: Aadifidelis (loan distribution) about ₹225 cr, iDATA (Turkey, visa) ₹72.5 cr, Citizenship Invest ₹17.5 cr (₹11 cr a year ago), Aadhaar centres ₹17.5 cr, UK hotels ₹16 cr (₹2.5 cr) [Q1 call].
- Digital: 1.58 lakh touchpoints; BC gross transaction value ₹29,500 cr in Q1 vs ₹26,200 cr [Q1 call]. FY26 GTV ₹1.1 lakh cr vs ₹87,000 cr [Q4 call].

## Key numbers (Screener consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly revenue: Q1 FY26 711, Q2 737, Q3 736, Q4 815, Q1 FY27 891. Operating profit: 204, 213, 198, 204, 252. OPM: 29%, 29%, 27%, 25%, 28%. Net profit: 181, 186, 170, 187, 202 [Screener].
- Q1 FY27: revenue +25%, EBITDA +24%, but net profit only +12% because depreciation rose to ₹32 cr (₹23 cr), interest to ₹8 cr (₹6 cr) and tax rate to 14% (10%) [Screener; Q1 call].
- Annual revenue: FY24 1,677, FY25 2,193, FY26 2,998 (+37%). Net profit: 326, 540, 724. OPM: 21%, 30%, 28%. Screener shows FY26 operating profit ₹849 cr; the company reports EBITDA ₹819 cr (the four quarters also add to ₹819 cr) — a classification difference, not an error in the business [Screener; Q4 call].
- Five-year sales CAGR 44%, profit CAGR 68%; three-year sales CAGR 26%, profit 49%. TTM sales ₹3,178 cr, TTM profit ₹744 cr [Screener].
- Margin mix: Q1 FY27 group margin 28.3% was slightly below 28.7% a year ago, as the low-margin digital arm grew faster [IP Q1FY27 p.21].
- Other income is meaningful: ₹95 cr in FY26 (about 12% of pre-tax profit), mostly interest on deposits; ₹5–6 cr of Q4 FY26 was a one-off forex gain [Screener; Q4 call].

## Guidance history
- **Q4 FY26 call (20 May 2026):** "our target is on an increased base to grow the company at 20%-25%"; later, "we'll continue to maintain our growth trajectory of 20% to 25% every year". Visa margin about 40% and digital 7–8% to be maintained; blended margin to hold "subject to the mix" [Q4 call, official].
- **Q1 FY27 call (11 Aug 2026):** target became "15% to 20% for the next 5 years", with management saying "even last time we announced" this — which the official Q4 transcript does not support. In effect, **the growth guidance was cut from 20–25% to 15–20%** while being presented as unchanged. Separately the CFO framed visa growth as travel-market growth of 7–8% plus about 5%, i.e. 12–15%, and "10% to 15%" after contract expiries [Q1 call, official].
- Delivery: Q1 revenue +25% and EBITDA +24% beat both ranges, helped by acquisitions (Aadifidelis was in the base; Atyati was not yet). Visa applications were flat YoY in the seasonally strongest quarter, despite new Slovakia (80+ countries) and Cyprus mandates [Q1 call].
- Margin: Q4 promise "maintain current margins" — delivered (visa 40.3%, digital 8.2%) [Q1 call].
- Tax: Q4 said 10–12% (maybe +1% from Dubai tax change); Q1 printed 14%, full-year guide about 12% [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- UIDAI Aadhaar centres: Q4 said Phase 1 of 40–50 offices done, 200+ offices eventually, "at least 1–1.5 years" to full revenue. Q1 said Phase 3 done next quarter and full revenue "from Q4 to Q1" (Jan–Jun 2027) — a faster timetable than stated in May [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- Aadhaar Seva Kendra project: ₹75 cr spent so far, ₹125 cr total, done by Q2 FY27. Management calls it a ₹2,500 cr, six-year contract at 10–15% EBITDA margin (Q1 call) or 15–20% (Q4 call). The annual report states the UIDAI work order at **₹2,055 cr**, plus a separate ₹100 cr Bihar Aadhaar enrolment order [Q1 call; Q4 call; AR FY26 pp.12–14]. If ₹2,055 cr is earned over six years, that is about ₹340 cr a year of revenue at full run-rate (about 11% of FY26 revenue) and ₹35–50 cr of EBITDA — small next to visa EBITDA of ₹738 cr, and lowering the blended margin.
- Payback: ₹125 cr capex against roughly ₹35–50 cr of annual EBITDA implies about 3 years — acceptable, though centres are leased so depreciation and lease interest rise (Q1 depreciation ₹32 cr vs ₹23 cr) [Q1 call; my estimate].
- Routine capex is small: ₹82 cr in FY26, ₹75 cr in FY25 [IP Q1FY27 p.30]. Capital commitment at Mar-26 ₹31 cr [AR FY26 note 30].
- Acquisitions are the real capital use: ₹1,119 cr investing outflow in FY25 (iDATA, Aadifidelis, Citizenship Invest); FY26 investing outflow ₹446 cr; Atyati Technologies (BC business, mostly non-SBI banks, plus bank software) bought for ₹138 cr in Q1 FY27 (FY26 revenue about ₹275 cr, EBITDA ₹20–21 cr, i.e. about 7 times EBITDA) [Screener; Q1 call, official]. Management's M&A hurdle: 17–20% return [Q1 call].
- Management said in an interview it would put about ₹2,000 cr into acquisitions over 4–5 years (quoted by an analyst on the Q4 call and not disputed) [Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net cash ₹1,617 cr at 30-Jun-26 (₹1,434 cr at Mar-26) — about 17% of market cap [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.8].
- Gross borrowings still ₹431 cr at Mar-26 (current borrowings ₹359 cr) alongside cash and bank balances of about ₹1,182 cr, so it borrows against deposits while holding cash. Deposits of about ₹11 cr (standalone) and further amounts in subsidiaries are pledged against bank guarantees [Screener; IP Q1FY27 p.29; AR FY26 notes].
- Cash flow: operating cash flow ₹903 cr in FY26 (₹829 cr FY25); free cash flow ₹766 cr; CFO/operating profit 116% [Screener]. Applicants pay upfront, so working capital is negative (working-capital days −38) [Screener; IP Q1FY27 p.8].
- Goodwill ₹1,213 cr at Mar-26 (₹1,026 cr) — about half of shareholders' funds of ₹2,422 cr; tested for impairment each year, none booked [IP Q1FY27 p.29; Q4 call].
- Dividend ₹2.5/share for FY26 (about ₹103 cr, 15% payout). A trust bought treasury shares worth ₹137 cr in FY26 and ₹161 cr in FY25 (employee scheme) [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 p.30]. No buyback: "As of now, it is not under consideration, but it may be discussed at the next Board Meeting" [Q1 call].

## Moat evidence
- Global share about 17% by value of outsourced visa market ex-USA; exclusive country contracts of 5–10 years; 46 client governments [IP Q1FY27 p.10].
- Pricing: net revenue per application up from ₹1,613 (FY23) to ₹3,302 (FY26), mainly from moving from partner-run to self-run centres; management now says it has "stabilized", so the easy margin gain is done [IP Q1FY27 p.27; Q1 call].
- Visa margin rose from 14.6% (FY22) to 40.1% (FY26) [IP Q1FY27 p.12].
- Resilience to government risk: the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) barred BLS from new Indian-mission tenders for two years in Oct 2025; the order was quashed in December 2025 and BLS has since won the MEA China tender and several Spain orders [CRISIL Apr-26].
- Digital arm has scale (1.58 lakh touchpoints, ₹1.1 lakh cr annual GTV) but low margins (7–8%) and bank-commission economics; the moat there is network density, not pricing power [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Guidance cut presented as continuity** (20–25% → 15–20%) and flat visa volumes in Q1 despite new contracts [Q4 and Q1 calls].
- **Headline contract size inconsistency:** calls say ₹2,500 cr for UIDAI; the annual report says ₹2,055 cr; office count varies between calls (200+, 300) [AR FY26; calls].
- **Acquisition-led growth and goodwill:** goodwill is half of equity; management could not quantify value created when asked; the CRISIL rationale lists acquisition strategy as a key weakness [Q1 call; CRISIL Apr-26].
- **Government contract risk:** MEA debarment (Oct 2025, quashed Dec 2025) shows the downside. The auditor's emphasis of matter notes an old Punjab government contract termination (2018) that left subsidiary BLS E-Solutions with no revenue [CRISIL Apr-26; AR FY26 auditor's report].
- **Audit coverage:** 21 subsidiaries (total assets ₹3,523 cr, revenue ₹1,595 cr) were audited by other auditors; the main auditor (S S Kothari Mehta) relied on them. Audit trail (edit log) was not enabled at database level — a common but real control gap. No key audit matters; opinion unmodified [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report].
- **Related parties:** services sold to BLS-branded entities where the group has "significant influence" (Kuwait about ₹88 cr in FY26 vs ₹33 cr; also Russia, Argentina, Brazil, Qatar) plus small loans to Spain, Chile, Argentina and Brazil entities; rent paid to promoter family members (Diwakar Aggarwal and relatives). Amounts are small next to group revenue but the Kuwait sales jump deserves a question [AR FY26 note 31].
- Contingent liabilities are small: income-tax demands ₹10.4 cr plus a ₹9.6 cr AY 2024-25 demand under rectification, bank guarantees ₹2.7 cr, claims ₹1.7 cr [AR FY26 note 30].
- Margin dilution as digital, Aadhaar (10–15%) and Atyati (about 7%) grow faster than visa [Q1 call].
- FIIs cut holding from 10.9% (Dec-24) to 4.1% (Jun-26); promoters steady at 70.4%, no pledge disclosed [Screener].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from defending the Middle East war impact (May) to "record quarter" (Aug); management says "The war is over now, so, numbers have started to come back" [Q1 call].
- Growth target cut (20–25% → 15–20%) while the quarter printed 25%.
- Aadhaar full-revenue timing pulled forward to Q4 FY27–Q1 FY28; margin guide lowered from 15–20% to 10–15% [calls].
- Investor pressure for a buyback became the main Q&A theme; answers stayed non-committal.
- Tax rate rose to 14%; depreciation jumped on Aadhaar leases.

## My read
The visa business is superb — 40% margins, negative working capital, ₹900 cr of annual operating cash — but its organic growth has slowed to roughly low-teens value growth on flat volumes, and the self-run margin uplift is complete. Group growth now leans on low-margin digital and acquisitions, and management quietly trimmed its target to 15–20%. At about 13 times earnings with 17% of the market cap in net cash, the price needs only high-single to low-double-digit profit growth, which the base business plus Aadhaar should deliver. What would change the view up: a buyback or sustained visa volume growth from Slovakia/Cyprus/China; down: another government debarment, a goodwill write-down, or an expensive acquisition.
