# Thomas Cook (India) (NSE: THOMASCOOK) — deep research, Sep 2026

Price ₹103, market cap ₹4,867 cr, P/E 21.6, ROCE 14.9%, ROE 9.2%, dividend yield 0.48%, 52-week range ₹86–169 [Screener, 27-Sep-26]. Fairfax Financial owns 64.77% [Screener, Jun-26].

## Business and segments
- Four segments [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]:
  - **Financial Services (forex):** currency notes, prepaid forex cards, student and other remittances under an Authorised Dealer Category II licence. Reported on a net basis. Q1 FY27 revenue ₹89.2 cr (+6%), EBIT ₹40.4 cr, margin 45.3%. Card float about ₹1,800 cr (Jun-26) earns interest.
  - **Travel & Related Services:** B2B about 63% (MICE, corporate travel, inbound destination management "DMS" in India and abroad — Asian Trails, Private Safaris, Allied T-Pro USA, Desert Adventures in the Gulf) and B2C about 37% (Thomas Cook and SOTC holidays). Q1 revenue ₹1,710.6 cr (−14%), EBIT ₹40.5 cr (2.4%) vs ₹81.1 cr (4.1%).
  - **Leisure Hospitality (Sterling Holiday Resorts):** 78 resorts, about 3,800 rooms, 65+ destinations. Q1 revenue ₹161.4 cr (+19%), EBIT ₹52.3 cr (32.4%) vs ₹40.9 cr; EBITDA about ₹62 cr (37%); occupancy 77% (+7 points); average room rate ₹7,809 (+10%); RevPAR +20%.
  - **Digital Imaging (DEI):** souvenir photography at theme parks and attractions, about 50% from the Middle East. Q1 revenue ₹130.7 cr (₹209.7 cr), EBIT −₹15.2 cr (+₹10.6 cr).
- Q1 travel detail: MICE ₹542 cr (+14%); corporate travel net revenue ₹35 cr (+7%) on gross turnover over ₹700 cr (+15%); DMS ₹542.2 cr (−31%), with Desert Adventures revenue −89% and Allied T-Pro −40%; B2C domestic +29%, short-haul +6% (+21% excluding Middle East), long-haul −28% [Q1 call].
- FY26: Financial Services revenue ₹326 cr, EBIT ₹149 cr (46%); Travel revenue ₹6,702.5 cr (+4%), EBIT ₹221.8 cr (3.3%); Sterling revenue ₹548.7 cr, EBITDA ₹170 cr (31%), PBT ₹114 cr [Q4 call].
- CRISIL: international DMS was about 34% and DEI about 10% of FY26 operating income [CRISIL Sep-26].

## Key numbers (Screener consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly revenue: Q1 FY26 2,408, Q2 2,074, Q3 2,146, Q4 1,771, Q1 FY27 2,092 (−13% YoY). Operating profit: 127, 108, 114, 78, 93 (OPM 5%, 5%, 5%, 4.4%, 4.5%). Other income: 45, 64, 11, 35, 61. Net profit: 74, 71, 45, 31, 64 [Screener].
- Company Q1 FY27: total income ₹2,153 cr (−12%), EBIT ₹111.9 cr (−17%), PBT ₹88.5 cr (−21%), PAT ₹63.7 cr (−13%). Excluding DEI and Desert Adventures, group EBIT +8% [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- Annual revenue: FY24 7,299, FY25 8,140, FY26 8,398. Operating profit: 437, 478, 428. Net profit: 271, 258, 220 (FY26 down 15%). FY26 EBITDA margin 6.4% vs 7.1% (CRISIL basis) [Screener; CRISIL Sep-26].
- TTM revenue ₹8,082 cr, net profit ₹211 cr; three-year sales CAGR 18%; ROE last year 9% [Screener].
- Unit economics: forex retail spread about 2–2.2%, wholesale 0.6–0.7% [Q4 call]; travel take rate target 14–15% gross, EBIT 4–5% [Q1 call]; short-haul packages carry similar margin % to long-haul but 20–25% lower ticket value [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- Earlier (per an analyst on the Q1 call): double-digit earnings growth in FY27 under normal conditions. **Q1 call: withdrawn** — "I really don't have an answer to say what will be my full year forecast"; only that H2 should beat H1 "if the situations remain constant" [Q1 call].
- Travel EBIT margin: Q4 said the 5% objective "is not getting disturbed"; Q1 said "internally, we are working in a range of about 4% to 5%". Q1 delivered 2.4% — **missed** (Middle East drag) [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- MICE: Q4 said deferred demand would return in Q2–Q3; Q1 MICE +14% — **delivered early**.
- Sterling: Q4 "extremely optimistic" on Q1 — **beat** (best quarter ever, revenue +21%, PBT +30%) [Q1 call]. Target of 95+ resorts and 4,500 rooms in 2027; pipeline 35+ properties, 2,000+ rooms [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- DEI: Q4 said costs cut and Middle East revenue to recover "approximately a 50% to 60% recovery towards the end of the year". Q1: losses widened to ₹15.2 cr from ₹10 cr in Q4; Middle East recovery sub-20% in April–June, 30–35% in July; cost benefits now expected in Q2–Q3 — **missed so far** [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- External forecast: CRISIL expects FY27 consolidated operating income to fall 2–5% with EBITDA margin above 5% [CRISIL Sep-26].

## Capex and capacity
- Capital commitments ₹40.9 cr at Mar-26 (₹37.0 cr) [AR FY26 note 39]. Capex mainly technology (travel AI platform, DEI's "WeC" software) and Sterling resorts; Sterling grows "asset-right" — 62% of rooms P&L-led (owned/leased), 38% managed [Q4 call].
- A travel AI platform is being co-developed through a joint venture (Indian Horizon Marketing Services) with Atirath Technologies; TCIL subscribed ₹2.5 cr of Class A shares in March 2026 [AR FY26 Board's Report].
- Screener FY26 investing outflow ₹384 cr; FCF ₹566 cr [Screener].
- Payback: Sterling EBITDA margin 31–37% on a mostly leased model; management's group return goal is 20% ROE ("our overall target is to deliver a 20% ROE") vs actual 9% [Q1 call; Screener].
- **Demerger:** board approved (20-Mar-26) a scheme to demerge the resort business into Sterling Holiday Resorts Ltd (SHRL), which will list; TCIL shareholders get 0.81 SHRL share per TCIL share; TCIL's shares will be consolidated 4-for-1 and face value then reduced from ₹4 to ₹3. Completion expected by Q1 FY28; stock-exchange approvals under way, NCLT pending [Q4 call; AR FY26; News: Whalesbook/NSE filing 20-Mar-26].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Cash and short-term investments about ₹2,650 cr at 30-Jun-26, but about ₹1,750 cr is customer float on forex cards; after that, unencumbered cash is about ₹900 cr (CRISIL). Total debt ₹269 cr [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Sep-26]. The CFO confirmed net cash of about ₹730–740 cr at Mar-26 [Q4 call].
- Sterling alone holds ₹374 cr cash and no debt [IP Q1FY27].
- Screener borrowings ₹539 cr at Mar-26 (includes lease-type items); operating cash flow ₹643 cr FY26 (₹717 cr FY25); working-capital days −87 (customers pay in advance) [Screener].
- Goodwill ₹1,183 cr — a key audit matter [AR FY26 auditor's report].
- Dividend ₹0.50/share for FY26 (payout 11%) [Q4 call; Screener].

## Moat evidence
- Forex: one of few non-bank card issuers across Mastercard, Visa and RuPay; RBI will issue no fresh money-changer licences and now lets AD-II players do trade remittances up to ₹25 lakh — a new product line [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]. Education forex turnover +36% in Q1 while RBI data showed education remittances −27% (Apr–May) [Q1 call].
- Float grew from ₹4 bn (FY21) to ₹16 bn (FY26), ~₹18 bn in Jun-26 [IP Q1FY27].
- Sterling: occupancy 77% with 17% more rooms; TripAdvisor awards; NPS above 81% [Q1 call].
- Travel: scale in MICE (110+ groups in Q1, double-digit gross margin in FY26 for the first time) and corporate travel (8,000 clients); but B2C faces OTAs such as MakeMyTrip and "competing with other operators" limits B2B pricing [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Credit: CRISIL AA/Stable reaffirmed Sep-26, lifted by Fairfax parent support [CRISIL Sep-26].

## Risks and red flags
- **Middle East exposure:** DEI (50% Middle East) and Desert Adventures drive the losses; no visibility on recovery [Q1 call].
- **Demerger reduces what remains:** CRISIL expects post-demerger consolidated EBITDA margin to fall to "in excess of 4%" as the high-margin resort business leaves [CRISIL Sep-26].
- **Audit coverage gap:** 47 subsidiaries (total assets ₹2,003 cr, revenue ₹938 cr) were **not audited** by anyone; another 7 (assets ₹991 cr, revenue ₹2,297 cr) were audited by other auditors. Audit trail missing at database level for consolidation software until 19-May-25, and not verifiable for the Sterling software run by a third party [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report, B S R & Co.].
- **CARO adverse remarks:** TCIL and Sterling on clause (i)(c) (title deeds of properties not in the company's name) and TCIL, Sterling, TC Tours and SOTC on clause (vii) (statutory dues). Sterling resorts with disputed titles include Kodai Valley View (carrying ₹102 cr) and Puri (₹201.5 cr) [AR FY26 CARO annexure and notes].
- **Contingent liabilities ≈ ₹382 cr** at Mar-26: tax matters ₹209.5 cr, luxury-tax demands under appeal ₹93.0 cr, disputed claims ₹67.6 cr, ED/FEMA penalty ₹6.2 cr (tribunal upheld ₹3.0 cr on TCIL in April 2026; appeal to Madras High Court), stamp duty ₹4.9 cr — about 15% of net worth [AR FY26 note 38].
- **Pay:** ex-gratia of ₹17.1 cr paid to retiring executive chairman Madhavan Menon (May 2025), approved by special resolution [AR FY26 Board's Report].
- Profit quality: other income (₹61 cr in Q1) was two-thirds of operating profit; FY26 tax rate 33% [Screener].

## What changed between the two calls
- May: "perfect storm" framing, hope that DEI recovers 50–60% by year-end, MICE to come back in H2. August: MICE came back early and Sterling had a record quarter, but DEI and Desert Adventures worsened and long-haul fell 28%.
- Full-year guidance (double-digit earnings growth) was withdrawn.
- July/August long-haul trends improving: "deficit percentages have come down" from 28–30% [Q1 call].
- Tax collected at source on foreign tours cut to a flat 5% — management sees little demand effect [Q1 call].

## My read
Two good businesses (forex at 45% EBIT margins; Sterling at 32–37%) sit beside a large, thin-margin travel arm and a Middle East-exposed imaging business that is losing money. The demerger will hand shareholders Sterling directly, which should make each part easier to value, but it also leaves TCIL with a lower-margin travel-plus-forex company (CRISIL: margin above 4%). At about 22 times trailing profit, the price needs profit to recover towards FY24–FY25 levels (₹258–271 cr) — plausible only if the Gulf businesses stabilise. I would turn more positive on clear DEI/Desert Adventures recovery in Q2–Q3 and a clean demerger; more negative if losses persist or the unaudited-subsidiary and title-deed issues grow.
