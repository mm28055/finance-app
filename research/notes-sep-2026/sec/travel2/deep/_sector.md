# Indian travel services — sector research (Sep 2026)

Scope: online travel agencies (OTAs), outbound and inbound tourism, visa and consular outsourcing, forex, airline capacity, competition from MakeMyTrip, and government-contract risk. Listed names covered: BLS International, ixigo (Le Travenues), Thomas Cook (India), Yatra Online.

## 1. Market size and growth
- **Travel spend:** Phocuswright puts India's travel gross bookings at $41.5 bn in 2024 (+11%), with online penetration of 56% [Phocuswright, India Travel Market Essentials 2025 — search-result summary; page blocked]. Yatra's presentation (citing a Videc report) shows the total Indian travel market rising from about $56 bn (FY24) to $78 bn (FY27) and online penetration from ~41% to ~47% [Yatra IP Q1FY27 p.4].
- **Corporate travel:** about $16–20 bn (FY25–FY27) with online penetration only ~22–23% — the least digitised part of the market [Yatra IP Q1FY27 p.4, citing Videc].
- **Outbound:** 32.71 million Indian departures in 2025 (+5.9%); **inbound** foreign tourist arrivals fell 9.4% to 9.02 million — more than three Indians left for every foreign visitor who came [Ministry of Tourism data via Argus, 2026].
- **Outbound spending:** total LRS outward remittances fell ~2% to about $29 bn in FY26 (from $29.56 bn); international-travel remittances also slipped ~2%; education remittances fell ~21% on US visa curbs [Business Standard / Whalesbook on RBI data, May 2026]. Remittances fell 7.85% YoY in April 2026 [Business Standard, Jun 2026].
- **Air:** domestic passengers 167.7 million in FY26, up only 1.4% (FY25 ~165 million); Indian carriers' international traffic +7.7% in Apr–Feb FY26 [ICRA, Apr 2026].
- **Visa outsourcing:** BLS (citing industry reports) sizes the outsourced visa market (ex-USA) at about $2.2 bn in 2024 rising to $3.2 bn by 2029 (~8–9% a year), with the outsourced share of applications rising from 38% to 50% [BLS IP Q1FY27 p.33–34]. A broker note puts the industry at $2.6 bn with VFS Global at 50–55% share and BLS and TLScontact at 10–15% each [search summary of Nuvama/ValuePickr material — secondary].

## 2. Demand drivers
- Rising incomes, low-cost air and new airports; hotels and buses in small towns; corporate formalisation and global capability centres (GCCs) driving business travel [Yatra AR FY26 MD&A; ixigo Q1 call].
- Shift from long-haul to short-haul and domestic: Thomas Cook long-haul −28%, domestic +29%, short-haul ex-Middle East +21% in Q1 FY27; MakeMyTrip bus +32%, hotels +21% (adjusted margin) [TCIL Q1 call; MMT 6-K, 3-Aug-26].
- Study-abroad and US visa policy: education remittances down ~21% in FY26 [RBI data via Business Standard].
- Visa demand grows with outbound travel, but government pricing and tender cycles, not volume alone, set outsourcer revenue (BLS applications flat YoY in Q1 FY27 while revenue per application rose 11%) [BLS Q1 call].

## 3. Policy
- **"Avoid foreign travel" appeal:** on 11-May-26 the Prime Minister urged Indians to avoid non-essential foreign travel and destination weddings to save foreign exchange, amid high oil prices and falling reserves [TravelBiz Monitor, May 2026]. A direct sentiment headwind for outbound operators (Thomas Cook, Yatra's international MICE) and a tailwind for domestic hotels and buses.
- **TCS cut:** Budget 2026 cut tax-collected-at-source on overseas tour packages to a flat 2% (from 5%/20%) and on education remittances above ₹10 lakh to 2%, from 1-Apr-26 [multiple tax advisories, e.g. Samco/1Finance, Feb 2026]. (Thomas Cook's CEO referred to "a stable 5%" on the Q1 call — this conflicts with the Budget text.)
- **Forex rules:** RBI's FEMA (Authorised Persons) Regulations, 2026 (notified 30-Apr-26, in force 6-May-26): no new full-fledged money-changer licences; AD Category-II players may do trade remittances up to ₹25 lakh per transaction; **existing franchisee arrangements must end within 2 years** (franchisees can become "Forex Correspondents") [BDO India alert, May 2026]. Positive for large AD-II players (Thomas Cook) through less new competition and a new trade-remittance line; a transition cost for franchise-heavy networks.
- **Rail:** IRCTC tightened Tatkal booking windows for OTAs, cut waitlist quota and added Aadhaar/OTP checks — online train volumes fell (ixigo train segments −8% in Q1 FY27) [ixigo Q1 call].
- **Government contracts:** MEA debarred BLS from Indian-mission tenders for 2 years in Oct 2025; order quashed Dec 2025 [CRISIL, Apr 2026]. Delhi High Court on 15-Jul-26 set aside the MEA's award of Indian consular/visa services in Australia to VFS Global as "arbitrary, irrational", ordering a fresh tender [The Australia Today, Jul 2026]. Tender outcomes are litigated and reversible.

## 4. Cost drivers
- **Airline capacity and fares:** West Asia hostilities from end-Feb 2026 closed airspace, raised jet fuel and fares; domestic fares in July still ~18% higher YoY; ICRA expects the airline industry to lose ₹36,000–38,000 cr in FY27 (₹32,000–34,000 cr in FY26) [ICRA via Upstox, 24-Jul-26]. Air India cut Jul–Sep capacity ~20% and IndiGo ~10% [ixigo Q1 call].
- Higher fares lift ticket values but cut volumes and delay airline incentive (PLB) deals — Yatra's air margin fell to 4.2% from 4.6% [Yatra Q1 call].
- Marketing/discounting: ixigo keeps customer-acquisition spend near 4% of GTV; OTAs are adding AI and hotel-supply costs ahead of revenue [ixigo Q1 call; Yatra Q1 call].
- Rupee weakness raises outbound package costs; OTAs and tour operators absorb some to keep demand [TCIL Q4 call].

## 5. Competitive structure and share
- **OTAs:** MakeMyTrip is the scale leader — Q1 FY27 gross bookings $2.85 bn (+19.9% constant currency), revenue $285.6 mn (+16.1%), adjusted operating profit $51.4 mn; FY26 gross bookings a record $10.4 bn [MMT 6-K, 3-Aug-26; MMT FY26 6-K]. By comparison ixigo's Q1 GTV was ₹5,524 cr (~$0.65 bn) and Yatra's ₹2,101 cr (~$0.25 bn).
- ixigo leads online train booking (~63% OTA share) and is #2 in buses behind MakeMyTrip's redBus; it claims to be the fastest-growing flight OTA [ixigo Q1 call].
- Yatra leads managed corporate travel (1,300+ large clients, 97% retention) but is a minor B2C player [Yatra IP Q1FY27].
- **Visa outsourcing:** an oligopoly — VFS Global (~50–55%), BLS (~17% by value, ~10% by volume ex-USA, per BLS), TLScontact; price competition is intense (in a Cyprus tender VFS and BLS both bid €1.90 per visa and the award went by drawing lots) [BLS IP; search summary of Politis/industry reports]. Note: an IndiaTenders blog (Nov 2025) credits VFS with the Slovakia global contract that BLS announced on 18-Nov-25 — unresolved; the BLS exchange filing is the primary source.
- **Forex:** Thomas Cook is the largest non-bank AD-II player; banks and fintechs (e.g. BookMyForex) compete on digital [TCIL Q1 call].

- The profit pool is concentrated in **government-mandated, fee-based services** (BLS visa: 40% margins, negative working capital) and **forex spreads/float** (Thomas Cook forex: 45% EBIT). Pure travel intermediation (tickets, packages, MICE) earns low single-digit margins on gross value; hotels and buses carry higher take rates (ixigo bus CM 53%; MMT hotels the largest adjusted-margin pool).
- ICRA sees premium hotels holding 34–36% operating margins in FY27 (37% in FY26) [ICRA hospitality, Mar 2026].

## 7. Cycle position
- **Down-leg in outbound and air, up-leg in domestic.** Domestic air grew only 1.4% in FY26 and is forecast at 3–6% in FY27; international at 0–3% [ICRA, Jul 2026]. Outbound remittances fell in FY26 and April 2026. Q1 FY27 results show the split: BLS +25% revenue (diversified), MakeMyTrip +16%, ixigo +13%, Thomas Cook −13%, Yatra −10%.
- Management teams (Thomas Cook, Yatra) expect H2 FY27 to be stronger than H1 as "revenge travel" returns once West Asia settles; CRISIL is more cautious [calls; CRISIL Sep-26].

- **My synthesis:** FY27 is a weak year for air and outbound (ICRA/CRISIL), with domestic hotels and buses holding up. FY28–FY29 should see recovery if West Asia stabilises and airline capacity returns (Air India/IndiGo restoring from the festive quarter), but no named forecaster yet gives FY28–FY29 numbers for OTA or outbound volumes; the only multi-year external forecasts are ICRA's long-term aviation CAGR and BLS-cited visa-outsourcing growth.

## 9. Sector risks to watch
- Duration of the West Asia conflict and airspace closures (drives DEI/Desert Adventures at Thomas Cook, Yatra MICE, BLS Gulf volumes).
- Government tender reversals and debarments (BLS/MEA, VFS/Australia).
- IRCTC policy on OTA train access (ixigo).
- Airline capacity discipline and incentive (PLB) deals (Yatra, ixigo flights).
- Policy nudges against outbound travel (May 2026 appeal) and forex-franchise transition by 2028 (Thomas Cook).
