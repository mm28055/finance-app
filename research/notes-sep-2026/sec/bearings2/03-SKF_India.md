## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# SKF India

## Executive Summary
SKF India exists to solve friction. Following the FY26 demerger of its industrial business, the listed entity is now an almost pure-play automotive components manufacturer providing mission-critical bearings, wheel hubs, and drivetrain subsystems. It solves a precise problem for vehicle manufacturers: engineering efficient power transmission that meets increasingly stringent emission standards (BS7/TREM) while facilitating the complex transition to electric vehicles (EVs). 

SKF is unequivocally a **Category Leader**. Unlike commoditized bearing suppliers competing strictly on price, SKF co-engineers proprietary solutions with major OEMs (e.g., TVS, Honda, Suzuki). Stripped of its higher-margin industrial arm, SKF India is transitioning into a leaner, highly localized automotive supplier. While the business recently underwent a painful margin reset during the demerger, profitability has rapidly rebounded, proving the disruption was genuinely transitional as the company capitalizes on strong domestic OEM demand and repositioning for the EV supercycle. 

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** Highly engineered mechanical components—specifically chassis/axle bearings, wheel hubs, powertrain subsystems, and high-speed, electrically insulated bearings for EV motors. 
- **Who pays them?** Domestic automotive Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) across passenger, commercial, and two-/three-wheeler segments, alongside aftermarket distributors and a small cadre of export clients. 
- **How do they make money?** Revenue is generated through long-term volume supply contracts with OEMs (which include raw material pass-through clauses) and spot sales to the fragmented aftermarket distribution network.

### Segment & Geographic Mix
Following the recent demerger, the company's revenue profile has structurally shifted toward automotive dependency. 

| Segment | Estimated Q1 FY27 Mix | Commentary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Automotive OEMs** | ~62% | The core growth engine (2W: 54%, PV: 31%, CV: 15%). Sales volumes grew an impressive 22% YoY in Q1 FY27, driving total company revenue up 27% YoY. |

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
- **The Moat is Durable but evolving.** SKF benefits from high switching costs in the OEM space. Automotive bearings are mission-critical to vehicle safety and performance; once designed into a vehicle's architecture, OEMs rarely switch suppliers to save basis points. 
- **Evidence:** The pivot to EVs and localization is actively expanding SKF's moat. A newly secured, major long-term wheel-end contract with a large Passenger Vehicle (PV) manufacturer (commencing Q4 2028) proves that SKF's localized supply is successfully replacing global imports. EV components further allow SKF to command pricing premiums that offset ICE volume losses.
- **The Bear Case:** The aftermarket moat is highly vulnerable. Management openly admits the vehicle aftermarket is a "completely different game," where intense competition requires continued steep discounts, eroding the brand premium. Furthermore, raw material pass-throughs to OEMs historically lag by 1-2 quarters, creating near-term gross margin volatility during commodity spikes.

## Growth Vectors
- **Where is the next $100M coming from?** EV penetration, regulatory redesigns, and import substitution. Currently, EV platforms are in the developmental phase with revenue conversion slated to ramp up between mid-2027 and late-2028. The impending BS7 and TREM emission norms are forcing OEMs to redesign drivetrains, while new PV wheel-end localization contracts open long-tail revenue pipelines. Management aggressively upgraded its FY27 revenue growth target to ~20% to reflect these tailwinds.
- **Management Execution:** Management is executing well on capital allocation, aggressively pursuing portfolio rationalization to exit structurally unprofitable SKUs. To support future growth and eliminate margin-dilutive trading with the unlisted industrial entity, they are executing a ₹5 billion capex program from FY26 to FY28 (deploying ₹1.7-1.8 billion in FY27) targeted heavily at expanding localized channels at the Haridwar facility, slated for commissioning in Q4 FY27.

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Forensic Red Flags:** High related-party transactions require scrutiny. Currently, ~10% of revenues rely on traded goods purchased from or sold to the unlisted SKF Industrial entity. While management expects this to normalize once Haridwar capacity goes live in Q4 FY27, the blurring of listed vs. unlisted entity value capture remains a near-term governance overhang.
- **Business Risks:** 
  - *Margin Volatility:* While the worst of the demerger margin shock has passed (PBT surged 650 bps sequentially to 14.6% in Q1 FY27), commodity inflation is currently squeezing gross margins due to the lag in OEM price pass-through agreements.
  - *Aftermarket Weakness:* Continuous discounting pressures and shrinking volumes outside the OEM channel signal a structural headwind.
- **What could derail the thesis?** A prolonged slowdown in domestic automotive production. Because management is intentionally limiting exports to serve domestic OEMs, any localized demand shock, delayed EV adoption, or permanent structural decline in the aftermarket leaves the company vulnerable.

## Investment Thesis
- **The Bull Case:** SKF India is a clean, highly localized play on the Indian automotive secular growth story and the EV transition. The initial margin shock of the demerger is completely over. With management upgrading guidance to ~20% topline growth and sustainable ~17% operating margins, the turnaround is firmly in motion. As the Haridwar capacity expansion comes online in Q4 FY27, reliance on related-party traded goods will fall, and the highly sticky PV wheel-end and EV bearing mix will drive long-term compounding. 
- **The Bear Case:** The company is fundamentally constrained by spinning off its high-growth industrial business. Investors are left with an automotive supplier fighting brutal aftermarket price wars against cheaper competitors, chained entirely to OEM cyclicality. Even with stabilized profitability, a prolonged lag in passing through sudden commodity price spikes creates continuous quarter-to-quarter margin volatility, capping multiple expansion as it settles into a traditional auto-ancillary valuation.
