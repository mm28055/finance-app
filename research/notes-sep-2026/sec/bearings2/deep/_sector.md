# Indian bearings — sector research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Coverage: Timken India, SKF India (Industrial), SKF India (automotive), NRB Bearings, Harsha Engineers (cage supplier). Sources are tagged. Figures from company files are taken from the deep-research files in this folder, which cite Screener and the filings.

## Market size and growth
- **Size.** The Indian bearing market was about **US$2.9 bn in FY26**, growing at a CAGR above 9.4%, per Timken's annual report ("internal insights") [Timken AR FY26 p.4]. Timken's chairman put it at "$2 billion, $2.5 billion" against about $20 bn for China [Timken Q4 FY26 call].
- **Mix.** About **65% of Indian demand is "mobile"** (two-wheelers, cars, trucks, tractors, earth-movers, appliances) and 35% stationary or industrial. Mature markets are about 50/50, so industrial should gain share as India adds steel, cement, power and mining capacity [Timken Q4 FY26 call].
- IMARC (quoted in SKF Industrial's AR) values the "industrial bearings" market at about ₹66,000 cr in 2024, growing only **4.3% a year to 2033**. That size is hard to reconcile with Timken's US$2.9 bn for the whole market. Treat IMARC's figure with caution [SKF India (Industrial) AR FY26 p.103].
- **Global.** Mordor Intelligence expects the global industrial bearings market to grow from US$54.6 bn (2025) to US$92.5 bn (2031), a 9.2% CAGR [SKF India (Industrial) AR FY26 p.101].
- Listed Indian bearing makers in this group had FY26 revenue of about ₹11,300 cr (Timken ₹3,419 cr, SKF Industrial ₹3,440 cr, SKF auto continuing ₹2,130 cr, NRB ₹1,335 cr, Harsha ₹1,627 cr including solar). Schaeffler India, the other large listed player, is not covered here [deep files; Screener].

## Trade: imports, exports and China
- India's bearing trade (HS 8482) runs a deficit. **Imports rose to ₹12,380 cr in FY25** (FY23 ₹10,422 cr, FY24 ₹11,037 cr) while **exports were flat at ₹6,387 cr** (FY23 ₹6,654 cr) [Ministry of Commerce TradeStat via busy.in].
- **Imports from China reached US$746 m in 2025**, up about 15% from US$648 m in 2024 [Trading Economics, UN Comtrade-based]. China is the largest single source and supplies most low-cost standard ball bearings.
- The US keeps anti-dumping duties on Chinese tapered roller bearings. The ITC ruled that revocation would likely renew injury, and a 2024–25 review is under way. This helps India-made tapered bearings in the US: Timken India ships tapers to its US parent and says China's tariff on tapers is "far more" than India's [USITC; Federal Register May 2026; Timken Q1 FY27 call].
- The US additional tariff on cages from India fell to zero in 2026, according to Harsha [Harsha Q4 FY26 call].
- Company exports are mixed. Timken's overseas sales rose 17% in FY26 to ₹679 cr, but its US sales fell 15% [Timken AR FY26]. NRB's standalone exports grew only 2.7% [NRB AR FY26]. Harsha's exports from India grew 17% in FY26 and 22% in Q1 FY27 [Harsha calls].

## Policy
- **BIS quality control order for bearings.** A draft Bearings (Quality Control) Order covering about 10–12 bearing standards was published in 2025. These include tapered (IS 12102), deep-groove ball, cylindrical and needle bearings. It would make the BIS mark compulsory for domestic and imported bearings, with 6–12 months to comply depending on enterprise size. Exports and sub-assemblies are exempt [certification-india.com, Jul 2025; ERCS]. **Whether the final order has been gazetted could not be confirmed from primary sources**; secondary sites disagree. Timken said in August 2026 it had secured BIS certification for CRB and TRB rollers [Timken Q1 FY27 call]. If the order is enforced, it raises the bar for cheap Chinese imports and favours local makers.
- **Steel safeguard duty.** India imposed a 3-year safeguard duty on flat steel imports: 12% until Apr-2026, 11.5% to Apr-2027, 11% to Apr-2028 [Argus / Business Standard, Dec 2025 — from search-result summaries, articles not opened]. This supports domestic steel prices. Bearing makers mostly use special alloy bar and strip, but prices move together.
- **GST cuts** in September 2025 (including on two-wheelers under 350 cc) lifted FY26 auto volumes [ICRA 2W report, Mar 2026].
- **Railways.** FY27 budget capex is **₹2.93 lakh cr (+10.5% on FY26 RE)**, of which ₹2.78 lakh cr is budget support. FY26 freight revenue came in 5.1% below budget. The rolling-stock line grows only low single digits in FY27 per the PRS table. The table did not extract cleanly, so treat the exact figure as indicative [PRS Railways DFG Analysis 2026-27]. Wagon output was about 35,000 in FY26E against about 36,000 in FY27E; coaches 9,997 (a record) against 10,122; locomotives 1,914 against 1,870 [Timken AR FY26 p.5].

## Demand drivers by end-market (FY26 actual, FY27 forecast)
- **Two-wheelers:** a record in FY26 (+9%). **ICRA forecasts 3–5% for FY27** [ICRA 2W report, 26 Mar 2026]. This matters for NRB (about 30% of revenue) and SKF auto (two-wheelers are 54% of its OEM sales).
- **Cars:** FY26 +8.6% to a record 4.7 m units. **ICRA says growth will moderate "on an elevated base"**, with trade press quoting 4–6% for FY27 [ICRA PV report, 27 Apr 2026; Autocar Professional].
- **Commercial vehicles:** FY26 volumes crossed 1 m units [Timken AR FY26 citing SIAM/FADA]. **ICRA forecasts 4–6% for FY27** (M&HCV 1–3%, LCV 6–8%), even though April–August FY27 was +23.4%. It expects **a year-on-year fall in H2 FY27** on the base effect [ICRA via HDFC Sky, 18 Sep 2026]. This matters for Timken (trucks and tractors 20%) and NRB (27–30%).
- **Tractors:** FY26 was a record (retail above 10.5 lakh, wholesale above 11.6 lakh, +23%) [Timken AR FY26 citing TMA/FADA]. FY27 depends on the monsoon, which management calls "weak" [SKF Industrial Q1 FY27 call]. No verified ICRA FY27 tractor forecast was found.
- **Auto components overall:** **ICRA forecasts 7–9% revenue growth in FY27**, with ₹28,000–32,000 cr of capex [ICRA via Autocar Professional, 27 Mar 2026].
- **Industrial:** India's wind additions were **6.34 GW in 2025 (+85%)**. GWEC expects 178 GW globally in 2026 and a 5.2% CAGR for 2026–30 [GWEC via Timken AR FY26]. Crisil expects **160–170 MT of cement grinding capacity additions over FY26–FY28**, against 95 MT in the prior three years [Crisil via Timken AR FY26]. Steel capacity is about 220 MT, targeted at 300 MT by 2030 [Ministry of Steel via Timken AR FY26]. These drive the fastest-growing lines: Timken process +28–30% in Q1 FY27, SKF Industrial wind +91% QoQ in Q4 FY26, Harsha bushings +35%, NRB industrial +34%.
- **Rail:** buying is slow in H1 FY27. Timken said government rail procurement is "slow" and tenders are delayed a couple of months as funds go to defence and infrastructure [Timken Q1 FY27 call].

## Cost drivers
- **Steel:** makers raised prices about ₹5,000/t in 2026 (Jan +₹1,500–1,600, Apr +₹3,500) [Timken Q1 FY27 call]. Harsha's raw materials rose 6–8% in Q1 FY27 across brass, copper, zinc, steel and polymer [Harsha Q1 call]. NRB imports 30–35% of its key raw material (steel) [CRISIL NRB, Jul 2026].
- **Energy and consumables:** the West Asia conflict and Hormuz disruption forced a switch from LPG to natural gas at Timken plants. Carbide, grinding wheels, base oils and packaging are all up [Timken Q1 call]. Harsha had ₹3 cr of extra indirect costs and NRB about ₹10 cr of extra other expenses in Q1 FY27 [company calls].
- **Forex:** rupee weakness hurts importers. SKF Industrial took a ₹14.7 cr forex hit on ₹974 cr of finished-goods imports in Q1 FY27 [SKF Industrial Q1 call; AR]. Harsha booked a ₹4 cr hedge-settlement loss [Harsha Q1 call].
- **Pass-through:** OEM contracts pass costs through with a 1–2 quarter lag (SKF auto) or about 4 months (Harsha). Annual rate contracts with the railways and PSUs cannot be changed until renewal (Timken) [company calls].
- **Parent charges:** royalty is about 2.5% of sales at Timken (₹84 cr) [Timken AR]. SKF India paid royalty and trademark fees of ₹89 cr in FY26 [SKF India AR]. SKF Industrial has shareholder approval for up to about ₹222 cr of royalty and trademark fees in FY27, against about ₹25 cr in H2 FY26 [SKF Industrial AR AGM notice].

## Competitive structure, share and where profit sits
- **MNC-led.** SKF (now two listed companies), Schaeffler and Timken dominate. NRB is the largest Indian-owned maker, with **65–70% share in needle and cylindrical roller bearings** [CRISIL NRB, Jul 2026]. Harsha supplies more than 50% of Indian cages and has 80–90% wallet share with the majors' Indian plants [CARE Harsha, Apr 2026; Harsha Q1 call].
- **Made or traded:** Timken 65% made / 35% imported [Timken Q4 call]. SKF Industrial imported ₹974 cr of finished goods in FY26 [AR]. SKF auto is more than 90% localised [SKF India Q4 call]. The traded share caps margins and exposes companies to currency swings.
- **Profit pool (FY26, Screener operating margin):** Timken about 18% (ROCE 18%), NRB about 17% (company EBITDA 19.5%), SKF auto continuing about 15% EBITDA, Harsha 15% consolidated (India engineering about 22–24%), **SKF Industrial about 11% (lowest)**. Profit sits with **localised, engineered niches**: rail tapers, needle and CRB, and cages. Import-heavy distribution models and low-cost standard ball bearings earn less [deep files].
- **Channel economics:** distribution earns "high double-digit" margins against OEM "high single-digit" at SKF Industrial [Q1 call]. NRB says it avoids the "sell at a loss to OEM, make 4× in replacement" model [NRB Q4 call]. Aftermarket is under pressure from fakes and discounting at SKF auto [SKF India Q1 call].
- **Localisation wave:** Timken's ₹720 cr Bharuch plant (SRB/CRB) and ₹120 cr Jamshedpur rail line; SKF Industrial's ₹900–950 cr new Pune plant (2028); SKF auto's ₹500 cr (FY26–FY28); NRB's ₹270–400 cr plus the Unitec JV; Harsha's Advantek plus a ₹90–100 cr China expansion. That is **about ₹2,500–3,000 cr of committed capex** across the group through FY28–FY29, a supply response to BIS rules and import substitution [deep files; CRISIL; CARE].

## SKF India demerger
- The industrial business was demerged into **SKF India (Industrial)**, effective 1 Oct 2025. The remaining **SKF India Ltd is automotive-only** and is to be branded "SKF Vertevo". It is a "reverse carve-out": in India, industrial left the listed company [SKF India Q4 FY26 call; AGM presentation].
- Costs to shareholders: SKF Industrial paid **₹164 cr stamp duty** to transfer land plus about ₹29 cr of IT and demerger costs, and still has about ₹15 cr a quarter of IT duplication to about Q3 FY27. The two companies still trade with each other (about 10% of SKF auto sales go to Industrial) and share the Pune plant, with rent paid [SKF Industrial AR FY26; SKF India calls].
- Globally, AB SKF plans to list SKF Vertevo on Nasdaq Stockholm in **Q4 2026** [SKF press release via MAinsights, 26 Feb 2026]. Vertevo's FY25 adjusted EBITDA was SEK 1.6 bn on SEK 25.9 bn revenue, about 6%, far below SKF India auto's about 15–17%. How the Indian listed company's promoter holding (52.58%) moves to Vertevo, and whether that triggers any open-offer obligation, **has not been disclosed**. It should be watched.
- Investors complained on the Q4 call that post-demerger margins (auto 12% PBT at the time; Industrial about 11–12%) were below what was promised before the demerger (16–19% EBITDA) [SKF India Q4 FY26 call].

## Cycle position
- **Auto:** at the top of a GST-cut upcycle. FY26 set records in two-wheelers, cars and tractors, and FY27 is guided to low-to-mid single digits by ICRA, with H2 FY27 CV declines likely [ICRA reports].
- **Industrial:** mid-cycle and rising, led by wind, cement, steel and metals projects. Distribution/MRO is mixed: steel MRO slow, cement good [Timken Q4 call].
- **Rail:** a temporary procurement lull in H1 FY27, despite record FY26 coach and loco output and +10.5% FY27 capex [Timken Q1 call; PRS].
- **Costs:** an inflationary phase (steel, energy, forex), with pass-through lags compressing Q1 FY27 margins at Harsha, SKF Industrial and NRB.

- **My synthesis:** sector revenue grows about **9–11% a year over FY27–FY29**. Industrial (wind, cement, steel, rail) grows at mid-teens and auto at mid-single digits from FY27's high base. Margins depend on how fast new local plants fill (Bharuch, Pune 2028, Haridwar, Bhayla, Aurangabad). Until then, FY27 margins are held back by cost inflation and pass-through lags.

## Key risks for the sector
- A second-half auto slowdown on the high base and a weak monsoon (two-wheelers, tractors).
- Chinese import pressure if the BIS order is diluted or delayed. Imports are already rising about 12–15% a year.
- Raw-material and forex swings, with railway and PSU rate contracts slow to reprice.
- Parent-company extraction through royalty and trademark fees (the SKF Industrial FY27 approval) and related-party pricing (Timken's FY27 approvals nearly double Timken Corp flows).
- Execution on about ₹2,500–3,000 cr of capex, with lower ROCE while the plants ramp up.
