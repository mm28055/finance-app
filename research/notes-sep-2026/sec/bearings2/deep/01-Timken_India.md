# Timken India — deep research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Screener code TIMKEN. Figures standalone unless marked "consol". Consolidated accounts exist only from FY25 because of the Timken GGB subsidiary (bought Dec 2025, merger with the parent company pending at NCLT). Price ₹3,206, market cap ₹24,116 cr [Screener].

## Business and segments
- Indian subsidiary of The Timken Company (USA), which holds 51.05%. It makes tapered roller bearings (TRB) at Jamshedpur and, since Q2 FY26, spherical and cylindrical roller bearings (SRB/CRB) at a new plant in Bharuch. It also trades imported bearings, gear drives and power-transmission products, and from FY27 GGB plain bearings [AR FY26 p.7, p.16].
- FY26 revenue by segment (standalone ₹3,419 cr): Rail ₹781 cr (23%), Mobile, meaning trucks and tractors, ₹681 cr (20%), Distribution/aftermarket ₹582 cr (17%), Process industries ₹651 cr (19%), Exports/intercompany ₹707 cr (21%) [Q4 call, official].
- Q1 FY27 split of ₹929 cr: Rail ₹200 cr (22%, about +3% YoY per the analyst), Mobile ₹184 cr, Distribution ₹154 cr, Process ₹186 cr (about +28–30% YoY, led by wind and metals projects), Exports ₹200 cr (about +21% YoY) [Q1 call].
- Geography (FY26): India ₹2,740 cr, USA ₹268 cr (down 15% from ₹317 cr), other overseas ₹411 cr (up 57% from ₹261 cr). Total overseas ₹679 cr, +17% [AR FY26, Note 34].
- One customer group bought ₹625 cr in FY26, 18% of revenue (FY25: ₹586 cr). It is not named; it is most likely Indian Railways and its wagon builders [AR FY26, Note 34].
- Sources of supply: about 65% of FY26 sales were made in India and 35% imported for trading. Management expects roughly this mix for 2–3 years because the market keeps widening (for example, 5 MW wind-turbine bearings are bought from sister plants) [Q4 call, official].
- Timken does not make ball bearings. It stays out of cars, two-wheelers and white goods [Q1 call].
- Management sizes the Indian bearing market at US$2–2.5 bn on the call [Q4 call] and about US$2.9 bn in FY26, growing more than 9.4% a year, per the annual report's "internal insights" [AR FY26 p.4].

## Key numbers (Screener, standalone, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly sales / operating profit (OPM) / net profit:

- Q1 FY27: sales +14.8% YoY, PBT ₹150 cr (+15%), net profit +10.6% (tax 23% vs 20%). Management's "19.6% EBITDA" includes ₹10 cr of other income. Screener's operating margin is 18.5%. Gross margin was 39.9%, up 100 bps YoY and flat QoQ [Q1 call; Screener].
- Consolidated Q1 FY27: sales ₹943 cr, PBT ₹156 cr, net profit ₹120 cr [Screener consol; Q1 call].
- Annual: FY24 sales 2,910 / OP 572 (20%) / PAT 392 · FY25 3,148 / 592 (19%) / 447 · FY26 3,419 / 612 (18%) / 398 [Screener]. Sales grew about 8% a year FY24–26, while PAT was flat. FY25 PAT was lifted by a one-time tax item (tax rate 19% vs 25%) [AR FY26 p.8].
- The margin trend is down. OPM fell from 23% (FY22) to 18% (FY26) as Bharuch depreciation, labour-code costs and higher material costs landed. Depreciation rose from ₹85 cr in FY25 to ₹106 cr in FY26 and runs at about ₹124 cr a year on Q1's ₹31 cr [Screener; AR FY26 p.8].
- The Q3 FY26 dip (OPM 13%) came from Bharuch start-up costs, which the company put at about 170 bps, plus a labour-code charge [Q4 call].
- ROCE has fallen from 29% (FY22) to 18% (FY26). The ₹720 cr Bharuch plant was still earning little in FY26 [Screener; Q4 call].
- Unit economics are not disclosed. Q1 FY27 gross margin was 39.9%. Bharuch revenue was about ₹12 cr in Q3 FY26, about ₹60 cr in Q4 and about ₹50 cr in Q1 FY27, with the plant "very close to breakeven" [Q4, Q1 calls].

## Guidance history
- There is no numeric revenue or margin guidance. Management would not accept "10% growth" as a base case and only says it will "outgrow the market" [Q4 call].
- **Bharuch utilisation.** In May, management promised about 70% utilisation "by July", then "July, August". In August it said SRB lines were at 40–45% in Q1 and would reach 70% in August–September, with CRB lines lower and ramping from Q2-end into Q3. **The target slipped by about 1–2 months** and now applies to SRB only. Bharuch revenue fell from ₹60 cr in Q4 to ₹50 cr in Q1, where the Q4 call had implied a steady rise [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Bharuch peak potential.** Management expects about 2× asset turns on roughly ₹310–320 cr of plant and machinery, excluding the ~₹350 cr building, out of ~₹720 cr total. That implies peak revenue of about ₹620–650 cr, and management said "won't be ₹1,000 cr" [Q4 call, official]. FY26 delivered ₹80 cr, and Q1 FY27 ran at about ₹200 cr a year.
- **Price pass-through.** In May, only 10% of the needed price increases had been collected, with the rest due over Q1–Q2 FY27. In August, most truck and tractor customers were paying. Rail and PSU annual rate contracts (ARCs, fixed-price yearly contracts) can only be repriced when renewed. Steel rose by about ₹5,000/tonne (Jan +₹1,500–1,600; Apr +₹3,500). Gross margin held at 39.9%, so **margins were defended** [Q4, Q1 calls].
- **Jamshedpur rail line** (about ₹120 cr). In May: first bearing by November 2026, production in December. In August: commercial production "by the calendar year-end". The annual report says completion in Q3 FY27. **This is on track**, with the wording slightly softened [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26 p.16].
- **Capex.** In May the guide was 8–9% of sales. In August it was "8–10%", with some possible spill-over into FY28 [Q4, Q1 calls].
- **Rail demand.** In May, management called rail "slow and steady". In August it said government buying was "slow" and tenders were delayed a couple of months because funds were diverted to defence and infrastructure. **The rail outlook was cut** [Q4, Q1 calls].

## Capex and capacity
- **Bharuch:** about ₹720 cr invested, including forex and a ~₹350 cr building. All lines are capitalised, and there is space to add size ranges. There are more than 100 new part introductions going through PPAP, the production part approval process customers require before buying [Q4 call]. PPE additions in FY26 were ₹742 cr [AR FY26, auditor KAM].
- **Plain bearings (GGB brand) at Bharuch:** about ₹38 cr, with commercial production due in Q2 FY27 [AR FY26 p.16].
- **Jamshedpur rail components (cups and cones):** about ₹120 cr, with robotic lines imported from Europe. Management expects about 2× asset turns, so roughly ₹240 cr of revenue at maturity. Management points to Africa and North American rail demand as well as India [Q4 call; AR FY26 p.16].
- **Heat treatment** at the new plant, plus solar power: 3.5 MWp on the Jamshedpur roof, 1.9 MWp ground-mounted, 2.3 MWp at Bharuch. There is also a 26% stake in a group-captive power company [AR FY26].
- Capex commitments still open at Mar-26 were ₹83 cr, net of ₹46 cr of advances [AR FY26 Note 33B].
- FY27 capex at 8–10% of sales comes to roughly ₹300–370 cr. That can be funded from operating cash of about ₹440 cr [Q1 call; Screener].
- Management said further capacity for 0–8-inch TRBs (trucks and tractors) is "under discussion", and that it would look at acquisitions [Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- The company is debt-free. Borrowings of ₹15 cr are leases [Screener].
- Cash plus mutual funds fell from ₹511 cr to ₹268 cr in FY26. The money went to capex, the GGB purchase (₹129 cr) and the FY25 dividend [AR FY26 p.9].
- Operating cash flow: FY26 ₹437 cr, FY25 ₹387 cr. That is 94% of operating profit in FY26. Free cash flow was ₹140 cr in FY26 and ₹14 cr in FY25 [Screener].
- Working capital (FY26): debtor days 85, inventory days 136, payable days 105, cash conversion cycle 116 days (FY24: 142). Inventory rose ₹134 cr and receivables ₹98 cr [Screener; AR FY26 p.10].
- Dividends: ₹36/share for FY25 (a large one-off, 61% payout) against ₹2.5/share for FY26 (5%). Management says it pays a big dividend "every 3–4 years" and prefers to fund growth [Q4 call, official; Screener].

## Moat evidence
- Rail is a market leader in freight tapered bearings (Q1 call), and the business is approval-heavy. A customer group, likely Railways, bought ₹625 cr in FY26 [AR FY26].
- The company holds BIS certification for CRB and TRB rollers. BIS is India's quality mark, and it matters for Make-in-India preference in public buying [Q1 call; AR FY26 p.7].
- The 100+ part PPAP pipeline at Bharuch shows how long switching takes. Management said a cement customer reported longer bearing life from Timken [Q4 call].
- Pricing power: gross margin rose 100 bps YoY while steel cost about ₹5,000/t more [Q1 call].
- Group backing: parent technology, a 2.5% royalty, and access to sister-plant products such as large wind bearings. India is also used as an export base, with FY26 overseas sales of ₹679 cr [AR FY26].

## Risks and red flags
- **Related-party dependence.** In FY26 the company bought ₹479 cr of goods from Timken Wuxi (China), ₹359 cr from The Timken Corporation and ₹60 cr from The Timken Company. It sold ₹298 cr to Timken Corp and ₹201 cr to the parent. The royalty was ₹84 cr (about 2.5% of revenue), up from ₹81 cr. The auditor and board say these deals are at arm's length [AR FY26 AOC-2, p.37; p.11216].
- **FY27 approvals nearly double Timken Corporation flows.** Estimated purchases are ₹708 cr against ₹359 cr, sales ₹617 cr against ₹298 cr, and the royalty estimate is ₹97 cr. This is a sign the parent is routing more supply through India, which is good for exports, but it also raises the share of revenue priced by transfer pricing [AR FY26, AGM notice].
- **Timken GGB was bought from the parent group** (Timken Europe B.V.) for ₹128.8 cr in cash on 1 Dec 2025. Because it is a common-control deal, it was accounted by pooling. GGB earned PBT of ₹4.6 cr on ₹16.6 cr revenue in Q4 FY26, a margin of about 28% [AR FY26 consol note; Q4 call].
- **Contingent liabilities total about ₹182 cr**, about 6% of net worth. A provident-fund damages order is ₹103.6 cr, from the June 2024 RPFC order on closing the company's PF trust, now under appeal in the Jharkhand High Court. Transfer-pricing tax claims are ₹63.3 cr (up from ₹50.2 cr) and GST ₹15.3 cr. No provision has been made [AR FY26 Note 33].
- A distributor suit: Artech International is seeking ₹107.9 cr in damages in the Calcutta High Court after being terminated as a distributor. Timken contests maintainability and expects no financial impact [News: ScanX, 30 May 2026].
- **The auditor's opinion is unqualified.** The one key audit matter is CWIP and capitalisation timing: ₹742 cr of additions and ₹100.7 cr of CWIP. Audit-trail compliance was confirmed. CARO lists ₹7.4 cr of unpaid disputed dues [AR FY26 auditor's report].
- **Promoter selling.** The parent cut its holding from 67.8% (FY23) to 57.7% (Mar-24) and then to 51.05% (Jun-24) through block sales. There has been no change since [Screener shareholding].
- US exports fell 15% in FY26, even though management spoke of a strong North American pull. The USA/other split in the AR does not match the "66% export jump" said on the Q4 call. Total overseas sales grew 17% [AR FY26 Note 34; Q4 call].
- Rail ARCs lag cost inflation. Wagon-builder cash flow and government fund diversion make rail volumes lumpy [Q1 call].
- Bharuch depreciation of about ₹25 cr a year is extra, while plant revenue is only about ₹200 cr a year. ROCE will stay below 20% until utilisation passes about 70% [Screener; calls].

## What changed between the two calls
- The tone went from "demand is not in my top three worries" (May) to "railway buying is slow" (August).
- The parent's 80/20 programme came up in August. Under it, Timken globally is selling belts and reviewing automotive OE. For India, management said the focus stays on trucks, tractors, off-highway and rail, with no exit signalled [Q1 call].
- LPG was replaced by natural gas at all plants after the Hormuz disruption. Carbide and base-oil costs are still rising [Q1 call].
- Bharuch revenue went from ₹60 cr to ₹50 cr sequentially. The 70% target now applies to SRB only, with CRB later.
- Export growth normalised from +40% QoQ in Q4 to about +21% YoY in Q1.

## My read
The business is growing about 15% in revenue, but FY26 earnings went backwards because the ₹720 cr Bharuch plant is still near breakeven, and ROCE has slid from 29% to 18%. The investment case rests on three things. Bharuch must reach 70%+ utilisation and move towards about ₹600 cr of revenue. The ₹120 cr Jamshedpur rail line must start by December 2026. And the parent's plan to roughly double Timken Corporation trade in FY27 must turn into export growth. Against that, rail buying is slow and fixed ARCs delay cost recovery. At about 57× trailing earnings, the price needs something like mid-teens-plus earnings growth for years. That is plausible only if margins recover to 20%+ as Bharuch fills. The view would improve if Q2–Q3 FY27 show Bharuch above ₹80 cr a quarter and OPM back above 20%. It would worsen if the SRB 70% target slips again or rail stays flat into FY28.
