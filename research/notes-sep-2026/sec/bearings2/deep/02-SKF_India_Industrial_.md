# SKF India (Industrial) — deep research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Screener code SKFINDUS (standalone; there is no consolidated page). The company was carved out of SKF India Ltd with an appointed and effective date of 1 Oct 2025 and listed as a separate company. Periods before Oct 2025 are **management carve-outs built on assumptions**, so year-on-year comparisons are only indicative [AR FY26 Note 35; Q4 call]. Price ₹2,918, market cap ₹14,426 cr, promoter AB SKF (Sweden) 52.58% [Screener].

## Business and segments
- It sells industrial bearings (tapered, cylindrical, spherical and deep-groove ball), housings, seals, lubrication, condition monitoring and bearing remanufacturing. Customers are industrial OEMs and about 40+ industrial segments through distributors [AR FY26 p.34; Q1 call].
- **One plant** (Chinchwad, Pune). It also buys from SKF's unlisted Ahmedabad plant, from Nilai (Malaysia), and from SKF India Ltd (the automotive company) under job-work. Bearings above 150 mm outer diameter are imported [AR FY26 p.34; Q4 call].
- FY26 channel mix: distribution about 35%, OEM about 51%, exports about 6%, sales to SKF India (auto) about 4%, other about 3%. Within OEM: rail 11%, general machinery 7%, wind and defence 7%, heavy industry 7%, metals 6% [Q4 call, official].
- Q1 FY27 channel mix: OEM 54%, distribution 34%, exports plus sales to SKF India (auto) 12%. In Q4 FY26 it was 52% / 33% / 15%. By industry (Q1 presentation pie), general machinery is the largest at about 26%, and wind, rail, agri/electrical, heavy and metals make up the rest (12–20% each). The pie labels did not extract cleanly [IP Q1FY27 p.14].
- Margins by channel: OEM earns "relatively high single-digit" margins, distribution "high double-digit" [Q1 call].
- The solutions business (remanufacturing, condition monitoring, plant O&M) is about 6–7% of sales and growing at double digits [Q1 call].
- Import dependence: foreign-currency spending on finished goods was ₹974 cr in FY26 [AR FY26 Annexure G].
- Market: IMARC puts the Indian industrial bearings market at about ₹66,000 cr in 2024, growing 4.3% a year to 2033 [AR FY26 p.103]. That estimate looks generous against the listed peers' combined sales.

## Guidance history
- **Margins.** On the Q4 call, management said "around 13%" by the end of CY2026 once demerger costs stop, "15% range" by FY29–30, and a 16–18% aspiration it would not commit to. In Q1, reported PBT was 9% and adjusted 12%. IT separation costs of about ₹15 cr a quarter now run "for the next two quarters" (to about Q3 FY27), ending "Q4 or Q1 next year". The target became "a path to 14–16%" after the 2028 plant, with an aspiration of 17–19%. **The near-term target slipped; the long-term aspiration was raised.** On the Q4 call it was the CY-end 13%, not 14–16%, that was near-term [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Growth.** On the Q4 call, management expected about 8% growth until the new plant arrives, being capacity-limited. In Q1 it spoke of an aspiration to "double the business" within 5 years, about 15% a year. Q1 delivered +18% YoY on carve-out numbers. **The target was raised and so far is beaten** [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Capex.** The AR showed ₹650+ cr of total proposed investment. On the Q4 call it was ₹800 cr+ over FY27–FY30. On the Q1 call it was ₹900–950 cr (the CFO said ₹850–900 cr; the presentation says ₹800–950 cr). **The budget crept up 40%+ in four months** [AR FY26 p.34; Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- **Plant timing.** The new Pune plant becomes operational in mid-2028, with all channels transferred by end-CY2028. This was unchanged. One additional TRB channel is due by early 2027, a new item [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Pricing.** On the Q4 call, management expected 3–4% cost escalation from oil, steel and forex, to be passed on (easier with distributors, harder with OEMs), with distribution price rises already taken. Q1 still showed a ₹14.7 cr forex hit, so **pass-through is lagging** [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- New "Factory of the Future" in Pune: ₹900–950 cr (latest). It covers the building plus deep-groove ball and TRB channels, some moved from the automotive plant and from SKF sites abroad, some bought new. Payback is guided at 5–7 years [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- CWIP was ₹134 cr and open capital commitments ₹46.7 cr at Mar-26 [Screener; AR FY26 Note 34B].
- Space is the constraint today. Until 2028, growth depends on de-bottlenecking, job-work by SKF India (auto), and imports from sister plants [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Funding comes from ₹569 cr of cash (Mar-26) and operating cash flow. There is no debt [AR FY26 balance sheet].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Debt-free. Cash and equivalents were ₹569 cr at Mar-26 [AR FY26].
- Receivables ₹828 cr, inventory ₹536 cr (Mar-26). Screener's FY26 days: debtors 88, inventory 84, payables 116, cash conversion cycle 56 [AR FY26; Screener].
- Net working capital was ₹627.5 cr in Q1 (17.5% of annualised revenue). Management guides 19–20% [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call].
- FY26 operating cash flow was ₹164 cr, net of one-offs such as the stamp duty. Q1 FY27 operating cash was ₹54.7 cr, lowered by name-change and land-transfer charges [Screener; Q1 call].
- Dividend: ₹10/share for FY26, about ₹49 cr [AR FY26].
- There is no credit rating because there are no debt instruments [AR FY26 p.6965].

## Moat evidence
- Brand and approvals in mission-critical uses. Examples: a suburban-rail unitised bearing that lifts axle load from 18 to 21 t and extends maintenance from 1.0 to 1.2 m km, now being tested for 180 km/h, and remanufacturing with 3-year warranties [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Wins: ₹140 cr from a global wind gearbox OEM over 1 year; ₹35 cr (Q1) and ₹32.5 cr (Q4) from tractor OEMs, designed in at platform stage and localised [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Localisation is letting SKF **re-enter segments it had quit on cost**, namely wind, freight rail and some tractor business. Management admits it wins share at lower margins [Q4 call].
- The weakness is a cost position tied to imports. Competition at OEMs is "intense", and the higher rail mix "brought down our margin" [Q4 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Royalty and trademark fees could jump.** In FY26, covering only the six months since the demerger, royalty was ₹15.3 cr and trademark fees ₹10.2 cr, about 1.4% of that half-year's sales. For FY27, shareholders are asked to approve up to ₹121.5 cr of royalty plus ₹100.6 cr of trademark fees to AB SKF. That is about ₹222 cr, around 6% of FY27 sales if fully used, with no sunset clause. The group charges 3–4.6% royalty elsewhere. The approval is a ceiling, but it is the single biggest risk to the 14–16% margin path [AR FY26 AGM notice, Annexure E].
- Total FY27 related-party approval with AB SKF is ₹464.7 cr, including ₹133 cr of IT services. FY26 group IT services were ₹48 cr [AR FY26 AGM notice].
- The demerger cost shareholders ₹164 cr in stamp duty and transfer premium to move land between two SKF companies, plus ₹29 cr of IT and other demerger expenses. IT duplication of about ₹15 cr a quarter continues [AR FY26 Note 31; Q1 call].
- Contingent liabilities: civil court cases ₹103.8 cr. Pre-demerger tax disputes stay with SKF India Ltd, but any settlement is shared "in the agreed contingent liability proportion", so the exposure is not zero. Provisions include ₹77.7 cr for dealer and industrial-market incentives and special-price claims [AR FY26 Note 34; provisions note].
- The auditor's report is unqualified with **no key audit matters** [AR FY26 auditor's report].
- The MD changed on 1 Sep 2026 (Sujeeth Pai replaced Mukund Vasudevan) in the first year after listing. The CFO is unchanged [Q1 call; BSE filing 1 Sep 2026].
- Channel discipline: in Q4, shipments to distributors with overdue payments were held back, and the distribution share fell from 38% to 32% [Q4 call].
- Forex: finished-goods imports of ₹974 cr are exposed to the rupee against the euro and SEK. Q1 carried a ₹14.7 cr loss [AR FY26; Q1 call].
- Merger with other unlisted SKF India entities (Ahmedabad, wind bearings, lubrication) is "not actively considered" [Q1 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- The MD changed. The emphasis moved from the ACES strategy and channel discipline (Q4) to innovation, data centres, humanoid robots and the solutions business (Q1).
- The margin language went from "13% by CY-end, 15% by FY30" to "adjusted 12% now, 14–16% on a path to 2028, 17–19% aspiration".
- Capex rose from ₹800 cr+ to ₹900–950 cr. Growth talk went from "about 8%" to "double in 5 years".
- New in Q1: the ₹14.7 cr forex loss from the Middle East conflict and the weak monsoon.

## My read
Demand is strong: +18% YoY, with wind, general machinery and rail orders. The localisation plan is credible but back-ended to 2028. Profitability is the problem. The underlying margin is about 12% versus 13% two quarters ago, and three possible drags stack up: IT separation (about ₹15 cr a quarter until early 2027), forex on ₹974 cr of imports, and a shareholder-approved ceiling of about ₹222 cr for royalty and trademark fees in FY27 against about ₹25 cr in the last half-year. The capex budget has also crept from ₹650 cr to ₹950 cr. For the price to hold, investors need both faster growth (about 15% a year) and margins climbing to about 15% by FY29. I would get more comfortable if Q3 FY27 shows a clean margin of 13%+ after IT costs end and actual royalty stays near 3% of sales. I would turn negative if royalty and trademark payments rise towards the approval ceiling or plant capex passes ₹1,000 cr.
