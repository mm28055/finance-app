# SKF India (automotive; to be branded "SKF Vertevo") — deep research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Screener code SKFINDIA. Since 1 Oct 2025 the listed company is the **automotive** bearing business. The industrial business was demerged into SKF India (Industrial). Screener's FY26 annual figures and the quarters up to Sep-25 still include the industrial business, so only the quarters from Dec-25 onward are like-for-like [Screener; AR FY26 Note 39]. Price ₹1,540, market cap ₹7,612 cr, promoter AB SKF 52.58% [Screener].

## Business and segments
- Products: bearings, hub units and seals for wheel-ends, transmissions and powertrains, plus EV motor bearings. Customers are two-wheeler, car and truck makers and the vehicle aftermarket. There are three plants (Pune, Bengaluru, Haridwar), 933 employees and 330 distribution partners [AGM presentation Aug-26; AR FY26].
- Q1 FY27 sales mix: OEM 62%, aftermarket (distribution) 20%, exports 8%, sales to SKF India (Industrial) 10%. In Q4 FY26 it was 66% / 20% / 8% / 6% [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- OEM split in Q1 FY27: two-wheelers 54%, passenger vehicles 31%, commercial vehicles 15%. The normal range is 48–52% / 28–30% / 11–12% [Q1 call].
- Customers: the top 5 are about 50% of sales and the top 10 about 70%. Bajaj is number 1, then Mahindra, then Tata (commercial and passenger combined). Royal Enfield and Honda are also in the top 10 [Q4 call].
- Share of EV two-wheeler bearings is about 50%. EV content today is about 6% of the two-wheeler business and about 4% of cars [Q4 call].
- Localisation is above 90%, so the company "does not trade" automotive bearings. Traded purchases were 15–20% of revenue in Q3–Q4 FY26, mostly bought from SKF Industrial because of the auto company's own capacity shortage [Q4 call].

## Key numbers (Screener, ₹ cr)
- Like-for-like quarters, sales / operating profit (OPM) / PBT / net profit:
  Dec-25 577 / 82 (14%) / 86 / 62 · Mar-26 595 / 33 (6%) / 47 / −20 · Jun-26 588 / 88 (15%) / 84 / 62 [Screener].
- Q1 FY27: revenue ₹590 cr, +27% YoY against the carve-out base and −1% QoQ. Product sales were ₹550 cr, with 22% higher volume. PBT was 14.3%, +60 bps YoY. EBITDA including other income was about 17%, flat YoY and +540 bps QoQ. Gross margin was 51% against 42.5% in Q4, a swing management put down to mix and FIFO inventory revaluation [Q1 call, official].
- Q4 FY26: PBT about 9%. Management blamed Q3 one-off gains (forex, fixed-deposit interest, bonus reversal) and gross-margin pressure. Net profit was negative because of a ₹61.5 cr catch-up tax charge from the Bilateral Advance Pricing Agreement, a transfer-pricing settlement with the tax department covering FY13–FY21 (tax rate 142%) [Q4 call; AR FY26 Note 38].
- FY26 continuing operations (automotive only, standalone): revenue ₹2,129.6 cr (+15.4% from ₹1,845.3 cr carve-out). PBT before exceptional items ₹262 cr (12.3%) against ₹355 cr (19.2%). PAT ₹117 cr. The AGM presentation shows 15% EBITDA margin for FY26 [AR FY26 Directors' Report; AGM presentation].
- Management admitted the FY26 PBT margin fell 694 bps. Its reasons were mix (OEM grew about 20%, aftermarket fell on discounts), discounts and demerger costs [Q4 call].
- Unit economics: in Q1, volume was −6.3% QoQ while price/mix added +5.6%. The company "unlocked about 5 million pieces" of capacity through debottlenecking and runs at about 93% loading [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- **Growth.** On the Q4 call, management guided **6–8% revenue CAGR** to 2028. In Q1 it said "close to 20%" for FY27, calling that "much better than the 12% guidance we had given earlier". The official Q4 transcript records 6–8%, not 12%. **Guidance was raised sharply**, backed by 27% growth in Q1 [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Margins.** On the Q4 call, "sustainable" PBT was 11–12% in the near term. In Q1, the company delivered 14.3% PBT, and management confirmed "17% margin", meaning EBITDA including other income, as normal "for the next 2 years or so". **Raised.** Commodity pass-through to OEMs runs through index clauses with a 1–2 quarter lag [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Capex.** The plan is ₹500 cr over FY26–FY28. On the Q4 call, FY27 was put at about ₹200 cr; on the Q1 call, ₹170–180 cr, mainly Haridwar channels for two-wheeler traction-motor bearings, starting in Q4 FY27. **Trimmed slightly.** Revenue from the new capacity comes in FY28 [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Aftermarket.** On the Q4 call, net sales were down on discounts. In Q1 there was a "plateau or small decline", with discounts given for two months that "will not be continued". **Missed.**
- **EV.** Development platforms are to ramp from mid-2027 and be fully visible by Q4 CY2028. A new passenger-vehicle wheel-end localisation win starts in Q4 CY2028 [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- ₹500 cr over FY26–FY28, largely finished by FY28 with some spill into FY29 [Q1 call].
- Its purposes are (a) to replace the volumes now bought from SKF Industrial, which will not add revenue, and (b) growth [Q4 call].
- Open capital commitments were ₹69 cr at Mar-26 [AR FY26 Note 38B].
- The Pune plant is shared. SKF Industrial pays rent and service charges until its new plant is ready (end-CY2028) [Q4 call]. When Industrial moves out, the auto company gets space but loses that rent.

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Debt-free. Cash and equivalents were ₹292 cr at Mar-26, down from ₹711 cr before the demerger split. There is no credit rating [AR FY26 Note 14].
- FY26 dividend: ₹40/share final, about ₹198 cr, a payout of about 74% on Screener. Dividend yield is 2.6% [AR FY26; Screener].
- FY26 working capital (Screener, part-year distorted): debtor days 70, inventory days 52, cash conversion cycle 33. Management said Q4 net working capital rose 4.6% QoQ on one-offs. FY26 cash conversion was about 85% [Screener; Q4 call].

## Moat evidence
- Deep OEM approvals and awards: TVS "Clean Energy Champion", Suzuki Motorcycle best delivery, Honda sustainability [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- About 50% share in EV two-wheeler bearings, plus a new first-time wheel-end win at a large car maker after "so many audits" [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Technology: a high-speed deep-groove ball bearing of about 20,000 rpm for EV and hybrid drives, with about 30,000 rpm in development [AGM presentation].
- The aftermarket moat is weak. Management called it "a completely different game" with "fake products" and many competitors [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Disclosure quality.** On the Q4 call, a long-time shareholder complained that disclosures had "dropped substantially" since the demerger. He noted that before the demerger the auto business was pitched at 16–19% EBITDA. Management promised more detail [Q4 call].
- **Related-party flows with SKF Industrial run both ways at a markup.** About 10% of Q1 sales go to Industrial, and 15–20% of revenue was bought from it in H2 FY26. Rent and service charges also flow. Management says these will fall once Haridwar ramps up [Q4, Q1 calls].
- **Royalty and trademark fees to AB SKF** were ₹89 cr in FY26 (FY25 ₹132 cr, combined company). Management says the rates are unchanged by the demerger [AR FY26 RPT note; Q4 call].
- **Tax.** The BAPA settlement cost ₹61.5 cr extra tax in FY26 (including ₹7.3 cr interest shown as exceptional). A new FY22 transfer-pricing addition of ₹69.4 cr is under appeal [AR FY26 Note 38].
- **Contingent liabilities** fell to ₹214 cr from ₹582 cr: income tax ₹158 cr, excise/customs about ₹26 cr, GST/service tax about ₹21 cr, others ₹9 cr. There is also an old advance-licence customs bond issue from FY1997–98. Pre-demerger tax disputes are shared with Industrial "in the agreed ratio" [AR FY26 Note 38].
- The auditor's opinion is unqualified per the board report. The key audit matter text was not legible in the extracted AR [AR FY26].
- **Concentration.** The top 10 customers are about 70% of sales, and two-wheelers are more than half of OEM sales [Q4, Q1 calls].
- There was management churn around the demerger: an interim CFO, then Mayank Holani; a new company secretary [Q4 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- The tone moved from defensive (Q4: an 11–12% PBT floor, 6–8% growth, an angry shareholder) to confident (Q1: about 20% growth, about 17% EBITDA "normal").
- Demerger costs have been dropped from the discussion. The new emphasis is on the passenger-vehicle wheel-end win, EV ramp timing and plant decarbonisation.
- The aftermarket is still weak, and the discounting has now been admitted.
- FY27 capex moved from about ₹200 cr to ₹170–180 cr.

## My read
The automotive company looks better than the demerger quarter suggested. Q1 grew 27%, PBT recovered to 14.3%, and management now guides about 20% growth and about 17% EBITDA for two years. Much of this rides on GST-cut auto demand, and about 10% of sales are low-value sales to its sister company that will fade. Near-term growth is capped by 93% loading until the Haridwar channels start in Q4 FY27. The aftermarket (20%) is losing on price, and disclosure is thinner than before. The price needs roughly mid-teens earnings growth. That is achievable if margins hold at about 14% PBT and sales grow 15–20% in FY27, then about 8–10% after. I would turn more positive if Q2–Q3 PBT stays above 13% with aftermarket volumes stabilising. I would turn more cautious if margins fall back towards 11–12% once commodity pass-through lags bite.
