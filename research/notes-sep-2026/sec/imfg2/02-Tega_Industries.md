## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Tega Industries

## Executive Summary
Tega Industries exists to solve a singular, high-stakes problem for the global mining industry: minimizing downtime and maximizing throughput in mineral processing circuits. The company designs and manufactures mission-critical, wear-resistant consumables—primarily highly engineered composite mill liners—that are essential for grinding and sizing operations. 

Tega is a definitive **Category Leader** in composite mill liners. Unlike pure commodity players, Tega's highly specialized polymer-alloy composites (like its flagship DynaPrime line) command significant pricing power and extreme customer stickiness. Following the transformational June 2026 acquisition of Molycop in partnership with Apollo Funds, Tega has evolved overnight from a formidable niche player into a global mining solutions behemoth. The acquisition fundamentally reshapes the investment debate: it offers unprecedented scale and cross-selling potential into Molycop's massive distribution network, but it also introduces substantial leverage and formidable integration risks. 

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** Highly engineered, wear-resistant consumable parts (composite mill liners, trommels, screens) that must be replaced regularly. They also sell mineral processing equipment (screens, pumps, crushers), which acts as a strategic wedge to drive future consumable sales. 
- **Who pays them?** Global and regional operators of copper and gold mines. These two metals account for over 75% of Tega's legacy revenue, with a geographic footprint skewed heavily towards mining hubs in Latin America, Australia, North America, and Africa.
- **How do they make money?** Primarily through recurring replacement cycles. Consumables are funded via miners' operating budgets (OpEx), leading to sticky, predictable cash flows once a product is spec'd into a mine. The equipment segment relies on capital expenditure (CapEx) cycles but creates a captive installed base.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
**The moat is Durable and expanding.** 
- **High Switching Costs:** Mill liners are a fraction of a mine's total cost but carry massive asymmetric risk. A failed liner causes unscheduled mill downtime, costing a mine millions of dollars a day in lost production. Once Tega validates its products via site trials, operators are loath to switch to unproven competitors to save a few pennies.
- **Pricing Power:** The ultimate proof of Tega's standalone moat is its resilient and expanding gross margin, which reached 62% in Q1 FY27. Management successfully neutralizes raw material and freight volatility by passing costs through to customers.
- **The Bear Case for the Moat:** The legacy moat is secure, but the Molycop acquisition drastically dilutes the consolidated margin profile (Molycop operates at ~13% EBITDA margins vs. Tega’s standalone ~22%). While Molycop's contracts are tied to steel indexes to protect profit per ton, if Tega fails to export its high-margin composite technology into Molycop's higher-volume customer base, the consolidated moat could devolve into a highly levered commodity play.

## Growth Vectors
- **The "Minnow Swallows the Whale" Integration:** The June 2026 Molycop acquisition is the ultimate growth vector. Tega plans to drive revenue synergies by pushing its high-margin legacy products through Molycop's vast global network, and formally targets $20 million in cost and operational synergies over the next 2-2.5 years.
- **Chile Plant Commissioning:** To support Latin American expansion and overcome capacity constraints, a new localized manufacturing facility in Chile is completing civil works. It is slated for soft commissioning in January 2027 and commercial production by March 2027.
- **Equipment Segment Turnaround:** While the equipment business previously showed rapid scale, it suffered a sharp 44% revenue contraction in Q1 FY27 due to lumpiness in customer site clearances. Normalizing this segment's execution remains a crucial lever for incremental margin expansion independent of the consumable cycle.
- **Structural Supercycles in Copper & Gold:** Global copper supply is growing at a constrained pace while demand compounds due to the electrification transition, grid investment, and AI data center buildouts. Similarly, gold structural scarcity has driven prices to record highs, incentivizing aggressive global mining activity. This dynamic supports Tega's robust legacy order book of INR 1,230 Cr, of which INR 960 Cr is executable over the next 12 months.

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Massive Leverage & Refinancing Risk:** The Molycop deal is highly levered, though management is deleveraging faster than initially anticipated. Total group debt stands at INR 11,200 Cr, with Molycop net debt reduced to $672.5 million post-closing. Management is aggressively targeting a 3x leverage ratio within 3-4 years, leaving little room for macro missteps or cash flow interruptions.
- **Project Lumpiness:** The sharp decline in Q1 FY27 Equipment revenues proves that project-based revenue introduces severe lumpiness and execution risk, contrasting heavily with the predictability of the core Consumables business. 
- **Exceptional Item "Noise" & Earnings Quality:** FY26 earnings were heavily muddied by acquisition-related expenses. In Q1 FY27, Tega booked a final INR 195 Cr ($19.5 million) hit in Molycop integration and advisory fees. Tracking normalized free cash flow going forward will be critical now that the bulk of transaction noise has cleared.
- **Integration Friction:** Aligning IT systems, fiscal calendars, and corporate cultures across a target that vastly exceeds Tega's legacy revenue size represents severe execution risk. 

## Investment Thesis
**The Bull Case:** 
Tega is a high-quality asset playing directly into the structural supply deficits of copper and gold. The standalone business is printing 62% gross margins and 22% EBITDA margins, having successfully cleared its prior logistical logjams. By acquiring Molycop, Tega just bought a massive global distribution highway. With management already deleveraging the balance sheet and targeting $20 million in actionable synergies, cross-selling premium composite liners into Molycop’s vast customer base over the next 24 months should drive a dramatic equity re-rating as earnings compound and debt is paid down. Management's guidance of a ~15% consolidated EBITDA margin for FY27 suggests immediate stabilization.

**The Bear Case:** 
This remains a classic case of corporate indigestion waiting to happen. Tega’s management team has taken on a massive asset and loaded the balance sheet with over INR 11,200 Cr in total group debt at a time when global interest rates remain punitive. Furthermore, Molycop’s structurally lower margin profile (~13% EBITDA) dilutes Tega's historical return on capital, while the legacy Equipment division has proven to be a lumpy drag on overall profitability. If copper/gold prices mean-revert or if Chinese competition pressures Latin American market share, the debt burden could choke the equity. Investors must carefully monitor early integration cash flows and sustainable equipment conversions before assigning full credit for the Molycop synergies.
