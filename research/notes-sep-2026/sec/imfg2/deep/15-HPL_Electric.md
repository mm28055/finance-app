# HPL Electric & Power — deep research (Sep 2026)

Screener code HPL (consolidated). Price ₹278, market cap ₹1,789 cr, P/E 18.8, 52-week range ₹256–481 [Screener, 28-Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- Two reported segments. **Metering, Systems & Services** (smart, prepaid and net meters, head-end software, one AMISP project in West Bengal) sold to power utilities and AMISPs (the private "advanced metering infrastructure service providers" that win discom tenders and buy meters). **Consumer, Industrial & Services (C&I)**: wires and cables, industrial and domestic switchgear, lighting, fans, sold through 900+ dealers and 85,000+ retailers. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 p.55]
- FY26 segment revenue: Metering ₹1,026 cr (−4.6% YoY; EBIT margin 17.5% vs 17.0%), C&I ₹785 cr (+25.6%; EBIT margin 10.2% vs 11.5%). C&I share rose from 37% to 43%. [AR FY26 p.56; segment note p.216]
- Q1 FY27: C&I ₹277.6 cr (+55%) = ~54% of revenue; Metering ₹237.7 cr (+16.5%) = ~46%. Wires & cables ₹145.8 cr (+78.6%, already 43% of FY26's ₹340.7 cr); lighting ₹56.2 cr (+78%); industrial switchgear +19%; domestic switchgear +6.9%. [IP Q1FY27 slides 13–15]
- Segment EBIT margins fell in Q1: Metering 14.2% (17.7% a year ago), C&I 8.4% (11.3%). [IP Q1FY27 slide 18]
- Capacity: 1.1 crore meters a year (11 million units), 26 million switchgear units, 16 million lighting units; seven plants in Gurugram, Kundli, Gharaunda (Haryana) and Jabli (Himachal). Claimed ~20% share of domestic electric meters and ~50% of on-load change-over switches — but the share claims are footnoted "Frost & Sullivan 2016", i.e. ten years old. [IP Q1FY27 slides 6, 8]
- ~98% domestic; exports small. No customer is above 10% of revenue. A Middle East utility has recently approved HPL meters. [AR FY26 segment note; Q1 call]
- One subsidiary, Himachal Energy (97.15% owned, FY26 revenue ₹197 cr, PAT ₹8.4 cr). Two lighting-project JVs (AOPs) were closed on 31-Mar-26. [AR FY26 p.56, note 43]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated)
- Revenue: FY24 ₹1,461 cr → FY25 ₹1,700 cr → FY26 ₹1,811 cr (+6.5%); TTM ₹1,943 cr. 5-year sales CAGR 16%, 10-year only 5% (the business went sideways from FY15 to FY22). [Screener]
- Operating profit: FY24 ₹193 cr (13%) → FY25 ₹255 cr (15%) → FY26 ₹282 cr (16%). Net profit ₹44 cr → ₹94 cr → ₹91 cr. [Screener]
- Last five quarters (revenue / OPM / net profit): Q1 FY26 ₹383 cr / 15% / ₹18 cr; Q2 ₹434 cr / 15% / ₹22 cr; Q3 ₹474 cr / 15% / ₹20 cr; Q4 ₹520 cr / 17% / ₹31 cr; Q1 FY27 ₹515 cr / 12% / ₹19 cr. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27 detail: gross margin 30.3% vs 38.0%; EBITDA ₹63.2 cr (+8.9%); depreciation ₹18.1 cr vs ₹11.6 cr; finance cost ₹22.1 cr (flat); PAT ₹18.7 cr (+1%). [IP Q1FY27 slide 19]
- The drag below operating profit is heavy: FY26 interest ₹94 cr plus depreciation ₹63 cr ate 56% of the ₹282 cr operating profit. ROCE 13–14%, ROE ~10%. [Screener]
- Unit economics: the company does not disclose meter volumes or prices. Management said meter prices have been falling as volumes rise and copper tonnage in cables is "1.5 to 2 times" last year. [Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (17-Jun-26): C&I "should be looking at 1,000 crores of revenue this year" (up from ₹785 cr, +27%); metering to continue the Q4 run-rate; FY27 capex mainly maintenance; debt to stay at current level; the 2.42% promoter pledge "expected to be reduced"; water meter (Neeram Pulse) "marginal revenues" in H2 FY27; Q4 metering EBIT ~17.5%. No input-cost headwind was flagged ("right now, we don't see anything, no headwind"). [Q4 call]
- Q1 delivery (11-Aug-26): C&I ₹278 cr — on a ₹1,100 cr+ run-rate, so the C&I target is on track or beaten. Revenue +35%. But margins were cut sharply: EBITDA margin 12.3% (vs ~15–17% in FY26), gross margin down 8 points, metering EBIT 14.2%. Management now calls Q1 margins "a conservative… baseline" and hopes for recovery "as we reach Q3"; a return to 16–17% only "if the Middle East situation eases". [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- So revenue guidance was kept/raised in effect; margin expectations were cut. The Q4 call's "no headwind" statement was wrong within weeks. [Q4 call vs Q1 call]
- CRISIL (Apr-26) expects revenue growth of 11–13% in FY27 and margins of 15–16%; Q1 is ahead on revenue and well below on margin. [CRISIL Apr-26]
- No formal FY27 revenue or margin guidance; Q1 call said C&I will see "high double-digit growth" over 12–18 months, and the order book will run 1.5–2 years. [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 was a heavy capex year: investing cash outflow ₹223 cr vs ₹70 cr in FY25; fixed assets rose from ₹498 cr to ₹614 cr and CWIP to ₹41 cr. Segment capex in FY26: metering ~₹125 cr, C&I ~₹52 cr. [Screener cash flow/balance sheet; AR FY26 segment note]
- Result: depreciation jumped from ₹42 cr (FY25) to ₹63 cr (FY26) and ₹18 cr in Q1 FY27 alone (₹72 cr annualised). [Screener]
- FY27: "maintenance capex" in meters (tools and dies can be "substantial") plus automation — 7–8 imported machines, one MCB line doing the work of 44 workers with a 3–4 year payback. Management refused to cap capex at ₹50–100 cr, hinting at "different projects" with separate revenue streams (data-centre cables due May–June 2027; possible relay manufacturing "through a different structure"; gas meters later). [Q1 call]
- A dedicated panel-meter and AMI water-meter plant was inaugurated at Gurugram in FY26. [AR FY26 p.56]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings rose to ₹755 cr (Mar-26) from ₹639 cr (Mar-25) to fund capex; equity (capital + reserves) ₹1,000 cr; debt/equity 0.75. Management said debt will stay "at this level", not fall. [Screener; AR FY26 note 48; Q4 call]
- Operating cash flow was the best in a decade in FY26 at ₹226 cr (93% of operating profit), but free cash flow was only ₹10 cr after capex. FY21–FY25 free cash flow ran ₹57–72 cr a year. [Screener]
- Working capital is heavy: FY26 debtor days 154, inventory days 236, payable days 169; cash conversion cycle 221 days (down from 381 in FY21). CRISIL puts gross current assets at 320–325 days. Trade receivables were ~₹745 cr at Mar-26; about ₹29 cr is over three years old and ~₹8 cr is flagged credit-impaired. [Screener ratios; CRISIL Apr-26; AR FY26 receivables ageing]
- Dividend ₹1 a share (payout ~7%). [AR FY26 p.57; Screener]
- Rating: CRISIL upgraded long-term bank lines (₹1,614 cr) to A+/Stable from A on 9-Apr-26; interest cover ~3.2x expected in FY26; gearing ~0.7x. [CRISIL Apr-26]

## Moat evidence
- Metering: three decades of utility pre-qualification, BIS/IS 16444 approvals, in-house tool rooms, 100+ moulding machines, sheet metal and electronics — CRISIL notes no guarantee has ever been invoked. Management says it is an approved vendor to "almost every AMISP", including both Adani and IntelliSmart. [CRISIL Apr-26; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- But the moat is not visible in pricing: meter prices "have been declining" as AMISPs buy in bulk, and management could only "hope" price rises get into new orders — "it is a competitive scenario". Metering EBIT fell 3.5 points in one quarter. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- C&I: a value brand in tier-2/3 towns; wires and cables growth far above the market (+50% in FY26, +79% in Q1), mostly volume-led per management. Pricing power is modest: cables pass on costs quickly; lighting prices rose for the first time in 7–8 years; switchgear lags. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Order book ₹3,200+ cr (Aug-26), 96% metering; CRISIL notes it was ₹3,177 cr in Feb-26 — i.e. flat for several quarters; orders now come in 3–4 month tranches. [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Apr-26; Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Margin shock**: gross margin −8 points in Q1 on metals, industrial plastics (crude-linked), rupee, and a ~40% minimum-wage rise in Haryana (May 2026). Fixed-price meter contracts cannot pass these on. [Q1 call]
- **Customer consolidation**: Adani has bought IntelliSmart and news reports suggest it may buy a meter maker, which could take captive volume away. Management "will have to wait and see". Tamil Nadu has halted tenders; fresh AMISP tendering is slow. [Q1 call]
- **Promoter pledge** ~2.42% of shares by one promoter entity; management said on the Q4 call it would be reduced but gave no reason. [Q4 call]
- **Disclosure**: management stopped announcing individual orders, citing SEBI rules requiring price and quantity details "for competitive reasons". [Q4 call]
- **Auditor**: unmodified opinion; key audit matter is tax litigations. Contingent liabilities are small (tax/excise/VAT/GST demands in lakhs, well under ₹10 cr in total). [AR FY26 auditor's report p.177; note 47]
- **Related parties**: purchases of services ₹3.6 cr from promoter firm HPL India Ltd; deferred receivable ₹4.7 cr from it; KMP pay ₹7.2 cr. Promoter-held 10% cumulative redeemable preference shares (₹21 cr) were redeemed in FY26. Modest. [AR FY26 note 42]
- **Havells settlement (Nov-25)**: ended a decades-old trademark fight; Havells paid ₹129.6 cr to the "HPL Group" (promoter entities) — the listed company's FY26 accounts show no such receipt. [News: Business Standard, 10-Nov-25; AR FY26]
- Small exceptional charge of ~₹5 cr in FY26 (Q3 other income was −₹5 cr). [Screener; AR FY26 segment note]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone went from a record-year celebration ("walked the talk") to defending margins. Revenue momentum improved (C&I +55% vs +26% in FY26), but EBITDA margin fell from 16.5% in Q4 to 12.3%.
- New issues: 52-week semiconductor lead times, wage hike, Adani consolidation risk, capex no longer purely "maintenance".
- The metering order book stayed at ₹3,200 cr; a new "execution-led, mature" framing replaced the growth framing for smart meters (~15% growth "healthy for an industry of this scale"). [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 16]

## My read
HPL has grown into a two-engine business, and the C&I engine is now the faster one: cables and lighting growing 50–80% is real volume, and a ₹1,000 cr+ C&I year looks likely. But the company earns thin money for its size: interest and depreciation absorb over half of operating profit, working capital runs over 200 days, and FY26 free cash flow was only ₹10 cr. Q1 showed metering has little pricing power against consolidating AMISPs, and the margin fall wiped out the revenue growth at the PAT line. The stock at ~19x trailing earnings already reflects some of this after a 42% fall; it needs margins back to 15%+ by H2 FY27 and debt to start falling to re-rate. A clear sign of metering price increases in new orders, or proof that Adani will not go captive, would improve the view; another quarter at 12% margin would weaken it.
