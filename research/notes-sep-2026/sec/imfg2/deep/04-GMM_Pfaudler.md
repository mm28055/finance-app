# GMM Pfaudler — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code GMMPFAUDLR. Price ₹1,505, market cap ₹6,767 cr, P/E 53.7, book value ₹268, ROCE 13.6%, ROE 6.7%, dividend yield 0.13% [Screener, 28-Sep-26]. Calls read: Q4 FY26 (21 May 2026) and Q1 FY27 (6 Aug 2026), both checked against the official BSE transcripts.

## Business and segments
- World leader in **glass-lined equipment** (reactors, storage, columns for corrosive chemical and pharma processes) plus mixing, filtration/drying, sealing, heavy engineering (heat exchangers, pressure vessels) and engineered process systems. Owns the 140-year-old Pfaudler group (Germany, US, Italy, UK, China, Brazil, Mexico, Poland JV "GMM Inox"). About 18 tax jurisdictions. [Q4 call; AR FY26]
- Global glass-lined share (CRISIL): ~50% in the Americas, ~40% Europe, ~20% China, ~50% India; "near monopoly" in large vessels, intense competition in small vessels; ~70% of top customers served for 20+ years. [CRISIL Sep-26]
- **New reporting from Q1 FY27 (four global divisions):** CRT (corrosion-resistant/glass-lined) revenue ₹466 cr (+10%), order intake ₹502 cr (+23%); PPT (mixing, filtration, drying, sealing) ₹255 cr (+23%), intake ₹367 cr (+64%); HET (heavy engineering, India-based) ₹74 cr (flat), intake ₹58 cr (+719%); PST (process systems/skids) ₹131 cr (+46%), intake ₹80 cr (–78% against a ₹355 cr defence order a year earlier). No division margins disclosed. [IP Q1FY27 p.15–17]
- FY26 geography: India ₹976 cr (+14%), overseas ₹2,548 cr (+9%) of ₹3,524 cr. [AR FY26 consol. note 41]
- Non-traditional industries (defence, nuclear, oil & gas, petrochemicals, metals & minerals, semiconductors) were 43% of FY26 order intake (33% FY25, 31% FY24) and ~43% in Q1 FY27. [AR FY26 MD&A; IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Sep-26]
- Niche units: Edlon (high-purity linings for chip fabs such as Micron/Intel, US nuclear glove boxes) grew from ~US$10 mn to ~US$25 mn revenue; Vatva (Gujarat) heavy-engineering plant ~₹300 cr revenue, could reach ₹700–800 cr with small investment. [Q4 call (official)]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (21 May): no numeric FY27 guidance ("we do not want to be too bullish"); medium-term EBITDA margin 15% "minimum we should aspire to" (then 11.4–11.5%); at least double-digit revenue growth over three years; German restructuring to save ₹45 cr a year; repay US$20 mn of group debt; refinance debt and fix the Luxembourg tax structure "in the coming financial year"; three-year plan and investor day around September (may slip because of the Middle East). [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: revenue +16% (ahead of "double digit"); EBITDA margin 10.1% vs 12.7% a year earlier — **missed** margin improvement (blamed on pricing pressure, mix toward heavy engineering and hiring for the new structure); PAT doubled from a low base. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 FY27 guidance: repay ~€7 mn debt by end-Q2 (CRISIL says repaid); full refinancing in 12–18 months; tax rate ~30% in 18–24 months (**pushed out** from "coming financial year"); 15% margin kept as aspiration, no timeline ("let us deliver a few quarters of consistent results"); investor day "around Q2 or… end of this year". [Q1 call (official); CRISIL Sep-26]
- Segment disclosure promised on Q4 was **delivered** in Q1 (revenue and orders by division, not margins). [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Consolidated capex ₹97 cr in FY26 (₹123 cr in FY25); depreciation ₹158 cr — i.e. capex below depreciation. [AR FY26 note 41; Screener]
- Footprint changes: German factory right-sized, UK and Hyderabad (India) plants closed, Poland JV (GMM Inox) fully utilised and needs more sheds; ~120-person Global Engineering Centre in India to shift design work from Europe. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Recent acquisitions (Lenco, Inox stake) added ₹150–160 cr of intangibles/goodwill in FY26; management says the acquisition phase is "largely behind us". [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- No large debt-funded capex planned. [CRISIL Sep-26]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Screener borrowings ₹850 cr (Mar-25) → ₹1,041 cr (Mar-26). CRISIL gross debt ₹1,437 cr including pension liabilities ₹396 cr and leases ₹206 cr; gearing ~1.3x; cash ₹691 cr spread across countries; net debt/EBITDA 1.9x; interest cover 4.5x. [Screener; CRISIL Sep-26]
- Cash flow is the strong point: operating cash flow ₹284 cr (FY24), ₹378 cr (FY25), ₹389 cr (FY26); free cash flow ₹221 cr → ₹318 cr → ₹367 cr (~90% of EBITDA). [Screener; Q4 call]
- Why debt stays high with cash on hand: financing agreements run to 2028, cash sits in many jurisdictions and includes customer advances; an intercompany loan between the German and Luxembourg entities creates FX swings in interest and tax. Interest rate 6–7% on euro/SOFR debt. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Working capital: debtor days 48, inventory days 167, payable days 123, cash-conversion cycle 92 days (FY26); CRISIL: inventory incl. unbilled 100–140 days, receivables 40–50 days. [Screener; CRISIL Sep-26]
- Pledges: shares of subsidiaries are pledged against borrowings of GMM International S.à r.l. and its subsidiaries (acquisition debt). No promoter share pledge noted. [AR FY26 standalone note on investments]
- Dividend payout ~16%. [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Share: ~50% of glass-lined markets in the Americas and India, ~40% Europe; largest installed base, with an aftermarket/service franchise (reglassing, spares). [CRISIL Sep-26; Q1 call]
- Pricing: gross margin ~60% held through FY26 despite steel/gas inflation; contracts above a 5% cost-change threshold have pass-through clauses. [CRISIL Sep-26; Q4 call]
- Switching costs: long customer relationships (70% of top customers for 20+ years); regulated pharma/nuclear users must re-validate on change. [CRISIL Sep-26]
- Weak points: "continued pricing pressure" in standard vessels; European chemicals weak; "1, 2, and 3 units that are really underperforming" drag group margin. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Low profit conversion:** EBITDA ₹403 cr became only ₹52 cr of net profit in FY26 — interest ₹123 cr, depreciation ₹158 cr, tax ~49%, exceptional restructuring costs. Fixing this depends on a complex multi-country refinancing and tax restructuring now guided at 18–24 months. [Screener; Q1 call]
- **Management churn:** Group CFO Alexander Poempner resigned on 15 Sep 2026 "to pursue new opportunities" (leaves after Q2 results on 4 Nov 2026); Deputy CFO Ankit Nayyar becomes Group CFO. New Group CEO (Gregory Gehlhaus, a family member) from May 2026. [BSE 15-Sep-26; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Promoter stake only 25.18%** (Patel family; Pfaudler's former owner fully exited over FY22–FY24); FIIs fell from 26.5% to 14.4% over three years. [Screener]
- Pension liabilities ₹396 cr (closed plans, sensitive to interest rates). [CRISIL Sep-26; Q4 call]
- Auditor: S R B C & Co LLP (EY network); key audit matters on revenue recognition for long contracts, business-combination accounting and goodwill impairment (goodwill ₹229.6 cr). Audit-trail note: database-level log not evidenced for the parent's ERP. [AR FY26 auditor's report, note 35]
- Contingent liabilities are small (indirect-tax and income-tax disputes of roughly ₹12 cr in total). [AR FY26 consol. note 34]
- Unrealised FX loss of ₹72 cr went through other comprehensive income in FY26. [Q4 call]

## My read
The franchise is strong — half the world's big glass-lined vessel market, ~60% gross margin, and ₹350–370 cr of free cash flow a year — and demand is turning up in pharma (India CDMO, peptides, US onshoring) while non-traditional industries now give ~43% of orders. The problem is what reaches shareholders: an 11% EBITDA margin, high interest and a ~49% tax rate leave only ₹50–65 cr of profit, which is why the P/E looks high. The re-rating case needs margin toward 15% and PAT conversion fixed via refinancing and tax restructuring, both now 12–24 months away, and the CFO who designed that plan is leaving. I would watch Q2/Q3 margins (need a move back above 12–13%), the size of debt repaid, and the promised investor-day targets; another quarter of margin slippage would weaken the view.
