## Business and segments
- Three businesses: (1) **Manufacturing of machinery and equipment** — process-plant equipment (reactors, columns, heat exchangers, some in exotic alloys), boiler tubes and panels, mechanical presses and press brakes (~70% auto-linked), steel castings, contract manufacturing for defence/nuclear/hydro/steel; (2) **Industrial projects (EPC)** — boilers (10–11 technologies), sugar plants and refineries, distilleries, power plants, air-pollution control (FGD), water treatment; (3) **Sugar & ethanol** — Saraswati Sugar Mills (Yamunanagar) and Cavite Biofuel Producers, a Philippines ethanol plant. Sugar & ethanol was 13% of FY26 consolidated revenue; engineering 87%. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Subsidiaries/JVs: Isgec Hitachi Zosen (process equipment; FY26 total income ₹672 cr, PBT ₹103 cr, orders ₹889 cr at Jun-26), Eagle Press (~₹150 cr), Isgec Titan Metal (₹102 cr FY26, ~₹150 cr FY27), Saraswati Sugar, Bioeq Energy Holdings/Cavite Biofuel (Philippines). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Customers in 99 countries. Standalone exports ₹1,169 cr in FY26 (22% of revenue, up from ₹532 cr); ₹385 cr in Q1 FY27 (25%). Export order book ₹1,450 cr at Mar-26; >₹750 cr of export orders booked in Q1 (Africa, Latin America, Southeast Asia; sugar refineries). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Standalone segment margins (EBIT): manufacturing 12.46% (FY26), 12% (Q1 FY27); projects 4.58% (FY26), 5.25% (Q1 FY27). [Q4 call; Q1 call]

[Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: standalone +51% (well ahead), manufacturing margin 12% (in range), projects 5.25% (improving); Philippines only 65–70% utilised with an EBITDA loss (**missed**); FY27 Philippines depreciation now ~₹95 cr (revised down from ~₹150 cr); net borrowings fell sharply (**delivered**). [Q1 call]

## Moat evidence
- Technology breadth: licences/JVs (Hitachi Zosen, Sumitomo SHI FW, others), 10–11 boiler technologies, "perhaps the best technology in the world for sugar machinery"; management now bids only where technology earns a premium and avoids civil-heavy EPC. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Pricing discipline: manufacturing EBIT held at 12–13% for three years; steel/forging costs hedged by back-to-back supplier orders plus contingency. [Q4 call]
- Export share gains: exports doubled in FY26; presses gaining share in Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia. [Q4 call]
- Weakness: projects margin only ~5%; order cancellations (₹550 cr) show customer risk. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Philippines ethanol plant (Cavite Biofuel):** FY26 loss ₹295 cr (₹170 cr depreciation, ₹95 cr forex, ₹70 cr interest, ₹26 cr operating loss); a sale fell through when the buyer could not fund it; reclassified from "held for sale" to continuing operations in March 2026. The subsidiary's auditor flagged a **material uncertainty on going concern**: Bioeq Energy Holdings had a capital deficiency of ₹443 cr (P2.84 bn) at Mar-26 and depends on the parent's undertaking of support; a Singapore subsidiary's accounts also rely on parent support. Isgec's consolidated opinion is not modified (emphasis of matter only). [Q4 call; AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report]
- **Profit conversion:** consolidated tax rate 50% (FY26) and 67% (Q1 FY27) because Philippines losses cannot be offset. [Screener]
- Sugar: Saraswati's output fell on poor cane availability; revenue down in FY27. [Q1 call]
- Contingent liabilities: claims not acknowledged as debt ₹80.9 cr (₹58.1 cr a year earlier); export-obligation bonds ₹720 cr and customs bonds/guarantees ₹48 cr (normal for EPCG/advance licences). [AR FY26 consol. note 42]
- Logistics: fewer ships, higher freight and "opportunistic pricing" on export projects; covered by contingencies so far. [Q1 call]
- Auditor: SCV & Co. LLP; KAM on revenue recognition for industrial-project contracts (percentage of completion). [AR FY26 auditor's report]
- Senior management churn is minor (CHRO resigned 21 Sep 2026; new Chief Supply Chain Officer for projects appointed 15 Sep 2026). [BSE 15/21-Sep-26]

## My read
The engineering core is clearly improving: exports have doubled, project margins are recovering toward 5–6% as old long-duration contracts roll off, manufacturing holds 12–13%, and ₹500 cr of self-funded capacity arrives in FY28–FY29 with ~₹1,200 cr of revenue potential. Consolidated profit, however, keeps being dragged down by the Philippines ethanol plant (FY26 loss ₹295 cr, going-concern doubt at the subsidiary) and sugar volatility, so reported ROE is only ~4% and the tax rate ~50%. The standalone business alone appears to earn well over ₹300 cr pre-tax, so the stock's value hinges on whether the Philippines drain shrinks (sale, or reaching 90% utilisation with positive EBITDA). I would watch Q2 Philippines EBITDA, whether the 10–12% guide is raised at H1, and project-margin progress; a further cash call for the Philippines or order cancellations would change the view.
