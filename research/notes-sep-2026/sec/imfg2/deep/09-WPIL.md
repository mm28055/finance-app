# WPIL — deep research (28 Sep 2026)

## Business and segments
- Kolkata-based pump maker (ex-Worthington Pumps India, controlled by Prakash Agarwal since 2002) with two segments: **Products** (vertical turbine, split-case, concrete-volute, drainage, API pumps for power, irrigation, water, oil and gas) and **Projects** (turnkey water supply, irrigation and wastewater schemes). [IP Q1FY27; CARE Apr-26]
- Built by acquisition: Gruppo Aturia, Finder Pompe and MISA (Italy); APE Pumps, Mather+Platt SA, Eigenbau and Paterson Candy International "PCI Africa" (South Africa; 55% bought 9 Jun 2025 for ~₹90 cr); Sterling Pumps and United Pumps (Australia); WPIL Thailand (joint venture). Rutschi (Swiss nuclear pumps) was sold in FY24. 12 plants; ~1,500 staff. [IP Q1FY27; CARE Apr-26]
- Q1 FY27 revenue by geography: South Africa 43%, Italy 30%, India 23%, Australia 4%. International revenue ₹386 cr vs ₹197 cr (+96%). [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Q1 FY27 segment revenue: Products ₹240 cr (domestic ₹71 cr, international ₹169 cr); Projects ₹260 cr (domestic ₹43 cr, international ₹217 cr). The project segment result was only ₹19 cr (~7%) because Indian project revenue has collapsed while site costs continue. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Order book ₹5,270 cr at June 2026: products ₹1,029 cr (55% international) and projects ₹4,241 cr (55% international); domestic project book ₹1,921 cr = ~₹1,400 cr EPC (mostly Jal Jeevan Mission) + ₹530 cr O&M; international order book ₹2,891 cr. Q4 call had cited "about ₹6,000 cr" including South African orders won after March. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- CARE notes one counterparty accounts for ~40–50% of the standalone order book — a regional/client concentration (state water departments of MP, West Bengal, Telangana, Assam, Maharashtra). [CARE Apr-26]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated)
- Revenue: FY24 ₹1,664 cr → FY25 ₹1,807 cr → FY26 ₹1,855 cr (+3%). Operating profit ₹298 → ₹293 → ₹318 cr; OPM 18% → 16% → 17%. Net profit ₹684 cr (FY24, incl. ~₹500 cr gain on Rutschi sale) → ₹127 cr (FY25, hit by a prior-year tax charge on the discontinued business) → ₹200 cr (FY26). [Screener; IP Q1FY27]
- Quarters (revenue / operating profit / net profit, ₹ cr): Q1 FY26 379/49/26; Q2 426/80/52; Q3 539/113/76; Q4 511/76/47; Q1 FY27 501/75/59. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27 company figures: revenue ₹500.5 cr (+32%), EBITDA ₹75.3 cr (15.0% vs 13.0%), other income ₹11.9 cr, share of JV/associate profit ₹11.2 cr (vs ₹3.1 cr), PAT ₹59.0 cr, but diluted EPS only ₹3.47 — so about ₹34 cr belongs to WPIL shareholders and ~₹25 cr to minority partners in the subsidiaries. [IP Q1FY27]
- Standalone (India) business is shrinking fast: FY24 ₹1,077 cr → FY25 ₹1,148 cr → FY26 ₹763 cr (−34%); Q1 FY27 ₹115 cr (−37%), EBITDA ₹14 cr (12.2%), PAT ₹6 cr. Domestic project revenue was only ₹33 cr in Q1 per the call (the presentation chart shows ₹43 cr; ₹57 cr a year earlier); Q4 FY26 ₹92 cr vs ₹253 cr a year earlier. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- International FY26: revenue ₹1,133–1,136 cr (FY25 ₹668 cr), EBITDA ₹152 cr (FY25 ₹88 cr) — about 13% margin; India EBITDA margin 21% in FY26 on falling revenue (product-led). [Q4 call]
- ROCE: 24% (FY23), 20% (FY24), 16% (FY25), 15% (FY26). ROE last year 11%. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- Standing guidance: consolidated EBITDA margin 15–20%; no revenue number given, only "good growth". [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Q4 FY26 call (May 2026): Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) receivables of ~₹350 cr overdue; "We hope to start receiving it from this quarter onwards"; JJM momentum expected "by the second quarter". South African orders to move into execution from H2, peak revenue in year 2; margins there "much higher". Management was also pursuing acquisitions (US front-end, Saudi Arabia) and had taken an enabling resolution to raise authorised capital. Capex: "small brownfield additions". [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: **JJM cash — missed**: "No" money received in Q1; now expected as "a substantial inflow this quarter" (Q2). **International ramp — delivered**: international revenue nearly doubled and margin reached 15%. **Margin guidance — kept** at the bottom of the band (15.0%). **Acquisitions — dropped from the narrative**; focus moved to buying out minority partners (PCI to 100% within three years). [Q1 call]
- New negative in Q1: Madhya Pradesh Jal Nigam issued a **debarment notice** (cannot bid for new projects until old ones are finished); projects are 65–70% complete with a one-year completion target. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.20]

## Capex and capacity
- Capex is small: "Nothing substantial" planned for FY27; only small brownfield additions for Indian product growth. Fixed assets rose from ₹503 cr (FY25) to ₹654 cr (FY26), mostly from consolidating PCI Africa. [Q1 call; Q4 call; Screener]
- Growth capital goes into acquisitions and minority buy-outs instead: PCI Africa 55% for ~₹90 cr (June 2025), with a contractual path to 100% in three years; similar intent for other South African units and Singapore. [CARE Apr-26; Q1 call]
- No capacity or utilisation data disclosed. [IP Q1FY27]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Screener: borrowings ₹502 cr, cash roughly ₹525 cr at March 2026 → about neutral net debt. CARE: consolidated free cash ~₹546 cr at Dec 2025; gearing 0.38x (FY25); interest cover 7.5x (FY25); fund-based limits 78% used on average. [Screener; CARE Apr-26]
- **Cash flow is the problem**: operating cash flow −₹90 cr (FY24), −₹150 cr (FY25), −₹93 cr (FY26); free cash flow −₹131 cr, −₹187 cr, −₹147 cr. Three straight years of negative operating cash despite ~₹300 cr a year of operating profit. [Screener]
- Debtor days 160 (FY24) → 168 (FY25) → 196 (FY26); working capital days 63 → 70 → 130. Consolidated trade receivables rose from ₹820 cr to ₹1,184 cr in FY26 on flat revenue. [Screener; Q4 call official]
- Standalone (India) receivables are ₹781 cr gross at March 2026 — more than a full year of standalone revenue (₹763 cr) — plus ₹234 cr of unbilled contract assets. Receivables overdue more than one year rose to ~₹289 cr (FY25 ~₹99 cr), yet the loss allowance is only ₹10.6 cr. The company also changed "the basis of adjustment of receipts from customers… changing ageing classification" during FY26. [AR FY26 standalone notes 11–12]
- Management's "stuck" JJM figure is ₹300–350 cr, mostly West Bengal; it says the money is "sitting in the treasury accounts". [Q1 call]
- Minority interest: ₹375 cr of equity belongs to non-controlling partners (FY26), up from ₹299 cr. Dividend payout 12% (FY26). [IP Q1FY27 balance sheet; Screener]

## Moat evidence
- 70-year pump franchise with engineered-pump qualifications (power, navy, irrigation, oil and gas); international subsidiaries give access to MENA oil and gas (gas-turbine pumps), Australian LNG/mining and South African water treatment. [IP Q1FY27; CARE Apr-26]
- South African project terms are much better than India's: "They get advances, payments are typically within 7 days"; work is process/electro-mechanical, little civil or pipeline. Large wins in FY26–27: City of Cape Town Macassar and Lower Umkomazi (~₹1,172 cr and ~₹630 cr); South African book ~₹3,100 cr. [Q4 call]
- Product order book at a record (₹1,029 cr) and domestic product revenue growing (₹72 cr vs ₹65 cr). WPIL Thailand crossed ₹300 cr revenue in FY26 (from ~₹50 cr pre-COVID). [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Pricing: most project contracts have price-variation clauses; steel is ~70% of cost of sales. [Q1 call; CARE Apr-26]

## Risks and red flags
- **Receivables and cash conversion**: three years of negative operating cash flow; standalone receivables > a year of sales; over-one-year overdues tripled; thin provisioning; ageing method changed. If JJM money does not arrive, write-offs are possible. [Screener; AR FY26]
- **Government counterparties**: JJM payments stuck for more than a year; MP debarment; a terminated MP contract in arbitration for 2.5 years. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- **Audit reliance on others**: the group auditor did not audit subsidiaries with total revenue ₹1,146 cr (~62% of group revenue) and total assets ₹1,707 cr — it relies on foreign component auditors; one JV's profit (₹6.3 cr) was taken from board-certified, unreviewed accounts. Key audit matters: estimation of project cost (variable consideration/claims) and property, plant and equipment. No qualification. [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report]
- **Minority leakage**: ~40% of Q1 consolidated profit belongs to minority partners; the reported P/E on total PAT understates the true multiple for WPIL shareholders. [IP Q1FY27]
- Contingent liabilities (consolidated): bank guarantees ₹271 cr and excise/service-tax disputes ₹12.5 cr (total ₹285 cr vs ₹321 cr in FY25). Swiss and French tax dispute on the Rutschi sale: paid under protest; France won, Switzerland pending (~18 months). [AR FY26 note 46; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Related parties: sales to the Thai JV ₹46.5 cr (FY25 ₹21.1 cr); rent dealings with promoter group firms (Bengal Steel, HSM etc.); standalone receivables include ₹59 cr from related parties (FY25 ₹19 cr). [AR FY26 note on related parties; standalone note 12]
- Disclosure quality: on the Q1 call management could not give the cash balance or explain the project segment margin. NSE trading is only in the "permitted" category; permanent listing needs a capital raise. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Currency: ~77% of revenue in rand, euro and Australian dollar; FY26 had a ₹139 cr exchange-translation gain in other comprehensive income. [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone on India went from "renewed momentum… funds are being released" (Q4) to "we are patiently waiting" (Q1); no cash received. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- New: MP debarment disclosed; minority buy-out plan replaced the acquisition/capital-raise talk. [Q1 call]
- Numbers: international margin rose to 15% from ~13% in FY26; consolidated EBITDA margin 15.0% (Q4 FY26 14.9%). Order book stated as ₹5,270 cr vs "about ₹6,000 cr" in May (the May number was an approximation including South African orders). [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## My read
WPIL is two businesses: a growing, better-paid international pump and water-treatment platform (now ~77% of revenue, 15% margin, South African orders just starting) and an Indian project arm that has stalled on government payments. The consolidated profit line looks healthy, but the cash does not — three years of negative operating cash, receivables above a year of Indian sales, and a changed ageing method mean earnings quality is weak until the ₹300–350 cr of JJM dues are collected. About 40% of profit also leaks to minority partners. The price needs JJM cash to arrive in Q2–Q3 FY27 and South Africa to ramp as promised; a positive change would be operating cash flow turning positive and receivables falling, while another quarter of no JJM collections, a debarment spreading beyond MP, or provisions against old receivables would make me more cautious.
