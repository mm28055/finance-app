# Industrial Manufacturing (India) — sector research (28 Sep 2026)

Scope of the note's 21 companies: process equipment for refining/petchem/fertiliser (JNK, Anup, Kilburn, Isgec, John Cockerill, DEE piping), pharma/chemical process equipment (GMM Pfaudler, HLE Glascoat, Standard Glass), ethanol/biofuel plants (Praj, Isgec), mining consumables (Tega/Molycop), water-control and pumps (Jash, WPIL), heat transfer and refrigeration (United Heat Transfer, Shree Refrigerations, ICE Make), cylinders (Everest Kanto), electrical/components (Pitti, HPL, Gala) and CCTV (Aditya Infotech/CP PLUS).

## Market size and growth — the capex backdrop
- **Private capex is still rising, but the mix is power/infrastructure-heavy.** RBI's September 2026 bulletin estimates private corporate capex (bank/FI-funded projects) at ₹3.2 lakh cr in FY27, up from ₹2.6 lakh cr in FY26; ~89% of sanctioned projects are greenfield and infrastructure (power generation/transmission, roads) is more than half of sanctioned project cost. [RBI Bulletin via Whalesbook, 25 Sep 2026]
- **NSO's capex survey** (Oct–Dec 2025, 5,366 firms): provisional private corporate capex ₹11.44 lakh cr in FY26; stated intentions for FY27 ₹9.55 lakh cr (firms report conservatively; the FY25 realisation ratio was 96%). 65% of FY26 capex was funded from internal accruals, 23% domestic debt; 48.6% of firms were investing in core assets, 42% upgrading existing capacity. [NSO/MoSPI press release, 23 Mar 2026]
- **ICRA (April 2026)**: new project announcements halved to ₹13.5 trillion in Q4 FY26 (₹25.5 trillion a year earlier); private announcements fell to ₹7.0 trillion from ₹17.3 trillion; manufacturing announcements hit a 7-quarter low of ₹1.5 trillion while services (transport, data centres) hit a 12-quarter high of ₹10.0 trillion. Project completions were a record ₹10.4 trillion in FY26. Government capex budgeted at ₹12.2 trillion in FY27 (+11.5%), with slippage risk from energy subsidies. ICRA pegs FY27 GDP growth at 6.5% (from 7.5%) assuming $85 crude and says the West Asia crisis "would weigh on the profitability of the corporate sector and consequently impact their capex plans in the near term". [ICRA Investment Tracker, Apr 2026]
- **Capital goods (CRISIL, May 2026)**: sector revenue growth 12–14% in FY27, operating margin stable at 12–13%; order books up 1.5x in two years to ~₹5.2 lakh cr (Dec 2025); book-to-bill 3.7x in FY26 vs 3.1x in FY24; power's share of order books up to 50% from 32%; data centres and EV infrastructure named as new drivers; limited Middle East exposure. [CRISIL via BizzBuzz, 22 May 2026]

## Demand drivers by sub-sector
- **Refining/petchem/fertiliser (JNK, Anup, Kilburn, Isgec)**: company calls describe a refinery and petchem cycle in India (BPCL Bina, IOCL Paradip/Haldia pending, PTA/PVC projects), Middle East gas (ADNOC) and Africa (Dangote Phase 2, six fertiliser trains) — but awards are slipping by quarters because of the West Asia war and crude prices ("it would take a bit of a time … six months to a year" — JNK). JNK says its heater/reformer scope is 6–7% of project cost. [JNK Q4/Q1 calls; Anup Q4 call; Kilburn Q1 call]
- **Thermal power restart and gas turbines (DEE, Anup, Isgec)**: Anup cites 14 GW+ of new thermal projects in India and won >₹150 cr of thermal-power heat-exchanger orders in FY27; DEE's piping book reached ₹2,428 cr on HRSG/gas-turbine demand, with global HRSG makers reserving its capacity. CRISIL sees 58–62 GW of power capacity additions in FY27 (mainly renewables). [Anup Q4/Q1 calls; DEE deep file; CRISIL May-26]
- **Pharma/CDMO and chemicals (GMM, HLE, Standard Glass)**: ICRA estimated capex for 25 large pharma companies at ₹42,000–45,000 cr in FY26 (incl. ~₹25,000 cr inorganic) (Sep 2025). Specialty chemicals are going the other way: CRISIL expects FY27 capex to be pared to ~₹16,500 cr, revenue growth ~6% (8% in each of the prior two years) and operating margin down 150–200 bp to 14–14.5% on weak exports, crude-linked inputs and Chinese pricing. So glass-lined reactor demand is split — CDMO/pharma up, commodity/specialty chemicals down (HLE saw India glass-lined prices fall). [ICRA pharma PR, 18 Sep 2025; CRISIL via BusinessToday, 2 Jul 2026; HLE/GMM deep files]
- **Ethanol and biofuels (Praj, Isgec)**: E20 was reached ahead of schedule; Infomerics (May 2026) estimates E20 needs ~1,016 crore litres a year (~1,350 crore litres total demand incl. other uses) against installed capacity near 2,000 crore litres with ~400 crore litres more coming — a potential surplus of ~100% over the blending requirement, implying "suboptimal capacity utilisation" for new distilleries unless E25–E30 arrive. BIS notified E22/E25/E27/E30 fuel standards (IS 19850:2026) on 15 May 2026, but no mandate date. Result: domestic 1G ethanol plant orders have dried up; CBG, SAF, Bio-IBA and exports (Praj's Brazil 800 KLPD order) are the substitutes. [Infomerics, May 2026; Praj deep file]
- **Mining consumables (Tega/Molycop)**: consumables are bought from miners' operating budgets, so volume tracks ore throughput, not capex; global mill-liner market ~US$2.97 bn in 2026 growing to ~US$4.59 bn by 2034 (Fortune Business Insights, cited in Tega AR); Molycop ~85% steel-indexed contracts. [Tega deep file]
- **CCTV/security (Aditya Infotech)**: STQC cybersecurity certification for internet-connected cameras became compulsory after the grace period ended on 1 April 2026; Hikvision/Dahua could not certify. Counterpoint Research: Chinese brands had about one-third of sales, Indian companies held >80% of the market by February 2026. CP PLUS's share went from 20.2% (FY25) to 43.3% (FY26) (Frost & Sullivan). The rule covers IP cameras only (analogue/DVR/NVR unregulated). [Quest Review Center citing Counterpoint, 6 Sep 2026; Aditya deep file]
- **Data centres (cross-cutting)**: new demand for enclosures (Kilburn/Monga), cooling pressure vessels (Jash), heat exchangers/air-coolers (Anup, United Heat Transfer–Vertiv trial), and modular skids (Praj GenX, Standard Glass GScale). ICRA notes data-centre projects drove the services spike in announcements. [ICRA Apr-26; company deep files]

## Policy (subsidies, duties, rules)
- STQC/Compulsory Registration Order for CCTV (in force April 2026) — the single biggest share-shift policy in the group. [Quest Review Center, Sep 2026]
- Ethanol: BIS standards for E22–E30 notified (May 2026); OMC procurement and price support built the capacity; next mandate timing unknown. [Infomerics, May 2026]
- Public capex ₹12.2 trillion budgeted FY27 (+11.5%), exposed to subsidy slippage. [ICRA Apr-26]
- US tariffs (25–50% in 2025, 15.6% later for some goods) and a threatened 100% tariff hurt exporters such as Jash; pharma exempted so far. [Jash deep file; ICRA pharma PR]
- Nuclear: NPCIL fleet (Kaiga-5/6) opening supply chains to Anup, Kilburn and United Heat Transfer. [Anup Q4 call; Kilburn Q4 call]

## Cost drivers
- **Steel and alloys**: most contracts are fixed-price with no escalation (Anup, JNK, Kilburn); the West Asia war raised steel and freight costs in March–May 2026. Anup held back material purchase on ₹200–250 cr of orders and guided FY27 EBITDA to ~15% (21% in FY26); CARE expects 16–18%. Kilburn books ~80% of material within 48–72 hours to limit exposure. [Anup Q4/Q1 calls; CARE Aug-26; Kilburn Q1 call]
- **Energy and industrial gas**: gas shortages slowed fabrication in Q4 FY26 (Kilburn/Monga, Anup). [Kilburn Q4 call; Anup Q4 call]
- **Logistics**: Strait of Hormuz/Red Sea disruption — over-dimensional cargo for ADNOC cannot move by road; most contracts FOB India port, so freight is the buyer's cost, but storage and handling costs fall on the maker. [Anup Q1/Q4 calls]
- **Working capital**: milestone billing leaves 50–60% of contract value until delivery (CARE on Anup); gross current-asset days 241 (Anup), ~470 (JNK, CRISIL), 361 (Kilburn FY25, Acuité). Most companies in the group had weak or negative operating cash flow in FY25–FY26. [CARE Aug-26; CRISIL Sep-26; Acuité Nov-25; Screener]

## Competitive structure and share
- Niche, certification-led franchises with 2–5 serious rivals: fired heaters (JNK vs European players; Heurtey exits EPC bids), heavy heat exchangers (Anup, with lower-tier fabricators now undercutting in legacy products), nuclear drying (Kilburn vs Lloyds Engineering), water gates (Jash, ~two-thirds of India), glass-lined equipment (GMM ~half of the world big-vessel market; HLE the other large Indian player), naval HVAC (Shree ~64% of tenders), CCTV (CP PLUS ~43–45%). [company deep files]
- Share evidence is mostly company-claimed; independent data exist only for CCTV (Frost & Sullivan, Counterpoint) and mill liners (Fortune Business Insights).
- Parent/partner dependence is common: JNK (JNK Global, ~82% of book), John Cockerill (Belgian parent), GMM (Pfaudler brand), Anup (Lummus, Brembana licences). [company deep files]

## Where profit sits
- Highest margins: certified niche product makers selling bespoke equipment — Anup 21–23% (FY24–FY26), Kilburn 23%, Tega legacy liners ~22%, Jash India ~13% PAT margin, Shree 21%, DEE ~19% guided. [Screener; deep files]
- Middle: licensed/partnered EPC equipment — JNK 11–14%, GMM ~11% EBITDA, HLE ~7–10%, Isgec manufacturing 12–13% but projects 5–6%. [deep files]
- Lowest: plant EPC and commoditised products — Praj ~4% (FY26), ICE Make <2% (Q1 FY27), John Cockerill negative. [deep files]
- Pattern: profit sits with those who own design/approvals and control material buying; it leaks where firms take fixed-price EPC risk, run new plants below capacity, or depend on a single policy (ethanol).

## Cycle position (Sept 2026)
- **Orders at or near records**: DEE (₹2,428 cr), Anup (₹985 cr), WPIL (₹5,270 cr), John Cockerill (~₹4,600 cr), HPL (₹3,200 cr+), Standard Glass (~₹1,400 cr). **Stalling/falling**: JNK (₹1,961 → ₹1,801 cr), Kilburn (₹485 cr, intake well behind plan), ICE Make (₹237 → ₹222 cr), Everest Kanto (~$75 mn, weaker). [deep files]
- **Q1 FY27 was weak almost everywhere**: Anup profit ~nil, Kilburn revenue −9%, HLE margin 7%, Jash profit = tariff refund, Praj/ICE Make thin margins — explained by West Asia input shocks, delayed customer approvals and back-ended billing. Most managements now guide to H2-heavy years. [deep files]
- Conclusion: mid-cycle for power/refining equipment (strong books, delayed awards), trough for ethanol plants, soft for specialty-chemical capex, strong-but-maturing for pharma/CDMO equipment and data-centre adjacencies.

## Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecaster named)
- **FY27**: CRISIL — capital goods revenue +12–14%, margins 12–13%; RBI — private capex ₹3.2 lakh cr (+23%); ICRA — GDP 6.5%, private capex "subdued", government capex +11.5%; CRISIL — specialty chemicals revenue +6%, capex ~₹16,500 cr, margin −150–200 bp; CARE — Anup revenue +7–10%, margin 16–18%. [sources above]
- **FY28–FY29**: no agency gives sector numbers this far out. CRISIL's 3.7x book-to-bill and record ₹5.2 lakh cr capital-goods order book imply multi-year execution into FY28–FY29; NSO's FY27 intentions (₹9.55 lakh cr) and RBI's pipeline point to continued, power-led private capex; CARE expects Anup's margins to recover from FY28; Infomerics warns ethanol capacity will stay underused until higher blends are mandated. My read (not a forecast by an agency): order books support ~12–15% revenue growth a year for the process-equipment group in FY28–FY29, with margins recovering 2–4 points from FY27's depressed levels if steel and freight normalise; ethanol-plant demand depends on an E25/E27 mandate date.
- **Key swing factors**: duration of the West Asia conflict (energy, freight, Gulf project awards), steel prices under fixed-price contracts, government capex slippage, US tariff policy, and the E25/E30 decision.
