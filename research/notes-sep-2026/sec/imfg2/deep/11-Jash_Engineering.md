# Jash Engineering — deep research (28 Sep 2026)

## Business and segments
- Indore-based maker of water-control equipment for sewage, water treatment, flood defence, desalination and industry: water control gates (sluice/penstock gates), screens, knife-gate and other valves, Archimedes screw pumps/turbines, sludge conveyors, and — via the WesTech India purchase — process equipment for mining, alumina and pulp. Customers are mostly EPC contractors (e.g., VA Tech Wabag) and municipal utilities. [AR FY26 MD&A; Ind-Ra Feb-26; Q4 call]
- Product mix FY25: water control gates 52%, screens 24%, valves 13%, others ~11%. FY26: gates still the largest, screens and valves second and third, others ~8%. Management says Jash holds about two-thirds of India's ~₹150 cr water-control-gate market. [Ind-Ra Feb-26; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Group: Jash Engineering (India, incl. merged Shivpad, Chennai); Rodney Hunt Inc. (USA, plant at Orange, Massachusetts; FY26 income US$30.1 m, PAT US$1.28 m, down 56%); Mahr Maschinenbau (Austria, design only, manufacturing closed); Waterfront Fluid Controls (UK, 80%; FY26 loss £0.31 m) plus Penstocks UK (bought April 2026); Jash Process Equipment (ex-WesTech India, bought ~Jan 2026; FY26 income ₹47 cr); Jash Invent (50% JV). [AR FY26 Directors' report; Q4 call]
- Geography: FY26 ~44.5% India, ~55% outside India; exports were 63% in FY25. Standalone FY26 domestic revenue +18% to ₹318 cr, exports −28% to ₹176 cr (US tariffs, Middle East war). Q1 FY27: ~₹70 cr India, ~₹80 cr outside India. [Q4 call; AR FY26; Q1 call; Ind-Ra Feb-26]
- Capacity: Unit 1 expansion commissioned in Q1 FY27 — cast gate and valve capacity up 30% — "completing our decade-long manufacturing expansion programme across India". Management says India capacity can support ₹1,500 cr revenue; a third shift can add ₹100–200 cr. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated)
- Revenue: FY24 ₹516 cr → FY25 ₹735 cr (+43%) → FY26 ₹736 cr (flat). Operating profit ₹99 → ₹127 → ₹102 cr; OPM 19% → 17% → 14%. Net profit ₹67 → ₹87 → ₹76 cr. [Screener]
- Quarters (revenue / operating profit / net profit, ₹ cr): Q1 FY26 128/−4/−5; Q2 158/20/11; Q3 161/17/13; Q4 291/69/57; Q1 FY27 150/8/5. Very back-ended: Q4 alone was ~40% of FY26 revenue and ~75% of its profit. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27 company figures: revenue from operations ₹149.9 cr (+17%), total income ₹156 cr, gross margin 60.2% (50.8% a year ago), EBITDA ₹14.2 cr *including* ₹6.1 cr other income (company definition), PBT ₹6.6 cr, PAT ₹5.1 cr. Included a ₹5.6 cr US tariff refund (booked in material cost); ~₹7.5 cr more refund claimed. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Q1 FY27 by entity: Jash India revenue ₹103 cr (+21%), PAT ₹13 cr (~13% margin); Rodney Hunt ₹46 cr (−11%), loss ~US$0.8 m; Waterfront ₹13 cr (+8%), small profit; Jash Process ₹8 cr (−50%), loss ~₹1 cr. [IP Q1FY27]
- Unit economics: gross margin ~57% in FY26 (company), EBITDA margin 16.2% (incl. other income). ROCE 25% (FY24–25) → 18% (FY26); ROE 16%. [IP Q1FY27; Screener]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (May 2026): FY27 consolidated revenue ₹875 cr (India ₹320 cr, outside India ₹555 cr; Jash ₹530 cr, Rodney Hunt ₹330 cr, Waterfront ₹60 cr, Jash Process ₹40 cr, Mahr ₹15 cr), PAT margin 12–13% ("quite conservative"); ₹1,500 cr revenue in five years (by FY31). ₹36 cr of March invoicing spilled to Q1. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 FY27 call (Aug 2026): ₹875 cr **kept** ("easily achievable"; order book + Q1 revenue >₹1,080 cr); PAT guidance nudged up to "Rs.100–105 crore" (~12%) with 13–14% margin "possible"; FY28 revenue "around ₹1,025 cr" (FAQ). Rodney Hunt: US$35–36 m revenue and 8–9% PAT margin in FY27; 10% maybe in FY28. [Q1 call official]
- Delivery vs Q4 promises: ₹36 cr spillover recognised (net of ~₹26–28 cr June reversal, so only ~₹10 cr net help). Q1 revenue +17% but "not what I expected" — ~₹15 cr of goods for Qatar and Singapore stuck (Gulf shipping; a Singapore client's unpaid dues). Rodney Hunt revenue fell 11% and lost money ("first quarter blues") against a full-year target of +25%. Jash Process worse than hoped: "we lost close to Rs.150–200 crore worth of orders in last three four months". [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Track record: FY26 guidance was missed badly (revenue flat at ₹736 cr vs a growth plan), blamed on US tariffs (25% then 50%) and the Middle East war. [Q4 call; Screener]

## Capex and capacity
- India: expansion programme complete (Unit 1 foundry and gate/valve capacity +30%, commissioned Q1 FY27). Ind-Ra expected FY26 capex of ₹40–45 cr plus ~₹30 cr for WesTech India and ~₹5 cr for Penstocks. [IP Q1FY27; Ind-Ra Feb-26]
- Overseas plants: Houston, Texas (land acquired; ~US$12 m) and Saudi Arabia (approvals received, land applied for; ~US$4 m), both targeted for December 2027 and operation by March 2028. Funding: ~US$3 m already deposited in the US, internal accruals and some debt; "Money is not the criterion for me. The team is." [Q1 call]
- Data-centre cooling pressure vessels: first order of 4 vessels (₹30–60 lakh each), negotiating 32, quoted >600; current setup can make 75–80 a year (~₹25–30 cr potential); a new plant only if demand proves sustained, "not before '28". [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹103 cr, cash ~₹94 cr at March 2026 → roughly neutral net debt. Ind-Ra: net leverage 0.3x (FY25), interest cover ~10x (FY25), fund-based limits ~55% used; repayments ₹5.5 cr FY26, ₹10.6 cr FY27. [Screener; Ind-Ra Feb-26]
- Cash flow from operations: ₹58 cr (FY24), ₹55 cr (FY25), ₹56 cr (FY26) — only 55–75% of operating profit; free cash flow ₹34 cr, ₹10 cr, ₹19 cr. Ind-Ra expects FCF near zero in FY27. [Screener; Ind-Ra Feb-26]
- Working capital is heavy: debtor days 149, inventory days 219, payable days 117 → cash conversion cycle 251 days (FY26); working capital days rose from 71 to 103. Standalone inventory ₹195 cr, with a ₹7.8 cr write-down to net realisable value. [Screener; AR FY26 KAM, note 16]
- Dividend payout ~13%. [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Global top-five position in water control gates with a very broad product basket (gates, screens, valves, grit separators, screws) that lets EPC contractors buy a package; ~two-thirds share of India's gate market. [AR FY26; Q1 call]
- Pricing discipline: new US quotes build in 25% tariff (vs 15.6% actual) and management deliberately slowed US order-taking while a possible 100% tariff (Russian-oil bill) is unresolved; ~70% of Rodney Hunt's ~US$40 m book is "make in America". [Q1 call]
- Typical order tickets ₹30–40 cr; recurring EPC relationships; Ind-Ra estimates order book of ₹868 cr at H1 FY26 rising towards ₹1,000 cr. Order book ₹932 cr at 1 Aug 2026 (India ₹293 cr, outside ₹639 cr); pipeline ₹72 cr negotiated + ₹60 cr under negotiation. [Ind-Ra Feb-26; IP Q1FY27]
- Margins hold up in India: standalone PAT margin ~13% even in a bad export year. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **US trade policy**: tariff swings (25% → 50% → 15.6%) wrecked FY26; a threatened 100% tariff on countries buying Russian oil would hit the US strategy until the Houston plant runs (FY28). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Acquisition integration**: Jash Process (ex-WesTech) lost ₹150–200 cr of orders, revenue halved in Q1, relies on vendors; Waterfront still loss-making in FY26; Rodney Hunt profit fell 56% in FY26. [Q1 call; AR FY26]
- **Seasonality and execution**: Q1–Q3 profit is thin; a delay in Q4 dispatches (shipping, payments) can move the whole year. Singapore client payment problem and Qatar shipment stuck. [Screener; Q1 call]
- **Working capital**: 250-day cash cycle; auditor's key audit matter is inventory valuation, with overheads allocated manually in Excel. [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report]
- Audit scope: two subsidiaries (total revenue ₹43 cr) audited by other auditors and two (revenue ₹16.5 cr) unaudited, management-certified — immaterial. Opinion unmodified. [AR FY26 auditor's report, Other Matters]
- Contingent liabilities small: tax demands ~₹3.8 cr; bank guarantees for own performance ~₹93 cr; ₹0.5 cr corporate guarantee for Jash Invent JV. [AR FY26 standalone note 46]
- Promoter holding has fallen from 51.0% (Sep 2023) to 43.2% (June 2026). [Screener shareholding]
- Disclosure change: from Q1 FY27 the presentation dropped the product-wise and region-wise revenue split (to be given only yearly). [Q1 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone: from "quite disappointing" FY26 (Q4) to "quite bullish now because I see most of the things falling in place" (Q1). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Numbers: PAT guidance moved from a 12–13% margin to ₹100–105 cr with 13–14% "possible"; FY28 revenue ~₹1,025 cr added. Order book up from ₹899 cr (1 May) to ₹932 cr (1 Aug). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- New issues: Jash Process order losses; Singapore payment issue; possible 100% US tariff; data-centre pressure vessels as a new opportunity. [Q1 call]

## My read
Jash is a good niche franchise — a global top-five gate maker with a strong, profitable India business and a record order book — but FY26 showed how exposed it is to US tariffs and shipping, and the acquired businesses (Rodney Hunt, Waterfront, Jash Process) are dragging rather than adding. Q1 FY27 profit was essentially the tariff refund, so the year rests again on a big Q4. The price needs FY27 revenue near ₹875 cr and PAT near ₹100 cr; that requires Rodney Hunt to recover and exports to ship. Positive signs would be Rodney Hunt profitable by Q2–Q3 and the Houston/Saudi plants on schedule; negative would be a new US tariff shock, further Jash Process losses, or another back-ended year that misses guidance.
