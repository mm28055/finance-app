# Kilburn Engineering — deep research (28 Sep 2026)

## Business and segments
- Thane-based maker of custom process equipment: industrial dryers (rotary, fluid-bed, flash, paddle, spray, calciners), heat exchangers, pressure vessels, gas/air drying packages, vapour recovery skids for offshore gas, and heavy-water drying/vapour recovery systems for nuclear plants. Customers: fertiliser, petrochemicals, carbon black, soda ash, chemicals, pharma, tea, oil & gas, nuclear (NPCIL / Heavy Water Board via EPC firms). Company says 8,000+ installations and 20+ sectors. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Group of three: Kilburn standalone (FY26 revenue ₹448 cr, EBITDA 25.9%); M.E. Energy, Pune (100%, bought Feb 2024 — waste-heat-recovery boilers, hot-air gas generators for cement, thermal oil heaters); Monga Strayfield, Pune + Theale UK (100%, bought 28 Jan 2025 — radio-frequency dryers and precision sheet-metal enclosures, roughly 50:50 by revenue, short 3-month cycle). Management refuses to give subsidiary revenue or order books ("treat these 100% subsidiaries like individual factories"). [Q4 call; AR FY26; Acuité Nov-25]
- Only one reportable segment (engineering products). Geography FY26: India ₹353.5 cr, outside India ₹270.4 cr (43%) — up from ₹101.3 cr (24%) in FY25, driven by a large Moroccan order. Management sees exports at 30–40% of revenue long term. [AR FY26 note 39; Q4 call]
- New thrust: engineered enclosures for global UPS/data-centre OEMs (₹14.8 cr "AI data centre" orders in Q1 FY27, 11% of intake) and fertiliser dryers for global EPC firms (first order from Casale SA, Switzerland, ₹70.2 cr, June 2026). [IP Q1FY27; News: Business Standard, 16 Jun 2026]
- Capacity: 36,000+ sq m across Saravali and Ambernath (Thane), M.E. Energy Pune and three Monga units in Pune. Saravali expansion and M.E. Energy Phase II due "end October" 2026 (presentation says Q3 FY27); Monga sheet-metal expansion "under planning". Management says these take capacity to >₹1,000 cr revenue. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; AR FY26]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated)
- Revenue: FY24 ₹329 cr → FY25 ₹424 cr (+29%) → FY26 ₹629 cr (+48%, first full year of both acquisitions). Operating profit ₹77 → ₹102 → ₹147 cr; OPM 23% → 24% → 23%. Net profit ₹51 → ₹62 → ₹96 cr. EPS ₹12.1 → ₹13.1 → ₹18.2 (dilution from warrants). [Screener]
- Quarters (revenue / operating profit / net profit, ₹ cr): Q1 FY26 129/33/21; Q2 154/40/27; Q3 157/36/23; Q4 189/38/25; Q1 FY27 117/21/13. OPM slid from 26% (Q1–Q2 FY26) to 23%, 20% and 18% (Q1 FY27). Q1 FY27 revenue −9% y/y, profit −38%. [Screener]
- Company definitions differ: it counts ~80% of other income (mostly export exchange gains) as operating, so its EBITDA is higher — FY26 ₹161.6 cr / 25.1% vs Screener ₹147 cr / 23%; Q1 FY27 ₹24.2 cr / 20.1% vs Screener ₹21 cr / 18%. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27; Screener]
- TTM revenue ₹617 cr, net profit ₹88 cr; ROCE 23%, ROE 17%. [Screener]
- Unit economics: typical tickets ₹2–5 cr for smaller machines, tea dryers ₹0.4–0.5 cr, some ₹40 cr and ₹100 cr+ projects; M.E. Energy quoting jobs over ₹300 cr; M.E. Energy steel-sector enquiries in the ₹10–15 cr range. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (27 May 2026): FY27 revenue ₹750–800 cr (+20–25%), EBITDA "20% plus" with 22–23% targeted (including other income); order intake ₹800–1,000 cr, with "at least half … ₹450–500 cr" closed by end-Q2 (elsewhere "₹500–600 cr of orders by September"); capex ~₹40 cr; Saravali and M.E. Energy Phase II done by end-Q2; ₹1,000 cr revenue "by FY28". Presentation slide showing 25–30% CAGR was called an "IR typo". [Q4 call official]
- Q1 FY27 call (17 Aug 2026): revenue guidance **cut** to "around ₹700 cr" (+11%) with EBITDA "of 20%"; order-intake target **cut** to ₹800 cr (bottom of the range); capacity completion slipped to "end October"; FY28 ₹1,000 cr **no longer dated** — "Why we have not given any guidance for FY28 is that we are … creating capacities … to cater to a INR1,000 crores plus revenue." [Q1 call official; IP Q1FY27]
- Delivery vs Q4 promises: Q1 orders ₹134.5 cr and ₹190 cr year-to-date by 14 Aug vs ₹450–500 cr promised by end-September; Q1 revenue ₹117 cr (needs ~₹194 cr a quarter for the rest of the year to hit ₹700 cr); Moroccan receivable collection only partly done ("has not substantially changed, but it is better"); Granules India order still on hold. Delays blamed on customers slowing drawing approvals (3–4 projects), slow nuclear approvals, a carbon-black project stuck on land/environment clearances, and Middle East decisions (two large Gulf enquiries were at final negotiation before the war). [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Track record: FY26 was delivered (+48% revenue), but order intake has missed guidance two quarters running and orders expected "by March" slid first to Q1–Q2 and now to H2. [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26]

## Capex and capacity
- FY27 capex ~₹40 cr across Kilburn and subsidiaries (same guidance "for the last 2, 3 calls"); capital commitments ₹10.2 cr at March 2026 (₹3.3 cr a year earlier); CWIP only ₹5 cr on Screener. A ₹2–3 cr write-off was booked in FY26 for demolishing the old office where the new bay goes. [Q4 call; AR FY26 note 37; Screener]
- Funding: warrants converted at ₹166 (FY26) and ₹425 (balance ₹97 cr received in Q1 FY27, 30.4 lakh warrants); management says no more equity raising needed for ₹1,000 cr revenue. Promoter company and investors subscribed. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; AR FY26 Directors' report]
- Payback: the plan is to lift revenue capacity from ~₹650–700 cr to >₹1,000 cr for ~₹40 cr of capex — very high asset turns if orders come; the constraint is order intake, not plant. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Acquisitions: M.E. Energy (Feb 2024) and Monga Strayfield (Jan 2025) built ₹121 cr of goodwill; the chairman says further "adjacent" acquisitions remain possible. [IP Q1FY27 balance sheet; Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Cash from operations was negative two years running: −₹10 cr (FY25) and −₹18 cr (FY26) against operating profit of ₹102 cr and ₹147 cr; free cash flow −₹45 cr and −₹31 cr. Acquisitions (₹100 cr + ₹103 cr investing outflows in FY24–FY25) and working capital were funded by equity and debt. [Screener]
- Working-capital days rose 100 → 169 → 184; debtor days 87 → 91 → 117; cash conversion cycle 58 → 107 days (FY26). Acuité put gross current-asset days at 361 (FY25) with ₹199 cr of unbilled revenue. Q4 FY26 dispatches of ~₹130 cr (vs ₹50–60 cr a year earlier) inflated year-end receivables, incl. ₹50–60 cr from the Moroccan order. [Screener; Acuité Nov-25; Q4 call]
- Borrowings ₹90 cr at March 2026 (₹85–88 cr in FY24–25); interest ₹15 cr a year. Management calls the group "net debt-free" after the Q1 FY27 warrant money — not debt-free. Acuité gearing 0.25x (FY25). Dividend payout 0% in FY26 on Screener (a dividend for FY26 was declared later; TDS notice Sep 2026). [Screener; Q1 call; Acuité Nov-25]
- Rating: Acuité upgraded to A-/Stable and A2+ (19–25 Nov 2025) on ₹210 cr of bank lines, citing acquisition synergies; weaknesses flagged were working-capital intensity, steel-price exposure and end-market cyclicality. [Acuité Nov-25]

## Moat evidence
- Qualified vendor in nuclear (heavy-water drying/vapour recovery) — "we were already qualified and we didn't have to go through this qualification again"; main rival named is Lloyds Engineering. Nuclear order book ₹57 cr (12% of book) but these orders "move quite slow" (another year+). [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Fertiliser dryers: first global EPC order (Casale, ₹70 cr); fertiliser is ₹86 cr (18%) of the book and 52% of Q1 intake. [IP Q1FY27; News: Business Standard, Jun 2026]
- Margins high for a fabricator — 23% operating margin FY24–FY26 (Screener) — helped by bespoke design and by booking ~80% of raw material within 48–72 hours of order (reduces steel risk; contracts are fixed-price, "we don't have a pass-through"). [Screener; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Order book ₹485 cr at 30 Jun 2026 (petrochem 16%, fertiliser 18%, iron & steel 14%, nuclear 12%, oil & gas 10%) — only ~0.8x FY26 revenue, thin for a project business guiding ₹700 cr. [IP Q1FY27]
- Weakness in the moat: no pricing power evidence; margins slid 8 points from Q2 FY26 to Q1 FY27 as volumes fell; customers can stretch execution by delaying drawing approvals. [Screener; Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Order intake**: ₹800 cr target needs ~₹610 cr in the remaining 7.5 months after ₹190 cr by mid-August; a ₹4,000 cr "enquiry pipeline" has been quoted for several quarters (an investor on the Q1 call asked management to stop citing it). [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- **Cash conversion**: two years of negative operating cash flow; 184 working-capital days; auditor's only key audit matter is over-time revenue recognition (judgement on cost estimates) — relevant given unbilled revenue. Opinion unmodified. [Screener; AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report]
- **CARO remarks at subsidiaries**: M.E. Energy — clause vii(b) (disputed statutory dues); Monga Strayfield — ii(b) (quarterly stock statements to banks not matching books), vii(a) (delay in depositing statutory dues) and vii(b). [AR FY26 consolidated CARO xxi]
- **Related parties / promoter group**: promoter company Firstview Trading (FTPL; FY25 turnover only ₹7.8 cr) gives a corporate guarantee to lenders for a 2% a year fee — ₹1 cr paid in FY26 on ₹50 cr; AGM approval sought to raise it to ₹115 cr (fee up to ₹2.3 cr a year). A director's personal guarantee of ₹120 cr also backs group loans. Promoter group includes Williamson Magor and McLeod Russel (Khaitan group); Williamson Magor charged ₹15 lakh "corporate charges"; legal fees to Khaitan & Co. Subsidiary directors' firms (Sri Aurobindo Motors, Maxenergy, Lamiso Tech) trade with the group (₹4.8 cr purchases, ₹5.8 cr sales in FY26). [AR FY26 AGM notice item 6; note 38]
- Contingent liabilities small (~₹1 cr of tax/employee claims) plus a ₹25 cr corporate guarantee for a subsidiary's bank lines. [AR FY26 consolidated note 37]
- **Dilution and board changes**: promoter holding 53.9% (Sep 2023) → 44.5% (Jun 2026) after warrant issues; non-executive director Aditya Khaitan resigned 19 Mar 2026, independent director Manmohan Singh 20 Apr 2026, company secretary changed Sep–Nov 2025. [Screener; AR FY26 note 38]
- Disclosure: no subsidiary revenue/order split; Q4 presentation uploaded ~10 minutes before the call and carried a "typo" CAGR slide. [Q4 call]

- Tone: from "very positive" and "we have not missed orders" (Q4) to "Q1 FY27 was a quarter where revenue performance was below the level we would have ideally targeted" and a heavily H2-weighted year (Q1). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Numbers: FY27 revenue ₹750–800 cr → ~₹700 cr; margin 22–23% target → 20%; order intake ₹800–1,000 cr → ₹800 cr; FY28 ₹1,000 cr dropped as a dated target; capacity end-Q2 → end-October. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## My read
Kilburn has turned from a ₹100 cr dryer maker into a ₹600 cr+ process-equipment group with unusually high margins for fabrication, but FY27 has started badly: orders are well behind plan, guidance has been cut, margins are sliding with volume, and the business has not generated operating cash for two years. The ₹97 cr warrant money covers capex and makes the balance sheet safe, so this is a timing and execution problem rather than a solvency one. The price needs a strong H2 (≈₹580 cr in nine months) and order intake near ₹800 cr to keep the ₹1,000 cr ambition credible for FY29; I would treat FY28 as ₹800–850 cr at best until orders show up. Watch Q2 order intake (needs ₹200 cr+), receivable collection (debtor days back under 100), and whether the promoter-guarantee fee and subsidiary CARO issues are cleaned up.
