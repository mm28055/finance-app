## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Borosil Renewables

## Executive Summary
Borosil Renewables exists to secure India’s renewable energy supply chain. As the country's sole non-captive manufacturer of ultra-clear patterned solar glass, the company provides critical import substitution for domestic solar module manufacturers navigating a highly volatile geopolitical landscape. Borosil is currently a definitive **Category Leader** operating under a highly favorable regulatory umbrella. By replacing cheap Chinese and Vietnamese imports, Borosil solves a fundamental supply-chain security problem for Indian module makers who are scaling rapidly to meet aggressive government installation targets. Driven by recently imposed anti-dumping duties and a newly extended countervailing duty on Malaysia, the company has transformed from a margin-pressured commodity supplier into a highly profitable domestic monopoly—printing exceptional 35% EBITDA margins—uniquely positioned to capitalize on India’s transition to self-reliance in solar energy. 

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** Borosil produces high-transmission textured and patterned solar glass used in the manufacturing of solar photovoltaic (PV) modules. A newly launched asset-light division selling outsourced, Borosil-branded rooftop solar solutions has proven underwhelming and is now being treated as a cautious, low-margin experiment.
- **Who pays them?** Domestic solar module manufacturers constitute the vast majority of the client base, alongside a shrinking cohort of specialized export clients in the EU and US. Currently, Borosil's top 10 customers account for 65-68% of total volumes.
- **How do they make money?** Sales are predominantly spot and short-term contracts. Pricing is highly dynamic, pegged to the landed cost of Chinese imports plus the premium afforded by government-mandated anti-dumping duties (ADD), countervailing duties (CVD), and temporary fuel surcharges.

### Segment & Geographic Mix
Borosil operates essentially as a single-segment manufacturer (Solar Glass), with geography telling the real growth story. Rising domestic demand and geopolitical headwinds in the West have rapidly concentrated the business almost entirely within India.

| Segment / Geography | Q1 FY27 Revenue Contribution | YoY Trend / Commentary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Domestic India** | ~99.7% | **Surging.** Benefiting from explosive domestic module capacity additions and strict government import substitution policies (ALMM 1, 2, and impending 3). |

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
Borosil’s moat is highly lucrative but largely **Fleeting**, built almost entirely on **Regulatory Capture**. 
- **The Moat:** The government's imposition of anti-dumping duties (ADD) on Chinese and Vietnamese glass, alongside a recent 5-year extension of the countervailing duty (CVD) on Malaysia (effective June 2026), serves as a deep artificial price floor. This regulatory shield allowed Borosil to push its ex-factory realizations to a record INR 160.30/sqm in Q1 FY27, sustaining EBITDA margins of 35%. 
- **The Bear Case:** Without these tariffs, Borosil lacks a durable cost advantage over heavily subsidized Southeast Asian manufacturers. Furthermore, the moat is vulnerable to domestic overcapacity. Over 33 GW of captive domestic solar glass capacity is slated to come online by March 2027, which will cap Borosil's pricing power and shrink its addressable market as massive module makers vertically integrate.

## Growth Vectors
- **Capacity Expansion (The Next $100M):** Borosil is executing a 60% capacity expansion (600 TPD via SG4 and SG5) at its existing domestic facility, perfectly on schedule for construction completion in December 2026 and commissioning by Jan-Mar 2027. This fully funded expansion is expected to drop an additional INR 80-85 Cr in EBITDA per quarter once scaled. Management envisions this structural growth pushing total company revenues from the current ~INR 2,500 Cr run-rate to INR 4,000 Cr over the next 3 to 4 years.
- **Policy-Driven Demand:** The phased rollout of the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM)—with Phase 2 mandating domestic cells as of June 2026, and Phase 3 slated for wafers/ingots by 2028—virtually guarantees a sustained, captive domestic market for Borosil's output. 
- **Management Execution:** Management has been ruthlessly pragmatic. To defend against West Asia-driven fuel spikes, they successfully forced an INR 9.50/sqm fuel surcharge onto customers, protecting their 35% margins, while proactively rolling it back as global fuel costs now normalize. On the cost front, a newly commissioned solar-wind hybrid captive power plant has pushed renewable power usage to 93%, generating INR 18 Cr in annual savings. Finally, the structural drain of their German subsidiary (GMB) has been formally concluded, with a one-time tax shield write-back fully normalizing the balance sheet.

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Forensic Red Flags & Governance:** The previous quarter raised red flags regarding a sudden, unexplained enabling resolution to raise up to INR 750 crores in equity, contradicting earlier guidance. However, management has since clarified that the current 600 TPD expansion is fully funded and explicitly denied any current need for equity dilution, opting to rely on internal accruals for near-term capex. While this mitigates immediate dilution fears, the Board's erratic signaling warrants continued monitoring.
- **Business Risks (Single Point of Failure):** The entire margin profile rests on the Ministry of Finance continuously renewing ADD and CVD tariffs. Any softening of trade barriers would immediately crash realizations.
- **Operational Risk (The 90-Day Shutdown):** A significant new operational headwind has emerged. A major 90-day maintenance shutdown is required to rebuild older furnaces (SG1 and SG2) in early FY28. This extended downtime on their 1,000 TPD base capacity will temporarily, but severely, mute the volume gains expected from the new SG4/SG5 expansions.

## Investment Thesis
- **The Bull Case:** Borosil Renewables is the cleanest proxy for India's renewable energy sovereignty push. The company is operating at absolute peak efficiency, sustaining exceptional 35% EBITDA margins, and continuously running at 100% capacity utilization. With the 5-year extension of the Malaysian CVD further entrenching its regulatory monopoly, and an extra 60% capacity arriving flawlessly on schedule by Q4 FY27, Borosil offers a guaranteed runway of volume growth. The impending ALMM-3 rollout in 2028 will further catalyze captive domestic manufacturing, permanently locking in Borosil's customer base.
- **The Bear Case:** Peak margins of 35% are highly likely to compress. Q1 realizations were heavily artificially padded by an INR 9.50/sqm fuel surcharge that management is already rolling back. Additionally, much of the hyped growth narrative is cracking: the B2C rooftop venture has proven to be an underwhelming distraction with slashed targets, and the anticipated volume surge in FY28 will be heavily handicapped by a looming 90-day shutdown of older furnaces. As major module manufacturers bring massive captive glass capacity online by 2027, Borosil may face a sudden pricing war just as its new capacity scales. Stay away unless pricing implies a significant margin of safety.
