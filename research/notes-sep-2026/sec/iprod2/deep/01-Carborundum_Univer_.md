## Business and segments
- Murugappa-group materials company with three segments: **Abrasives** (bonded, coated, super-abrasives, power tools; ~44% of sales), **Electrominerals/EMD** (fused alumina, silicon carbide, zirconia grains; ~32%) and **Ceramics** (industrial ceramics + refractories; ~24%) [CRISIL Mar-26].
- FY26 consolidated segment sales: Abrasives ₹2,271 cr (+5.1%), Ceramics ₹1,268 cr (+9.3%), EMD ₹1,632 cr (+3.7%); total ₹5,149 cr (+6.5%) [Q4 call].
- **Where the profit sits (FY26 segment EBITDA):** Ceramics ₹313.8 cr on ₹1,268 cr sales (~25%), Abrasives ₹198.9 cr (~9%), EMD ₹156.7 cr (~10%). FY25 was ₹333.0 / ₹243.2 / ₹230.1 cr. Segment PBT FY26: Ceramics ₹256 cr, Abrasives ₹97 cr, EMD ₹91 cr [AR FY26, consolidated Note 32]. Ceramics is ~24% of sales but more than half of segment profit.
- Standalone (India) FY26: sales ₹3,024 cr (+8.6%) — Abrasives ₹1,270 cr, Ceramics ₹1,000 cr, EMD ₹906 cr; standalone PBIT ₹525 cr [Q4 call]. India is ~58% of consolidated revenue; international 50–55% incl. exports [CRISIL Mar-26].
- Industrial Ceramics (57% of Ceramics, ₹569 cr FY26) splits roughly into thirds: wear ceramics, engineered ceramics (SOFC plates, spark plugs, image-intensifier tubes) and metallized cylinders for vacuum interrupters; refractories are 43% (₹437 cr) [Q4 call]. Over 80% of Industrial Ceramics is exported; CUMI calls itself world No. 2 in metallized cylinders [Q4 call].
- Key subsidiaries: VAW Russia (silicon carbide; under US OFAC sanctions since Jan-25), Rhodius and Awuko (Germany, abrasives), Foskor Zirconia (South Africa, 51%), PLUSS (India), CUMI Australia/America; JVs Murugappa Morgan Thermal Ceramics and Wendt (associate) [AR FY26; Q4 call].

## Guidance history
- **Q3 → Q4 track record (FY26):** consolidated sales guided +5.5–6.5%, delivered +6.5%; Abrasives +4–5%, delivered +5.1%; EMD +1–2%, delivered +3.7%; Ceramics +13–14%, **missed at +9.3%** (deferred refractory projects, metallized cylinder production issue in H1) [Q4 call].
- **Q4 FY26 guidance for FY27:** consolidated sales +4–4.5% reported, or +11–12% like-for-like excluding Foskor and Awuko (₹343 cr of FY26 sales); Abrasives +11–12% ex-Awuko; Ceramics +15–15.5%; EMD +8–9% ex-Foskor; margins: Abrasives 9.5–10%, Ceramics 20.5–21%, EMD 9–9.5%; capex ~₹400 cr [Q4 call].
- **Q1 FY27 update — raised:** like-for-like sales growth "could be 15%"; Ceramics raised to +23–25%; EMD nudged to +9–10%; Abrasives growth and all margin guides kept; capex ₹400 cr kept [Q1 call].
- **Delivery vs promise in Q1:** Abrasives consolidated PBIT ₹40 cr includes the ₹25 cr Sterling gain; an analyst calculated underlying Abrasives margin near 2.5% vs the 9.5–10% guide, and management said it "still hold[s]" the guide because Awuko losses will fall away [Q1 call]. Ceramics PBIT was flat YoY (₹74 cr vs ₹75 cr) despite +16.5% sales because of fuel/alumina costs and unabsorbed costs on new semicon and defence lines [Q1 call]. The Q1 standalone Abrasives margin fell to 10.4% from 15.3% FY26 average, a ₹15–16 cr hit, mostly oil-linked resins after the US–Iran conflict [Q1 call].
- Longer term: no numeric 2030 target. "Aspiration 2030" goals are mix targets — in EMD, treated grains from 5–6% to ~20%, zirconia specialty products from 8% to 18–20%, "transformational" products (SOFC powders, nitrides, high-purity SiC) to ~10% by 2030 [Q4 call]. Semiconductor ceramics "material revenue" from 2029 onwards, peaking FY30 [Q4 call; Q1 call]. CRISIL expects only 4–5% medium-term revenue growth and margins recovering to ~14% [CRISIL Mar-26].

## Capex and capacity
- Capex: ~₹250 cr (FY25), ₹309 cr (FY26; standalone ₹235 cr) vs ₹350 cr guided; ₹400 cr planned for FY27; Q1 FY27 spend only ₹53 cr (13% of the full-year plan) [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Completed in FY26: first module for semiconductor wafer-fab-equipment ceramics (₹66 cr; management expects this to go "at least 3x to 4x higher"); defence ballistic ceramics plant (₹49 cr; STANAG 4 and BIS level 5/6 qualifications); white fused alumina furnace upgrade 2→4.5 MVA (₹53 cr, peak revenue ₹95 cr); treated-grain facility (₹30 cr, peak revenue ₹120 cr); thin-wheel line at Hosur from DRONCO assets (₹83 cr, 46 million wheels, peak revenue ₹120 cr) [Q4 call]. Asset turns on these are ~1.1–4x.
- FY27 programme: power-electronics ceramics (substrates via AMB/DBC, metallized tubes, brazed assemblies), brown fused alumina expansion, thermal-spray powder furnace, zirconia furnace, 110 kV substation, refractory tunnel kiln; fired refractory capacity +75%; metallized-cylinder capacity to be expanded in three phases over 18–24 months [Q4 call]. Project-wise split not disclosed [Q1 call].
- Metallized substrate: anchor customer and technology tie-up in place, revenue from FY28 [Q1 call]. Capital commitments outstanding ₹63.6 cr at Mar-26 (₹135.6 cr a year earlier) [AR FY26 Note 34].
- Funded internally: "CUMI is capable of funding itself" [Q4 call]; CRISIL sees ₹450–500 cr cash generation covering ₹350–400 cr capex and no material new debt [CRISIL Mar-26].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹413 cr at Mar-26 (₹216 cr Mar-25); reserves ₹3,881 cr [Screener]. Debt/equity 0.08 at FY26 and 0.05 at Q1 FY27 [Q4 call; Q1 call]. Management calls the company "net debt-free" [Q4 call].
- But ₹297 cr of VAW's cash is **not available to the rest of the group** because of repatriation restrictions [AR FY26 Note 29A]. CRISIL put group cash surplus at >₹385 cr excluding VAW at Dec-25 [CRISIL Mar-26].
- Cash flow: CFO ₹304 cr (FY25), ₹367 cr (FY26); FCF ₹28 cr and ₹60 cr [Screener]. Management's own FCF/PAT was 56.6% in FY26 vs 16.1% in FY25 [Q4 call].
- Working capital is heavy: debtor days 62, inventory days 200, cash conversion cycle 187 days in FY26 (163 in FY24) [Screener].
- Dividend payout 39% of FY26 profit (26% FY25) [Screener].

## Moat evidence
- Over 30% share of Indian bonded abrasives; one of the largest domestic abrasive makers; No. 2 globally in silicon carbide and No. 3 in zirconia (with VAW and Foskor) [CRISIL Mar-26].
- Backward integration (mines, fusion, captive hydel power) gives a cost advantage [CRISIL Mar-26; AR FY26].
- Qualification barriers: 4–6-year material qualification for semiconductor-equipment ceramics now cleared for a set of products [Q4 call]; anchor-customer supply to a leading SOFC maker with line of sight to ~2028 [Q4 call].
- Pricing power is weak in abrasives and EMD: China's export rebate cut (from April 2026) helped only at the margin; Chinese intensity in EMD "continues to be there"; Q1 cost push of ₹16 cr was not passed on [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- EU anti-dumping duties on Chinese grains helped EMD exports double in FY26 [Q4 call].
- R&D spend is ~1% of sales; management wants 2–3% [Q4 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Auditor emphasis of matter (FY26 consolidated):** Awuko and Foskor accounts prepared on a realisable-value (non-going-concern) basis; ₹134.6 cr of exceptional charges (₹118.6 cr Awuko write-down and restructuring, ₹16.0 cr Foskor). Opinion not modified [AR FY26, auditor's report para 4; Notes 29B/29C].
- **Key audit matter — VAW Russia:** carrying value of VAW assets ₹1,190.8 cr incl. ₹163.7 cr goodwill = **22.5% of group assets**; ₹104 cr impaired in FY25; no further impairment in FY26 [AR FY26 KAM; Note 29A]. VAW rouble sales fell 35% in FY26 and pre-tax profit dropped from RUB 1.7 bn to RUB 617 mn [Q4 call]; Q1 FY27 PAT RUB 52 mn vs RUB 72 mn [Q1 call]. Management has "no alternate solution" until sanctions lift [Q4 call].
- Loss-making subsidiaries: Rhodius lost ₹45 cr and Awuko ₹75 cr in FY26 [Q4 call]; Awuko still lost €1.69 mn and Foskor ₹17 cr in Q1 FY27 while being wound down [Q1 call]. Foskor exit route still open ("close this by Q2") [Q1 call].
- Contingent liabilities are small: disputed taxes ₹47.2 cr (₹34.5 cr a year ago), electricity board ₹3.9 cr, other claims ₹67.7 cr [AR FY26 Note 34] — under 3% of net worth.
- Input-cost risk from oil (resins, fuel) — see Q1 hit [Q1 call].
- Management change: new CFO Sriram Seshadri appointed 7-Sep-26 [BSE announcement via Screener].
- Promoter holding drifted from 39.82% (Mar-25) to 38.90% (Jun-26) [Screener].
- CRISIL flags "volatility in operating profitability across business segments" as the main weakness [CRISIL Mar-26].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from surgery (closing Awuko, Foskor; big Aspiration 2030 presentation) to execution and a cost shock. Growth guidance went **up** (like-for-like ~15%, Ceramics 23–25%) but Q1 margins went **down** (consolidated OPM 9%, lowest in the eight quarters shown) [Q4 call; Q1 call; Screener].
- New issue: oil-linked resin and fuel inflation (Brent up to $117 in May) hit Abrasives [Q1 call].
- SCOMET export approval for ballistic ceramics was "awaited" in May; in August the MD did not say it had been received, answering only about product standards [Q4 call; Q1 call official].
- Capex pace: only ₹53 cr in Q1 against ₹400 cr for the year [Q1 call].

## My read
The India businesses are clearly improving: standalone sales grew 21% in Q1, EMD exports have tripled as a share, and Ceramics has real, qualified new products (SOFC, semiconductor-equipment parts, metallized cylinders). But the group's reported profit is still weighed down by Russia (a fifth of assets, cash trapped), two subsidiaries being shut, and weak pricing power in abrasives — margins have fallen from 16% to 9–11% and ROCE from 20% to 11%. At about 97x trailing earnings the price assumes the FY24 margin returns and the new ceramics lines scale on time; CRISIL only models 4–5% revenue growth and ~14% margins. What would change the view: clean Foskor exit and Awuko closure by Q2–Q3 FY27, Abrasives margins back above 9% without one-offs, and evidence that the ₹400 cr capex is actually being spent (Q1 was only ₹53 cr).
