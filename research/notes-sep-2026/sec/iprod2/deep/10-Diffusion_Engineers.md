# Diffusion Engineers Ltd — deep research (Phase A1)

Inputs read: stored file (metrics, analysis, profile, Q1 FY27 and Q4 FY26 transcripts — both are AI copies); official Q1 FY27 transcript (BSE, filed 18 Aug 2026) and official Q4 FY26 transcript (BSE, filed 22 May 2026), both read in full; Q1 FY27 investor presentation (BSE); Annual Report FY26 (BSE) — MD&A, auditor's report, note 37 contingent liabilities, note 38 related parties, investments note; CRISIL rationale (22 Sep 2026); Screener consolidated page (raw HTML).

## Business and segments
- Nagpur-based maker of special (hardfacing/repair) welding consumables — electrodes and flux-cored wires — plus chromium-carbide wear plates, wear parts, heavy engineering equipment (roller-press rolls, VRM rollers, classifiers, separators, fans, kiln tyres, air-preheater rotors) and on-site repair services [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call].
- Revenue split, as management "always indicated": ~30% consumables, ~30% wear plates and parts, ~30% heavy engineering, ~10% services [Q4 call]. Only 2–3% of consumables are conventional welding electrodes; the brand is in maintenance and hardfacing [Q4 call].
- Reported as a single operating segment in the accounts, so no segment margins [AR FY26 segment note].
- End-markets: cement, steel, power, mining, sugar; defence ~1.5–2% of revenue; railways small but developing (Vande Bharat developmental items; L1/L2 in several tenders) [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Customers: >80% repeat; top-10 customers "anywhere between 60% and 70%" of revenue (RHP said ~70%) [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Heavy engineering: ~70% for new plant projects, ~30% spares for the installed base; often built as the "manufacturing arm" of European/Japanese OEMs; only forgings/castings bought out [Q1 call].
- Geography: 35+ countries; subsidiaries in Singapore (largest overseas contributor, with the Philippines), Turkey (now out of losses), UAE (service facility live, revenue from Q2 FY27); associates LSN Diffusion (UK, 21.56%) and Mecdiff (Malaysia) [Q1 call; AR FY26]. International target ~15% of revenue [Q4 call].
- Capacity: wires 85–90% used and wear plates/heavy engineering 85–90% before the new plant; Unit 3 only 2–3% used; heavy-engineering capacity doubling from 9,000 t to 18,000 t [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- **Q4 FY26 call (18 May 2026):** FY27 revenue growth ">20%"; EBITDA margin +80–100 bps in FY27; ₹650 cr+ revenue platform in "2 to 3 years" with 15–16% EBITDA; the ₹100 cr capex should carry revenue to ₹800–900 cr without more capex; Unit 4 (Nimji) fully commissioned by end-Q1 FY27; debtor days back to 80–85 (from 98) and inventory 60–65 days; international sales to grow >20% and reach ~15%; significant railway revenue "from next year"; no Tejorup revenue in FY27 and "nothing significant" in FY28 [Q4 call].
- **Q1 FY27 call (13 Aug 2026):** ~20% growth in FY27, FY28 "and continuously for next 3-4 years", doubling in 3 years; EBITDA +100–200 bps "over the next year, year and a half"; Q2 to show similar growth; new plant running "in a phase-wise manner", 2–3 years to ramp; railway conversion in 9–12 months after workshop approval [Q1 call].
- Delivered vs Q4 promises: revenue +36.5% (**beat**); EBITDA margin 12.85%, down 27 bps YoY (**behind** the +80–100 bps FY27 promise, with the timeline stretched to 12–18 months); Unit 4 phased rather than fully live (**slipped slightly**); railway orders still awaiting workshop approval (**slipped**) [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Debtor-day target: Screener shows 115 debtor days for FY26 and CRISIL 132 days — far from the 80–85 target; not addressed on the Q1 call [Screener; CRISIL Sep-26].

## Capex and capacity
- IPO (listed Sep/Oct 2024) capex ~₹100 cr: ~₹70 cr at the Nimji (Unit 4) wear-plate/heavy-engineering plant (~170,000–180,000 sq ft) and ~₹30 cr at B33 (Unit 5) for a 10 t/day electrode plant and a strip-slitting line for flux-cored wire (live since Dec 2025) [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A].
- 18 new hardfacing torches added at Nimji, lifting overlay capacity by 16,848 m²/yr to >70,000 m²/yr [AR FY26 MD&A].
- About ₹67 cr of IPO money was still unspent in Aug-26, to be used by year-end; savings may be redeployed with shareholder approval [Q1 call].
- Expected asset turn ~3–3.5x on new assets is quoted in the stored profile but **not found** in either official transcript; management instead says the ₹100 cr supports ₹800–900 cr revenue [Q4 call].
- Next expansion to start as soon as utilisation reaches 70–80%; possible further backward integration, not yet planned [Q1 call].
- Strategic investment: ₹2.15 cr for ~10% of Tejorup Sunmay Systems (VSHORADS air-defence prototype; DRDO sanction mentions 200 launchers and 1,200 rockets) [AR FY26 investments note; Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹29 cr (FY26) vs ₹23 cr (FY25); cash and bank ₹108 cr; gearing 0.1x; interest cover 29.3x; bank lines ~18% used [Screener; CRISIL Sep-26].
- CFO weak: FY24 ₹36 cr, FY25 ₹9 cr, FY26 ₹23 cr (65% of operating profit); free cash flow −₹18 cr in FY26 [Screener].
- Working capital: debtor days 87 (FY24) → 92 → 115 (FY26); inventory days 119; cash-conversion cycle 159 days; working-capital days 114 [Screener]. CRISIL: gross current assets 288 days, debtors 132 days, inventory 78 days [CRISIL Sep-26]. Management says 98 debtor days, inflated by ~₹20 cr of rollers sold in March [Q4 call].
- Heavy-engineering terms: 10–20% advance, most of the balance after dispatch [Q1 call].
- Dividend: final ₹1.50/share for FY26 (payout ~11%) [AR FY26 AGM notice; Screener].

## Moat evidence
- Integration: makes its own strip, flux-cored wire, electrodes, wear plates and then wear parts and equipment — "one of the most integrated manufacturers" in this niche; claims roller-press rolls that run 5–6 years without removal [Q4 call].
- Stickiness: >80% repeat customers; heavy-engineering OEM clients "become… de facto manufacturing arm" relationships [Q1 call].
- Pricing: price revisions are "an ongoing activity", with very short offer validity for tungsten/cobalt/nickel items and back-to-back raw-material buying; <10% of the order book has price-variation clauses [Q4 call].
- Margins have held in a narrow 13–15% band despite input spikes [Screener; Q4 call]. CRISIL notes margins of 11.8–15.4% over five years [CRISIL Sep-26].
- Peers (per management): ex-Ador Fontech (now in Ador Welding) and EWAC (now ESAB) in special consumables and wear plates; ThyssenKrupp, ISGEC, Walchandnagar in heavy engineering [Q4 call]. Limits: consumables and wear parts still involve competitive bidding; defence sub-assemblies bids not yet won [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Working capital and cash conversion** are the main weak spot: rising receivables, FCF negative for three years [Screener; CRISIL Sep-26].
- **Earnings quality**: treasury income on IPO cash and a one-off associate profit made up a large share of recent PAT; both fade as cash is spent [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- **Concentration**: top-10 customers 60–70%; cement-heavy mix [Q1 call].
- **Raw materials**: steel is 55–65% of revenue; fixed-price orders absorb spikes [CRISIL Sep-26; Q4 call]. MD&A flags cheap Chinese electrodes, wires and wear plates in commoditised segments [AR FY26 MD&A].
- **Execution**: heavy engineering is lumpy and Q4-heavy (Q4 FY26 = 35% of the year's revenue) [Screener; Q4 call].
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; key audit matters are revenue timing and IT controls [AR FY26 auditor's report]. Contingent liabilities tiny: ₹0.72 cr GST appeal [AR FY26 note 37].
- Related parties modest: MD Prashant Garg ₹2.4 cr pay; spouse Neelu Garg (GM-Procurement) ₹0.55 cr, with an AGM resolution raising it to ₹66.2 lakh and allowing up to 20% annual increases to FY31; rent to promoter Chitra Garg ₹0.44 cr; sales to associate LSN ₹5.1 cr [AR FY26 note 38; AR FY26 AGM notice].
- Defence start-up investment (Tejorup) is small in money but pre-revenue [AR FY26; Q4 call].

## My read
Diffusion is growing fast (21% in FY26, 36% in Q1 FY27) with a doubled order book and a new plant that removes the capacity cap, and management tends to under-promise on revenue. The weaker parts are cash and profit quality: debtors have climbed to 115–132 days, free cash flow has been negative for three years, and a meaningful slice of recent profit came from interest on unspent IPO money and a one-off associate gain. Operating margin has stayed at 13–14% and the promised expansion has been pushed out to 12–18 months. The price needs about 20% growth sustained for several years plus margin moving toward 15%, which the order book supports if the Nimji plant fills. Proof would be receivable days falling toward 90 and operating profit growing faster than revenue by H2 FY27; another quarter of sub-13% margin with rising receivables would weaken the case.
