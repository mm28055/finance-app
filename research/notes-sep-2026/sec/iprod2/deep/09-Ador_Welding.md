# Ador Welding Ltd — deep research (Phase A1)

Inputs read: stored file (metrics, profile, Q4 FY26 and Q2 FY26 transcripts — Ador holds calls only half-yearly; there was **no Q1 FY27 call**, only a presentation and an in-person group meet on 2 Sep 2026); official Q4 FY26 transcript (BSE, filed 4 May 2026); Q1 FY27 investor presentation (BSE, 21 Jul 2026); Annual Report FY26 (BSE) — MD&A, auditor's report, note 41 contingent liabilities, segment note, note 57 exceptional items, related-party note; CARE rating rationale (2 Jan 2026); BSE filing on senior-management change (13 Aug 2026); Screener consolidated page (raw HTML).

## Business and segments
- India's oldest welding company (1951, ex-Advani-Oerlikon), promoted by J. B. Advani & Co. (Malkani/Advani family); makes welding consumables (electrodes, wires, fluxes; ~90,000 t/yr capacity at Raipur, Silvassa, Bengaluru), welding and cutting equipment (~30,000 units/yr at Pune), maintenance & repair products (Nagpur, ex-Ador Fontech, merged w.e.f. 1 Apr 2022; scheme effective Sep 2024) and automation [IP Q1FY27; CARE Jan-26].
- FY26 segment revenue (consolidated): welding ₹1,078.7 cr (FY25 ₹1,032.9 cr, +4.4%); flares & process equipment (F&PE) ₹56.7 cr (FY25 ₹83.9 cr) [AR FY26 segment note].
- FY26 segment result before exceptional items: welding ₹164.3 cr (15.2% of segment revenue; FY25 ₹140.5 cr, 13.6%); F&PE loss ₹23.1 cr (FY25 loss ₹16.3 cr); unallocable −₹23.3 cr [AR FY26 segment note].
- From FY27 F&PE is folded into a single welding segment as a ₹20–30 cr product line [Q4 call].
- Split between consumables and equipment is **not disclosed** ("we don't give the revenue numbers for specific reasons") [Q4 call].
- Geography: India ~80–83%; exports ~₹154 cr in FY26, flat on FY25 (CARE: ₹153 cr in FY25); Middle East the main export market, Saudi Arabia fell from #2 to #3 export market [Q4 call; Q2 call; CARE Jan-26].
- 80–85% of sales go through 400+ distributors, so there is no meaningful order book [Q4 call; CARE Jan-26].
- Capacity utilisation ~70% overall; some consumable lines at 90–95%+ [Q4 call].
- Market share: management estimates 17–18% of a fragmented domestic market [Q4 call]. CARE sizes the Indian welding industry at ₹10,000–12,000 cr: consumables 70%, equipment 15%, cutting 10%, automation 5%; organised players 50–55% [CARE Jan-26].

## Key numbers (Screener consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Annual — FY24: sales 1,074, operating profit 119 (11%), PAT 86 | FY25: 1,123, 104 (9%), 60 | FY26: 1,140, 123 (11%), 82 | TTM: 1,198, 161 (13%), 114 [Screener].
- Quarterly — Q1 FY26: sales 252, OP −4 (−1.8%), PAT −4 | Q2 FY26: 281, 35 (12%), 25 | Q3 FY26: 288, 44 (15%), 27 | Q4 FY26: 319, 47 (15%), 34 | Q1 FY27: 309, 36 (12%), 28 [Screener].
- One-offs distort the run-rate: Q1 FY26 carried a ₹27.9 cr cost-overrun/liquidated-damages charge on the ONGC Uran flare project; FY26 also includes a ₹14.1 cr write-back of a Kuwait (KOC) receivable provision and a ₹3 cr ONGC re-order gain [CARE Jan-26; IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]. Company "clean" EBITDA: FY24 ₹117.6 cr (11.0%), FY25 ₹101.9 cr (9.1%), FY26 ₹132.0 cr (11.6%) [IP Q1FY27].
- Q1 FY27: revenue ₹309 cr (+22.9% YoY, on a base hit by the project loss), gross margin 36.5% (−240 bps), EBITDA excl. other income ₹36 cr (11.6%), PAT ₹28 cr [IP Q1FY27].
- FY26 standalone: revenue +2% to ₹1,135 cr; gross margin 38% (+250 bps); volume growth roughly equal to revenue growth; domestic volumes +4–5% in H1 [Q4 call; Q2 call; AR FY26 MD&A].
- Q4 FY26 clean EBITDA ~₹38 cr (+200 bps margin), gross margin ~36% [Q4 call].
- Unit economics: welding consumables are ~2% of the steel weight in fabrication (industry thumb rule) [Q4 call]. Price discipline, not steel deflation, drove the FY26 gross margin gain [Q2 call].
- Returns: ROCE 20% (FY25) → 23% (FY26); ROE 16% [Screener].

## Guidance history
- **Q2 FY26 call (15 Oct 2025):** no topline number ("increasingly hard to see beyond five, six months"); current ~12.5% EBITDA margin "sustainable throughout"; Uran project to be completed in Q3 FY26; F&PE needs ₹15–20 cr revenue to break even; exports flattish for FY26 [Q2 call].
- **Q4 FY26 call (30 Apr 2026):** EBITDA base ~12%, with "another 100, 200 basis points going forward over the coming periods", "not immediate"; growth at least in line with 6–8% GDP/IIP; FY27 capex ₹30–35 cr (up to ₹40 cr) a year for two years, mainly consumables lines; ₹2,000 cr revenue by FY29 remains a "principle" and the aim is to triple earnings; F&PE to break even on ~₹20 cr revenue; Uran at 96–97%, delayed by gas issues [Q4 call].
- **Q1 FY27 delivered (presentation, 21 Jul 2026):** revenue +23% (well above the 6–8% floor, flattered by the weak base); EBITDA margin 11.6% — **below** the 12% base and not yet showing the promised 100–200 bps; the message softened to "operating margins are expected to remain at similar levels"; capex restated as an ₹80 cr modernisation and expansion programme "over the next couple of years" (consistent with ₹30–40 cr a year) [IP Q1FY27].
- Track record: Uran completion slipped from Q3 FY26 (Oct-25 promise) to "96–97%" in Apr-26; the Q2 promise of "no surprise" was broadly kept (no new project charge after Q1 FY26) [Q2 call; Q4 call]. **Margin guidance: kept in words, not yet delivered.**

## Capex and capacity
- ₹80 cr modernisation/expansion over about two years, funded from internal cash, "without additional leverage" [IP Q1FY27]. Maintenance capex ₹10–12 cr a year; two or three new consumable lines in FY27–FY28 [Q4 call].
- Doubling volume over 5–7 years would need ₹100–150 cr of plant and machinery (ex-land) [Q4 call].
- FY26 capex was only ₹26.3 cr (FY25 ₹60.2 cr) [AR FY26 segment note]; capital commitments ₹10.4 cr at Mar-26 [AR FY26 note 42].
- Asset turn: ~₹1,140 cr revenue on ~₹181 cr of property, plant and equipment (≈6x) [IP Q1FY27], so new lines should pay back quickly if filled.
- Technology tie-ups rather than capex for equipment: Miller (ITW) for submerged-arc systems, Hypertherm master distributor, Liburdi Dimetrics for orbital welding; acquisitions in automation being evaluated [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Essentially debt-free: borrowings ₹0.9 cr at Mar-26 (₹42 cr at Mar-24); cash ₹42.3 cr + other bank balances ₹50.2 cr + current investments ₹30.9 cr + non-current investments ₹16.4 cr ≈ ₹140 cr [IP Q1FY27].
- CFO: FY24 ₹58 cr, FY25 ₹137 cr, FY26 ₹116 cr (122% of operating profit); free cash flow ₹94 cr in FY26 [Screener].
- Working capital: debtor days 74, inventory days 78, payable days 100; cash cycle 53 days (FY25: 64) [Screener]. MD&A puts the working-capital cycle at 93 days (FY25: 89) on its own definition [AR FY26 MD&A]. Trade payables rose to ₹193.7 cr from ₹124.6 cr [IP Q1FY27].
- Dividend payout 49% of FY26 profit (58% in FY25) [Screener]. ESOP scheme 2025: 29,500 options granted Sep 2025 [AR FY26 secretarial audit].
- CARE: gearing 0.04x, interest cover 33x (FY25); bank lines ~5% used; liquidity "strong" [CARE Jan-26].

## Moat evidence
- Brand and reach: the only Tier-1 Indian welding brand not owned by a global MNC; 400+ distributors; exports to 15+ countries [Q4 call; CARE Jan-26; IP Q1FY27].
- Share: ~17–18% domestic (management estimate) in a ₹10,000–12,000 cr market [Q4 call; CARE Jan-26].
- Pricing power: FY26 gross margin up 250 bps to 38% by holding prices rather than chasing volume; welding segment margin rose to 15.2% from 13.6% [Q4 call; AR FY26 segment note].
- Approvals: covers ~75–80% of shipyard requirements; NPCIL nuclear approvals; wind-tower flux approvals under way [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Limits: management concedes global MNCs hold product advantages in some areas ("somewhere between a Tier 1, Tier 2") and that Ador lagged in automation and wind [Q2 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **BIS compounding penalty ₹36.4 cr** (alleged offence under the BIS Act, 2016; order June 2023); writ petition led the Bombay High Court to send it back to the BIS appellate authority; still pending before the Bhiwandi Magistrate Court; **not provided** [AR FY26 note 41; AR FY26 secretarial audit; Q4 call].
- Other contingent liabilities at Mar-26: income tax ₹15.8 cr, excise ₹9.4 cr (₹9.0 cr paid under protest), sales tax ₹6.6 cr, other ₹4.2 cr [AR FY26 note 41]. A fresh ~₹14 cr income-tax demand was appealed in Apr-26 [Q4 call].
- **F&PE history**: loss-making since FY23; cumulative losses ₹61.3 cr up to H1 FY26; the ₹123 cr ONGC flare project (signed Jul 2022) still not closed in Apr-26 [CARE Jan-26; Q4 call]. Any further LD claims or recoveries run through the same line [Q4 call].
- Related parties: purchases of goods/services from holding company J. B. Advani & Co. ₹31.0 cr in FY26 (₹28.6 cr in FY25); sales to it ₹4.9 cr; dividend to it ₹15.7 cr [AR FY26 related-party note].
- Auditor (standalone and consolidated): unmodified opinion; only key audit matter is revenue timing; audit trail was not enabled in earlier years [AR FY26 auditor's report]. Secretarial audit notes a minor delay in circulating board minutes [AR FY26 secretarial audit].
- Promoter holding fell from 56.9% to 53.2% in Dec 2024 when merger shares were issued; now 53.76% [Screener].
- Cyclicality: growth tracks steel consumption; exports exposed to Middle East disruption (Q4 FY26 dispatches hit for ~3 weeks) and US tariffs [Q4 call; Q2 call]. Steel price swings are passed on with a lag; no commodity hedging [CARE Jan-26].
- Management change: Company Secretary V. M. Bhide gave up the HR role; new Head-HR appointed 13 Aug 2026 (routine) [BSE filing, 13 Aug 2026].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "tight… hard to see beyond five, six months" (Oct-25) to "we are standing on solid ground" with merger, Kuwait and Uran "behind us" (Apr-26) [Q2 call; Q4 call].
- New in Apr-26: explicit 100–200 bps margin-expansion ambition, a ₹30–40 cr/yr capex plan, F&PE dissolved into welding, Miller partnership, and openness to technology acquisitions [Q4 call].
- Exports: from "flattish" to "should do better", with Saudi enquiries healthier since Dec-25 [Q2 call; Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27 then showed strong revenue but a 240 bps gross-margin fall and flat EBITDA margin [IP Q1FY27].

## My read
Ador's core welding business is in better shape than its headline history suggests: FY26 welding segment margin reached 15%, cash conversion is excellent (CFO 122% of operating profit), the balance sheet holds ~₹140 cr of cash and investments, and the loss-making projects division is finally being wound into a small product line. What it lacks is growth — revenue rose only ~2–4% in FY26, broadly tracking steel consumption — and Q1 FY27's 23% jump flatters because the base carried a ₹28 cr project loss. The price needs mid-teens earnings growth, which depends on margins moving from ~12% toward 13–14% and on exports and shipbuilding lifting volumes. The ₹36 cr BIS penalty is the main unprovided risk. Two more quarters of 12%+ clean EBITDA with volume growth above 8% would support the view; another gross-margin slide like Q1's (−240 bps) would undercut it.
