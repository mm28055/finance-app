# DDev Plastiks Industries (DDEVPLSTIK) — deep research, Phase A1

Transcript check: both stored transcripts (Q1 FY27, 11 Aug 2026; Q4 FY26, 26 May 2026) are AI copies and were compared with the official BSE transcripts. Substance matches, with these errors in the stored copies: (1) stored Q1 says Q1 EBITDA per kg was "18.36" — official: **₹19.6/kg**; (2) stored Q4 says raw-material prices "saw inflation of over 15%" — official: **"beyond 50% inflation"**; (3) stored Q4 names a low-voltage competitor as "Kkalpana Polymers" — official: **"KLJ Polymers"** (this matters because Kkalpana Industries is a DDev group company; there is no evidence it competes); (4) names: official spelling is **Narrindra Suranna** (CMD) and **Ddev Surana** (CEO).

## Business and segments
- Makes polymer compounds — plastics blended with additives and cross-linking agents — mainly for wire and cable insulation and sheathing: **XLPE** (cross-linked polyethylene, via Sioplas silane or peroxide routes) for low/medium/high-voltage power cables (up to 132 kV), **HFFR** (halogen-free flame-retardant) for public-building and solar cables, PVC compounds, semiconductive compounds, anti-fibrillation (packaging) and engineering plastics. 200+ SKUs, 6 plants (West Bengal, Dadra/Silvassa, Daman, Bhiwadi). [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- Chemistry/raw materials: polyethylene and PVC resin bought from large Indian petrochemical makers and Middle East/European producers; ~10–15% imported. Cost-plus pricing with a 7–15 day lag; no annual fixed-price contracts (max 90 days, mostly exports, backed by matching purchases). [Q4 call; CRISIL Jul-26]
- Mix: PE-based compounds ~82% of Q1 FY27 revenue (CRISIL: ~87% in FY26 vs 76% four years earlier), PVC ~16%; wires & cables 89% of revenue, packaging 10%. Segment margins (company): XLPE ~8–12%, HFFR ~10–12%, engineering plastics ~10–15%, PVC ~4–6%, anti-fibrillation ~3–5%. [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Jul-26]
- Capacity: 3,16,400 tpa at Jun-26 (XLPE 2,14,500 incl. new 48,000 t Bhiwadi plant from Apr 2026; HFFR 10,000; PVC ~69,000), rising to ~3,34,400 tpa by Mar-27 (20,000 t HFFR target, MV capacity in the East). Utilisation: FY26 77% on 2,68,400 t (AR says 75%); Q1 FY27 ~66%; Bhiwadi 20–25%, target ~50% average in FY27. [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- Market share: ~1/3 of India's ~2.5 lakh tpa cable-compound market; ~50% of Sioplas, ~33% of XLPE; one of two HFFR makers. Competitors: Dow and Borealis (imports) in XLPE, KLJ Polymers in low voltage, Shakun (Orbia) in HFFR. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Geography/customers: Q1 FY27 India 68% / overseas 32%; exports ₹312 cr in Q1 ("doubled"), FY26 exports ₹718 cr (+30%, volume +23%), mainly MENA and Europe, 55+ countries. Apar, Havells, KEC, KEI, Paramount and Polycab together ~22% of revenue. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26; Q4 call]

BESS (battery energy storage) — FY27 revenue ₹200–250 cr at break-even; FY28 1 GWh, ₹800–900 cr revenue, EBITDA margin "10%-plus"; 5 GWh over time (₹2,000–2,500 cr extra by FY30). FY27 capex ₹175 cr incl. ~₹70 cr for BESS. [Q4 call]

- Q1 call (Aug 2026): core guidance **kept** (15% volume, 13% revenue, ₹5,000 cr by FY30); BESS **delayed** 1–2 quarters — plant moved from the West (Gujarat) to West Bengal, awaiting the new state industrial policy; BESS now recognised as a segment only **from FY28**; phase-1 margin **cut** to 6–8% (from "10%-plus" for FY28), with 11–15% only after moving to EPC/system-integrator work. The stored analysis wrongly calls this an "upgrade" from 2–3%. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ₹96 cr; FY27 ₹150–175 cr (MV plant in the East, HFFR +20,000 t, a new Vapi site, BESS); CRISIL: ₹350–400 cr over 2–3 years, all from internal cash. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; CRISIL Jul-26]
- Asset turns are very high in the core: Bhiwadi's 48,000 t cost ~₹77–80 cr and should add ~₹500 cr revenue at full use (≈6x); FY26 sales ₹2,948 cr on net fixed assets ₹304 cr (~10x). [IP Q1FY27; Screener]
- BESS phase 1: 1 GWh container-assembly line, ₹150–200 cr (capex + working capital), cells imported from China initially, own BMS/EMS software planned; company targets ROCE 25–30%, payback 2–3 years, working capital 60–75 days. [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net-debt-free since Q4 FY24 (company); Screener borrowings ₹57 cr; CRISIL counts ~₹223 cr of borrowings (working-capital lines), cash surplus ₹59 cr, bank limits ₹759 cr used ~31%. Net worth ₹1,013 cr. [AR FY26; Screener; CRISIL Jul-26]
- Working capital is stretching: cash cycle 41 days (FY23) → 72 (FY25) → 85 (FY26); debtor days 69, inventory days 59; inventory ₹392 cr at Mar-26 (auditor key audit matter). Q1 interest rose to ₹11 cr on higher working-capital borrowing; management targets 55–60 days. [Screener; AR FY26 auditor's report; Q1 call]
- Cash flow: CFO ₹85 cr in FY26 (₹137 cr in FY25); FCF −₹11 cr. Dividend ₹1.25/share final (payout ~9%). [Screener; Q4 call]
- Rating: **CRISIL A+/Stable / A1+** reaffirmed 17 Jul 2026. [CRISIL Jul-26]

## Moat evidence
- Leading share (~1/3 overall, ~50% Sioplas) and the only Indian maker offering 66–132 kV compounds; "zero rejections"; approvals transfer across plants so Bhiwadi needed no new customer approvals. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Water-tree-retardant XLPE tested at a German (VDE) lab — import substitute. Certification to the next voltage (220 kV) needs 3–4 years of field performance, a barrier for new entrants (and for DDev: target ~2030). [AR FY26; Q4 call]
- No trade protection: "no anti-dumping-duty investigation into any of these products" and no BIS standard for compounds; indirect Chinese pressure exists in all segments. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Customer backward integration is a stated risk; management argues scale and range make it uneconomic for cable makers. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **BESS is outside the core**: assembly of imported Chinese cells, project re-tendering after cell-price rises (bidders forfeiting deposits), relocation delay, thinner margins (6–8%), PSU counterparty and working-capital risk. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- **Volume shortfall**: Q1 +1% vs 15% target; demand fell when resin prices spiked; H2 needs ~60,000 t a quarter. [Q1 call]
- **Export premium fades**: Q1 margin boost came from a war-risk premium; management already says Q2 exports will be lower in value. [Q1 call]
- **Related parties/governance**: promoter Bbigplas Poly holds 74.99% (the regulatory maximum; free float only ~25%). FY26 purchases of ~₹13.4 cr from group company Kkalpana Industries (India) Ltd, from which DDev's business was demerged in 2022; royalty of ~₹4.6 cr was paid to Kkalpana in FY25 (nil shown for FY26); rent to Bbigplas. Two independent directors also sit on Kkalpana's board. Amounts are small. [AR FY26 note 41; Corporate governance report; Screener]
- Contingent liabilities small: income tax ~₹2.2 cr and other demands ~₹9.2 cr; bank guarantees ₹24 cr; capital commitments ₹75 cr. Auditor unmodified; KAMs are inventory and revenue recognition. [AR FY26 note 36; auditor's report]
- Q4 FY26 other income included ~₹10 cr mark-to-market gain on debtors, offset by ~₹11 cr forex cost in other expenses. [Q4 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "margins squeezed by March price spike" to "record EBITDA from export premium", but volume growth collapsed to 1%.
- BESS moved East, slipped 1–2 quarters, lost FY27 revenue and its FY28 margin was cut from 10%+ to 6–8%.
- New: West Bengal industrial policy dependence; industry re-tendering; higher interest cost from working-capital borrowing; India–EU FTA as an export tailwind.

## My read
The core compounding business is a high-return, asset-light leader (ROCE ~31%, ~10x asset turns) riding India's power-cable build-out, and management's cost-plus model protected profit through the 2026 price shock. But growth in real terms is slower than the headline — FY26 volume grew 6%, Q1 FY27 only 1% — and the Q1 profit jump was a temporary export premium. The BESS diversification is where the risk now sits: lower margins, imported cells, relocation and delays, and it will absorb cash as working capital stretches. The price needs the 15% volume target (or close to it) and EBITDA/kg holding ~₹16–17; H2 volume near 60,000 t a quarter would support the view, while another low-volume quarter or a BESS cost overrun would weaken it.
