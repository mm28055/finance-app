## Business and segments
- Three chemistries from minerals: **chromium** — chrome ore (chromite) is roasted with soda ash to sodium chromate, then acidified to **sodium dichromate**, the base for basic chrome sulphate (leather tanning), chromic acid (electroplating, wood preservation), chrome oxide green (pigments, refractories, feedstock for chromium metal) and potassium dichromate; **barium** (subsidiary Vishnu Barium) — barytes reduced with carbon to barium sulphide, then barium carbonate (ceramics/tiles glazes, speciality glass, caustic-soda brine purification), precipitated barium sulphate (paints, plastics) and by-products sodium sulphide and sulphur; **strontium** (Vishnu Strontium, ex-Jayansree Pharma) — strontium carbonate from imported celestite, mainly for ferrite magnets. [IP Q4FY26; Q1 call; Q3 call; CARE Nov-25]
- India's largest producer of chromium, barium and strontium chemicals; ~60% domestic share in PBS; first Indian strontium carbonate maker (India demand ~4,000 t/yr, all imported). [IP Q4FY26; Q3 call]
- Revenue mix: FY26 domestic ₹833.7 cr (52%), overseas ₹769.3 cr (48%); Q1 FY27 exports ₹239 cr (55%, +52% YoY) vs domestic ₹193 cr (+2%). Barium carbonate ~70% exported; North America ~9% of revenue (target 14–15%). Barium was ~25% of consolidated revenue in FY25; one sole reportable segment in the accounts. [AR FY26 note 37; IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; Q3 call; CARE Nov-25]
- Customer concentration: standalone top-10 customers 43% of revenue in FY25 (names include Solara Active Pharma, IOL Chemicals, Montana Quimica). [CARE Nov-25]
- Capacity/utilisation: sodium dichromate 82,000 t, running >90% in FY26, being expanded to 92,000 t by Q3 FY27; barium carbonate ~67% and barium sulphate ~53% utilised in FY25, "optimum" now; strontium 10,000 t (expandable to 17,000 t) at ~50% in Q1 FY27 (20–25% in Q3 FY26), targeting 65–75% by year-end. [Q3 call; CARE Nov-25; Q1 call]
- Plants: 7 manufacturing units (Vizag/Parawada, Bhilai, Telangana, AP barium, Ramadas Minerals barytes beneficiation), plus the Ruighoek chrome mine in South Africa (Batlhako Mining, 74% after a 26% dilution to a local partner in Feb 2026). [AR FY26 note 43; CARE Nov-25]

## Capex and capacity
- DMSO (dimethyl sulfoxide, a pharma/agro solvent; "only producer in India"): 10,000 t phased (5,000 t first), ₹205–240 cr; with a chrome oxide green line (20,000 t at Vizag) CARE put the combined project at ₹320 cr, funded ₹240 cr term debt + ₹80 cr accruals. [Q1 call; Q3 call; CARE Nov-25]
- Chromium metal: 50:50 JV with DCX Chrome SAS (France) signed 8 Sep 2026 for a 6,000 TPA greenfield plant at Vizag using DCX's aluminothermic technology; Vishnu supplies chrome oxide green to the JV. India imports ~2,000 t a year of chrome metal. JV capex and funding not disclosed. [News: company press release, 8 Sep 2026; Q3 call]
- Mine: acquired via Bonmerci Investments on 21 Nov 2025 for ~₹58 cr (CARE); ~10 Mt reserves, ~4 lakh t open-accessible (2.5–3 years of needs). Solar: 4.3 MW now, +~20 MW via SPV (Vishnu's outlay ₹5–6 cr). [AR FY26 note 43; CARE Nov-25; IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Asset turns: FY26 sales ₹1,610 cr on ₹836 cr net block ≈ 1.9x; management guides 1.5–1.8x gross asset turn on new capex and 1.5–1.8x for strontium at 80% use. Capital commitments ₹43.3 cr at FY26 (₹8.5 cr FY25). [Screener; Q3 call; AR FY26 note 36]

## Moat evidence
- Scale and integration: largest Indian producer across three chemistries; own barytes beneficiation; captive chrome ore from H2 FY27; flexible product mix across 10+ end uses and 50+ countries. [IP Q4FY26; CARE Nov-25]
- Trade protection helps: EU 84% anti-dumping duty on Chinese barium carbonate allowed 10–15% price increases and ~4–5% extra margin; customs duty on celestite removed; EU FTA would remove a 6% duty for European customers. [Q3 call; Q1 call]
- Contracts: a long-term formula-priced chrome oxide green supply deal (10 years, take-or-pay) was described on the Q1 call as imminent; the DCX JV (Sep 2026) ties chrome oxide green offtake to the JV. Whether a separate 10-year contract has been signed is not disclosed. [Q1 call; press release Sep-26]
- Limits: chrome demand from leather is weak; competitors in South Africa and Turkey avoid Red Sea/Hormuz freight; margins have stayed at 15–17% for three years despite integration steps — an analyst on the Q1 call noted gross margin shows no improvement yet. [Q1 call; Screener]

## Risks and red flags
- **Related-party logistics**: transport, CHA and hire charges paid to promoter-linked concerns (Vasantha Transport, Vasantha Shipping & Logistics) were ₹46.1 cr in FY26 (₹42.9 cr FY25) — about 3% of revenue, in a business where freight is 9–10% of sales; year-end payables to these concerns ₹11.7 cr. [AR FY26 note 38]
- Repeated slippage: mine start moved Sep 2025 → end-Q4 FY26 → Q1 FY27 → H2 FY27; DMSO from end-Q3 FY27 to FY28. Transfer pricing between the South African mine and India will decide where the margin sits. [CARE Nov-25; Q3 call; Q1 call]
- Freight and West Asia: freight could double to >20% of revenue in Q2 FY27 with only partial pass-through. [Q1 call]
- Debt-funded capex (₹240 cr term debt) on top of ₹527 cr debt; working capital already stretched; FCF negative. [CARE Nov-25; Screener]
- Auditor: Jampani & Associates (unmodified opinion; key audit matter revenue recognition) finishes its second 5-year term; **M. Anandam & Co** appointed from the 33rd AGM (Aug 2026) — a mandatory rotation, not a resignation. Subsidiaries audited by other auditors. Contingent liabilities small (~₹9 cr: GST transitional credit ₹3.7 cr, sales tax ₹1.8 cr, guarantees ₹3.2 cr). [AR FY26 auditor's report, note 36; BSE filing 30-May-26]
- Housekeeping: Vishnu International Trading FZE (UAE) wind-up still incomplete after a year, another ~6 months expected. Environmental risk: chromium (hexavalent) chemistry is tightly regulated. [Company filing 22-Sep-26; CARE Nov-25]

## My read
Vishnu is a steadily growing (11–25% revenue growth) but margin-stuck (15–17%) mineral-chemicals maker; the case for re-rating rests on captive chrome ore, strontium and DMSO lifting margins to 20% in FY28. Execution has repeatedly slipped on the mine and DMSO, debt and working capital are rising, and freight is squeezing exports in Q2 FY27, so FY27 is likely to show growth without margin expansion. The price needs revenue growth in the high teens and a visible gross-margin step-up once ore flows in Q3–Q4 FY27; watch Q2 freight impact, first ore shipments, DMSO commissioning and the related-party logistics spend.
