## Business and segments
- Two legs. (1) Textile specialty chemicals (pretreatment, dyeing, finishing auxiliaries) plus cleaning & hygiene, made in India (Ambernath 76,000 MTPA; Mahape 36,500 MTPA) and Malaysia (Biotex, 72% owned; 6,500 MTPA). (2) Oilfield specialty chemicals via CrudeChem Technologies Group, Texas (Brookshire/Midland; 1,48,000 MTPA after doubling from ~80,000 MTPA in Q1 FY27) — friction reducers, production chemicals, water treatment, flow assurance, plus field technical service and own last-mile trucking (Pratmax). Group capacity ~2,68,000 MTPA. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- CrudeChem: 53.33% stake bought via UAE subsidiary Fineotex Biotex HealthGuard FZE, completed 9 Dec 2025; four US entities; ~$65 m annual revenue at acquisition (ICRA); FY26 turnover ₹252 cr, PAT ₹35.9 cr, net worth ₹38.1 cr (whole year, consolidated). Sellers must sell a further 25% to Fineotex in January 2028 (stake → ~78–80%); price not disclosed. [ICRA Feb-26; AR FY26 AGM notice; Q4 call]
- Mix Q1 FY27: oil & gas ~65% of revenue (~₹245–250 cr, ~$100 m annualised) and ~55% of volume; textiles + others ~₹132 cr (vs ₹137 cr a year ago — a small decline). International 77%, domestic 23% (Q4: 70/30). [Q1 call official; IP Q1FY27]
- End markets: US Lower-48 oilfield service companies and producers (names cited by management: Halliburton, Schlumberger, Baker Hughes, ExxonMobil, Shell); first sales to Canada; enquiries from Suriname/Guyana; a customer won an "$8 billion" Aramco project using Fineotex's specialty package. Textiles: India, Bangladesh, ~70 countries. [Q4 call; Q1 call official]
- Utilisation: Texas 63% of 1,48,000 MTPA on a single shift; group ~60–62% at Q4. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Chemistry/raw materials: formulated blends (polymer friction reducers, surfactants, biocides, scale inhibitors); raw materials imported/bought in dollars; West Asia-driven price rises passed on through US "war/fuel surcharges". 44% of CrudeChem revenue from "green chemistries". [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Near debt-free (borrowings ₹8 cr, Mar-26); investments ₹317 cr; management: ">₹300 crores in cash" after the acquisition. Equity raised: ~₹192.6 cr (FY25 preferential shares + warrants) and ~₹50 cr warrant conversions (FY26). ICRA A+ (Positive)/A1+ (Feb 2026). [Screener; Q4 call; ICRA Feb-26]
- Operating cash flow **negative ₹24 cr in FY26** (₹69 cr FY25, ₹97 cr FY24). Asked about it on the Q4 call, management linked it to the acquisition and said operating cash flow would turn positive; under Ind AS, acquisition payments normally sit in investing cash flow, so the working-capital build (receivables and inventory) is the more likely cause. [Screener; Q4 call]
- Working capital: company says 79 days (Q4) → 72 days (Q1); Screener FY26 debtor days 137, inventory 112, payables 117, cash conversion cycle 133 days (year-end balances include a full CrudeChem balance sheet against only ~4 months of its revenue, so Screener overstates). CrudeChem alone 80–85 days. [Q4 call; Q1 call; Screener]
- Dividend payout 48% in FY26. Promoters 62.3% (slowly falling from 62.9%). [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Service-led model in the US: product + field technical service + own trucking; management says it takes ~2 years to get introduced to majors and wallet share "can easily grow 5x". Evidence of share gain: CrudeChem revenue from ~$65 m (Dec-25) to ~$100 m run-rate (Jun-26). [Q4 call; ICRA Feb-26; Q1 call]
- Addressable market: management cites ~$4 bn North American market for its products; target 5% share by 2028. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Pricing: cost pass-through via surcharges; gross margin rose to 35.4% from ~33%. [Q1 call]
- Textile brand strength: Bluesign, ZDHC certifications; JVs with EuroDye-CTC (Belgium), Healthguard (Australia). No China/anti-dumping angle raised on calls. [IP Q1FY27]
- Weakness: no fixed-term contracts ("not based on fixed-term contracts"); oil & gas order book not quantifiable. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Governance around CrudeChem**: executive directors Sanjay Tibrewala and Aarti Jhunjhunwala are also "Managers" of the CCT group companies; up to ₹400 cr of loans/advances to a 53%-owned subsidiary with ₹38 cr net worth sought for FY27; acquisition price and purchase-price allocation not found in the annual report. [AR FY26 AGM notice]
- **Audit coverage**: the principal auditor did not audit 12 subsidiaries (total assets ₹434.8 cr, revenue ₹348.8 cr, PAT ₹36.1 cr) — i.e. most of the US business is audited by others; consolidated report lists **no key audit matters** despite a transformational acquisition. Opinion unmodified; contingent liabilities negligible (~₹0.3 cr tax, ₹3 cr bank guarantees). [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report; note 37]
- Concentration: ~65% of revenue now depends on US oilfield activity; management's claim that a fall to $60 crude "doesn't matter much" is optimistic — US shale activity is price-sensitive. [Q1 call]
- Minority leakage: 46.67% of CrudeChem profit belongs to its founders until the Jan-2028 purchase; price of that 25% unknown — could be expensive if earnings keep rising. [Q4 call; Screener derived]
- Cash conversion: FY26 CFO negative; receivables and inventory up sharply. [Screener]
- Legacy textiles stagnant for ~3 years (analyst's point, not disputed); hygiene volumes fell in FY25. [Q4 call; ICRA Feb-26]
- Disclosure: segment revenue and CrudeChem margin not reported formally; numbers come from call answers.

## My read
Fineotex has become a US oilfield-chemicals business with an Indian textile-chemicals arm; the US piece is growing fast (~$65 m to ~$100 m run-rate in six months) and capacity is in place for $200 m. But group margins have fallen from the mid-20s to ~16% because the acquired business earns ~13–14%, textiles are flat, and a large slice of profit belongs to CrudeChem's founders. At ~58x trailing earnings the price needs CrudeChem to double again by FY28 at improving margins, with oil prices cooperating. Governance items — directors in dual roles at CCT, ₹400 cr of loans sought to the subsidiary, undisclosed acquisition price, most of the US business audited by other auditors — need watching. I would turn more positive on disclosed CrudeChem segment accounts and 18%+ blended margins; negative on a US drilling slowdown or large cash moving to the subsidiary without clear returns.
