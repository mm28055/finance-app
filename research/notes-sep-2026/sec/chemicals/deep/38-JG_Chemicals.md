## Business and segments

**What JG makes.** JG Chemicals is India's largest zinc oxide (ZnO) maker, with about 31% domestic share (as of Mar 2025), and ranks among the top 5 globally. [IP Q1FY27]
- It recycles zinc dross, ash and scrap (by-products of galvanising and zinc die-casting) into 90+ grades of ZnO.
- Zinc ash is turned into zinc sulphate for use as a fertiliser micronutrient.
- ZnO is the curing activator in rubber: 3.5–5% of a tyre compound but only 1–1.5% of tyre cost. [Q4 call]

**Where the revenue comes from.** [IP Q1FY27]

| End market | FY24 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|
| Rubber and tyre | 89.7% | 85.0% |
| Pharma and chemicals | 6.9% | 8.2% |
| Agri | 1.2% | 3.7% |
| Others (ceramics, paints, electronics, etc.) | 2.2% | 3.1% |

- Q1 FY27 non-rubber share was "close to 18%". [Q1 call]
- Zinc sulphate is under 5% of revenue. [Q1 call]
- Zinc oxide is about 95% of revenue. [CARE Sep-26]

**Customers.**
- 200+ domestic and 50+ global customers.
- Supplies every major Indian tyre maker and 9 of the top 10 global tyre makers.
- More than 95% of sales go direct to end customers; more than 90% of customers are repeat buyers.
[IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- **Concentration is high.** Customers that each exceed 10% of revenue together bought **₹494.1 cr in FY26, about 51% of revenue** (₹465.3 cr, about 55%, in FY25). [AR FY26 Note 36]
- CARE puts the top four customers at about 45% of FY25 sales. [CARE Sep-26]

**Geography.**
- FY26 export revenue was ₹109.4 cr, 11% of revenue (₹67.2 cr, 8%, in FY25). [AR FY26 Note 36]
- Management says exports are 10–15% of sales and will stay at about 15%. [Q1 call]
- JG accounts for 99.25% of India's zinc oxide exports. [IP Q1FY27]

**Capacity.** The company describes the group as about 70,000 MTPA. CARE puts the group at about 77,000 MTPA of zinc products. [IP Q1FY27; CARE Sep-26]

| Plant | Owner | Capacity (MTPA) |
|---|---|---|
| Jangalpur (West Bengal) | JG Chemicals | 14,400 ZnO + 5,040 recycled zinc ingots |
| Belur (West Bengal) | JG Chemicals | 1,800 ZnO |
| Naidupeta (Andhra Pradesh) | BDJ Oxides, **94.13%-owned** | 43,704 ZnO + 2,016 ingots + 10,080 zinc sulphate |

- Naidupeta is the only IATF-certified ZnO plant in the world and also holds WHO-GMP. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 AGM notice]

**Utilisation.**
- Zinc oxide ran at 77% in Q4 FY26 and in the "early 80s" of achievable capacity in Q1 FY27. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Zinc sulphate ran at about 60%. [Q4 call]
- Break-even is at 60–65% utilisation, and JG starts looking at expansion above about 80%. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity

**Dahej greenfield (Gujarat).**
- 11.43 acres, 40,000+ MTPA in two phases.
- About ₹100 cr total cost, funded entirely from internal accruals, for about ₹900 cr of revenue potential (about 9× asset turns).
- Phase I costs about ₹50 cr; ₹33 cr had been spent by March 2026.
- Phase II starts when Phase I utilisation reaches about 70–80% (FY29).
- Target payback is 3–4 years, implying a mid-20s ROCE.

[IP Q1FY27; CARE Sep-26; Q1 call]

**Who Dahej will serve.**
- Q4 call: ceramics 30–35% and tyres about 30–35%, with the rest specialty chemicals, pharma and agri.
- Q1 call: "tyre about 60%, others 40%".
- The Morbi ceramics cluster is the main new market, and two large tyre plants are within 10–15 km.
- Ceramic customer approvals take 1–3 months, against about 5 years for tyres.

[Q4 call; Q1 call]

**Other projects.**
- Naidupeta brownfield adds about 5,000 t in Q3 FY27; its capex was not quantified.
- Solar phase 1 cost about ₹2 cr, with a 3–3.5-year payback.
- The "JG-PUR"/"Devulc" recycled (devulcanised) rubber project has completed pilot trials. A commercial plant is planned within about 12 months; capex has not been disclosed.

[Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]

**Capital commitments** were ₹8.6 cr at March 2026 (₹1.5 cr a year earlier). CWIP was ₹8 cr and fixed assets rose from ₹39 cr to ₹67 cr. [AR FY26 Note 35; Screener]

**Use of IPO money (Mar 2024 IPO, ₹165 cr fresh issue).**
- By March 2026, ₹116.2 cr had been used.
- **₹33.1 cr remained unused and sits in bank deposits two years after listing.**
- Unused items: working capital for BDJ Oxides (₹16.9 cr of ₹60 cr unused), JG's own working capital (₹12 cr unused) and the R&D centre (₹4.3 cr unused).

[AR FY26 IPO note]

## Cash, debt and working capital

**Debt and liquidity.**
- Borrowings were ₹6 cr at March 2026 (nil at March 2025).
- Investments were ₹136 cr. Free cash plus liquid investments were about ₹156 cr, which makes JG **net cash of about ₹150 cr**.
- Gearing is 0.01× and interest cover is 110×.
- Working-capital limits were only about 6% used.

[Screener; CARE Sep-26]

**Working capital (FY26).**
- Debtor days 64, inventory days 42, payable days 5. The cash conversion cycle is 101 days, versus 91–119 days historically.
- Working-capital days rose to 156, from 119, as IPO money moved into current investments.
- Inventory was ₹91.8 cr.
- Payable days of about 5 mean JG pays dross suppliers almost immediately. That is a buying advantage but a heavy working-capital burden.

[Screener; AR FY26 KAM]

**Cash flow.**
- Operating cash flow swings: FY24 ₹76 cr, FY25 −₹11 cr, FY26 ₹44 cr (81% of operating profit).
- Free cash flow was ₹5 cr in FY26.
- Payout is small (dividend yield about 0.2%).

[Screener]

**Credit rating.** CARE assigned **A+ (Stable) / A1+** on ₹19.75 cr of bank lines on 25 Sep 2026, the first CARE rating; CRISIL rated it earlier. Upgrade triggers:
- scale above ₹1,800 cr;
- operating cycle below 50 days;
- net worth above ₹850 cr.

The downgrade trigger is a PBILDT margin below 9%. [CARE Sep-26]

## Moat evidence

**Market position.**
- About 31% domestic share, with India's top 3–4 players holding about 60–67%.
- It supplies every Indian tyre major. Tyre approvals take 4–5 years, and IATF certification is unique among ZnO plants worldwide.
- Management says these barriers let customers "place larger share of business" with JG.

[IP Q1FY27; Q4 call; Q1 call]

**Sourcing.**
- Dross must be sourced globally. During the March 2026 supply freeze, JG kept supplying while smaller rivals struggled.
- Management says this won it share from the "less organised" players.

[Q4 call; Q1 call]

**Pricing is pass-through, not premium.**
- Selling prices are LME-linked on a one-month lag. Energy and freight surcharges were passed on from 1 April.
- Management describes JG as "neutral to zinc prices" but keeps any inventory gains or losses.
- The audit KAM notes the group also uses **commodity derivatives** to hedge zinc.
- Margins have stayed at 7–11% for five years, which shows a processing spread rather than pricing power.

[Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26 KAM; Screener]

**China.**
- The company says there is "no threat of China", because ZnO prices are set off LME zinc, so imports have no cost edge.
- I found no Indian anti-dumping duty on zinc oxide.
- Raw material (dross) is imported from Europe and the Middle East, not China.

[IP Q1FY27; search of DGTR, none found]

**Value-add push.**
- New grades include Lab Pure ZnO and JG-ZRA. A patent for a rubber curing activator, developed with an Indian research institute, is at an advanced stage.
- Pharma-grade ZnO (IP/USP/BP/Ph.Eur licences), battery-grade ZnO and recycled rubber are further options.
- None of these is yet material.

[IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags

**Customer and product concentration.** About half of revenue comes from customers each above 10% of sales (51% in FY26). The top four take about 45%. Tyres are 85% of revenue and ZnO about 95%. [AR FY26 Note 36; CARE Sep-26]

**Auditor.** The auditor gave a clean opinion. The key audit matters were revenue cut-off and inventory valuation (commodity hedging, FIFO). CARO reports no qualifications. [AR FY26 Auditor's reports]

**Contingent liabilities are small.** They total ₹1.15 cr (₹2.72 cr in FY25): customs ₹0.97 cr and entry tax ₹0.18 cr. [AR FY26 Note 35]

**Related parties are modest.**
- FY26 KMP pay: Anirudh ₹1.61 cr, Suresh ₹1.67 cr, Anuj ₹1.48 cr, plus Aakriti Jhunjhunwala ₹0.37 cr.
- Rent and fit-out paid to promoter entities: Crystal Towers ₹0.75 cr, BDJ Impex ₹0.89 cr, Vision Projects ₹0.16 cr.
- A material related-party approval is sought for sales and purchases with BDJ Oxides; this is intra-group, with a 5.87% minority.

[AR FY26 Related-party note; AGM notice]

**Governance events.**
- Independent director Ashok Bhandari died on 3 Aug 2026, so a board seat must be filled.
- Promoter holding is 70.99%, unchanged.
- FII holding fell from 6.1% to 3.2%.

[Q1 call; Screener]

**Disclosure gaps.** Volumes are not disclosed. Quarterly capex and the Naidupeta capex were not quantified on the call. The reported "EBITDA" includes other income. [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]

**Execution.** Dahej has slipped from H1 FY27 to November 2026. FY28 utilisation guidance was trimmed. [CARE Sep-26; Q1 call]

## What changed between the two calls

- **Tone.** It moved from defence (the March cost shock, "last man standing") to offence (record quarter, "new normal" realisations, strong Q2).
- **Numbers.** FY28 Dahej utilisation went from 65–70% to 50–60%. The margin goal went from 13–14% to 14–15% (inconsistent within the call). Dahej went from "this calendar year" to November.
- **New items.** A Dubai trading subsidiary (DDG Materials and Metals Trading FZCO, under BDJ Oxides) for sourcing and sales. The new grades (Lab Pure, JG-ZRA). The R&D centre opened.
- **Dropped.** Talk of the Middle East shipping freeze and Morbi gas shortages.

## My read

JG is a well-run, cash-rich, debt-free zinc recycler with real entry barriers in tyre approvals and dross sourcing. But its economics are a thin processing spread: operating margin has stayed at 7–11% for five years, and three-year profit CAGR is only 7%. The next leg is Dahej, a cheap ₹100 cr project for about ₹900 cr of potential revenue. It adds volume at 11–12% margins, and diversification into ceramics and pharma could lift the blend.

At the current price the market is paying for that growth arriving on time. The rise in the share price since the Q1 rally also relies on a quarter helped by rising zinc prices.

What would change my view:
- **Positive:** Dahej hits about 60% utilisation in FY28 with Screener OPM holding above 10%.
- **Negative:** margins fall back to 8% once zinc stops rising, or ceramic customers ramp up slowly.
