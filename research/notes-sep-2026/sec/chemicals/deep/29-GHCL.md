## Business and segments
- **Chemistry:** soda ash (sodium carbonate, Na2CO3) is made by the synthetic Solvay process at Sutrapada, Gujarat. Brine (salt) and limestone are calcined with coke; the CO2 and ammonia are recycled; steam and power come from lignite and coal. Rivals abroad are natural trona producers (US, Turkey, Inner Mongolia), which have a structurally lower cash cost, and Chinese synthetic (Solvay/Hou) plants. [Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- **Capacity:** soda ash 12 lakh t/yr; sodium bicarbonate 1.2 lakh t/yr (capacity doubled; running at 80–85%); salt works at Vedaranyam, Tamil Nadu (consumer/industrial salt). New: **bromine 2,800 t** and **vacuum salt 1.7 lakh t**, built for ~₹330 cr from existing brine and surplus energy. Soda ash utilisation was ~96% in FY26 (95% in FY25). [AR FY26 MD&A; IP Q1FY27; CARE Aug-26; Q4 call]
- **End markets:** dense soda ash goes to glass (flat, container, solar) and light soda ash to detergents. GHCL's dense share rose from ~40% to ~55% without major capex. Indian demand mix: glass 31%, detergent 34%, bicarbonate 10%, other 25%. Solar glass uses ~1.5 lakh t today and should reach ~3.5 lakh t (≈8% of demand) once new plants start in Jan–Mar 2027. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- **Market position:** ~**26% share** of Indian demand (17% in FY05); India's second-largest producer; the top four players hold ~85% of capacity. Indian capacity is 4.85 MMT and production 3.92 MMT; imports are ~0.9–1.0 MMT (~20% of demand). [AR FY26 MD&A; CARE Aug-26]
- **Exports / customers:** FY26 revenue from outside India ₹114 cr (3.7%); no customer above 10% of revenue. The 2023 textiles demerger left one reportable business plus salt. [AR FY26 note on segments]

[Screener consolidated & standalone]
- Company-reported Q1 FY27: revenue ₹798 cr (−3% YoY), EBITDA ₹233 cr at a **29.1%** margin (27.3% a year ago, 23.9% in Q4), PAT before exceptional ₹151 cr. The exceptional item is a ₹40 cr gain from the ESOS Trust's settlement with a broker. Management said Q1 margin was lifted by "better realization and the benefit of lower cost input inventory", which it called "transient". [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Annual (Screener consolidated): sales FY24 ₹3,447 cr → FY25 ₹3,183 cr → FY26 ₹3,064 cr; OPM 25% → 28% → 22%; PAT ₹794 cr → ₹624 cr → ₹472 cr. FY23 was the cycle peak (₹4,551 cr sales, 33% OPM, ₹1,142 cr PAT). Company-reported FY26: revenue ₹3,144 cr, EBITDA ₹769 cr (24.4%), PAT ₹479 cr. [Screener; Q4 call]
- **Unit economics:** average FY26 realisation **₹23,709/t** (−7% YoY). Q4 FY26 volume was up ~11% and price down ~10%. EBITDA per tonne is roughly ₹6,500–7,000 (my estimate: ₹769 cr ÷ ~1.15 MMT). Landed import cost is ~$180–190/t. Only a 7.5% basic customs duty applies. [CARE Aug-26; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Key raw-material moves:** thermal coal index rose from ~$120 to ~$136/t after the West Asia war began; limestone imported from the Middle East and its freight both cost more; coke and salt prices rose in FY26 while coal and lignite averaged lower. GHCL holds 4–5 months of raw-material inventory. [Q4 call; CARE Aug-26]
- Returns: ROCE 17% (FY26) vs 24% (FY25) and 36% (FY23); ROE 13%. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- **Q4 call (May 2026):** no revenue or margin guidance; "the worst of the pricing pressure may be behind us"; bromine + vacuum salt "full commissioning… in Q1 FY27", ₹120 cr revenue in FY27 and ₹160–170 cr at full run-rate at 40–45% EBITDA margin; FY27 soda-ash volume growth "may not be very significantly higher" (plant at ~100%); greenfield timing unknown because of "land acquisitions". Dividend policy raised from a 15% to a 25% payout, with ₹12/share kept. [Q4 call]
- **Q1 delivery:** margin 29.1% (beat, but flagged as one-off); bromine and vacuum salt **slipped to Q2 FY27** for commercial production, with meaningful revenue "maybe in the fourth quarter"; imports rebounded from 45–46k t/month in Q4 to 73–74k t/month. [Q1 call]
- **Q1 call (Aug 2026):** margins to "moderate" and "normalise" over FY27; new projects ₹150–170 cr revenue at 40–45% EBITDA, reaching full use in FY28 (both ₹150–160 cr and ₹160–170 cr were said on the same call); FY27 capex ₹140–150 cr; greenfield still with no date. [Q1 call]
- CARE had expected FY26 revenue growth of 4–6% and PBILDT of ₹800–900 cr; GHCL delivered lower revenue and PBILDT down ~21%. CARE now expects ~4–5% revenue CAGR and PBILDT of ₹750–850 cr a year. [CARE Aug-26]

## Capex and capacity
- **Bromine + vacuum salt:** ~₹330 cr; ~₹300 cr of the ₹450 cr CWIP at Mar-26 relates to them. Revenue ₹150–170 cr at peak on ₹330 cr → asset turn ~0.5x, but at a 40–45% margin. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- **Greenfield soda ash, Kutch:** 11 lakh t in two phases of 5.5 lakh t. Phase I is **~₹4,000 cr** including common utilities; Phase II is ~₹1,000 cr. Funding: internal cash plus **₹2,000–2,500 cr of term debt**; financial closure is still awaited; ₹59.2 cr is spent so far (CWIP). Board approval dates back to **FY2017**. [CARE Aug-26; AR FY26 note]
  - Asset turns are very low: Phase I would sell ~5.5 lakh t × ₹23,700/t ≈ ₹1,300 cr a year on ₹4,000 cr of capital (~0.33x). Both phases would be ~₹2,600 cr on ₹5,000 cr (~0.5x). This compares with ~1.7x on GHCL's depreciated existing block (FY26 sales ÷ net fixed assets). The payback depends on a price recovery. [derived from CARE Aug-26; Screener]
  - **Disclosure gap:** the annual report says the project's environmental and forest clearances "have been challenged before the Hon'ble National Green Tribunal by certain local residents, and the matter is currently pending". On both calls management cited only "land acquisition". [AR FY26 note; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- FY27 capex: ~₹140–150 cr (projects, infrastructure, maintenance) plus, per CARE, ~₹100 cr on the greenfield. [Q1 call; CARE Aug-26]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net cash surplus ₹1,058 cr at Mar-26, and "more than ₹1,000 cr" at Jun-26. Investments are ₹1,042 cr (Screener), borrowings only ₹83 cr, and gearing 0.02x. [Q4 call; Q1 call; Screener; CARE Aug-26]
- Cash flow (Screener): CFO ₹638 cr FY25, ₹687 cr FY26 (124% of operating profit); FCF ₹336 cr and ₹424 cr. Working capital released ₹153 cr in FY26. [Screener; Q4 call]
- Working capital: debtor days 21, inventory days 199, payable days 94; cash conversion cycle 126 days, down from 183 (FY26 vs FY25). [Screener]
- **Payouts:** ₹415 cr in FY26 (dividend plus a ~₹300 cr buyback), about 87% of PAT. The Q1 FY27 dividend was ₹109 cr. Equity capital fell from ₹96 cr to ₹92 cr with the buyback. [Q4 call; Q1 call; Screener]
- Ratings: CARE AA-/Stable/A1+ reaffirmed on 10 Aug 2026. Downgrade trigger: total debt/PBILDT above 2x, or the greenfield departing from its staggered plan. [CARE Aug-26]

## Moat evidence
- **Cost position:** captive lignite, limestone and salt in part; 96% utilisation; "best-in-class raw material consumption norms"; a single-site scale advantage. GHCL is profitable while Chinese synthetic producers are said to be making cash losses. [CARE Aug-26; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- **Share gain over time:** 17% → 26% share since FY05. [AR FY26 MD&A]
- **No pricing power:** domestic prices "largely track landed import price in absence of import duty or import quota". Realisation fell 7% in FY26. [CARE Aug-26]
- **Trade protection failed:** an anti-dumping duty was recommended by DGTR but not notified by the Finance Ministry, and management assumes it was "not considered favourably". A new application for safeguard **quantitative restrictions** is under investigation. Only a 7.5% basic duty applies. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Global supply: ~7 MMT of new natural soda ash capacity has been added in Inner Mongolia in three years (2.5–3 MMT last year). Closures so far are a 1.3 MMT US plant (mothballed), Lostock UK (0.4 MMT) and a 0.6 MMT Polish plant. Global demand grows only ~2.25–2.5% a year. [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A]

## Risks and red flags
- **Commodity cycle:** profit has fallen from ₹1,142 cr (FY23) to ₹472 cr (FY26). Q1 FY27's margin was inventory-led, and imports are rebounding.
- **Greenfield:** a ₹5,000 cr project for a company with ₹3,100 cr revenue; NGT litigation is not mentioned on the calls; nine years since approval; low asset turns; needs ₹2,000–2,500 cr of debt.
- **Contingent liabilities** (FY26; my reading of the note): income tax ~₹47 cr (₹107 cr in FY25), excise/customs/service tax ~₹123 cr, other claims ~₹3.5 cr. Uncertain tax positions include 80-IA and transfer pricing. [AR FY26 note]
- **Auditor:** S.R. Batliboi (EY) gave an unqualified opinion with a single key audit matter on revenue cut-off. In the consolidated report the audit trail was not enabled for direct database changes. Deloitte is proposed as auditor from FY27 (routine rotation). [AR FY26 auditor's report; AGM notice]
- **ESOS Trust legacy:** GHCL had written off ₹53.6 cr of loans to its old employee share trust; the Apr 2026 broker settlement produced the ₹40 cr Q1 gain. [AR FY26 note]
- **Governance:** promoter (Dalmia family) holding is only ~19.8%. FIIs cut from 26.4% to 22.4% over the year to Jun-26. No pledge is reported. Related-party dealings are limited to KMP and director pay. [Screener; AR FY26 note 36]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "worst… may be behind us… approaching an inflection point" (Q4) to "caution against reading this as a new normal… margin to moderate" (Q1).
- Imports went from a 45–46k t/month low in Q4 back to 73–74k t/month in Q1.
- Bromine and vacuum salt slipped from "full commissioning Q1 FY27" to commercial output in Q2 and meaningful revenue in Q4; full use in FY28.
- The greenfield remained undated on both calls; the NGT challenge surfaced only in the annual report.
- Payout questions were raised from both sides: one investor on the Q4 call said 87% was too much ahead of a large capex.

## My read
GHCL is a well-run, low-cost, cash-rich commodity producer at the bottom of a long soda-ash down-cycle. Profits have more than halved since FY23, trade protection has failed, and Chinese and Turkish natural ash sets the price. At about 8.5 times trailing earnings, the price assumes almost no growth, and the ~₹1,000 cr of net cash and 3% dividend yield support it. The swing factors are the ₹4,000–5,000 cr greenfield (low asset turns, NGT litigation, new debt) and whether the solar-glass demand plus rupee weakness can lift realisations. I would turn more positive on safeguard curbs on imports or a price recovery above ~₹25,000/t. I would turn more negative if the board commits to the greenfield at today's prices.
