## Business and segments
- Marine-chemicals producer from sub-soil brine in the Rann of Kutch (Hajipir, Gujarat). Brine is solar-evaporated in pans: sodium chloride crystallises first (industrial salt); the remaining bittern ("liquor") is stripped of bromine (chlorine oxidises bromide to Br₂, steam-distilled); leftover kainite-type mixed salt is processed to sulphate of potash (SOP). One integrated site, own jetty access at Jakhau plus Mundra, and from Q2 FY27 Kandla. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 MD&A]
- End markets: bromine → flame retardants (the largest use, none made in India), agro/pharma intermediates, oilfield clear-brine fluids, water treatment, biocides, zinc-bromide batteries. Industrial salt → chlor-alkali (caustic soda/chlorine/PVC chain) in Japan, Korea, China, South and South-East Asia, Qatar. SOP → chloride-free fertiliser; India is a net importer. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Capacity: bromine 42,500 TPA (28,500 merchant + 14,000 captive); SOP 1,32,000 TPA; industrial salt "7,500,000 MTPA" in the Q1 deck but "installed capacity of 4 Mln MT" in the FY26 MD&A and 6 MTPA per CRISIL — the company's own figures disagree. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 MD&A; CRISIL Sep-25]
- Utilisation: FY26 bromine sales 13,263 t = ~31% of 42,500 t nameplate (~47% of merchant 28,500 t); salt 4.2 Mt sold; SOP only 644 t (<1%). Acume derivatives plant (Jhagadia, 28,000 TPA incl. 13,000 TPA clear-brine fluids and 5,000 TPA PTA catalyst) ran at ~45% in Q4 FY26 and ~40% in Q1 FY27; PTA line 30–35% through FY26. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 FY27 standalone mix: salt 54% (₹171.3 cr, 9.82 lakh t, −12% YoY), bromine 42% (₹133.3 cr, 4,175 t, +58% revenue), SOP 4% (₹11.3 cr, 1,952 t at ~₹58,000/t). Acume ₹29.6 cr (+28%), 1,714 t. Q4 FY26: salt 67%, bromine 32%. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Export share: 78% of FY26 revenue (domestic 22%); Q1 FY27 exports ~70% as bromine/SOP sold more at home. Salt is 100% exported. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Customer concentration: largest customer (a Japanese trading house — Sojitz, also an early investor) 36% of FY26 revenue; top 10 = 77%; top 20 = 87%; 77 customers in all. CRISIL put Sojitz at 25% of revenue and 50–55% of salt in FY25 — concentration has risen. QVC (Qatar Vinyl) is a key salt buyer, on hold since the West Asia war. [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Sep-25; Q1 call]
- Subsidiaries: Acume (bromine derivatives, 100%), Idealis Mudchemie (ex-Oren Hydrocarbons, bought via NCLT for ₹76.9 cr; five mud-chemical plants in Gujarat, Andhra, Tamil Nadu), Neun Infra → SiCSem (70%; silicon-carbide fab, Odisha). Stakes: Clas-SiC Wafer Fab UK 21.84%; Offgrid Energy Labs ~18–21% ($12 m). [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]

Price guidance: hold ~₹300/kg blended even though China landed prices fell 30–40% in 12 weeks. [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- SiCSem silicon-carbide fab + packaging (Bhubaneswar): project cost ₹2,067 cr ($249 m); capacity 60,000 wafers and 96 m units a year; Fiscal Support Agreement with India Semiconductor Mission signed 11 May 2026 (CRISIL: ~75% government co-funding). 15–20% already spent; 60–65% of the balance this year, rest in FY28. Construction to start late Aug/early Sep 2026; start of production 24–27 months after. ACI holds 70% via Neun. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Sep-25]
- Brine-field expansion Phase 1 mostly complete; commissioning possibly advanced to post-monsoon FY27. 40,000 t bromine needs further investment, timed with a flame-retardant project; decision in H2 FY28. MoU with Gujarat for a captive 7 MMTPA salt jetty. [Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Funding: CRISIL projected ₹100–125 cr a year of core capex and debt peaking at ₹550–600 cr by FY28. FY26 investing outflow ₹293 cr; CWIP rose from ₹69 cr to ₹182 cr. [CRISIL Sep-25; Screener]
- Asset turns: FY26 sales ₹1,081 cr on ₹1,383 cr of net fixed assets ≈ 0.8x (below 1x even before SiCSem). Acume has run at 30–45% for two years, so derivatives capex has not yet earned its cost. [Screener; derived]

## Moat evidence
- Resource position: CRISIL rates it India's largest bromine producer with 60–70% of India's bromine exports; brine fields take 4–5 years to develop and land is scarce, which keeps entry hard. [CRISIL Sep-25; CRISIL Jul-24]
- Cost: among the lowest-cost producers globally (integrated pans, good brine quality); pre-FY24 OPM of 40%+ shows the ceiling. [CRISIL Sep-25; Screener]
- Contracts: 60–70% of bromine sold on annual/long-term contracts; "majority" renegotiated upward in Q4 FY26; seven of the top ten customers are >5-year relationships. [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Weak points: salt competes on landed cost — Australian and Middle East capacity additions and 30–35% higher sea freight cut Archean's edge in East/South Asia; salt pricing fell ~10% YoY in Q4 FY26. Brine is getting weaker (lower GPL), and heavier Kutch rainfall dilutes brine — management admits process changes "could have [been] done faster". [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- China: China is the main bromine price-setter (landed China price is the reference index); India has no anti-dumping duty relevant to Archean's products; the competition is in export markets (Israel's ICL, Jordan, US Albemarle for bromine; Australia/Mexico for salt). [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Land lease expired 31 Jul 2018**: the Gujarat government lease for the Hajipir site lapsed; renewal applied for on 28 Dec 2017 and still pending eight years on. The auditor's CARO report flags that the factory land lease deed is not current. Company pays the revised rents demanded and "remains optimistic" of closure "in the coming months" (said on both calls). CRISIL lists non-renewal as a downgrade trigger. [AR FY26 CARO & note 3; Q1 call; CRISIL Sep-25]
- **Income-tax search**: IT department search concluded 9 Sep 2025; no demand yet, but the FY26 accounts mention a "High Pitched Scrutiny Assessment" communication. CRISIL lists a material tax liability as a downgrade trigger. [CRISIL Sep-25; AR FY26 note 37.1]
- Contingent liabilities ₹192 cr (FY26) vs ₹237 cr (FY25): disputed tax ₹56.3 cr, corporate guarantees for Acume ₹134.4 cr, power-demand dispute ₹1.6 cr. [AR FY26 note 37.1]
- Related parties: promoter-linked Goodearth Maritime provides jetty services, shipment management and barge transport (jetty-service expenses ≈ ₹25.8 cr in FY26 vs ₹23.5 cr); Jakhau Salt Company (related) reimburses jetty expenses. Promoter P. Ranjit (MD until 21 Jan 2026, now Executive Vice Chairman) was paid ₹9.0 cr in FY26 vs ₹20.2 cr in FY25. [AR FY26 note 35]
- Auditor: unmodified opinion (PKF Sridhar & Santhanam). Key audit matters: revenue cut-off on long-transit exports and existence of bulk salt inventory (measured by third-party survey). CARO: audit trail not enabled at database level. [AR FY26 auditor's report]
- CRISIL outlook **Negative** (A, Sep 2025) on weaker volumes/realisations and subsidiary delays; downgrade trigger is OPM below 15–16% — Q4 FY26 consolidated OPM was 15%. [CRISIL Sep-25; Screener]
- Concentration: one customer 36% of revenue; one site exposed to cyclones (Cyclone Asna cost ₹40.2 cr of salt inventory in FY25). [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Sep-25]
- Capital allocation: SiCSem (₹2,067 cr) and Offgrid are far from the core; management change (new MD Rampraveen Swaminathan from 22 Jan 2026; CFO changed Jan 2025; KMP N R Kannan left Apr 2026). [AR FY26 note 35; Q4 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from defending a trough (Q4) to claiming delivery (Q1): Acume EBITDA-positive, bromine at 5-quarter high, SOP process proven. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Bromine price story flipped: in May the war-driven shortage (spot to US$8–9/kg, then US$4–4.5) let Archean reprice contracts; by August the shortage had "normalized", China landed prices were down 30–40%, and customers were "raising concerns" — the ~₹300/kg is now something to defend. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Salt worsened: Q4 lost ~3.7 lakh t (deferrals + logistics); Q1 volume fell 12% and sea freight became a new problem; recovery pushed to Q3. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- New: grid-power cuts, a third port (Kandla), explicit 40,000 t timing (FY28–29), SiCSem capex phasing. [Q1 call]

## My read
The core is a scarce, low-cost resource business, but it has been shrinking: bromine volume has not recovered to FY22 levels, salt is losing its landed-cost edge, and consolidated margins fell from 44% to about 20%. Q1 FY27 shows the bottom may be in (bromine price +50%, Acume break-even), yet the bromine price gain is already fading in spot markets, and salt depends on a road and freight normalising. At the same time the balance sheet has swung from net cash to ~₹400 cr net debt ahead of a ₹2,067 cr semiconductor project and an eight-year-old lapsed land lease. The price needs bromine at 20,000+ t with ~₹300/kg held and salt back above 1.1 Mt a quarter; a signed lease renewal and a clean income-tax outcome would lift the view, while a CRISIL downgrade or bromine contract resets lower would cut it.
