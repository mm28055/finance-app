# Stallion India Fluorochemicals (STALLION) — deep research, Phase A1

Transcript check: both stored transcripts (Q1 FY27 call, 17 Aug 2026; Q4 FY26 call, 18 May 2026) were compared with the official transcripts filed on BSE (filed 20 Aug 2026 and May 2026). Substance and figures match, with these errors in the stored copies:
- Q1 FY27: an analyst asked about sourcing **AHF** (anhydrous hydrogen fluoride, the raw material for R-32). The stored copy calls it "HFO sourcing" and quotes the MD as expecting "a glut of HFO supply by 2028". The official text says **"by 28 there'll be a glut of AHF supply"**. This matters: it is a comment on raw-material supply, not on HFO selling prices.
- Q1 FY27: the "15–17%" FY28 figure was first said as EBITDA margin and then corrected to **PAT margin** in the official text. The stored copy has only "PAT", which is fine. The stored copy spells the plant "Akhalapur"; it is **Khalapur**.
- Q4 FY26: the stored copy says R-32 sales were cut to "less than 500 tons"; the official text says "less than 400–500 tons". The stored copy spells the plant "Mumbato"; it is **Mambattu** (Andhra Pradesh).

## Business and segments
- Stallion processes, blends, repacks ("debulks") and distributes refrigerant and industrial gases: 40+ gases, including HFCs (e.g. R-32, R-134a, R-410A), HFOs (the newer low-global-warming refrigerants licensed from Honeywell), hydrocarbons, specialty/semiconductor gases and high-purity helium. It has 200+ customers in 15+ industry segments. [AR FY26 MD&A p.81]
- Today it is a trader and blender, not a chemical maker. It buys finished gas (mostly imported) in bulk, then blends it, tests it and fills cylinders at four sites: Khalapur and Panvel (Maharashtra), Ghiloth (Rajasthan) and Manesar (Haryana). [AR FY26 MD&A p.81]
- End markets: air-conditioning and refrigeration servicing (the aftermarket, the main one), auto AC, pharma, medical, electronics/semiconductor, defence, fire protection and glass. It reaches data-centre and semiconductor users indirectly, through channels such as Carrier, JCI and Trane. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call]
- Segments: the accounts report a single segment ("sale of gases & related products") and a single geography, so there is **no split by product, customer or export share**. Revenue from operations in FY26 was ₹430.7 cr (sale of gases ₹430.6 cr). [AR FY26 Note 19, Note 30]
- Customer concentration and export share are not disclosed. Before 2024 it imported more than 4,000–4,500 t a year of R-32 and resold it. Because margins were thin, it cut R-32 sales to under 400–500 t. The MD says that turnover would otherwise be about ₹600 cr. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Capacity and utilisation: no tonnage or utilisation figures are disclosed for the existing blending plants. MD&A says only that it "continues to optimise utilisation". [AR FY26 Directors' report]
- Projects under way (the investment case):
  - **Khalapur high-purity helium plant**: 1,200 TPA installed capacity. Commercial operations began **21 Sep 2026**. Helium is sourced from RasGas (Qatar) through Sharjah Oxygen Co.; the liquefaction/recovery technology comes from SYS Advance (Portugal). [Q1 call; Screener announcements; AR FY26 MD&A]
  - **Mambattu (Andhra Pradesh)**: an HFO/HFC blending, debulking and storage terminal, enlarged from 5 to 12 tanks (about 2.5× the IPO plan), adding hydrocarbons, helium and semiconductor gases. Planned capacity is reported as 7,200 MTPA. Completion is now "end of year". [Q1 call; Multibagg summary of IP Q1FY27]
  - **Bhilwara (Rajasthan) R-32 plant**: 10,000 TPA, its first real chemical manufacturing. The target is end-Dec 2026 (it was Oct 2026 on the Q4 call and in the offer document). R-32 (difluoromethane) is made from AHF and methylene chloride (MDC), with hydrochloric acid as a by-product. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
  - **HFO manufacturing plant** (next, also at Bhilwara): 10,000 t (5,000 + 5,000) of two HFO molecules. An MoU with Rajasthan for about ₹200 cr (plant and machinery only) has been signed; management puts the full cost at ₹350–400 cr. Construction was to start in 2027. [AR FY26 MD&A p.82; Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Rights issue (Feb–Mar 2026): 3.68 cr shares at ₹99, raising **₹363.9 cr gross**. Issue expenses were **₹36 cr** (about 10% of the issue, which is very high). Net proceeds were ₹327.9 cr. Use at 31 Mar 2026: land ₹6.0 cr of ₹10.3 cr; plant, machinery and EPC ₹33.1 cr of ₹309.8 cr (so **₹276.7 cr unspent**); general corporate purposes ₹7.8 cr, all unspent. [AR FY26 Note 40]
- IPO (Jan 2025, ₹90/share, ₹160.7 cr fresh issue): ₹102.7 cr went to working capital, **overshooting the ₹95 cr object**. Khalapur has used ₹17.5 cr of ₹29.2 cr and Mambattu ₹8.0 cr of ₹21.2 cr, both still part-unspent at March 2026. [AR FY26 Note 39]
- CWIP was ₹34.7 cr at March 2026 (almost all added in FY26) and capital advances ₹11.7 cr. Management said it pays suppliers only 20–30% in advance, so spending would "rise significantly" from June 2026. [AR FY26 Note 3, Note 5; Q4 call]
- Asset turns: the R-32 plant costs about ₹310 cr of plant and EPC for a claimed ₹500–600 cr of annual revenue at ₹500–550/kg, so asset turns are about 1.6–1.9×. The FY28 aim is above ₹1,100 cr of revenue on roughly ₹400 cr of total new fixed assets. [AR FY26 Note 40; Q1 call]
- The next ₹350–400 cr HFO plant is not funded. Technology-transfer payments and the environmental-clearance (EC) filing are needed before the R-32 plant stabilises, which is why funding is being raised now. [Q1 call]
- Build time: the MD says the R-32 plant is being built in about 9 months, against a typical 24 months (Chinese contractors quote 18). [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- At 31 Mar 2026: cash and equivalents ₹212.5 cr plus deposits over three months ₹221.2 cr, so **₹433.7 cr cash**. Borrowings were ₹34.0 cr (an overdraft against fixed deposits), leaving **net cash of about ₹400 cr**. Most of the cash is earmarked for the R-32 plant. [AR FY26 Notes 8, 9, 12]
- The company has ₹120 cr of unused overdraft lines. [Q4 call]
- Operating cash flow was negative for years (FY23 −₹3 cr, FY24 −₹73 cr, FY25 −₹13 cr) before turning to **+₹39 cr in FY26**. Free cash flow in FY26 was −₹10 cr. [Screener]
- Working capital is heavy because the business carries large stocks of imported gas:
  - FY26 inventory days 134, debtor days 66 (102 in FY25), payable days 19.
  - Cash conversion cycle 180 days.
  - Inventory rose to ₹126.6 cr (₹100.8 cr); receivables fell to ₹77.5 cr net (₹105.5 cr).
  [Screener; AR FY26 Notes 6, 7]
- Receivables quality: ₹4.2 cr is over one year old, with ₹2.0 cr over three years provided in full. The ECL allowance is ₹4.5 cr. A **₹1.65 cr provision write-back was booked in other income** in FY26. [AR FY26 Notes 7, 20]
- No dividend. The share count rose from 7.93 cr to 11.61 cr through the rights issue, and the IPO added 1.79 cr shares in FY25. [AR FY26 Directors' report; Note 39]

## Moat evidence
- Moat so far: distribution reach (four sites, pan-India cylinder logistics) and a long relationship with **Honeywell** (20 years) for HFO blends. Other suppliers "cannot touch" HFO blends because of IP. This is a service and access moat, not a cost moat. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Pricing power is opportunistic. Profits spike when supply shocks meet large stocks (Q4 FY25 sales ₹152 cr; Q1 FY27 margin 18%), then fall back. Normal operating margin is 11–15%. [Screener; Q1 call]
- R-32 plant economics depend on the **HFC production quota** under India's Kigali Amendment schedule. The government will set quotas in 2027 based on a baseline period that ends in 2026. Management "expects" a 10,000 t quota but admits nobody knows. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- China context: India levied a definitive anti-dumping duty on R-32 from China from **23 Sep 2021**. I could not verify whether it has been extended past its normal five-year term. China has about 8 lakh t of HFC quota, with a 10–15% cut due on **1 Jan 2028**. Management argues this will open export room for new Indian capacity. [Global Trade Alert; Q1 call]
- India R-32 demand is about 20,000–22,000 t. New Indian capacity is 40,000–50,000 t on the Q4 call and "70,000–90,000 t" on the Q1 call. That means a domestic oversupply of 2–4×, and new R-32 makers (SRF, Gujarat Fluoro, Navin, Chemours tie-ups) are backward-integrated into AHF, which Stallion is not. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Dilution and promoter stake:** promoter holding went from 67.9% (Mar 2025) to 64.8% (Dec 2025, a stake sale) to **47.8%** (Mar 2026, after a rights issue priced at ₹99 against the ₹140 first hoped for). On the Q4 call an investor alleged the promoter sold rights entitlements to an AIF and DIIs. The MD replied that the shares he sold he "bought back". Now more dilution is being floated. [Screener shareholding; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Related-party pay:** in FY26 the MD's remuneration was ₹4.98 cr (₹2.61 cr in FY25), total KMP/director pay was about ₹8.2 cr (**about 19% of PAT**), and rent of ₹1.2 cr was paid to the MD. A ₹2.43 cr interest-free director loan was repaid. [AR FY26 Note 35]
- **Contingent liabilities** (small): income-tax disputes ₹1.19 cr (₹0.56 cr), including an AY2015-16 demand of ₹0.70 cr at CIT(A). Bank guarantees to customs ₹0.62 cr, secured by the **MD's personal fixed deposit**. [AR FY26 Note 29; CARO vii]
- **Auditor:** Mittal & Associates gave a clean opinion with no key audit matters. CARO found no default, no fraud and no deviation in rights-issue use. The internal auditor changed in April 2025. [AR FY26 Auditor's report pp.84–95]
- **Execution:** all three projects slipped by 2–4 months in one quarter. The team was moved from Mambattu to Bhilwara. [Q1 call]
- **Market:** no offtake contracts for R-32; the MD refuses pre-selling as a matter of principle. AHF supply is not integrated, and a rival reportedly claimed Stallion buys HF from it, which the MD denied. The R-32 price is already falling from ₹900 to ₹800. [Q1 call]
- **Helium:** sourced from a single region (Qatar via Sharjah). Ramp-up is slow: about 20% utilisation next year, and 5 → 12 → 24 containers a year. [Q1 call]
- **Disclosure gaps:** there is no segment, product, export or customer data, and the headline "revenue" includes other income. No credit rating rationale was found; the bank lines appear to be secured by deposits.

## My read
The base business is a working-capital-heavy gas trader earning about 10% PAT margins. It grew only 10% in Q1 once inventory gains are set aside, and its earnings jump whenever supply shocks meet its large stocks. The investment case rests entirely on a 10,000 t R-32 plant arriving just as Indian R-32 capacity rises to 2–4× domestic demand, before quotas are known, with no captive AHF and no contracts. The price needs FY28 revenue of about ₹1,100 cr at 15% PAT (about ₹165–180 cr) to be delivered on time. Delays, a lower quota, R-32 prices falling towards ₹500/kg, or another dilutive raise would all cut that. I would turn more positive on a clean December start, a quota of about 10,000 t in 2027, and helium volumes ahead of the 20% plan.
