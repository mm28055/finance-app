# Indian chemicals — sector research (Phase A2), as of 27 Sep 2026

Scope: the 48 listed companies in `sec/chemicals/` (fluorochemicals, CDMO/custom synthesis, agro/pharma intermediates, commodity and petrochemicals, amines and solvents, carbon black, pigments and dyes, surfactants and personal-care ingredients, aroma chemicals, industrial gases and explosives, rubber chemicals).
Main outside sources: **Crisil Ratings** (2 Jul 2026, 126-company study), **ICRA** (29 Apr 2026), **CareEdge Research** (via an IPO prospectus summary, Sep 2026), **Fitch Ratings** (Dec 2025), the **US–India joint statement** (6 Feb 2026), the **European Commission** on the EU–India FTA (Jan 2026), **UNEP/Kigali** schedules, **ECHA** PFAS opinions (Mar 2026), plus what 48 managements said on their Q1 FY27 / Q4 FY26 calls (company deep files in this folder). Tags: [Crisil Jul-26] etc.; [Co. call] means the company deep file.

## 1. Size and growth
- India's specialty-chemicals market is estimated at **₹5,56,300 cr in FY26**, projected to reach **₹7,54,100 cr by FY29** — a 10–12% CAGR, versus ~5% for China over 2025–29; the historical series grew 13.9% a year to FY26. Forecaster: **CareEdge Research** (quoted in Prasol Chemicals' RHP). [CareEdge via Multibagg, 19 Sep 2026]
- Nearer term, **Crisil Ratings** expects specialty-chemical revenue growth to slow to **~6% in FY27** from **~8% in each of FY25 and FY26**; domestic sales (≈two-thirds of revenue) should grow 7–8%, exports (≈one-third) less. [Crisil Jul-26]
- Domestic end-use mix (Crisil): agrochemicals ~30%, dyes and pigments ~22%, flavours and fragrances ~14%; the rest spread across pharma, FMCG, auto, textiles, water treatment. [Crisil Jul-26]
- For context, Crisil sized the industry at ~₹4 lakh cr in 2023 with ~40% exports then; the export share has since fallen toward one-third as Western demand stayed weak. [Crisil via Indian Chemical News, Jul 2023; Crisil Jul-26]

## 2. Where the cycle stands: destock → restock → war spike → normalising
- **FY23–FY24: global destocking.** After the FY22 boom, Western customers ran down inventory while Chinese capacity kept coming. Crisil noted revenue growth "plunged to ~11% from 41% in fiscal 2022" and margins fell 300–350 bps in FY23. Company evidence: Excel's EBITDA fell to ₹25 cr in FY24 from ₹232 cr in FY22; Fairchem, Alkyl Amines, Aarti, Anupam all describe FY24–FY25 as "washed-out" agro/intermediate years. [Crisil Jul-23; Excel, Fairchem, Alkyl deep files]
- **FY25–FY26: slow, uneven restock.** Volumes recovered but prices did not — Chinese oversupply capped realisations. Industry revenue grew ~8% a year (Crisil), but many small-caps saw margins shrink further (e.g., Fairchem OPM 16% FY22 → 4.7% FY26; Excel 20% → 10%; Nocil and Alkyl profits roughly halved from FY22). [Crisil Jul-26; Screener via deep files]
- **Q4 FY26–Q1 FY27: the West Asia war shock.** The Strait of Hormuz disruption hit ~20% of world oil and LNG trade and a large share of fertiliser/chemical flows; ICRA assumed crude at $120–125/bbl, urea-pool gas rose to ~$19/mmbtu in Apr 2026 from $13. Chemical and polymer prices spiked and customers "panic bought and stockpiled"; ICRA warned demand "is unlikely to sustain at current levels". [ICRA Apr-26] Brent averaged $97/bbl in Q1 FY27 vs $78 in Q4 FY26. [PCBL Q1 call]
- Result: **Q1 FY27 was a windfall quarter** for spread businesses — Supreme Petrochem 20% margin (normal ~10%), Styrenix ~₹52/kg vs ~₹19/kg normal, BASF India ₹360 cr profit in one quarter, GNFC, Deepak Fertilisers, Alkyl and Balaji Amines, IG Petrochemicals, Laxmi Organic, Fairchem (+30% realisation) — and many managements called it temporary. [company deep files]
- **Now (Jul–Sep 2026): partial normalisation.** Crude has "come down sharply" (Aarti), R-32 has eased from ~₹900/kg to ~₹800/kg (Stallion), ECU from ₹35–36k/t to ₹31–32k/t (Epigral), bromine spot gains fading (Archean), and PCBL expects "rocky" Q2 volumes as customers destock again. Crisil's July forecast assumes West Asia easing holds. [Co. calls; Crisil Jul-26]
- My read: the sector is **past the trough in volumes but not in pricing power**; FY27 reported earnings will be flattered by Q1 and then fall back in H2 for commodity names.

## 3. China: overcapacity, dumping and the April 2026 rebate cut
- **Fitch** kept a "deteriorating" outlook on global chemicals into 2026: "significant capacity additions in China (e.g., in ethylene and polyolefins) … will likely offset global plant shutdowns and keep the market oversupplied"; restructuring's effects "are not expected to be felt in the short term" and "the situation could worsen in 2026". [Fitch via Indian Chemical News, 22 Dec 2025]
- **Rebate cut:** from **1 Apr 2026** China withdrew export VAT rebates on a list of products including agro actives (glufosinate, acephate, malathion, profenofos and others), which lifts their FOB export prices by ~9%; solar/battery-chain chemicals were cut too, with battery rebates falling to 6% and phasing out on 1 Jan 2027. [Fertilizer Daily 13 Jan 2026; search summaries of ICIS/HSF]
- Indian managements confirmed the effect: Excel says Chinese phosphorus-chemical prices rose on the VAT withdrawal plus input costs; Fairchem says Chinese dimer dumping "has gone down" after incentives of "as high as 15%" were removed; Crisil says the cut "could lend some support to pricing, although continued dumping … would limit any meaningful benefit". [Excel Q4 call; Fairchem Q4/Q1 calls; Crisil Jul-26]
- China still sets the price in soda ash (export $160–180/t FOB, "most challenging" in SE Asia — Tata Chemicals), PVC/VCM, carbon black, rubber chemicals, amines, dyes, pigments and phenol; Chinese supply cuts lifted H-acid and dye prices (Kiri). [Tata Chem, Nocil, Alkyl, Kiri deep files]

## 4. Indian trade policy: duties granted selectively, QCOs rolled back
- **Anti-dumping duties are patchy.** DGTR recommends often; the Finance Ministry notifies selectively:
  - Granted/extended: sulphonamide rubber chemicals (CBS/NS, 20 Jun 2026 — Nocil); phthalic anhydride from China ($40/t) and Korea ($140/t) extended five years on 5 Aug 2026 (IG Petrochem); acetonitrile (Alkyl Amines); vanillin (Camlin); four Chinese chemicals incl. PEDA, acetonitrile, vitamin-A palmitate and insoluble sulphur in 2025. [Co. calls; Business Standard Jun-25]
  - Refused or lapsed: TDQ antioxidant (Nocil); paste-PVC ADD on EU/Japan allowed to lapse (Chemplast, now relying on a Madras HC bond order); soda-ash ADD recommended but never notified (GHCL, DCW); S-PVC ADD never imposed. [Nocil, Chemplast, GHCL, DCW deep files]
  - Pending: Pilflex 13 (Nocil, final findings Jun 2026); HEDP/ATMP phosphonates from China (Excel + Aquapharm, final findings reported ~25 Sep 2026, duty level unverified); soda-ash safeguard quantitative limits. [Nocil, Excel, GHCL deep files]
- **Pro-user moves in the 2026 crisis:** customs duty on PVC resin exempted and VCM duty cut to nil from 2 Apr to 30 Jun 2026, which let Chinese PVC flood in before duty was restored with a six-month **minimum import price** (~₹80/kg). [Chemplast Q1 call; DCW deep file]
- **QCO rollback:** on 12–13 Nov 2025 the government withdrew 14 BIS Quality Control Orders covering PTA, MEG, polyester yarn/fibre, PP, PE, PVC, ABS and polycarbonate "to ensure raw material availability … and lower input costs for downstream MSMEs". This removed a non-tariff barrier that had shielded polymer and intermediate producers (Chemplast, DCW, Styrenix, Supreme). [Tribune 13 Nov 2025; Chemplast deep file]
- Takeaway: policy now leans toward **cheap inputs for downstream users**; commodity producers cannot count on protection, and a duty win (Nocil, IG) is the exception, not the rule.

## 5. Fluorochemicals: HFC phase-down and refrigerant quotas
- **Kigali schedules.** China (Article 5 Group 1) froze HFC consumption in 2024 at its 2020–22 baseline and must cut to 90% from 2029, 70% from 2035. Developed countries are at 60% of baseline in 2024–28 and 30% from 2029. [UNEP Kigali text] **India (Group 2)** freezes at its 2024–26 baseline from **1 Jan 2028**, then −10% by 2032, −20% by 2037, −30% by 2042, −85% by 2047; new or expanded HFC capacity must be producing by 31 Dec 2027 to count; an Apr 2026 office memorandum makes CY2027 a free year with quotas from 2028. [CARE Aug-26 via Navin deep file; Tanfac deep file]
- **China's quota**: ~8 lakh t of HFC production quota; 2026 quotas kept flat for most HFCs; R-32 and R-134a prices hit near-decade highs in mid-2026. [Stallion deep file; CHEAA/search summaries]
- **Indian R-32 capacity race:** India's R-32 demand is ~20,000–22,000 t, but announced capacity is 70,000–90,000 t (SRF, GFL, Navin +15,000 t in Q3 FY27, Tanfac, Stallion 10,000 t, Chemplast) — a **2–4× domestic oversupply** unless exports absorb it. [Stallion Q1 call via deep file; Navin deep file]
- Incumbents with HF integration and historic R-22 production hold the quota moat — "your right to win comes from the quota you already hold" (Navin); GFL is adding R-134a to use its full entitlement. New entrants without captive AHF (Stallion) or with undecided quotas (Tanfac) carry binary risk. [Navin, GFL, Stallion, Tanfac deep files]
- Navin's view: over a decade R-32 demand doubles while quota-driven supply "shrinks to half". R-32 prices in India have already eased to ~₹800/kg from a ₹900/kg peak. [Navin Q1 call; Stallion Q1 call]
- **PFAS overhang:** ECHA's committees (26 Mar 2026) backed an EU-wide class restriction on 10,000+ PFAS with time-limited derogations (up to 12 years); SEAC's final opinion is due by end-2026. Fluoropolymer makers (GFL, Navin) face long-term EU demand risk. [Arnold & Porter, Mar 2026]

## 6. Export markets: US tariffs, China+1, EU FTA, REACH/CBAM
- **US:** after the Aug 2025 50% penalty tariff, the **6 Feb 2026 joint statement** set an 18% reciprocal tariff on Indian goods including organic chemicals, plastics and rubber, and removed the extra 25%; generic pharma, gems and aircraft parts are to go to zero on conclusion of the deal. [White House, Feb 2026] Managements cite a restored level field vs China (Fairchem, Excel, PCBL). [Co. calls]
- **China+1 is real but slow:** CDMO and specialty wins are arriving (Navin CDMO, Anupam LOIs, Yasho MNC contract, Excel's two contracts, Fairchem "7–8 big customers" enquiring), but qualification takes 1–5 years and volumes are small relative to capacity. [Co. deep files]
- **EU:** the EU–India FTA was concluded on **27 Jan 2026**; EU tariffs on Indian chemicals of up to 22% are "largely removed at entry into force" (not yet in force). [European Commission, Jan 2026]
- **CBAM:** financial obligations began 1 Jan 2026 for ammonia/fertilisers, hydrogen, steel, aluminium, cement, electricity; chemicals are not yet covered — the Commission is to report on extending scope (incl. chemicals) in 2027. Exposure today is limited to ammonia/fertiliser exporters (Deepak Fertilisers, GNFC). [search summaries of EU/Coolset; ICRA]
- **REACH:** beyond PFAS, EU registration costs and SVHC listings remain a hurdle for small exporters; no Indian company in this list flagged a REACH loss on its calls.

## 7. Input costs
- **Crude-linked inputs** are ~one-third of the specialty sector's raw-material cost (Crisil); Brent $97 in Q1 FY27 vs $78 in Q4 FY26 (PCBL). Muted exports and "limited passthrough of higher crude-linked input costs" drive Crisil's margin cut to 14–14.5% in FY27 from ~16%. [Crisil Jul-26; PCBL Q1 call]
- **Benzene / aromatics:** benzene, sulphur, methanol and aniline spiked in Q1 FY27, hurting dye and agro buyers (Aarti); phenol margins widened as imports stopped (Deepak Nitrite). [Aarti, Deepak Nitrite deep files]
- **Ammonia / gas:** urea-pool gas ~$19/mmbtu (Apr 2026) vs $13 pre-crisis (ICRA); ammonia spikes gave amines and nitric-acid chains a windfall (Alkyl calls it temporary; Deepak Fertilisers benefits from its Equinor LNG contract, first cargo May 2026). Sulphur and ammonia also squeezed P&K fertiliser makers. [ICRA Apr-26; Alkyl, Deepak Fert deep files]
- **Coking coal / coal tar / CBFS:** carbon-black feedstock tracks crude; Russian CBFS exports shrank, tightening supply (PCBL). Himadri's coal-tar integration (600,000 t distillation) is the low-cost position in pitch. [PCBL, Himadri deep files]
- **Metals and minerals:** zinc/molybdenum water-treatment inputs at 120%+ of normal (Chembond); yellow phosphorus and sulphur (Excel); pinene +70–80% (Oriental Aromatics); vegetable-oil by-products (Fairchem) — small formulators with PSU or annual contracts absorb a lag. [Co. deep files]
- **Soda ash / salt:** global oversupply; Chinese and Turkish natural ash sets price; GHCL profits more than halved since FY23. [Tata Chem, GHCL deep files]

## 8. Capex cycle and asset turns
- Crisil: specialty-chemical capex is being cut to **~₹16,500 cr in FY27**, focused on backward integration, import substitution and niche chemistries; in FY23–FY24 it ran at ~₹22,000 cr a year, ~50% above pre-pandemic. [Crisil Jul-26; Crisil Jul-23]
- **Asset turns have collapsed** from the FY21–22 norm of 2–3x: Aarti ~1.0x (ROCE <7%), Anupam ~0.6x, Clean Science 1.1x (from 3x+ in FY21; HALS ~0.3x), Tatva 0.8x, Archean 0.8x, GFL ~0.75x incl. CWIP, Ellen 0.75x, Tata Chemicals 0.7x. Exceptions are asset-light or old depreciated plants: DDev ~10x, BASF ~20x (traded), Galaxy ~4.5x, Fineotex ~3.6x, Supreme ~3.4x. [Screener via deep files]
- **New projects promise 1.3–2.5x** but ramp slowly: GFL EV materials 2x target by FY29 on ₹6,000 cr; Tanfac R-32 ~2.4x; Alkyl new product ~1.5x; Epigral CT platform ~1.3x; GHCL greenfield soda ash only ~0.3–0.5x; GNFC new plants ~0.5x revenue/capex. [Co. deep files]
- **Big-ticket bets now under way:** Deepak Nitrite ₹11,500 cr polycarbonate (debt could rise to ~₹8,500 cr), GFL ~₹3,100 cr FY27 capex, GHCL ₹4,000–5,000 cr greenfield, Archean ₹2,067 cr semiconductor project, Neogen battery chemicals, Himadri LFP. These raise leverage just as margins peak. [Co. deep files]

## 9. Balance sheets and credit
- Crisil expects sector debt/EBITDA to rise to **~2.2x in FY27 from 1.9x**, and interest cover to fall to ~6x from ~7.5x. [Crisil Jul-26]
- **ICRA's outlook on fertiliser, basic chemicals and petrochemicals is Negative** (elevated input prices, global oversupply for basic chemicals). [ICRA Apr-26]
- Stress is concentrated in leveraged mid-caps: Aarti (net debt 3.65x, Crisil negative outlook), Chemplast (interest cover <1x, Crisil negative), PCBL (~5x, CARE negative), Oriental Aromatics (ICRA negative), Camlin (BBB-), Tata Chemicals (~3.4x). Net-cash names: Alkyl, Balaji, GHCL (~₹1,000 cr), Supreme, Navin (post-QIP), Chembond, Galaxy. [Co. deep files]

## 10. Competitive structure and where profit sits
- **Highest and most durable margins:** fluorine chain and CDMO (Navin ~33–34% EBITDA, Anupam standalone ~34%, Privi ~25%, Clean Science legacy), industrial gases (Ellen ~38%), bromine when priced (Archean). These depend on approvals, quotas or scarce resources. [Co. deep files]
- **Mid-teens, steady:** formulators and ingredient houses (Galaxy ₹19–22k/t, Chembond 13–15%, Rossari ~11–12%, SH Kelkar ~13%, Fineotex ~16%, legacy Sudarshan ~15%) — service and approvals, low capital. [Co. deep files]
- **Cyclical spreads:** phenol, PS/ABS, PVC, soda ash, PAN, amines, ethyl acetate, carbon black — normal margins 5–12%, war-quarter spikes to 20%+, set by China/import parity. [Co. deep files]
- Market leaders hold >50% domestic share in several niches (Supreme >50% PS/EPS, Deepak Nitrite >50% phenol, IG ~half of PAN, Excel 50–55% phosphonates, Chembond ~60–65% fertiliser-plant water treatment), but share alone has not given pricing power where China exports freely.

## 11. Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecaster named)
- **FY27:** Crisil — revenue +~6%, margins −150–200 bps to 14–14.5%, capex ~₹16,500 cr, debt/EBITDA ~2.2x. ICRA — Negative outlook for basic chemicals/petrochemicals; war-driven price gains to fade as stockpiling unwinds. [Crisil Jul-26; ICRA Apr-26]
- **FY26–FY29:** CareEdge Research — specialty chemicals market growing 10–12% a year to ₹7,54,100 cr, faster than China (~5%). [CareEdge via RHP summary]
- **Global:** Fitch — no quick rebalancing; Chinese petrochemical capacity keeps markets oversupplied through 2026. [Fitch Dec-25]
- **Company guides (for FY28–FY29):** Sudarshan ₹12,000–14,000 cr revenue and ₹1,400–1,500 cr EBITDA by FY29; Aarti ₹1,800 cr EBITDA by FY28; Himadri ₹1,100 cr PAT by FY28; Neogen ₹2,400–2,900 cr battery revenue by FY29; GFL EV earnings by FY29. Most of these have already slipped once. [Co. deep files]
- My view: FY27 earnings peak in Q1 and fade; FY28 depends on fluoro quotas settling, CDMO ramps, and whether Chinese discipline (rebate cuts, "anti-involution") outlasts the war. Commodity names face a lower FY28 base; formulators and fluoro/CDMO leaders have the clearest growth.

## 12. Five key sector findings
1. **Q1 FY27 was a war windfall, not a new cycle.** Hormuz disruption and stockpiling lifted spreads (ICRA); Crisil still sees FY27 margins 150–200 bps *lower* than FY26, so H2 FY27 comparisons will disappoint for spread businesses.
2. **China is disciplining itself a little, India is protecting itself less.** Rebate cuts from 1 Apr 2026 lifted Chinese export prices ~9% on affected items, but India rolled back 14 QCOs (Nov 2025), waived PVC duty during the crisis, and let several ADD recommendations lapse — trade protection is unreliable.
3. **R-32 is heading for oversupply before the 2028 freeze.** Indian capacity (70–90 kt) is 2–4× domestic demand; quota allocation on the 2024–26 baseline favours integrated incumbents (Navin, GFL, SRF) over new entrants (Tanfac, Stallion, Chemplast).
4. **Capital efficiency is the sector's real problem.** Asset turns of 0.6–1.1x at Aarti, Anupam, Clean Science, Tatva, Archean, GFL versus 2–3x historically; capex is being cut to ~₹16,500 cr, but several multi-thousand-crore bets (Deepak PC, GHCL, GFL EV, Archean semis) are now committed, and Crisil sees leverage rising to 2.2x.
5. **Exports are re-opening but slowly.** US tariff at 18% (Feb 2026) and the concluded EU FTA help; China+1 wins are real but small and slow to qualify, while EU PFAS rules and future CBAM scope are long-dated risks.
