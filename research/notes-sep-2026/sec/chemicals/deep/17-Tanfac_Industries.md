# Tanfac Industries (TANFACIND) — deep research, Phase A1

Transcript check: the stored Q4 FY26 transcript (12 May 2026) is the official BSE filing — accurate. The stored Q1 FY27 transcript (27 Jul 2026) is an AI re-transcription; content broadly matches the official BSE transcript, but it has errors: **PAT is ₹16.8 cr (official), not ₹15.8 cr**; the ₹61 cr preferential investment is by **Anupam Rasayan** (official), not "promoters and the Birla family"; it gives the R-32 project cost as ₹395 cr in one place — the presentation and ICRA say **₹390 cr** (the Q4 call said ₹405 cr for R-32 within a ₹495 cr programme). Tanfac has no subsidiaries; figures are standalone.

## Business and segments
- One 60-acre integrated site at Cuddalore, Tamil Nadu; ~1,35,000 MTPA total capacity: sulphuric acid 1,00,000 MTPA (captive, with a captive power plant), hydrofluoric acid (HF) 29,700 MTPA (doubled from 14,850 in Oct-2024 for ~₹100 cr), solar-grade diluted HF (DHF) 20,000 MTPA (two 10,000 MTPA phases commissioned Jun and Oct 2025), specialty/inorganic fluorides ~5,000 MTPA. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Chemistry: fluorspar (calcium fluoride, imported from South Africa, Vietnam, Thailand, Kenya) + sulphuric acid (made from sulphur) → anhydrous HF (AHF). AHF is diluted/purified to solar-grade DHF (metal impurities below 10 parts per billion) used to etch silicon wafers; AHF + methylene chloride → HFC-32 (R-32), an air-conditioner refrigerant (plant under construction). [Q4 call; AR FY26]
- Revenue mix: HF-based products ~75–80% of revenue, of which ~35% solar-grade DHF, ~40% AHF/commercial grade, ~25% other value-added fluorides; sulphuric acid and others ~20%. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- AHF end-use: one-third solar, one-third pharma/agro/refrigerant makers, one-third steel/glass surface treatment and captive fluorides; named customers include Jindal, Piramal, Tata, Adani, Premier Energies. [Q1 call]
- Geography: ~95% domestic, ~5% exports in FY26; management expects ~50:50 once R-32 runs (about 75% of R-32 contract volume is for export, incl. Japan). [AR FY26 MD&A; Q1 call]
- Utilisation: FY26 sulphuric acid 101%, HF 95%, specialty fluorides 41%. Q1 FY27: HF 73% (FY26 lost ~₹70 cr of HF output to unplanned maintenance on the old HF-1 plant; Q1 had a sulphuric-acid plant shutdown), sulphuric acid 85–88%, specialty fluorides ~50%; solar DHF "running at full capacity", 80–85% contracted. [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26]
- Market (company, citing Frost & Sullivan): India AHF demand ~40 KT (solar ~15 KT, surface treatment 15–18 KT, agro/pharma ~10 KT); India HFC-32 ~22 KT (FY25) → ~44 KT (FY30), global ~380 KT (CY25) → ~485 KT (CY30); solar DHF demand 24–30 KT today, company says 150–210 KT by FY29–FY31 as India's solar cell capacity goes from ~36 GW to ~210 GW. [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A]

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (May 2026): existing business margin 15–18%; R-32 in Q4 FY27 adds 3–4 points; FY28 revenue ₹1,600–2,000 cr; ₹3,000–3,500 cr in five years; R-32 commissioning by end-Q3 FY27 (Oct–Nov 2026). [Q4 call]
- Q1 delivery: revenue ₹187 cr (+6%, −3% QoQ), margin 15.3% — **at the bottom** of the 15–18% range because of sulphur, power (grid power bought during acid-plant shutdown) and West Asia costs. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 call (Jul 2026): FY27 revenue growth "at least 30%" (≈₹925 cr), blended FY27 margin 21–22%, 16–19% from Q2; FY28 growth over 60% and ~25% margin; R-32 plant 60% built, machinery September, firing late November, stabilise Dec–Jan, full ramp by mid-February 2027; R-32 utilisation 65–70% in Q4 FY27, 80–85% (maybe 90%+) in FY28. Guidance was **effectively raised** (numbers now given for FY27/FY28), but the timetable **slipped slightly** (Q4 call said commissioning Oct–Nov; Q1 call says firing late Nov, stable by Jan–Feb). [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- R-32 (20,000 MTPA) project cost ₹390 cr; ₹315 cr committed and ~₹100 cr spent by Q1 FY27; capital commitments at Mar-26 ₹257.7 cr. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; AR FY26 note 29.4]
- Next ~₹300 cr from Dec-2026: solar DHF doubling (₹30–40 cr, target ~June 2027), AHF +20–30 KT (~₹120 cr), electronic-grade HF for semiconductors (~₹150 cr, technology partner not chosen; 1–1.5-year customer approval). HFOs and specialty fluoropolymers by 2029–31. Total ₹1,500–1,700 cr over four years. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Asset turns: HF doubling cost ~₹100 cr for ~15,000 MTPA; R-32 ₹390 cr for ₹900–1,000 cr revenue (≈2.4x asset turn). Screener net block ₹212 cr + CWIP ₹29 cr at Mar-26 against ₹711 cr sales (≈3x). [Q4 call; Screener]
- Funding: QIP ₹250 cr (25 Jun 2026) + ~₹100 cr preferential issue (~₹61 cr from Anupam Rasayan) + accruals/term debt. [IP Q1FY27; ICRA Jul-26]

## Moat evidence
- Only Indian producer of solar-grade DHF, approved by "almost all domestic customers"; took two years to reach <10 ppb purity; ₹50–70 cr investment for an entrant. Orders ~₹1,068 cr to FY29 (~85% of capacity each year). [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- R-32 contracts disclosed: a Japanese customer 7,500 MTPA (₹337.5 cr a year), a multinational 5,000 MTPA (₹250 cr a year; MoU to be firmed), Blue Star (₹61 cr, open-ended) — ~12,500+ MTPA, ~₹649 cr a year; aggregate long-term value ~₹3,612–3,673 cr over 5–7 years; formula-based with cost pass-through and mutual take-or-pay once production starts. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Low-cost HF: ~₹100 cr for 15,000 MTPA, called "lowest per-ton investment in the industry". [Q4 call]
- China: management says AHF imports from China are not a threat (controlled chemical, dedicated ISO tanks); US anti-dumping duty on Chinese R-32 exists and helps exports. ICRA, by contrast, says Chinese import competition "limits its pricing power" in HF. [Q1 call; ICRA Jul-26]

## Risks and red flags
- **HFC-32 quota risk (biggest)**: India will fix HFC production quotas under the Kigali Amendment; per the 1 Apr 2026 office memorandum, CY2027 is a free year and quotas apply from 1 Jan 2028 based on capacity of operational units. Analysts argued incumbents (SRF, Gujarat Fluoro, Navin) could absorb the national limit (~115–125 KT) given their HCFC baselines. Management declined to discuss it further ("This is the third time answering it") and the R-32 contracts carry **no penalty if quota is denied**. [Q4 call]
- **Pledge**: ICRA notes Anupam Rasayan had pledged its **entire ~24.3% holding** in Tanfac as of 30 Jun 2026 — not mentioned on either call. [ICRA Jul-26]
- Promoter structure: TIDCO 26.02% + Anupam Rasayan 25.80% = 51.8% (Mar-26), 48.7% after QIP (Jun-26). Anupam has management control despite the smaller stake. [AR FY26 note 12; Screener; ICRA Jul-26]
- Related parties: sales to Anupam Rasayan ₹13.2 cr in FY26 (₹17.6 cr FY25; management said ~₹9 cr/1.2% on the call); receivable from Anupam ₹13.2 cr at Mar-26. Management says there is no product overlap with the parent. [AR FY26 note 29.2; Q4 call]
- Board/management changes in FY26: a new Managing Director appointed w.e.f. 9 Jan 2026 (Afzal Malkani signs as MD), one director ceased on the same date, a director died in Apr-2025, CEO Hemango Gupta joined Jul-2025; President-Technical (Yogesh Mittal) hired Jul-2026. The AR's KMP table is garbled, so exact roles before Jan-2026 could not be confirmed. [AR FY26 note 29.2; Q1 call]
- Single site handling hazardous HF (PESO transport permit needed); sulphur spikes pass through with a 30–45 day lag. [ICRA Jul-26; Q1 call]
- Auditor: unmodified opinion, **no key audit matters**. Contingent liabilities small (GST ₹1.3 cr, service tax/VAT ₹0.1 cr; old ₹0.5 cr sales-tax dispute). Bank guarantees/LCs ₹51.8 cr. [AR FY26 auditor's report; note 29.4]
- Cash conversion is poor (CFO well below PAT for three years) and the company is about to spend ₹1,500–1,700 cr over four years — more equity or debt is likely. [Screener; Q1 call]

## My read
The existing business is decent but not accelerating: Q1 margin sat at 15% and volumes dipped, so all the growth in the story sits on the R-32 plant firing in late 2026. At over 100x trailing earnings the price already assumes R-32 runs near full and earns ~30% margins from FY28, i.e. roughly ₹1,500 cr+ revenue at 25% margin. That depends on a government quota decision in 2027 that management will not discuss and that incumbents are positioned to dominate — a binary risk. The parent's full pledge of its stake is an unexplained governance flag. The view improves if a quota is granted in 2027 and R-32 runs at 80%+ with contracted exports; it worsens on any quota denial, commissioning delay, or further equity dilution.
