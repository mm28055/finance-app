## Business and segments
- Makes atmospheric gases in air-separation units (ASUs): air is compressed, cooled to about −190°C and distilled into oxygen, nitrogen and argon (argon is ~1% of air and comes only as a by-product of oxygen). Also hydrogen (electrolyser at Uluberia), CO₂/dry ice, acetylene, nitrous oxide, medical gases and some traded specialty gases. No raw material to buy — **power is the main cost** (₹22.0 cr in Q1 FY27 = 22% of revenue), then road freight. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Three supply models: onsite ASUs at a customer's plant on 15–20-year take-or-pay contracts (3 customers); bulk/merchant liquid by tanker on 3–7-year contracts (328 customers, average 5-year tenure); packaged cylinders (1,498 customers). Q1 FY27 revenue: bulk ₹69.4 cr, onsite ₹14.0 cr, packaged ₹13.6 cr; product split oxygen 50%, nitrogen 40%, argon & mixtures 10%. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- End markets: steel about one-third of revenue; rest pharma & chemicals, dealers/retail, healthcare, railways/aviation/space, engineering. Government customers 29% of FY26 revenue (e.g., O&M of two 1,250 TPD ASUs for NMDC Steel at Nagarnar, excluded from capacity). Project-engineering revenue fell from 16% of revenue (FY24) to 2% (FY26). [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Geography: West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana (market leader in those three states); no exports. Largest customer 6.7% of revenue, top 5 25.9%, top 10 38.8%; 86% of FY26 revenue from repeat customers. [IP Q1FY27]
- Capacity: bulk 915 TPD (Uluberia-1 115, Parawada 170, Jadcherla 130, Kurnool 260, Uluberia-2 220, plus 20) and onsite 1,018 TPD including the 320 TPD East India plant (Kharagpur 204 + 154, Kurnool 340). Total 1,933 TPD; the AR plans 1,370 TPD (FY25) → 2,149 TPD by end-FY27. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- Utilisation: all legacy plants "fully utilised"; only Uluberia-2 (commissioned Q4 FY26) has spare capacity. Merchant plants take 18–24 months to reach 80–90%. [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹247 cr (FY25) → ₹184 cr (FY26, Screener incl. leases); the balance sheet shows borrowings ₹59 cr at FY26 (₹147 cr FY25). Treasury: non-current investments ₹314 cr + current ₹83 cr ≈ ₹397 cr, so the company is comfortably net cash. [Screener; IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 note 7]
- Treasury mix is unusual for an industrial company: alternative investment funds (~₹154 cr non-current), market-linked debentures, bonds, mutual funds (~₹29 cr), listed Swiggy shares (₹2.2 cr current; ₹9.7 cr at FY25), unlisted IKF Finance shares (₹8.5 cr), plus ₹55.7 cr of further commitments to AIFs. This drives the high other income. [AR FY26 notes 7, 40]
- Cash flow FY26: CFO ₹133 cr, investing −₹436 cr (capex + treasury), financing +₹305 cr (IPO less debt repayment). [Screener]
- Working capital: debtor days 68 (FY26) vs 97 (FY25); working-capital days negative (−46). No dividend for FY26 (cash retained for capex). [Screener; AR FY26]

## Moat evidence
- Local density: liquid gases travel economically only 300–400 km, so plant location decides share; Ellenbarrie leads in West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Contracts: onsite plants are 15-year take-or-pay with cost pass-through (e.g., Jai Balaji, Jai Raj Ispat, Tata Metaliks); 86–92% repeat-customer revenue FY24–FY26. [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Aug-25; News: ScanX, 26 Sep 2026]
- In-house engineering: designs and builds its own ASUs (up to 600 TPD so far, capable of 1,000+ TPD) — one of few independent Indian players able to; this lowers capex per tonne. [Q4 call]
- Cost: newer plants use less power per tonne; one 25-year wind-solar PPA (Ellenbarrie holds 26% of the power SPV for ₹7.08 cr) covers 55–60% of one 170 TPD plant; grid power is 50–60% dearer than PPA power. West Bengal plants run on grid power (open access is hard there). [Q4 call]
- Limits: Linde, Air Liquide, INOX Air Products are several times larger; entering North/West-Central India means taking customers from them — management says it will target nearby customers and new demand rather than price. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- China/anti-dumping: not relevant — gases are not traded across borders; the competition is local. [derived]

## Risks and red flags
- Treasury risk: ~₹400 cr of cash parked partly in AIFs, market-linked debentures, unlisted shares and Swiggy stock; Q4 FY26 already carried an impairment on a "legacy non-core investment", and a further ₹1.0 cr impairment on financial assets in Q1 FY27. Profit quality depends on this book. [AR FY26 note 7; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- CARO remarks: quarterly stock/receivable statements filed with banks did not agree with the books (differences of roughly ₹1–1.4 cr per quarter); title deeds of ₹28.5 cr of property pledged with banks; audit trail at database level enabled only from 29 Apr 2025 and not for payroll software; slight delays in some statutory dues. Opinion unmodified; key audit matter is revenue recognition (MSKA & Associates). [AR FY26 auditor's report & CARO]
- Disclosure: before the IPO CRISIL had the company in "Issuer Not Cooperating" status (A-/A2+), migrating to A/Stable/A1 only in Aug 2025 once information was shared. [CRISIL Aug-25]
- Contingent liabilities small: tax disputes ₹4.1 cr (service tax/excise/GST; income-tax ₹2.5 cr resolved in FY26) plus a ₹3.5 cr supplier claim in court. Promoters have given personal guarantees on loans (₹149 cr at FY26). Related-party dealings are small (₹2.6 cr purchases from Karakoram Healthcare LLP; office rent to promoter companies). [AR FY26 notes 40, 42]
- Execution: East India onsite plant slipped ~3 months (promised June, commissioned 25 Sep 2026); merchant plants are built without contracts; power is 22% of revenue with little renewable cover (~18% of capacity). [Q4 call; Q1 call; News: ScanX, 26 Sep 2026]
- Concentration: promoter holding 77.15% (low free float); argon price swings move quarterly margins by several points. [Screener; Q1 call]

## My read
The operating business is sound and growing about 15–20% a year: legacy plants are full, new capacity (Uluberia-2, East India onsite, two merchant plants) roughly doubles merchant capacity by FY28, and newer plants lift margins toward 38–40%. But roughly two-fifths of pre-tax profit is treasury income from IPO cash parked in AIFs, debentures and equities, which will shrink as capex draws the cash down, so reported EPS growth will slow even as operating profit grows. The price needs operating profit to compound near 20% with margins held near 38%; watch Uluberia-2 and East India ramp-up, any new onsite win above 600 TPD, and the treasury book (impairments, AIF commitments). A clean argon recovery and renewable PPAs would help; another investment write-down or a stall in merchant ramp-up in North India would hurt.
