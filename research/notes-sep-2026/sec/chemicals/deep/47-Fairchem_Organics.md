## Business and segments
- Turns low-value by-products of soft-oil refining (acid oil ~1–1.5% of refining waste, plus fatty-acid distillates from soya/sunflower) into dimer acid, linoleic/soya fatty acid and isostearic acid; single plant at Chekhala, Sanand (Gujarat). [AR FY26 MD&A; CareEdge Jun-26]
- Q1 FY27 revenue mix: linoleic acid 42%, dimer acid 30%, isostearic acid 4%, by-products 24%. FY26: dimer ~₹140 cr (~30%), linoleic ~30%, isostearic ₹25.9 cr (₹35.9 cr in FY25, volume −29%). Three prime products = 73.1% of FY26 value. [Q1 call; Q4 call; CareEdge Jun-26; AR FY26 MD&A]
- End uses: linoleic → alkyd resins for premium paints (only Indian maker of the superior grade); dimer → polyamides for inks, adhesives, epoxy hardeners, drilling fluids; isostearic → cosmetics and bio-lubricants (one of 3–4 global makers; first in India). Paint industry ≈40% of revenue. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q1 call]
- Customers: Asian Paints, Uniform Synthetics, Astral, Arkema India; top customer ~24% of FY26 sales (31% FY25), top 10 ~63%. [CareEdge Jun-26]
- Geography: domestic 91% in FY26; exports 7–8% in Q1 FY27. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Capacity: 80,000 t/yr base plant at ~60% utilisation in Q1 FY27 (~44,000 t sold in FY26, i.e. ~55%). A 40,000 t "novel process" oleochemical line (first phase ~8,000 t, ₹20–25 cr) was due for trial runs in Q2 FY27. CareEdge already counts 1,20,000 t capacity at 31 Mar 2026. Tocopherol/sterol plant idle (no volume in FY26). [Q1 call; Q4 call; CareEdge Jun-26; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Promoters: Fairfax India (via FIH Mauritius 54.61% and FIH Private Investments 2.57%) holds 57.18%; the Jariwala family runs the business. [CareEdge Jun-26]

- Quarterly (Screener, ₹ cr — sales / operating profit / OPM / net profit): Jun-25 131 / 5 / 4.0% / 1; Sep-25 112 / 4 / 3.8% / 1; Dec-25 100 / 4 / 4.2% / −0; Mar-26 117 / 8 / 7% / 4; **Jun-26 176 / 18 / 10% / 10**. [Screener]

- Volumes: FY23 54,000 t, FY24 63,000 t, FY25 54,000 t, FY26 44,000 t; Q1 FY26 13,000 t → Q1 FY27 13,500 t (+4%). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Unit economics: Q1 FY27 realisation ≈ ₹130/kg (₹176 cr / 13,500 t), up ~25% QoQ and ~30% YoY (company); EBITDA ≈ ₹13/kg vs ≈ ₹4/kg a year ago. FY26 average ≈ ₹105/kg and ≈ ₹5/kg EBITDA. [Q1 call; derived]
- Returns: Screener ROCE 3.3%, ROE 2.2% (TTM). Company: FY24 revenue ₹621 cr at 10.8% EBITDA margin; FY26 ₹460 cr at 4.7%. [Screener; IP Q1FY27]

## Guidance history
- **Q4 FY26 call (May 2026):** FY27 EBITDA margin **8%**; utilisation "around 80%" of 80,000 t (elsewhere 75–80%); exports to ~20% of sales in FY27; bypass-fat plant live in June; new 40,000 t line to start by end-Q2 with revenue only after 2–2.5 years (theoretical ₹800–1,000 cr at full use in ~5 years); no FY27 capex beyond energy savings; working capital 100–120 days; ₹2,000 cr revenue "vision" by FY30. [Q4 call]
- **Q1 FY27 delivery:** margin 10.1% (**beat**); utilisation ~60% (behind a 75–80% full-year pace); exports 7–8% (**behind** 20%); volume +4% YoY. [Q1 call]
- **Q1 FY27 call (Jul 2026):** margin now "we will be able to maintain" ~10% (**raised in effect**); utilisation exit target **cut** to 70–75% (from ~80% average); volume +5–7% a quarter (15–20% by year-end); isostearic "positive outcome" by end-FY27; the Q1 presentation still states exports "initially 25% and eventually 50% of sales" — larger than the call's 20%. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Track record: two washed-out years (FY25–FY26); management blamed Chinese dumping and a raw-material duty jump (5.5% → 27.5% → 16.5%) while finished dimer attracts 7.5% duty. Isostearic ramp has slipped every year since launch (FY25). [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Capacity grew from 10,000 t (2011) to 120,000 t (2026) largely from internal accruals. [CareEdge Jun-26; IP Q1FY27]
- FY26 investing outflow ₹14 cr; CWIP ₹18 cr (FY25 ₹12 cr). Capital commitments fell from ₹3.4 cr to ₹0.34 cr (i.e. the new line's spending is done). [Screener; AR FY26 note 41]
- Management: "current business does not require any capex"; FY28 capex depends on whether the new product sells. [Q4 call]
- Energy audit cut power use >30% and solid fuel >35% (power & fuel fell to ~₹6 cr in Q4 FY26 from ~₹8 cr). [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings rose from ₹7 cr (FY24) to ₹63 cr (FY25) and ₹84 cr (FY26), all working-capital loans; nil term debt. Gearing 0.32x (0.21x). [Screener; CareEdge Jun-26]
- **Buyback while borrowing:** 4.25 lakh shares at ₹800 (₹34 cr, 3.26% of equity), Jan 2026, in a year with ₹6 cr PAT; reserves fell ₹289 → ₹250 cr. Promoter holding rose from 61.2% to 63.2%, i.e. promoters did not tender in proportion. [AR FY26 Directors' report; Q4 call; Screener]
- CFO ₹74 cr (FY24), −₹17 cr (FY25), ₹42 cr (FY26). Inventory days 57 → 87 → 96 (Screener); CareEdge puts the operating cycle at 128 days in FY26 (99 days FY25) — above its own **120-day downgrade trigger**. [Screener; CareEdge Jun-26]
- Dividend cut from ₹7.5 to ₹1/share for FY26 ("temporary earnings loss"). Yield 0.15%. [Q4 call; Screener]
- CareEdge A/Stable, A1 reaffirmed 29 Jun 2026; notes negative sensitivities "partially triggered" but kept the rating because of Q4 recovery, strong liquidity (27% average limit use) and Fairfax support. [CareEdge Jun-26]

## Moat evidence
- Raw-material access: uses refinery waste that is not traded internationally (freight), available only in small quantities; the MD argues there is room for "one company" in India. CareEdge calls cost advantage and procurement network a barrier to entry. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call; CareEdge Jun-26]
- Share: leading Indian dimer and linoleic producer; the only Indian maker of premium-paint linoleic acid and of isostearic acid. Chinese dimer imports had run at 800–1,000 t a month — about equal to Fairchem's own domestic sales — showing the moat does not stop imports. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call]
- Pricing power is weak and borrowed: management says Q1 realisations rose because Chinese dumping eased (export incentives of up to 15% withdrawn) and the West Asia war cut imports; "if dumping starts again, we cannot do anything about it". [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Contracts: no long-term contracts (monthly buy-sell on both sides); the MD called long-term fixed-price deals "hara-kiri". [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- Earnings depend on China and duty policy: inverted duty (16.5% on raw material vs 7.5% on finished dimer) costs ~9 points of margin by management's estimate; the company did not seek anti-dumping protection ("we are too small"). [Q1 call]
- Customer concentration: top customer ~24%, paint ~40%. [CareEdge Jun-26; Q1 call]
- Management pay: CMD remuneration ₹2.67 cr in FY26 (₹3.02 cr FY25) — about 43% of FY26 PAT; ratio to median employee 41.8:1. CFO/CS Rajen Jhaveri left Nov 2025. [AR FY26 note on related parties; Directors' report]
- Disclosure: R&D spend shown blank in the annual report (per an analyst on the call; CFO said ~1–1.5% and promised to check); new-product identity undisclosed; capacity figures differ between CareEdge (1,20,000 t) and management (80,000 t operating). [Q1 call; CareEdge Jun-26]
- Auditor (KPMG-affiliated per IP) opinion unmodified; key audit matter is revenue timing "due to pressure to achieve performance targets". Contingent liabilities small (₹0.6 cr; income-tax disputes fell from ₹0.28 cr to ₹0.11 cr). No material related-party transactions beyond dividends to promoters and pay. [AR FY26 auditor's report; notes 40–41]

## What changed between the two calls (Q4 FY26 → Q1 FY27)
- Numbers: margin 6.8% → 10.1%; realisation +25% QoQ; revenue +50% QoQ on similar volume.
- Tone: from "worst is over, 8% margin" to "happy with realisation; will maintain"; but volume targets were trimmed (utilisation exit 70–75%).
- New: new raw material/product to be introduced "next quarter"; 7–8 large customers enquiring on supply diversification; bypass fat commissioned; still no isostearic breakthrough (approvals at step 4–5 of 5 at a few buyers).

## My read
Fairchem is a small, cyclical spread business whose Q1 FY27 recovery came almost entirely from price (≈+30% realisation) rather than volume (+4%), and management itself credits Chinese export-incentive cuts and the West Asia war. The balance sheet is sound and Fairfax stands behind it, but a debt-funded buyback in a ₹6 cr-profit year, a 128-day operating cycle and a CMD paid ~43% of profit are governance negatives. The stock at ~56x trailing earnings already assumes the 10% margin sticks and volumes rise ~20%. The view improves if Q2–Q3 hold ~10% margins with utilisation rising toward 70–75% and isostearic/new-line orders appear; it breaks if Chinese dimer imports return or vegetable-oil costs spike.
