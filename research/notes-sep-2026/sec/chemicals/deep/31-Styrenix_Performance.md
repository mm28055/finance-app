# Styrenix Performance Materials (STYRENIX) — deep research, Phase A1

Transcript check: both stored transcripts (Q1 FY27, 5 Aug 2026; Q4 FY26, 16 May 2026) were compared with the official BSE filings. Substance matches, but there are errors in the stored copies: (1) stored Q1 says consolidated volume was **50.3 KT**; the official transcript and presentation say **50.8 KT**; (2) the stored Q1 copy attributes most answers to "Bhupesh Porwal" or "Management" — in the official text the answers are from **Rahul Agrawal (MD)**; Porwal (CFO) only read the opening numbers; (3) the stored Q4 copy says customers feared losing the "Nouryon brand" — the official text says the **"Novodur" brand** (INEOS Styrolution's ABS brand; Nouryon is an unrelated company); (4) the CFO's name is spelt "Purbwal" in the stored Q4 copy.

## Business and segments
- Makes styrenic engineering plastics: **ABS** (acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene; brand Absolac) for car interiors, two-wheeler parts, helmets, appliance housings, AC parts; **SAN** (styrene-acrylonitrile; Absolan), mostly used in-house to make ABS, 15,000–20,000 t/yr sold outside; **polystyrene** (GPPS clear grade and HIPS impact grade) for fridge liners, AC/TV housings, packaging, toys; plus small **blends** (Styroloy) and **ASA** (Asalac). [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Chemistry: ABS is made by grafting styrene and acrylonitrile onto polybutadiene rubber (Styrenix makes its own rubber at Nandesari, 27,000 TPA). Styrene monomer (SM) and acrylonitrile (ACN) are imported (~83% of raw material needs were imported in FY25); butadiene is bought locally. It is a **spread business**: profit = product price minus monomer cost. [CARE Oct-25; Q1 call]
- India capacity: ABS 100,000 TPA (Moxi), SAN 100,000 TPA (Katol, mostly captive), PS 100,000 TPA (Dahej), rubber 27,000 TPA. Thailand (Map Ta Phut, bought from INEOS Styrolution in Jan 2025 for ~US$21.9 m): ABS 85,000 TPA, SAN 100,000 TPA, HRG rubber 31,000 TPA. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Utilisation: India ABS and HIPS near full; the PS line was expanded from 66,000 to 100,000 TPA but the extra was mostly GPPS, which "remained below installed capacity" due to imports and weak demand. Thailand ABS ran at ~43% utilisation (CARE, mid-2025); break-even needs 60–70%. [AR FY26 MD&A; CARE Oct-25; Q4 call]
- Customers: 600+; no customer above 8–10% of sales. OEMs are ~70% of ABS sales and ~50% of PS; more than 70% of all business is on formula pricing linked to published monomer prices, usually reset monthly. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Only one reportable segment ("Engineering Polymers"); no product split is disclosed — management refuses a volume split by product "because none of our competitors do that". [AR FY26 segment note; Q4 call]
- Geography: India is the core; Thailand sells to China, Japan, Vietnam, Korea, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand itself; sales offices in Shanghai, Vietnam, Seoul, Osaka. Export share of the Indian unit is not disclosed. [Q1 call]
- Market: India ABS demand ~360 KT in FY26, expected to grow 7–8% a year to FY30; India PS demand 280 KT, 5–6% growth. Indian producers sold ~160–170 KT of ABS; ~45–50% of demand is imported, mostly from Taiwan and Korea (~87% of FY25 ABS imports). A third domestic ABS plant (70 KT; CARE does not name the owner) started in Sep 2025; the two existing domestic players named by analysts are Styrenix and Bhansali. [CARE Oct-25; Q4 call] [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call; CARE Oct-25]

## Guidance history
- The company gives **no volume, revenue or margin guidance** as policy. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Q4 call (May 2026): ABS Phase 1 (+50 KT) "in the second half… third and fourth quarter"; no cost escalation; normal-year India ABS growth 8–10% with Styrenix growing "similarly, if not higher"; spot-market gains called "opportunistic" and "not sustainable long term"; Thailand break-even at 60–70% utilisation unchanged; solar power expected "by next month". [Q4 call]
- Q1 delivery: India volume −26% (demand fell in the non-OEM trade as prices spiked); margin far above normal. ABS Phase 1 wording softened to "during this financial year" — MD refused to say Q3 or Q4 ("month 8, month 9, or month 11"). CARE's rationale had said commercial operations by **December 2026**. Solar-power savings were not mentioned on the Q1 call. [Q1 call; CARE Oct-25]
- On margins, management told analysts to model a return to pre-war levels ("assume that things normalize to the same levels"); the "10–12%" figure in the stored analysis was the analyst's number, which management did not contradict. Thailand margin improvement targeted within ~3 years "if not sooner". [Q1 call]
- Verdict: capacity timing **slipped slightly in wording**; volume **missed** (event-driven); margin **beat** but flagged as one-off.

## Capex and capacity
- Plan: ₹650–700 cr over three years (CARE) to take ABS from 100 to 150 KTPA in Phase 1, then a second phase ("essentially doubling at least our ABS and SAN output" in 2–3 years). SAN, rubber and compounding are being expanded alongside, so merchant SAN sales continue. [CARE Oct-25; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Polystyrene expansion is on hold: the extra GPPS capacity is under-used and a HIPS plant is still being studied. [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Progress: CWIP ₹105 cr at Mar-26 (₹29 cr a year earlier); capital commitments ₹104 cr (₹36 cr). Funding: internal cash plus "need-based" debt. [Screener; AR FY26 note 38]
- Asset turns: consolidated sales ₹3,438 cr on net fixed assets ₹962 cr ≈ 3.6x (FY26). Before Thailand (FY24) ≈ 7.5x on ₹295 cr. At ~₹130–140/kg normal realisation, 50 KT would add roughly ₹650–700 cr of sales a year, i.e. asset turns near 2x on the Phase-1 spend (my estimate). [Screener; derived]
- The Thailand plant was bought far below book: the final purchase-price allocation booked a **bargain purchase gain of ₹367.6 cr** (to capital reserve) on a ~US$22 m price, after raising fair values of buildings and plant — this lifts depreciation and was a key audit matter. [AR FY26 auditor's report KAM 3]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings (AR note): ₹309 cr at Mar-26 (₹206 cr) — mainly the HSBC Thailand acquisition loan ₹222 cr (THOR + 1.8%, 18 quarterly instalments Apr-2026 to Jul-2030), working-capital loans ₹48 cr, and an ₹10 cr ECB owed to INEOS due 31 Aug 2026. Screener shows ₹548 cr because it adds Thai lease liabilities (~₹191 cr at Mar-25 per CARE). Parent guarantees of ~₹222 cr and ~₹166 cr back the Thai loans. [AR FY26 borrowings note; CARE Oct-25; Screener]
- Cash flow (Screener): CFO ₹216 cr (FY24), ₹54 cr (FY25), ₹183 cr (FY26); FCF ₹178 cr, −₹435 cr (acquisition year), ₹26 cr. [Screener]
- Working capital (Screener consolidated): debtor days 46, inventory days 98, payable days 62, cash cycle 81 days in FY26 (58 in FY24) — Thailand carries more stock. Management held more raw material in Q1 FY27 because Middle East styrene is now 10–15 days further away. [Screener; Q1 call]
- Dividends: ₹54/share for FY26 (payout 52%); FY24 payout 100%. Policy: cash not needed for growth "is returned to the shareholders". [AR FY26; Screener; Q1 call]
- Rating: **CARE A+ (Positive) / A1+**, Oct 2025, on ₹650 cr facilities; downgrade triggers are margin below 9% or gearing above 1x. [CARE Oct-25]

## Moat evidence
- Oldest and largest Indian ABS/SAN maker (legacy of BASF/INEOS Styrolution technology, 53 years). CARE calls it the domestic market leader in ABS and SAN. [CARE Oct-25; AR FY26 MD&A]
- OEM specification: auto Tier-1 moulders buy direct and the OEM "specifies the grade of our product… and the share of business". Validation takes 12–24 months in Asia. [Q1 call]
- Pricing: formula pricing on >70% of volume passes monomer moves through within a month; in Q1 FY27 short supply let it lift prices faster than costs. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Import parity: India still imports ~200 KT of ABS; Korean suppliers were hit by the Hormuz disruption, and Styrenix avoided declaring force majeure. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Weakness: GPPS is a commodity where it has "not wanted to participate" at import prices; no backward integration into styrene (management argues that is a plus). [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Spread volatility**: FY26 had a 4.8% quarter and a 22% quarter; management itself calls Q1 FY27 "a snapshot in time". [Screener; Q1 call]
- **China/import competition**: ABS/PS carry no anti-dumping duty in India (none mentioned on either call or in the AR); GPPS volumes lost to imports; a new 70 KT domestic ABS rival from Sep 2025 adds competition. [Q4 call; CARE Oct-25]
- **Thailand**: 18 months in, volumes flat (~12–15 KT a quarter), higher cost base, brand switch from Novodur to Absolac; now carrying bank debt repayable to 2030 with parent guarantees. [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26]
- **Promoter selling**: promoter holding fell from 62.73% (Mar-24) to **46.24%** (Jun-24) and has been flat since; Shiva Performance Materials stopped being the holding company on 27 Jun 2024. [Screener; AR FY26 note 40]
- **Related parties**: FY26 royalty of ₹2.62 cr paid to the promoter company Shiva Performance Materials (₹1.77 cr in FY25); sales of goods to it ₹24.75 cr; purchases ₹5.48 cr; services from promoter-linked SES Engineering ₹5.80 cr (₹1.78 cr); dividend ₹43.91 cr to the promoter company. Small versus sales, but the royalty to a promoter entity is worth watching. [AR FY26 note 40]
- **Contingent liabilities**: ₹49.7 cr at Mar-26 (₹58.3 cr) — income tax ₹34.0 cr (transfer pricing, disallowances), excise/service tax ₹10.6 cr. New income-tax demand of ₹1.93 cr for AY 2023-24 received 24 Sep 2026; company will appeal. [AR FY26 note 37; BSE filing 25-Sep-26]
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; key audit matters were tax litigation, the ₹2.78 cr labour-code charge (exceptional) and the Thailand bargain-purchase accounting. CARO reported no defaults or fraud. [AR FY26 auditor's report]
- Disclosure gaps: no product, OEM or export split; no quarterly Thailand P&L on the call.

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "opportunistic" gains and business-as-usual (Q4) to defensive (Q1): non-OEM buyers stopped buying, India volume −26%, appliances hit harder than autos.
- Margin jumped further (standalone 19.2% → 26.1%) while management repeated that it is not sustainable.
- ABS Phase-1 timing moved from "second half… Q3 and Q4" to "this financial year"; brownfield work in a running plant cited.
- New: styrene supply rerouted away from the Strait of Hormuz; government cut natural gas to the plant; imports "not quoting aggressively". Dropped: solar power saving.

## My read
The Indian business is a well-run, cash-generating ABS franchise with normal EBITDA of roughly ₹19/kg on ~195 KT, and the Q1 FY27 profit (≈ ₹52/kg) is a war windfall that management itself tells investors to ignore. The real value drivers are the 50 KT ABS expansion (worth ~25% more volume from late FY27) and whether Thailand can reach 60–70% utilisation; both are behind the original timing. At the current price the market is paying for normal earnings plus some expansion, not for the windfall — so the stock needs Phase 1 to start by early 2027 and Thailand to stop diluting margins. A clean commissioning and Thai volumes above ~18 KT a quarter would improve the view; another delay, or GPPS-style import pressure spreading into ABS, would worsen it.
