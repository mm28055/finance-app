# IG Petrochemicals (IGPL) — deep research, Phase A1

Transcript check: both stored transcripts (Q1 FY27, 7 Aug 2026; Q4 FY26, 21 May 2026) were compared with the official SGA/company transcripts (linked from Screener; "edited for factual errors", audio on the exchange prevails). Substance matches, but the stored copies have several factual slips — listed under **Stored-data errors**. Important: all company-quoted figures (revenue ₹625 cr, EBITDA ₹120 cr, PAT ₹71 cr) are **standalone and include other income**; Screener consolidated figures are lower (see Key numbers).

## Business and segments
- Chemistry: orthoxylene (OX, a crude/naphtha → reformate → mixed-xylene derivative) is oxidised with air over a vanadium/titanium catalyst (Von Heyden fixed-bed process) into **phthalic anhydride (PAN)**, 99.8% purity. The reaction heat is recovered as steam; 85–90% of the plant's energy is waste heat. **Maleic anhydride (MAN)** and **benzoic acid** are recovered from PAN wash water, so they carry almost no raw-material cost. DEP (diethyl phthalate) is made on site from PAN (0.7 t PAN per t DEP). [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Capacity: five PAN plants, **2,75,110 tpa** at one site in Taloja (Maharashtra) — India's largest and the world's second-largest PAN producer; four MAN plants, one benzoic acid, one DEP (debottlenecked from ~8,400 t to ~12,000 t in March 2026) and one new **75,000 tpa plasticizer plant** (DOP, DINP, DBP, DIDP), mechanically complete March 2026. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call; Q1 call official]
- Market position: ~50% of India's PAN market; India's **only** domestic MAN maker. India PAN demand ~0.56 million t (2025), forecast 0.72 Mt by 2031 (~6.5% CAGR, as cited by the company). [AR FY26 MD&A]
- End markets (PAN as share of customer's input cost): plasticizers (35–40% of cost, the largest user), UPR resins, alkyd resins/paints (20–25% of IGPL's PAN tonnage goes to paints), CPC pigments, agrochemicals (~4%), specialty (4–5% of tonnage). 20+ industries, 120+ clients; 80–85% of sales within 150–300 km of the plant. [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Revenue split FY26: PAN ≈ ₹1,808 cr, non-PAN (MAN, benzoic, DEP) ₹146 cr of ₹1,954 cr total revenue (incl. other income). Q1 FY27 non-PAN ₹49 cr: DEP a record ₹28–29 cr, MAN ₹17–18 cr. FY26 MAN revenue ₹55–60 cr. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Exports: 10.33% of FY26 turnover (₹199 cr outside India vs ₹173 cr in FY25), mostly to the Middle East; ~10% of revenue in Q1 FY27. No customer above 10% of revenue. [AR FY26 BRSR & segment note; IP Q1FY27]
- Single reportable segment (PAN and derivatives). [AR FY26]
- Utilisation: FY26 PAN volume ~2 lakh t, flat vs FY25 (±1–2%) — about 73% of 2.75 lakh t; management put current utilisation at 65–70%, aiming for 80–90% "very soon". Q1 FY27 volume only 40,000–45,000 t (Q4: >50,000 t produced, >52,000 t sold). [Q4 call; Q1 call]

FY22 peak: sales ₹1,883 cr, OPM 22%, PAT ₹264 cr. TTM sales ₹2,073 cr, PAT ₹82 cr. 3-yr sales CAGR −6%, 3-yr profit CAGR −78%. [Screener]
- Why consolidated ≪ standalone in FY26: the UAE subsidiary IGPL International lost ₹20.9 cr (FY25: ₹3.8 cr), offset in OCI by a ₹22 cr currency-translation gain. Standalone also booked a ₹28 cr fair-value/forex loss (Euro loan mark-to-market) and ~₹20–25 cr inventory losses in 9M FY26. [AR FY26 consolidated note & other expenses; Q4 call]

- Unit economics: industry PAN–OX spread averaged $100–150/t in FY26, ~$150 in Jan–Mar 2026, **$250–300 in Q1 FY27**, ~$250 in July. IGPL earns **$100–120/t above the market spread** from yield and by-products (MAN, benzoic). Conversion + other costs ≈ $150–200/t, so Q1 EBITDA ≈ **$200/t** (company) — roughly ₹26,000/t on ~43,000 t, vs ≈ ₹5,000/t for FY26 (₹101 cr on ~2 lakh t). Gross margin 33% in Q1 vs 13% in Q4 (per call). [Q1 call; Q4 call; derived from Screener]
- Prices: international PAN $650–700/t until Jan 2026 → $1,100–1,150 in April → ~$1,000 in May; OX went from ₹70–80/kg to ₹130–140/kg. MAN $650–800 for two years → ~$1,000–1,100; MAN now trades 20–25% **below** PAN (historically 20% above) because of Chinese oversupply (2 Mt+ capacity built for BDO/PBAT). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Q1 included a ₹10–15 cr inventory gain (cheap barge stock sold at higher prices), roughly reversing March's loss. [Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (21 May 2026): FY27 PAN sales **2 lakh t ±2–3%**; plasticizer **20,000–25,000 t** ("24,000–25,000 on an annualised basis", nine months of operation) for ₹200–250 cr; DEP ~8,000 t; FY27 revenue **₹2,500 cr+** if prices stay high, ₹2,000–2,200 cr if they moderate; commercial plasticizer production "pretty soon"; CBG by June/July (official errata: Q2 FY27); two PAN plants to shut 25–30 days for catalyst change in Q3/Q4. Longer term: ₹3,000–3,200 cr revenue at normal prices once PAN is 2.2–2.3 lakh t and plasticizers 60,000–75,000 t (FY28). No margin guidance — only "$100–120 over market". [Q4 call official]
- Q1 delivery: PAN volume 40,000–45,000 t — **below** the ~50,000 t/quarter run-rate needed (blamed on April–May demand dip, container shortages and late-June dispatches); EBITDA ₹120 cr (incl. other income) — a year's worth in one quarter, **well above** any implied path. [Q1 call]
- Q1 call (7 Aug 2026): PAN 2 lakh t **kept**; plasticizer **cut** to 15,000–20,000 t for the last six months (2,000–3,000 t/month; ₹200–250 cr, also quoted as ₹250–300 cr), start slipped to end-Aug/Sep; CBG **slipped** to Oct–Dec quarter; EBITDA margin 15–16% if spread stays at $200–250; plasticizer to reach 50,000–65,000 t in FY28; PAN volume 2.3–2.4 lakh t within three years. [Q1 call official]
- Verdict: pricing beat, but volume and project timing both slipped; the plasticizer start has now moved from "soon" after March completion to ~6 months later.

## Capex and capacity
- Plasticizer plant: ₹180–200 cr capex, 75,000 t; at full use ~₹1,000 cr gross revenue (₹500–600 cr net of captive PAN at market transfer price) → **~5x asset turns**; EBITDA 10–12% (15% today), net margin 4–5% → ~₹50 cr PAT, **~4-year payback**. Consumes 30,000–35,000 t of PAN a year, lifting PAN utilisation. Adds ~₹10 cr interest + ₹8–10 cr depreciation a year. [Q1 call]
- Replacement cost of the 2.75 lakh t PAN complex ≈ ₹3,000 cr (vs market cap ₹1,827 cr). [Q4 call; Screener]
- CBG (compressed biogas) and pyrolysis-oil plants under construction (via I G Biofuels/at Raichur per BRSR; "Raigad" per call); negligible FY27 revenue; government raised CBG capital subsidy 40–50% and price 33% in Aug 2026. A small Amplus Polymer line: ~1,500 t, ₹25–28 cr revenue at peak, capex ≈ revenue. [Q1 call; Q4 call; AR FY26]
- Capital commitments ₹65.6 cr at Mar-26 (₹99 cr a year earlier); CWIP ₹173 cr; fixed assets up to ₹1,182 cr. [AR FY26 note 38; Screener]
- Funding: plasticizer financed by a ₹73.5 cr term loan (9.45%) plus accruals. [AR FY26 note 20]

- Borrowings ₹362 cr at Mar-26 (₹269 cr Mar-25) — Euro ECB largely converted/prepaid into rupee loans at 7.9–9.45%; ECB at 1.04% had been the main debt. Company net debt/equity 0.17 (FY25 0.17, FY23 0.22). The same presentation claims "a zero net debt position" — inconsistent with its own ratio chart. [Screener; AR FY26 note 20; IP Q1FY27]

- Working capital: debtor days 59 → 47 → 66; inventory days 42 → 64 → 50; payable days 79 → 98 → 94; cash cycle 21 → 13 → 23 days. Domestic credit 30–60 days; import OX transit stretched from 10–20 to 40–50 days (Hormuz/port congestion), export cycles longer. OX stock kept at 5,000–10,000 t (15–20 days). [Screener; Q1 call]
- Treasury: surplus parked in InvITs (IndiGrid, Maple, Vertis, Knowledge Realty, Capital Infra), Embassy Property Developments debentures, NBFC bonds/MLDs and a Mercedes-Benz Financial NCRPS — credit-risk instruments, not just bank deposits. [AR FY26 notes 5, 11]
- Dividend ₹5/share for FY26 (≈₹15 cr, ~66% of standalone PAT). [Q4 call]

## Moat evidence
- Cost: IGPL makes $100–120/t more than the market spread through yield and free by-products; 85–90% of energy from waste heat; single-site scale. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Logistics: 80–85% of customers within 150–300 km; just-in-time supply vs 30–40 days for imports; landed import parity only 3–5% different. [Q1 call]
- Share: ~50% of Indian PAN; sole MAN producer; imports fell from 1–1.2 lakh t to 30,000–40,000 t a year. [AR FY26; Q4 call]
- Trade protection: anti-dumping duty on PAN from China ($40/t) and Korea ($140/t) **extended five years** by Notification 20/2026-Customs (ADD), 5 Aug 2026, after DGTR's sunset review (final findings 7 May 2026). BIS quality rules keep out naphthalene-route Chinese PAN. Management itself says the duty is small at $1,000+ prices. [Q1 call; News: CAclubindia, Aug 2026]
- Weakness: PAN, MAN and plasticizers are commodities; quality is identical across producers (management's own words). India is "slightly oversupplied" after 2–3 new capacities (incl. a peer's large Dahej PAN project); balance expected ~FY29. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Spread risk**: Q1 profit rests on a $250–300 spread driven by the West Asia war; management expects $200–250 over time and said a US–Iran settlement could normalise spreads in 3–6 months. Every $50/t on ~2 lakh t ≈ ₹85 cr of EBITDA (at ~₹85/$) — close to the whole of FY26 EBITDA. [Q1 call; derived]
- **UAE subsidiary**: IGPL International (Dubai-incorporated, "yet to commence operations", activities "relate to investments") holds ₹107.9 cr of parent equity plus ₹50.1 cr loans (₹158 cr, 7% of assets); lost ₹20.9 cr in FY26. The auditor made its carrying value a **key audit matter**. What it invests in is not explained. [AR FY26 AOC-1, KAM, note 40]
- **Related parties**: sales to promoter-controlled Mysore Petro Chemicals / Bluesky Petrochem ₹49.9 cr (FY25 ₹25.4 cr); ₹14.5 cr of PP&E bought from them in FY25. BRSR states related-party purchases were 55.16% of total purchases (44.56% FY25), which contradicts note 40 (nil purchases) — a disclosure error to query. [AR FY26 note 40; BRSR]
- **Contingent liabilities** ≈ ₹100 cr: income tax ₹42.7 cr, Maharashtra electricity duty on captive power ₹40.3 cr (stayed by Bombay HC), excise ₹14.2 cr, service tax ₹3.0 cr, GST ₹0.3 cr — about 5–6% of market cap. [AR FY26 CARO & note 39]
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; KAMs on subsidiary investment and revenue cut-off; audit trail maintained. [AR FY26 auditor's report]
- Treasury in InvITs/real-estate debentures adds credit risk; forex loss on Euro loan in FY26 showed weak hedging. [AR FY26]
- Chinese MAN oversupply (2 Mt+), Huntsman shut German MAN plant; MAN profits depend on China. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Volume stagnation: ~2 lakh t for two years despite a fifth plant; exports cut by container shortages. Government stopped publishing chemical import data from March 2026 — harder to track dumping. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Promoter holding 68.74%, stable; no pledge noted. Credit rating: India Ratings 'IND A+/Stable' per search snippet — the rationale page would not load, so strengths/weaknesses could not be verified. [Screener; search]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "recovery, spread $150–200" (May) to "strong quarter, $250–300" (Aug), but the CFO kept refusing margin guidance.
- Demand: May warned of a 5–10% demand dip if prices stayed high; Aug said April–May dipped and June recovered to pre-February levels (paints/alkyd strong; plasticizers, specialty weak).
- Plasticizer: from 20–25k t / "soon" to 15–20k t / end-Aug–Sep; first mention of 10–12% EBITDA margin and 4-year payback.
- New: ADD extension notified; CBG policy upgrade; freight up 30–40%, demurrage, 60–90-day import cycles.
- 10-year average spread now given as $200–250 / $200–220 (Aug) — higher than the "$100–150" FY26 average implied in May — so "normal" was redefined upward.

## My read
The core franchise is real — half of India's PAN, a durable $100–120/t cost edge and fresh five-year duty protection — but earnings are almost entirely a function of the PAN–OX spread, which swung FY26 consolidated profit to ₹2 cr and Q1 FY27 to ₹66 cr. At ₹1,827 cr market cap the price already capitalises a good part of the war-time spread; it needs spreads to hold near $200+ and the plasticizer plant to reach 50,000 t+ in FY28 to earn a normal return on ₹1,300 cr of equity. Watch the Q2 volume (does PAN get back to 50,000 t/quarter?), the first plasticizer sales and margin, and any explanation of the loss-making UAE investment arm. A collapse of spreads to the FY26 $100–150 range would reset profit toward break-even; a clean ramp of plasticizers with spreads at $200–250 would support ₹400 cr+ annual EBITDA.
