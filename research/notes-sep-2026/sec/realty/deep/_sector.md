*Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Covers the residential development cycle, with office, retail and REITs as context for the 20 listed developers in this folder. Crore = Rs 10 million; msf = million sq ft.*

## 1. Market size and growth
- **Sales value, top 7 cities:**
  - ICRA: Rs 7.7–8.2 lakh cr in FY27, a 7–9% CAGR from FY24, even though area sold is "broadly flat" over the period. [ICRA]
  - CRISIL, on a narrower definition: Rs 5.1–5.3 trillion in FY27, up from about Rs 4.6 trillion in FY25, after a 26% CAGR over FY22–FY25. [CRISIL]
- **Volume is flat; value grows on price and mix:**
  - ICRA: area sold rose only 1.5% to 653 msf in FY26.
  - CRISIL: demand volume growth of just 0–2% in FY27.
  - Growth is therefore coming from price and premiumisation, not more homes.

  [ICRA] [CRISIL]
- **Unit sales, calendar H1 2026:**
  - Knight Frank (8 cities): 171,471 units, +1% YoY, "one of the strongest first halves in over a decade".
  - Anarock (7 cities): Q2 2026 sales of about 90,715 units, **-6% YoY and -11% QoQ**, the lowest since Jan 2023.
  - PropEquity (9 cities, a different method): Q2 2026 sales **+19% YoY** to 112,458 units.

  [KF] [Anarock] [PropEquity]
- **The data sources disagree on direction.** Anarock records bookings in its 7 cities; PropEquity includes Thane and Navi Mumbai as separate markets and counts RERA-registered transactions. Both agree on the geography: southern and west-coast strength, NCR and Kolkata weakness in PropEquity's data, and Pune weakness in Anarock's. [Anarock] [PropEquity]
- **Listed developers:** the 28 major listed developers booked **Rs 39,964 cr in Q1 FY27, -21% YoY**, mainly because some large players had few launches (DLF Rs 657 cr against Rs 11,425 cr). Godrej (Rs 8,651 cr) and Lodha (Rs 4,630 cr) grew. [News: Free Press Journal/BS summary, Aug 2026]
- **Consolidation:** key listed developers' share of industry sales value rose from about 15% (FY21) to about **23% (FY26)**. [ICRA]

## 3. Unsold inventory, months of supply and launches
- **Anarock:** available inventory in the top 7 rose **10% YoY to over 6.16 lakh units** (Jun-26).
  - Bengaluru's stock jumped **34% to about 79,180 units** (41% more launches against 1% more sales).
  - NCR was the only city with flat inventory.

  [Anarock]
- **Knight Frank:** unsold stock of 525,695 units (+4% YoY); quarters-to-sell steady at **six (18 months)**, called "healthy". [KF]
- **ICRA:** years-to-sell of 1.4–1.6 by Mar-27 ("comfortable"). Launches expected +4–7% to 760–785 msf in FY27, against sales of 665–685 msf, so supply runs slightly ahead of demand. [ICRA]
- **CRISIL (more cautious):** inventory rising to **3.2–3.4 years of sales in FY27**, from under three years, meaning "supply could slightly exceed demand in the short run". [CRISIL]
- **Launches:** Anarock Q2 launches of about 1.06 lakh units (+7% YoY, -16% QoQ). Listed developers are releasing projects on the large land bought in 2025. [Anarock]
- **Company evidence:** almost every mid-cap in this folder guides to launch-heavy, H2-weighted FY27 plans. That concentrates launch and approval risk into Oct 2026–Mar 2027.
  - Ajmera: 95% of Rs 6,508 cr of launch GDV in H2.
  - Shriram: about 6 msf, mostly Q3–Q4, none with approvals at June-end.
  - Suraj: Rs 1,600 cr, 85% in H2.
  - Arkade: Thane in Q4.
  - Eldeco: four parcels awaiting approval.

  [Co. files]

## 4. Affordable versus luxury mix
- **Sales:** mid-income (34%) and luxury (46%) made up 80% of sales volumes in FY26. Luxury area sold grew 14%; affordable **fell 9%**. [ICRA]
- **New supply (Anarock Q2 2026) by price band:**

  | Price band | Share of launches |
  |---|---|
  | Rs 80 lakh – 1.5 cr | 27% |
  | Rs 1.5 – 2.5 cr | 25% |
  | Above Rs 2.5 cr | 22% |
  | Rs 40–80 lakh | 19% |
  | Affordable (below Rs 40 lakh) | 6% |

  [Anarock]
- **Luxury share of launches:** about 12% (FY22), reaching 38–40% in FY27. [CRISIL]
- **Why affordable is disappearing:** land and construction cost inflation makes sub-Rs 50 lakh homes unviable. PMAY-U 2.0 interest subsidy (up to Rs 1.8 lakh on loans up to Rs 25 lakh for homes up to Rs 35 lakh) has FY27 funding of **Rs 3,000 cr**, and 16.2 lakh houses had been sanctioned by Jul-26. [News: PMAY summaries, 2026]
- **Company evidence:**
  - Low-ticket legacy projects drag margins: Shriram (Kolkata Grand One / Sunshine One at Rs 18–24 lakh tickets); Arihant (affordable PAT about 9–10%).
  - Premium lifts them: Arvind's Orchards (49% EBITDA quarter); Ashiana's senior living (30–35% project margin); Eldeco's plotted Imperia (about 60% gross).

  [Co. files]
- **Signs of fatigue at the top end:**
  - Arihant: homes above Rs 10 cr in Mumbai are slowing.
  - Ajmera: its Versova luxury project had zero Q1 sales.
  - Shriram: the investment-led top end could "taper off" with stock-market volatility.

  [Co. files]

## 5. Land deals and joint development
- **2025 land deals (Anarock):** at least **126 deals covering 3,772+ acres**, against 133 deals and 2,514 acres in 2024.
  - 96 deals (1,877+ acres) were for residential use: plots, townships, villas.
  - H1 2025 alone saw 2,898 acres (Rs 30,885 cr, about 233 msf potential).
  - JDAs rose 150% in 2024 and kept growing.

  [News: Anarock via Construction World, Jan 2026] [News: Anarock H1 2025 summary]
- **Why it matters:** the 2025 land wave is what drives 2026–27 launches (per Anuj Puri of Anarock). It also shows up as rising debt and negative operating cash in company accounts. [Anarock]
- **Company evidence on structure:**
  - **JDA/redevelopment "asset-light"** (little upfront land cost, often a revenue share of 25–80% to landowners or societies): Raymond (Parel slum redevelopment, Rs 8,500 cr), Arvind (Goregaon MHADA, 44% share), Marathon (Versova/Sewri), Arkade (Kandivali cluster), Suraj, Keystone, Ajmera. In Mumbai, "redevelopment has emerged as a key driver of future residential supply" (Arkade).
  - **Outright big parcels:** Arkade (Filmistan Rs 335–348 cr; Thane Rs 172 cr), Eldeco (LDA auctions plus 50 acres), Ashiana (Vadgaon 28.5 acres, funded by landowner NCDs), Signatureglobal (194 acres, Farrukhnagar).
  - **Governance and cost problems in land deals:**
    - Arkade wrote off Rs 182 cr of Filmistan tenancy rights.
    - Max Estates did a related-party land swap.
    - Ganesh Housing had large related-party advances.
    - Marathon lends Rs 823 cr on demand to its promoter.

  [Co. files]
- **Redevelopment competition** is rising. Marathon warned "when the offers are very high… projects… get stuck later on". [Co. files: Marathon]

## 6. Office, retail and REITs
- **Office leasing, H1 2026:**
  - JLL: **37.9 msf gross leasing (-3.9% YoY)**, the second-best first half on record. Q2 alone was 16.45 msf, a nine-quarter low, due to "deferred decision-making".
  - Net absorption hit a decade-high 26.9 msf for a first half. **Vacancy fell to a five-year low of 14.5%**; Mumbai and NCR are at 15-year lows.

  [JLL]
- **Who is leasing:** GCCs took **15.8 msf (41.7%)** in H1, on track to beat 2025's record. Flex operators took a record 10.23 msf (28.4% of Q2). Bengaluru led with 24.6% of Q2. [JLL]
- **Knight Frank's view:** H1 leasing 48 msf (-2% on a high base). **Rents rose in all eight markets**: NCR +13%, Bengaluru +8%, Chennai/Hyderabad +7%. Bengaluru added 10.4 msf of completions (+407%) and saw rents +8% to Rs 102 per sq ft per month. [KF]
- **REITs:** six listed REITs (Embassy, Mindspace, Brookfield, Nexus Select, Knowledge Realty, Bagmane) distributed **Rs 3,136 cr in Q1 FY27**, twice the Rs 1,559 cr paid by four REITs a year earlier. They have 214 msf, gross AUM above Rs 3.17 lakh cr, and market cap above Rs 2.17 lakh cr. [News: REIT Q1 FY27 summaries, Aug 2026]
- **Implications for the covered developers:** commercial/annuity plans are spreading.
  - Existing platforms: Brigade, Sunteck (Mumbai commercial), Max Estates (office annuity).
  - New entrants: Suraj (Business Bay, 25–30% margin on bought land), Marathon (Futurex / Monte South commercial), Arihant (ITC Mementos hotel plus gymkhana, Rs 500 cr programme), Arkade and Eldeco (small commercial).
  - These plans shift capital from fast-turn housing to slow-payback assets, which raises debt.

  [Co. files]

## 7. Home-loan rates and affordability
- **RBI:** repo **5.25%**, held unanimously on 5 Aug 2026 with a neutral stance after the 2025 easing cycle. [News: RBI MPC Aug 2026 summaries]
- **Mortgage rates:** SBI's floating rates start at about **7.25%** (range 7.25–8.45%); its external benchmark lending rate is about 7.90% (repo 5.25% plus a 2.65% spread). [News: SBI rate summaries, 2026]
- **Affordability:** Knight Frank calls home affordability "stable" across six major cities in H1 2026. CRISIL, however, says "the continuous rise in housing prices" will hold demand growth to 0–2% in FY27. [News: KF affordability summary] [CRISIL]
- **Company comments:** lower rates "improved affordability, particularly within the mid-income and aspiring premium segments" (Arvind, Q4 call). Housing-finance companies report no slowdown in loan applications (Shriram, Q1 call). [Co. files]

## 8. RERA, approvals and policy
- **RERA credibility under attack:**
  - On 12 Feb 2026 the Supreme Court orally remarked that RERA seemed to "help builders" and it may be "better to abolish" it. No order was passed.
  - Reform proposals circulating include mandatory registered sale agreements within 15 days of taking a booking advance.

  [News: SCC Online, Aug 2026; search summaries]
- **War-related relief:** all Eldeco projects received a **four-month RERA extension** for Middle East war disruption (labour, commodities). [Co. files: Eldeco]
- **Approvals are the main bottleneck across the covered companies:**
  - Bengaluru e-Khata / Kaveri portal disruptions hurt FY26 launches and handovers (Shriram, Sobha, Puravankara).
  - Maharashtra RERA and plan approvals delayed Pune and Mumbai launches (Shriram's Manjari, Ajmera's Pune/Borivali, Suraj's Business Bay Phase 2).
  - Kanjurmarg leasehold-to-freehold conversion (Ajmera) and Brigade's Morgan Heights environmental clearance revocation.
  - Dahisar height limits from a wireless station (Arkade).

  [Co. files]
- **GST 2.0 (22 Sep 2025):** GST on cement cut from **28% to 18%** (steel and other items also cut), lowering construction costs by an estimated 3–5%. Materials are about 40–45% of construction cost. [News: GST cut summaries, Sep 2025]
- **State policy:** Maharashtra's 2025 housing policy (senior-living incentives) is "more of an intent" until the rules are notified (Ashiana). West Bengal's settlement with Shriram unlocked about 105–110 acres. Lucknow Development Authority auctions (Eldeco) are becoming a land source. [Co. files]
- **Enforcement actions against developers in this set:**
  - ED/PMLA: Sobha (Rs 201.6 cr attachment), Shriram (auditor emphasis of matter).
  - Income-tax searches/surveys: Brigade, Sobha, Puravankara, Raymond.
  - Benami notice: Puravankara.
  - GST show-cause notice: Sunteck.
  - These are frequent enough to treat as a sector-level governance risk.

  [Co. files]

## 9. Collections versus pre-sales
- **Sector view:** CRISIL expects **collections +22–24% in FY27** and operating cash flow +15–17%, against sales value +4–6%. That is, cash lags bookings by 2–3 years and is catching up with the FY23–FY25 sales boom. ICRA expects key developers' collections and operating cash flow up over 30% in FY27. [CRISIL] [ICRA]
- **Company evidence is mixed:**
  - **Strong:** Arvind (collections +76% in Q1; 71% collection efficiency); Eldeco (+68%); Ashiana (FY26 record Rs 1,762 cr); Keystone.
  - **Weak or lagging:** Suraj (Q1 collections -25% while pre-sales rose 74%); Signatureglobal (pre-sales and collections both down); Sri Lotus (high reported margins, poor collections); Arkade (FY26 collections +2% against pre-sales +17%).
  - **Guidance gaps:** Shriram guides FY27 collections +26–30% against sales +40–50% because launches are back-ended.

  [Co. files]
- **Why reported profit is lumpy:** most mid-caps recognise revenue on completion (Ashiana, Arvind, Eldeco, Shriram). Quarterly profit therefore swings on occupancy certificates, not on sales, as with Arvind's Rs 97 cr Q1 PAT from one building-use permission and Ashiana's Rs 107 cr Q1 revenue. [Co. files]

## 10. Developer cash flow and debt
- **Sector:** CRISIL puts debt/operating cash flow at a comfortable **1.1–1.3x in FY27** (1.2–1.4x in FY26). ICRA keeps a **Stable** outlook and expects listed leaders to outgrow the market "supported by robust collections, healthy operating cash flows and comfortable leverage". [CRISIL] [ICRA]
- **The mid-cap set is splitting in two.**
  - **Net cash or very low debt:**
    - Ashiana: about Rs 785 cr net cash.
    - Keystone: net debt/equity 0.02x, but it says net debt "is bound to go into the positive territory".
    - Arkade: net debt Rs 5 cr, though cash from operations is negative.
    - Marathon: after a Rs 900 cr QIP, but with Rs 823 cr lent to its promoter.
    - Eldeco, until recently.
  - **Leverage rising to fund land (net debt/equity):**
    - Arvind: 0.29x, rising toward Rs 800–850 cr per India Ratings.
    - Shriram: 0.29x, "may rise".
    - Ajmera: 0.47x, guided to at most 1x.
    - Suraj: net debt Rs 614 cr, rising; income tax paid late.
    - Raymond: 0.7x; cash-negative for two years.
    - Puravankara: 1.57x at 11%.
    - **Arihant: about 1.8x**, including Rs 400 cr of promoter loans at 13%+, with interest above pre-tax profit.

  [Co. files]
- **Funding cost:** mid-cap borrowing costs of 11–13% remain far above the repo rate. For example: Ajmera 11.0%, Shriram 11%, Puravankara 11%, Arihant 12.75%. AA-rated names get cheaper money: Brigade, Sobha, Keystone, and Arvind (upgraded to IND AA- in Jun-26). [Co. files]
- **Ratings in this set:**
  - AA/AA-: Brigade (ICRA AA, CRISIL AA-), Sobha (ICRA AA-), Arvind (IND AA-), Keystone (AA-).
  - A+/A: Max, Signatureglobal, Raymond, Ashiana (CARE A).
  - A-: Ajmera, Shriram (Positive outlook).
  - BBB+: Arkade (IND BBB+).
  - BBB-: Arihant (Acuite).
  - **Unrated**: Suraj, Sri Lotus, Ganesh, Marathon (rating withdrawn), Eldeco.

  [Co. files]

## 11. Demand drivers
- **Structural:**
  - Urbanisation, rising incomes, and "increasing preference for branded and organised developers".
  - Infrastructure corridors that re-rate micro-markets: Navi Mumbai airport and Atal Setu (Navi Mumbai sales +61% in PropEquity's data), Mumbai 3.0 (Panvel/Karjat), the Goregaon–Mulund Link Road, the Kanpur–Lucknow Expressway.

  [PropEquity] [Co. files: Marathon, Arihant, Eldeco]
- **GCC employment:** GCC hiring underpins Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Chennai housing as well as office demand. [JLL] [Anarock]
- **Demographics:** senior living is emerging as a niche (Ashiana: senior-living bookings from Rs 100 cr to Rs 570 cr over about five years). [Co. files: Ashiana]
- **Near-term headwinds named by Anarock and companies:**
  - The Middle East war (supply chains, fuel, NRI and buyer caution).
  - **AI-related uncertainty in IT/ITeS employment** ("pushed more buyers onto the fence").
  - Stock-market volatility hitting investor-led luxury demand.

  [Anarock] [Co. files: Shriram, Arkade]

## 12. Cost drivers
- **Construction costs:**
  - JLL: +3–5% in 2026, led by labour (+5–6%).
  - CREDAI and press: a labour shortage of about 30% of a 33 mn daily requirement.
  - Middle East war: pressure on bitumen, aluminium, petrochemicals and steel intermediates.
  - The Sep-2025 GST cut partly offsets this.

  [News: JLL costs; labour and West Asia summaries, 2026]
- **Company estimates:**
  - Ashiana: +8–10% in FY27.
  - Shriram: 8–10% pressure.
  - Raymond: 3–4% impact if prolonged.
  - Arvind: about 4%.
  - Arihant: 3–5%.
  - Sobha: margin recovery delayed.
  - Most say price rises of 4–5% on sustenance sales, or budget contingencies, absorb it.

  [Co. files]
- **Land is now the biggest swing cost:** 15–20% of GDV in suburbs, up to 50% in South Mumbai (Arkade); 10–20% for Ashiana. Redevelopment deals are getting pricier. [Co. files]

## 13. Competitive structure and where the profit sits
- **Consolidation:** listed developers hold about 23% of sales value (ICRA). The top 28 listed booked about Rs 40,000 cr in Q1 FY27, led by Godrej, Prestige and Lodha. The 20 mid/small caps here are each 0.1–1.5% of national sales and mostly single- or two-city players. [ICRA] [News: FPJ summary]
- **Where margins are highest:**
  - **Plotted and horizontal developments on old or low-cost land:** 45–60% gross margin (Eldeco, Arihant's World Villas, Ganesh, Sri Lotus).
  - **Owned, legacy land:** Raymond Thane 25–26% EBITDA; Suraj 37–40%.
  - **Premium or senior-living niches:** Ashiana 30–35% project margin.
  - **JDA/redevelopment:** lower margins (Raymond's JDAs start at single digits; Arvind's JDs sit at the low end of 22–25%) but much better ROE.
  - **Commercial built for sale on bought land:** Suraj 25–30%.
  - **Legacy affordable:** single-digit PAT (Shriram, Arihant).

  [Co. files]
- **Annuity income:** REIT-grade office is where stable profit sits (vacancy 14.5%, rents +7–13%). Only a few names in this set own meaningful rent streams (Brigade, Max, Sunteck partly). [JLL] [KF] [Co. files]

## 14. Where the cycle is now
- **Position: late-mid cycle, "normalisation" not bust.**
  - Price growth has halved (Anarock +7% vs double digits; CRISIL 3–5% for FY27).
  - Volumes are flat to down.
  - Inventory is rising (Anarock +10%, Bengaluru +34%).
  - Launches are running ahead of sales.
  - Balance sheets remain far stronger than in 2013–19 (CRISIL debt/operating cash flow about 1.2x).
  - Management teams echo this: "the price increase cycle… is stabilizing" (Arvind); "euphoria… may have gone more towards a stable… demand" (Arvind); "market has started normalizing" (Arihant).

  [Anarock] [CRISIL] [Co. files]
- **Divergence between consultants:** PropEquity (+19%) versus Anarock (-6%) for the same quarter shows the market is at a turning point, where methodology matters. Registration data (Mumbai at a 14-year August high) argues against a sharp downturn. [PropEquity] [Anarock] [News: KF Mumbai]
- **Red-flag density is rising in small caps**, a typical late-cycle sign. This set includes promoter loans (Marathon, Arihant), large related-party advances (Ganesh), write-offs (Arkade), overdue taxes (Suraj), an auditor change (Arihant), and repeated guidance cuts within a quarter (Ajmera, Arkade, Arihant, Suraj). [Co. files]

## 15. Outlook FY27–FY29 (by forecaster)
- **ICRA (Aug 2026):**
  - FY27 area sold +2–5% (665–685 msf); prices +4–7%; value +8–11%.
  - Launches +4–7%; years-to-sell 1.4–1.6 by Mar-27.
  - Listed developers' sales +15–20% with collections and operating cash flow up over 30%.
  - Outlook **Stable**.

  [ICRA]
- **CRISIL Ratings (Mar/Apr 2026):**
  - FY27 sales value +4–6% (Rs 5.1–5.3 trillion); demand volume 0–2%; prices 3–5%.
  - Collections +22–24%; operating cash flow +15–17%; debt/operating cash flow 1.1–1.3x.
  - Inventory rising to 3.2–3.4 years; luxury 38–40% of launches.

  [CRISIL]
- **Anarock (Jun 2026):** "a more balanced housing market where new supply is catching up with absorption". Growth pockets: premium, GCC hubs, infrastructure corridors. [Anarock]
- **JLL (Jul 2026):** multi-year office runway; GCCs to surpass 2025's record; vacancy falling. [JLL]
- **FY28–FY29:** no rating agency forecast was found in the sources read. Company targets are aspirational:
  - Shriram: FY28 sales Rs 5,000 cr and PBT Rs 250 cr.
  - Ashiana: cumulative PAT Rs 2,000 cr by FY30.
  - Arkade: FY28 launches of Rs 5,000 cr+.
  - Eldeco: "FY28 onwards… pivotal change".
  - These should be discounted given this cohort's record of guidance cuts.

  [Co. files]
- **My synthesis:**
  - FY27 is a **cash-harvest year**: collections outpace sales, driven by the FY23–FY25 bookings wave.
  - FY28–FY29 depend on whether 2025–26 land purchases can be launched and sold at current prices while inventory builds.
  - Outcomes will be most sensitive to IT/GCC employment, approval speed (Bengaluru, Maharashtra), and whether mortgage rates hold near 7.25–8.5%.

## 16. What to watch (sector checklist)
- **Quarterly volumes:** Anarock and PropEquity Q3 2026 (due Sep–Oct 2026); whether Bengaluru inventory keeps rising; whether NCR prices (+13–18%) hold.
- **H2 FY27 launch delivery:** the mid-caps in this set (Ajmera, Shriram, Suraj, Arkade, Eldeco, Ashiana's Aaroham Phase 3) all rely on Q3–Q4 launches.
- **Collections against CRISIL's +22–24%;** debt at mid-caps funding land and hotels (Arihant, Suraj, Arvind, Raymond).
- **RERA reform and Supreme Court proceedings;** state approval systems (e-Khata, MahaRERA).
- **Construction costs** (labour, war-linked materials) against 3–5% price growth, since margin squeeze risk is highest for fixed-price projects sold in 2023–25.
