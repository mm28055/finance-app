# Ashiana Housing Ltd (ASHIANA, BSE 523716, ISIN INE365D01021)

*Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest reported quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Crore = Rs 10 million.*

## Business and segments
- **What it is:** a residential developer in business for about 40 years. It has built over 323 lakh sq ft across Jaipur, Bhiwadi, Jodhpur, Jamshedpur, Gurugram, Pune and Chennai. Promoters (Gupta family) hold 61.11%. [CARE Dec-25] [Screener]
- **Three product lines:** senior living (active-adult communities), premium homes and "kid-centric" homes. Senior living is where capital is being shifted; it is 23% of the ongoing portfolio, and all 5 mn sq ft of future land is earmarked for it. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- **Senior living bookings:** Rs 100 cr about five years ago, Rs 570 cr in FY26 (+55% YoY). Target: more than Rs 700 cr in FY27 and about Rs 1,500 cr by FY29–30. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Land deals:** a mix of outright buys, revenue-share deals (landowner gets 65–80% share) and profit-share partnerships (for example Swarang, Chennai). There is one reportable segment. [IP Q1FY27] [AR FY26]
- **Accounting:** revenue is booked on handover (completed-contract method), so quarterly revenue is lumpy. Unit sales are 97.4% of standalone revenue; maintenance services are consolidated as an annuity. [AR FY26] [Q4 call]
- **Ongoing book:** 32 ongoing projects with 79 lakh sq ft saleable, 85% sold as of Sep-25. [CARE Dec-25]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- **Funding model:** construction is funded mostly from customer advances; management says it does not land-bank. [Q4 call]
- **Committed receivables:** over Rs 2,200 cr, covering more than 100% of remaining project cost plus debt. Liquid balances Rs 442 cr (Sep-25). [CARE Dec-25]
- **Unsold inventory:** about Rs 1,100 cr, roughly three quarters of sales. [CARE Dec-25]
- **Debt:** Rs 323 cr (Mar-26), mostly NCDs.
  - IFC holds Rs 100 cr of unsecured NCDs in Aaroham. The IFC platform has now expired, and a new one is being discussed.
  - Redemption of the ICICI Pru MF NCDs began: Rs 31.25 cr (25%).

  [Screener] [Q4 call] [Q1 call]

## Moat evidence
- **Early mover in senior living.** Management says few rivals build 12–15 acre, amenity-heavy projects; most do 2–4 acres. Sales are mostly direct: in Pune (Amodh), 40% of buyers are from Mumbai. [Q4 call]
- **Strong sales before completion:** over 60% sold across 10 launches in FY25–H1 FY26 by Sep-25; 85% of the ongoing portfolio sold. [CARE Dec-25]
- **Senior-living margins:** project-level target of 35–40% gross, 5–6% selling cost and 30–35% project margin. The blended company target is 30% gross, 12% SG&A, 18% PBT and 12–13% PAT. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Premiumisation:** blended realisation is heading to about Rs 10,000 per sq ft; Jaipur and Jamshedpur are now above Rs 7,000. [Q4 call]
- **Weak spots:**
  - Pune premium housing (Malhar) earns a low margin, and management admits it has not "figured out" that market.
  - Jaipur senior living "didn't do so well".
  - In Gurugram the brand sits at the "bottom of the top tier".

  [Q4 call]

## Input costs
- **Cost structure:** hard construction 40–50% of revenue, land 10–20%, soft costs about 5%. [Q4 call]
- **Inflation:** construction costs are expected to rise 8–10% YoY in FY27 because of war-linked inflation. Land deals are underwritten with no inflation; CPI is assumed after launch. [Q4 call]
- **Hedge:** management says locked-in prices on low-cost land give headroom, and faster execution reduces exposure. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Weak quarter:** Q1 bookings and units fell YoY on thin regular-housing stock. Revenue depends on the timing of handovers; Q1 revenue was only Rs 107 cr. [Q1 call] [Screener]
- **Low-margin handovers:** Anmol Phase 3 and Amarah Phase 1 (about Rs 532 cr) received occupancy certificates in mid-July and fall into Q2 at a blended gross margin in the mid-20s. [Q1 call]
- **Litigation:**
  - A consumer complaint converted into a class action (Oct 2025) over an old delivered project. The company has gone to the Delhi High Court to contest class-action status.
  - Rajasthan government acquisition of 15 ha at Milakpur (Bhiwadi) is stayed by the High Court.

  [Q4 call] [AR FY26]
- **Contingent liabilities:** GST/service tax Rs 19.2 cr (up from Rs 16.9 cr); income tax Rs 1.4 cr; corporate guarantees Rs 4.8 cr. [AR FY26]
- **Mining penalty:** Rs 1.58 lakh paid to the Rajasthan mines department (small, but disclosed in the BRSR). [AR FY26]
- **Oversupply:** management itself sees "signs of some oversupply" in Gurugram and Pune, with unsold stock up for two quarters in a row. [Q4 call]
- **Concentration and execution:** a big bet on one theme (senior living) and on new cities (Bengaluru, Mumbai region). The Vadgaon project will take about 10 years to sell. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- **Auditor:** unmodified opinion; the key audit matters are inventory net realisable value (inventory Rs 2,701 cr) and revenue recognition. No emphasis of matter. [AR FY26]
- **Related parties:** routine (subsidiaries, JVs, promoter entities OPG Realtors, Karma Hospitality and Woodstory). No pledges found. [AR FY26]

## My read
Ashiana is a net-cash, conservatively run developer (CARE A) that is turning into a senior-living specialist. That niche earns better margins (30–35% at project level) and faces little large-scale competition. FY27 should be a strong profit year as Rs 2,000 cr of booked value is handed over, but pre-sales momentum is soft (Q1 bookings -17%) and depends on Aaroham Phase 3 and senior-living phase launches. The FY28–FY30 story rests on converting Rs 6,500 cr of new senior-living GDV (Vadgaon, Panvel, Wavarle, Bengaluru) on time in new markets. Watch whether land spend of Rs 800–1,000 cr erodes net cash, and whether the Gurugram and Pune oversupply management itself flagged reaches pricing.
