# Shakti Pumps — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code SHAKTIPUMP. Price ₹467, market cap ≈ ₹5,761 cr, 52-week range ₹455–839 (down ~44% in a year); promoters 50.36%, no pledge shown [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].

## Business and segments
- **What it sells:** complete solar water-pumping systems for farms (pump, motor, controller/VFD, structure, installation), plus submersible and industrial pumps, exports, solar rooftop inverters (subsidiary Shakti Energy Solutions) and EV motors/controllers (Shakti EV Mobility) [IP Q1FY27 p.5, p.23].
- **Who pays:** mainly state agencies running **PM-KUSUM** (the central farm solar-pump subsidy scheme) and state schemes such as Maharashtra's **Magel Tyala**; the farmer pays a small share. Government projects were ~77% of FY26 revenue, exports ~15%, others ~8% [IP Q1FY27 p.18]. FY26 revenue: solar pumps and related ₹2,232 cr; exports ₹411 cr; other businesses ₹206 cr [IP Q1FY27 p.15, p.20].
- **Share:** claims ~25% of PM-KUSUM Component B; 1,90,698 pumps installed under KUSUM and 66,926 outside it (2.58 lakh total) against 11.24 lakh KUSUM-B pumps installed nationally at 30 Jun 2026 (i.e. ~17% of all KUSUM-B installs to date) [IP Q1FY27 p.7, p.9].
- **Capacity:** 5 lakh pumps & motors a year (3.5 lakh main unit + 1.5 lakh SEZ export unit), 4 lakh VFDs/inverters, 2 lakh structures, all at Pithampur (MP); capacity utilisation 63% in Q1 FY27 [IP Q1FY27 p.5, p.17; Q1 call].
- **Exports:** FY26 ₹411 cr — Middle East 41%, Africa 33%, US 19%; export margin is "around 10% more" than domestic [IP Q1FY27 p.19; Q4 call]. Uganda solar-pump contract (US$35.3 mn) executed [IP Q1FY27 p.19].
- **New lines:** retail/cash sales ₹24 cr and rooftop ₹8 cr in Q1 FY27 (rooftop ₹2 cr a year earlier); EV still at vehicle-level trials, ₹70 cr invested of a planned ₹114 cr, meaningful revenue "from FY28" [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.10–11].

- Quarterly revenue / operating profit / net profit: Jun-25 622 / 144 / 97; Sep-25 666 / 136 / 91; Dec-25 551 / 59 / 32; Mar-26 858 / 83 / 38; Jun-26 859 / 83 / 52 [Screener]. Operating margin went 23% → 20% → 11% → 10% → 10% [Screener].
- Q1 FY27: revenue +38% (₹859 cr vs ₹623 cr); pumps installed +58% to 27,678; EBITDA ₹83 cr (9.6%) vs ₹144 cr (23.1%) a year ago; profit ₹52 cr vs ₹97 cr [IP Q1FY27 p.30–31; Q1 call].
- Annual: revenue FY24 ₹1,371 cr → FY25 ₹2,516 cr → FY26 ₹2,698 cr (+7%); operating profit ₹225 → ₹603 → ₹422 cr; margin 16% → 24% → 16%; profit ₹142 → ₹408 → ₹258 cr [Screener]. TTM revenue ₹2,934 cr, TTM profit ₹212 cr (−49%) [Screener].
- ROCE 31% (FY24) → 55% (FY25) → 24% (FY26) [Screener].
- **Unit economics:** realisation ≈ ₹2.48 lakh per pump in both Q4 FY26 (₹2,48,374) and Q1 FY27 (₹2,48,153) [Q1 call]. The Sep-2026 Maharashtra order prices 10,000 pumps at ₹235.92 cr including GST (~₹2.36 lakh each) [News: Business Standard, 10 Sep 2026]. The year-on-year EBITDA fall of ₹61 cr in Q1 was split ₹36 cr raw materials (copper, stainless steel, aluminium, mild steel) and ₹25 cr lower prices [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- **Q4 call (11 May 2026):** no FY27 revenue or margin guidance ("we will keep doing year-on-year growth"); margin hit called "temporary"; 0.5 GW module plant to be commissioned "by the end of Q1FY27"; expanded pump capacity from Q2 FY27; 2.2 GW cell plant by "March '28"; KUSUM 2.0 expected by end-Q1 FY27 with orders from Q2; ₹1,500 cr order book executable in two quarters; rooftop at 15% EBITDA margin [Q4 call].
- **Q1 delivery:** revenue a record ₹859 cr; margin 9.6% (no recovery); module plant **slipped** to September 2026; KUSUM 2.0 still waiting for the Prime Minister's Office; order book fell to ₹1,000 cr (incl. GST) [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.28].
- **Q1 call (27 Jul 2026):** new long-term target of **₹5,000 cr revenue by 2029** ("next three years") — about 23% a year from FY26's ₹2,698 cr; no segment split, no margin target; the module and cell plants together to add "approximately 3%" to EBITDA margin; pump plant ready November 2026; 2.2 GW plant targeted September 2027 (the presentation says "by FY28") [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.11].
- Verdict: near-term guidance **kept vague and dates slipped**; long-term ambition **raised**.

## Capex and capacity
- Total programme **₹1,500–1,700 cr by September 2027**, roughly ₹800 cr in FY27 and the rest in FY28 [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.11 ("~₹17,000 Mn")]. Components: doubling pump/motor, VFD and structure capacity; 0.5 GW DCR (domestic-content) module line (Sep 2026); 2.2 GW DCR cell + module plant (Sep 2027 / FY28); EV [IP Q1FY27 p.11].
- Rationale: DCR cells are 40–50% of a solar pump-set's cost, and from 1 Jun 2026 modules for PM-KUSUM, PM Surya Ghar and other government-linked projects must use cells from the approved ALMM List-II [AR FY26 MD&A]. The company now buys 50–60 MW of modules a month; the 0.5 GW line will ramp 40–50% → 60–70% → ~85% over three months [Q1 call].
- **Funding:** a ~₹800 cr term loan for the 2.2 GW project, plus QIP money — ₹292.6 cr raised in Jul 2025 (₹105 cr unused at Mar-26) and ₹200 cr raised in FY24 (₹101 cr still unused at Mar-26) [Q1 call; AR FY26 Board's report]. Working-capital lines ≈₹1,800 cr from ~10 banks [Q1 call].
- My arithmetic (not guidance): ₹1,600 cr of capex against FY26 net fixed assets of ₹315 cr is a five-fold jump in the asset base; to earn a 20% pre-tax return it needs ≈₹320 cr of extra EBITDA a year — more than FY26's entire ₹422 cr operating profit less today's run-rate. Payback depends on KUSUM 2.0 volumes and on cell prices staying above import parity [my estimate from Screener and Q1 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹505 cr at Mar-26 (₹170 cr a year earlier); cash ₹439 cr; debt/equity 0.3x [Screener; IP Q1FY27 p.12, p.36]. Current borrowings include ₹119 cr under **supplier-finance** arrangements and a **₹31 cr loan from a promoter-group company** (repayable on demand, 7.95–12% interest) [AR FY26 consolidated note 22].
- **Receivables jumped again in Q1:** ₹1,276 cr at Mar-26 (after a ₹420 cr cut in Q4) rose to **₹1,799 cr at 30 Jun 2026** — about 42% not yet due, ~31% under 180 days, ~15% 180–365 days and ~12% over a year (the last two include 10% retention money) [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 p.30]. The official Q1 transcript gives ≈₹760 cr not due, ≈₹560 cr and ₹477 cr retention [Q1 call].
- Payment terms: 90% after seven days of remote-monitoring (RMS) data, 10% after 90 days; historic debtor days 120–150 [Q4 call]. Screener: debtor days 178 (FY24), 152 (FY25), 173 (FY26); cash cycle 134 days [Screener].
- Operating cash flow ₹124 cr in FY26 (63% of operating profit), ₹20 cr in FY25; free cash flow negative three years running (−₹10, −₹85, −₹53 cr) [Screener]. Dividend payout 3–6% [Screener].
- Rating: IND AA-/Stable from India Ratings per the company [IP Q1FY27 p.12] (the rationale page did not load).

## Moat evidence
- Scale and track record: 2.58 lakh pumps installed, ~25% KUSUM share, first mover in most big states; maiden Karnataka order ₹476 cr in FY26 [IP Q1FY27 p.7; AR FY26 MD&A].
- Brand pull: a Maharashtra portal allotment of 25,000 Shakti pumps was fully subscribed by farmers "in 25 minutes" (Magel Tyala lets farmers choose the vendor) [Q4 call].
- In-house power electronics (VFDs, inverters), 15 patents granted; rooftop customers report ~10% better generation with Shakti inverters (company claim) [IP Q1FY27 p.8; Q1 call].
- **But no pricing power in tenders:** it matches the lowest (L1) rate; Magel Tyala prices cut realisation 3–4%; it walked away from a Haryana order and ₹200 cr of Maharashtra orders over margin/payment worries; analysts noted peers held margins better, which management declined to discuss [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Policy dependence:** ~77% of revenue from government schemes; KUSUM 2.0 has been "a week or a month away" since May; the order book dropped from ₹1,500 cr to ₹1,000 cr (both include GST, so ex-GST revenue cover is lower) [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.18].
- **Working capital:** receivables of ₹1,799 cr are ~2 years of FY26 profit and about 6.8 months of TTM revenue; ~27% is over 180 days [IP Q1FY27 p.30; Screener].
- **Capex into an uncertain order book:** ₹1,500–1,700 cr of mostly debt-plus-QIP-funded capacity while margins are 10% and utilisation 63% [Q1 call].
- **Auditor remarks (PwC, unqualified):** the only key audit matter is revenue recognition; but CARO notes the accounting software's audit trail "is not maintained for certain transactions" and is not enabled at database level, and slight delays in depositing provident fund and income tax [AR FY26 auditor's report & CARO vii(a)].
- **Related parties:** purchases and construction services from promoter-family companies (Shakti Irrigation India, Vintex Tools, Arsh Industrial, Shakti Construction & Developers — the last ≈₹67 cr in FY26 per the note, likely plant construction), a promoter-company loan, and family directors (Chairman Dinesh Patidar and WTD Sunil Patidar are brothers) [AR FY26 consolidated note 44; AR FY26 Board's report].
- **QIP cash idle:** ₹101 cr of the FY24 ₹200 cr QIP remained unused two years later [AR FY26 Board's report].
- Contingent liabilities are small: ₹19.8 cr of income-tax and indirect-tax disputes [AR FY26 standalone note 39].
- Disclosure: order book given inclusive of GST and dated at call date, not quarter-end — an analyst complained; management promised both figures [Q4 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- Q4 was about **balance-sheet repair** (₹420 cr receivables cut, ₹124 cr operating cash); Q1 swung to **growth ambition** (₹5,000 cr by 2029) while receivables rose ~₹520 cr [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.30].
- Module plant slipped from "end of Q1 FY27" to September 2026; KUSUM 2.0 timing moved from "end of Q1 FY27" to "next week or next month" [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Margin explanation became more specific (₹36 cr raw material + ₹25 cr price) but no recovery date; "no hedging" because the rise is "temporary" [Q1 call].
- Rooftop moved from "channel set-up" to registering as a PM Surya Ghar brand owner; EV slipped to "another 8–9 months" [Q1 call].

## My read
Shakti is growing volumes fast but the business has become weaker per pump: margins halved from ~24% to ~10% on commodity costs and lower tender prices, and receivables are back near ₹1,800 cr. The investment case now rests on three things landing together — KUSUM 2.0 orders, the 0.5 GW and 2.2 GW DCR plants delivering the promised ~3% margin gain, and cash collection — while ₹1,500–1,700 cr of capex is spent. At this price the market needs margins back to ~14–15% and the ₹5,000 cr path to look credible; the view would improve on a large KUSUM 2.0 award and a quarter of falling receivables, and worsen if the order book dips below ₹800 cr or the module plant slips again.
