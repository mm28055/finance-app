# Greaves Cotton — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code GREAVESCOT. Price ₹235, market cap ≈ ₹5,469 cr, 52-week range ₹120–272 [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].

## Business and segments
- Two halves since the Q1 FY27 recast [IP Q1FY27 p.14–19]:
  - **Core businesses (Q1 FY27 revenue ₹710 cr, +16%; +19% excluding exited lines):** Energy Solutions ₹203 cr (+21%) — gensets 58.5–500 kVA (+32%), a new in-house 650 kVA engine, a first 1,250 kVA "Megaseries" set in SAARC, service; Mobility Solutions ₹427 cr (+18%) — single-cylinder diesel engines for three-wheelers (auto engine sales +36%), Euro V+ engines for European microcars (Ligier), aftermarket spares, Excel Controlinkage (push-pull cables), Greaves Technologies (engineering services); Industrial Solutions ₹80 cr (−4%, +9% like-for-like) — fire-pump, marine, farm, defence engines.
  - **Investee businesses (₹284 cr):** Greaves Electric Mobility (GEML — Ampere e-scooters, ELE e-rickshaws, MLR Auto three-wheelers) ₹270 cr (+97%) and Greaves Finance (EV loans) ₹14 cr [IP Q1FY27 p.14, p.21].
- FY26 revenue by new segment: Energy ₹742 cr, Mobility ₹1,584 cr, Industrial ₹339 cr → core ₹2,665 cr; GEML ₹786 cr; GFL ₹39 cr; consolidated ₹3,437 cr [IP Q1FY27 p.28].
- Diesel three-wheeler engines were ₹545 cr in FY26 (15.8% of group revenue); Greaves holds ~60–63% of the diesel L5 auto segment, but diesel is only ~20% of that market (40% electric, 40% CNG) [IP Q1FY27 p.28; Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A].
- International was 13% of core revenue in FY26 (9% in FY25); a Dubai subsidiary was set up in Q1 FY27 [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 p.12].
- Capacity: nine manufacturing clusters; GEML two-wheeler capacity ~4.8 lakh units a year; Excel cable lines 70–75% utilised [IP Q1FY27 p.5; Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- **Q4 call (6 May 2026):** core revenue growth 16–18% a year; core EBITDA margin 13–15% as it scales; ₹500–700 cr capex over 4–5 years; last 20% of Excel to be bought in Q2 FY27; margins hit by commodity costs but a price pass-through "already activated in Q1"; GEML DRHP (IPO filing) extended by SEBI to 30 Sep 2026; no GEML forecast because of IPO rules [Q4 call].
- **Q1 delivery:** core growth 16% (19% like-for-like) — **on target**; core margin 12.6% — **below** the 13–15% band; price pass-through lagged; margins down 2–2.5 points across businesses [IP Q1FY27 p.19; Q1 call].
- **Q1 call (5 Aug 2026):** growth ambition restated as 16–20% CAGR; full-year FY27 margin target **kept** ("Q2 marginally better than Q1, H2 better than H1"); ~1.5 points of the margin dip is deliberate spending on brand and people [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.32]. GEML: EBITDA positive "over the next 4–6 quarters"; double-digit e-scooter share "over 4–8 quarters"; the ₹530 cr rights issue should fund "approximately the next 2 years" [Q1 call]. **The IPO was not mentioned**; the DRHP extension ran only to 30 Sep 2026 [Q4 call].

## Capex and capacity
- Core capex plan ₹500–700 cr over 4–5 years for new products (650 kVA and larger gensets, export-grade engines), automation (robotic gantry cell and AI inspection at Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, conveyorised lines at Excel) and international expansion [Q4 call; Q1 call]. FY26 actual PP&E spend was only ~₹30–36 cr (raised by an analyst; management did not dispute) [Q4 call]. Management prefers extra shifts before new plants [Q4 call].
- **The big cash use is the investee arm:** Greaves put ₹331 cr into GEML's ₹530 cr rights issue (≈62% share; Abdul Latif Jameel took the rest) and ₹50 cr into Greaves Finance in Q1 FY27 [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.12, p.23].
- Data-centre gensets above 1,000 kVA: "an area we are looking into" via a strategic supply partnership with a large global engine maker; no product yet [Q1 call].
- Payback: not disclosed. My read — the ₹381 cr sent to investees in one quarter is larger than two years of core capex at the FY26 run-rate.

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Standalone is debt-free ("rated AA- with nil debt on its balance sheet") with ~₹426 cr of standalone cash at Mar-26 before the ₹381 cr investee infusion [Q1 call; Analysis Q1 FY27 note in stored file; IP Q1FY27 p.12].
- Consolidated borrowings rose to ₹394 cr at Mar-26 from ₹81 cr, mainly from the finance arm and GEML [Screener].
- Operating cash flow was negative FY22–FY25 and only ₹33 cr in FY26; free cash flow negative five years running (−₹61 cr in FY26) [Screener].
- Working capital FY26: debtor days 37, inventory 50, payables 92 → cash cycle −6 days [Screener]. Top five customers owed ₹53 cr (19% of standalone receivables) vs ₹73 cr (26%) a year earlier [AR FY26 standalone note 31].
- Dividend payout 43% of FY26 profit; yield 0.85% [Screener].
- Greaves Finance AUM ₹560 cr (Jun-26) vs ₹521 cr (Mar-26); net worth ₹153 cr after the ₹50 cr infusion; PAT-positive (₹1.6 cr) in FY26 [IP Q1FY27 p.23; AR FY26].

## Moat evidence
- Diesel three-wheeler engines: ~60% share of the diesel L5 segment with long OEM ties (Piaggio and others) and contractual raw-material price indexing, though with a lag [AR FY26 MD&A; Q1 call].
- Aftermarket: 20,000+ mechanics, 7,000+ retailers; "more ability to pass on" price increases than in OEM supply [IP Q1FY27 p.5; Q1 call].
- Gensets: strong in medium-horsepower residential/commercial niches; "keeping pace with industry growth", i.e. no clear share gain claimed [Q4 call].
- EV: e-scooter share 3.2% a year ago → 5.6% in June 2026 (Vahan); 19%+ share in Bihar; ~14–15% across Bihar, Odisha, West Bengal and Tamil Nadu [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.21]. This is a small player in a subsidy-driven, crowded market.

## Risks and red flags
- **Capital drain to EVs:** GEML's total loss widened to ₹273 cr in FY26; the parent has now funded ₹331 cr more with no IPO path in sight [AR FY26; Q1 call].
- **Policy/subsidy:** GEML repaid ₹140 cr of FAME-II incentives in FY24 and wrote off ₹362 cr of claims in FY25 after the ministry alleged breaches; PM E-DRIVE support for e-two-wheelers was extended only to 31 Jul 2026 and the L5 e-three-wheeler subsidy ran out in Dec 2025 [AR FY26 consolidated notes; AR FY26 MD&A].
- **Contingent liabilities (consolidated, Mar-26):** customs ₹129.4 cr — mainly a ₹56.4 cr (plus interest/penalty) demand treating 2018–21 e-scooter part imports as complete knock-down kits, under appeal; sales tax ₹44 cr; excise ₹26 cr; claims ₹33.7 cr; income tax ₹6.1 cr; wages ₹14.5 cr [AR FY26 consolidated note 37].
- **Auditor (standalone):** unmodified opinion; key audit matter is impairment of investments in subsidiaries (indicators identified; no impairment booked). CARO: audit trail not kept for changes by certain privileged users and not enabled at database level [AR FY26 auditor's report].
- **Exceptional items FY26 (standalone ₹35.3 cr):** ₹16 cr write-off of an e-powertrain (ETA motors & controller) project that failed to win orders, ₹15.8 cr labour-code provision, ₹3.6 cr fee for breaching land-use rules [AR FY26 standalone note 30].
- **Structural:** diesel is ~20% of the L5 three-wheeler market and shrinking as EV share rose from 23% to ~33% in FY26 [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call].
- Management churn: new Group CFO (May 2026), new CTO (Q1 FY27), wider leadership rebuild in H2 FY26 [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## My read
The core engine and genset business is healthy — 16–19% growth, debt-free, 12–15% margins — but it is being asked to fund an EV arm whose total losses are still rising (₹273 cr in FY26) and whose IPO route has faded. Group profit (₹21 cr TTM on ₹3,665 cr revenue) is therefore tiny, and the valuation rests on the core earning 13–15% and GEML reaching break-even within 4–6 quarters as promised. What would change my view: core margin back above 13% in H2 FY27 plus a visible fall in GEML losses (positive); another capital call for GEML, a loss of the diesel three-wheeler franchise to CNG/EV, or an adverse customs ruling (negative).
