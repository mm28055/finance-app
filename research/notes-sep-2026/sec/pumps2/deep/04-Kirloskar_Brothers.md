# Kirloskar Brothers (KBL) — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code KIRLOSBROS. Price ₹1,772, market cap ₹14,070 cr, consolidated P/E 34.8, ROCE 20.4%, ROE 17.3%, dividend yield 0.40% [Screener, 28-Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- India's oldest large pump maker (est. 1888): agricultural/domestic pumps, industrial, irrigation and water-supply pumps, power-plant pumps (including concrete-volute cooling-water pumps), oil & gas, marine & defence, valves, hydro turbines. Ten Indian plants and seven abroad. [IP Q1FY27 p.12, p.15]
- Q1 FY27 entity split (revenue / EBITDA, ₹ cr): KBL standalone 673.8 / 92.0 (13.7%); Karad Projects & Motors (KPML, motors + steel castings, now incl. merged Kolhapur Steel) 208.7 / 15.3 (7.3%, from 12.5%); Kirloskar Corrocoat JV 18.5 / 4.1; international (KBIBV group) 401.8 / 20.7 (5.2%, from 7.0%); consolidated 1,104.9 / 130.6 (11.8%). [IP Q1FY27 p.8]
- International Q1 FY27: SPP UK revenue ₹191.6 cr, EBITDA −₹0.9 cr; SPP USA ₹159.8 cr, EBITDA ₹25.6 cr (16.0%); Rodelta (Netherlands) ₹7.5 cr, EBITDA −₹5.7 cr. FY26: SPP UK ₹785 cr at 7.4% margin (12.3% in FY25), SPP USA ₹540 cr at 13.3%. [IP Q1FY27 p.9]
- Standalone mix: made-to-stock ~51%, made-to-order ~25%, engineered-to-order ~21%, projects ~3% (FY25; projects were 54% in FY10). Small-pump retail (~45–50% of standalone) has no order book — sold and shipped the same month. [CARE Dec-25; Q1 call]
- US business: 35% fire pumps, ~25% data centres, rest water utilities and building boosters; 46 distributors (12 four years ago). UK: SPP holds ~85% of the water-utility pump business. [Q4 call]
- Capacity: no utilisation figure is disclosed; the Kirloskarvadi foundry (largest of five) completed modernisation and an SAP rollout in FY26–Q1 FY27. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

- FY26 split: domestic revenue ₹2,828 cr (−3%), international +7%; exceptional labour-code charge ₹38.9 cr. [Q4 call]

- Q1 FY27 company view: revenue +12.9%, EBITDA ₹130.6 cr (+2.4%, margin 11.8% vs 13.0%), PAT ₹67.6 cr (flat). Gross margin 52.6% (54.0%); other expenses +10% (digitalisation, advertising); finance cost ₹8.1 cr (+31%). [IP Q1FY27 p.6–7; Q1 call]
- Orders: Q1 FY27 consolidated order intake ₹1,395 cr (+4%); standalone intake +5.4% (the call first said 14.9%, corrected in the filed transcript). Pending orders ₹4,062 cr (Jun-26): domestic ₹2,558 cr (standalone ₹2,375 cr) + overseas ₹1,505 cr; a year earlier ₹3,345 cr. Standalone book by sector (Jun-26): irrigation & water ₹1,013 cr, power ₹599 cr, building & construction ₹233 cr, oil & gas ₹178 cr, industry ₹150 cr, customer support ₹104 cr, marine & defence ₹56 cr, valves ₹43 cr. [IP Q1FY27 p.10; Q1 call]
- Unit economics: ~10% price increase since January 2026; oil & gas petrol-pump (fuel dispensing) orders ₹217 cr cumulative, ₹74 cr dispatched. [Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- Every year: "we will strive for double-digit growth". FY26 delivered +1% consolidated and −3% domestic — **missed** (blamed on JJM state funding delays, weak agri season, foundry SAP disruption). [Q4 call]
- Q4 call (15 May 2026) promises vs Q1 delivery:
  - Double-digit FY27 growth for standalone and consolidated. Q1: consolidated +12.9%, standalone domestic +9% (below 10%). **Partly met.** [Q4 call; Q1 call]
  - Foundry "largely stabilised". Q1: modernisation "complete", but half-finished orders left ₹82 cr of inventory; "huge improvements" in July. **Late, now done.** [Q4 call; Q1 call]
  - SPP UK margins to "realign… around the third quarter of the calendar year" as water-utility service work (United Utilities framework) starts. Q1: SPP UK EBITDA negative; recovery still guided for Q2–Q3 FY27. **Not yet.** [Q4 call; Q1 call]
  - Nuclear primary heat-transport pump prototype "on schedule" for NPCIL. Q1: hydraulic and mechanical tests passed; metallurgy "hiccups" — castings now being trialled in Europe and India, expected clear "by the end of the month"; ~₹70 cr primary-circuit and ~₹40 cr secondary-circuit nuclear orders booked in Q1. **Progressing, one slip.** [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Two-thirds of the ₹2,500 cr domestic order book to be executed in FY27. [Q1 call]
- No margin target: "we will strive for double-digit growth year-on-year" is the only guidance. [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Policy: capex "equal to depreciation" (FY26 depreciation ₹100 cr), for modernisation, debottlenecking and quality. FY26 investing cash outflow ₹213 cr (includes treasury investments). CWIP ₹58 cr. [Q1 call; Screener]
- The main recent project was the Kirloskarvadi foundry modernisation + SAP ERP; it hurt FY26 output and is now finished. Growth capex is otherwise small and self-funded — including self-funded nuclear pump development (designs owned in India). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- No payback or capacity numbers are published; returns depend on better utilisation of existing plants (FY26 ROCE fell to 20% from 28%). [Screener]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹250 cr (Mar-26, mostly overseas subsidiary debt); investments ₹758 cr; standalone has no term debt. CARE: cash and liquid investments ₹862 cr (Mar-25), gearing 0.11×, interest cover 26.8×. [Screener; CARE Dec-25]
- Operating cash flow FY24 ₹370 cr, FY25 ₹386 cr, FY26 ₹334 cr (86% of operating profit); free cash flow ₹204 cr in FY26. [Screener]
- Working capital worsened in FY26: debtor days 40 → 53, inventory 142 days, cash conversion cycle 68 days; the FY26 annual report shows work-in-progress up from ₹180 cr to ₹241 cr and supplier advances ₹32 cr → ₹91 cr (analyst on the call; not contradicted). Q1 FY27 added ₹82 cr of inventory. [Screener; Q1 call]
- Strict terms: advance or letter of credit for small/medium industrial pumps, cash for retail; orders without commercial backing are not booked. [Q4 call]
- Dividend ₹7 per share (350%) for FY26; payout ~15%. [Q4 call; Screener]

## Moat evidence
- "World's largest manufacturers of concrete volute pumps"; first unit (1994) lost only 2% efficiency in 30 years on seawater; claims 100% share of metallic-volute cooling pumps in current Indian nuclear plants. [Q1 call]
- Nuclear: supplied primary and secondary sodium pumps for the Fast Breeder Reactor; developing primary heat-transport pumps for PHWRs — a direct challenge to KSB's incumbency. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- UK water: ~85% share of water-utility pumps (SPP). US data-centre packages (US$7.5–10 mn per hyperscale site, modular containers), 8 packages on the US shop floor; a global framework contract with a large US data-centre operator in the final signing stage (under NDA). Competitors: Armstrong, Taco, Grundfos, Xylem. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- IoT condition-monitoring panels — "the only ones who can supply this at the moment in India" (installed base "in hundreds"). [Q1 call]
- Pricing power: took ~10% price increases since January and expects them to cover costs; claims premiums against multinationals on life-cycle cost. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- CARE: "strong market position… leading share in the domestic market"; competition from unorganised players keeps agri margins thin. [CARE Dec-25]

## Risks and red flags
- **Family dispute:** on 11 Aug 2026 the Supreme Court sent KBL's and Sanjay Kirloskar's claims against Atul Kirloskar and others (breach of non-compete terms in the 2009 Kirloskar family settlement) to a three-member arbitral tribunal seated in Pune; "financial impact, if any… cannot be presently ascertained". Long-running management distraction; outcome could cut either way. [BSE filing 24-Aug-26]
- **Loss-making subsidiaries (FY26):** Kolhapur Steel loss ₹9.9 cr, negative reserves ₹155 cr (merged into KPML from Dec-25, which is why KPML margins fell); KBIBV holding company loss ₹14.3 cr; Rodelta loss ₹4.9 cr with negative reserves ₹30 cr, supported by a KBL guarantee of €3.675 mn to ICICI Bank; Kirloskar Brothers Thailand loss ₹1.2 cr, negative reserves ₹18.6 cr (KBL guarantees US$5.5 mn + THB 125 mn). [AR FY26 Form AOC-1, related-party note (E)]
- **SPP UK:** FY26 EBITDA fell ₹37 cr (₹95 cr → ₹58 cr) as UK energy costs idled chemical clients; Q1 FY27 EBITDA negative. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 p.9]
- **Contingent liabilities** (standalone) ₹159.9 cr at Mar-26 (₹143.3 cr): excise/service tax/GST ₹103.4 cr, sales tax ₹16.1 cr, income tax ₹27.9 cr (up from ₹11.9 cr), legal ₹7.6 cr, labour ₹4.9 cr. [AR FY26 note 28(A)]
- Auditor: key audit matter is revenue recognition on long-term contracts (₹68.6 cr of revenue recognised over time); CARO notes the auditor "considered whistle-blower complaints received by the company during the year". No qualification found. [AR FY26 auditor's report, CARO (xi)(c)]
- Government dependence: Jal Jeevan Mission investigations and state funding delays stalled water-segment dispatches; retention receivables from old projects (CARE adjusts net worth for them). [Q4 call; CARE Dec-25]
- Nuclear second-source ambitions depend on NPCIL qualification; metallurgy not yet proven with Indian castings. [Q1 call]
- Promoter holding 65.95%, unchanged; no pledge disclosed. [Screener]

## What changed between the two calls
- Q4: defensive on a flat year (+1%), three excuses (JJM, agri season, foundry ERP). Q1: better top line (+13%), foundry done, US strong, but margin down because of SPP UK and KPML/TKSL. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- New in Q1: near-final global data-centre framework contract; IoT traction in municipal water; ~₹110 cr of new nuclear orders; nuclear pump castings problem disclosed. [Q1 call]
- Dropped: JJM barely mentioned in Q1. [Q1 call]
- Stated standalone EBITDA margin improved (13.7% vs 12.8%) — the margin problem is overseas and in KPML, not in KBL itself. [Q1 call]

## My read
KBL's standalone business is in decent shape — 13.7% EBITDA margin in Q1, strict cash terms, a ₹4,062 cr order book and a real product edge in large engineered pumps — but FY26 was a lost year (+1% revenue, ROCE down from 28% to 20%) and the group keeps carrying weak overseas and foundry subsidiaries. The upside cases (US data-centre framework contract, nuclear second-source, thermal power orders) are real but not yet in the numbers. The price needs roughly 10–12% earnings growth with margins back near 14%; that depends on SPP UK recovering in H2 FY27 and domestic execution speeding up now the foundry is fixed. The view improves if H1 FY27 standalone growth reaches double digits and the data-centre contract is signed; it worsens if SPP UK stays loss-making, the nuclear castings fail qualification, or the family arbitration produces a cost.
