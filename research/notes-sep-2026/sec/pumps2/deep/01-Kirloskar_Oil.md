# Kirloskar Oil Engines (KOEL) — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code KIRLOSENG. Price ₹2,137, market cap ₹31,100 cr, consolidated P/E 54.6, standalone P/E 68.0, ROCE 14.6% (consolidated, dragged by the lending arm) / 18.7% (standalone) [Screener, 28-Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- Three businesses. **B2B Power & Energy** (standalone KOEL: diesel/gas gensets 7.5 kVA–3,000 kVA routine, up to 6.3 MW on projects; industrial engines 20–750 hp for construction, mining, railways, marine, defence; spares/service), **B2C Fluid Dynamics** (pumps, small engines, farm equipment, moved into subsidiary KOEL Fluid Dynamics, KFD, from 11 Oct 2025), and **Financial Services** (Arka Fincap NBFC). [AR FY26 p.31; IP Q1FY27 p.5]
- Q1 FY27 consolidated segment revenue: B2B ₹1,488 cr (+17%), segment profit ₹115 cr (−18%); B2C ₹301 cr (+3%), profit ₹26 cr (−7%); Financial Services ₹210 cr (+9%), PBT ₹9.4 cr (−31%). B2B is ~74% of revenue and ~77% of segment profit. [IP Q1FY27 p.16]
- Standalone B2B mix Q1 FY27: Powergen ₹720 cr (+18%), Industrial ₹368 cr (+19%), Distribution & Aftermarket ₹268 cr (+20%), International ₹106 cr (−11%). Powergen is 49% of standalone sales, industrial 25%, aftermarket 18%, exports 8%. [IP Q1FY27 p.6–7; Q1 call]
- Volume: FY26 Indian DG-set market grew 18% to ~1,79,000 units (incl. telecom); KOEL grew 41% to "upwards of 50,000 units" — roughly 28% unit share. HHP (high-horsepower, >750 kVA) share went from "negligible"/single digits two years ago to "approaching double digits". [Q4 call; AR FY26 p.31]
- Geography: gross international revenue crossed ₹1,000 cr in FY26 (+37%); Middle East & North Africa is the largest export region, 40–50% of international revenue per an analyst on the Q4 call (not contradicted). Products sold in ~60 countries. [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Capacity: Kagal is the main engine plant (company says capacity for more than 1.3 million engine builds cumulatively — the AR gives no current utilisation figure); KFD consolidated pumps into Sanand (output raised from 3 to 10 pumps a minute during FY26); Rajkot supplied 70,000+ small diesel engines in FY26. [AR FY26 p.85, MD&A]

## Key numbers
- Consolidated (Screener, includes Arka's interest income in sales and its interest cost below operating profit): sales FY24 ₹5,898 cr → FY25 ₹6,329 cr → FY26 ₹7,701 cr (+22%); net profit ₹440 cr → ₹476 cr → ₹562 cr; OPM 17% → 19% → 18%. TTM sales ₹7,937 cr, TTM profit ₹535 cr. [Screener]
- Standalone engine business (the cleaner view): sales FY24 ₹4,851 cr → FY25 ₹4,521 cr (restated after the B2C transfer) → FY26 ₹5,647 cr; OPM 12% → 13% → 13%; net profit ₹362 cr → ₹432 cr → ₹461 cr. [Screener standalone]
- Last five quarters, consolidated sales / operating profit / net profit (₹ cr): Jun-25 1,762/327/139; Sep-25 1,948/382/159; Dec-25 1,873/331/109; Mar-26 2,116/376/155; Jun-26 2,000/300/111. OPM fell to 15% in Jun-26 from 19% a year earlier. [Screener]
- Standalone quarters (company figures, continuing ops): Q1 FY26 sales ₹1,262 cr, EBITDA ₹172 cr (13.5%); Q4 FY26 ₹1,522 cr, ₹193 cr (12.6%); Q1 FY27 ₹1,461 cr (+16%), ₹165 cr (11.2%), PAT ₹99 cr (−9%). [IP Q1FY27 p.11–12]
- Why Q1 margin fell: raw-material cost ₹966 cr (65.7% of revenue vs 65.0% a year ago), employee cost ₹111 cr vs ₹80 cr (+39%, ~₹31 cr), other expenses ₹229 cr vs ₹194 cr. Management blames export mix, commodity inflation and a lag between price increases and realisation. [IP Q1FY27 p.12; Q1 call]
- Unit economics: FY26 standalone revenue ÷ ~50,000 gensets is not meaningful (engines, spares and industrial are mixed in); management refuses to split price vs volume or disclose HHP revenue. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Arka: AUM ₹7,651 cr (Jun-26) vs ₹7,947 cr (Mar-26); Q1 PAT ₹6.9 cr vs ₹10.1 cr; FY26 PAT ₹69 cr; GNPA 1.2%, NNPA 0.3% (Mar-26); borrowings ₹5,033 cr, debt/equity 3.7×. [IP Q1FY27 p.9; Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- Long-term target (set FY25, repeated every call since): "$2 billion" revenue by FY30. Q1 FY27 call gave the rupee figure as "close to ₹16,600 cr"; the FY26 annual report says "consolidated ₹16,000 cr". From FY26's ₹7,701 cr that needs ~20–21% a year for four years. **Kept.** [Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Margin: Q1 call — "higher double-digit EBITDA margin" by FY30, will improve from here, no number. CRISIL, on the other hand, expects consolidated manufacturing EBITDA margin to stay at 11–12% (FY26: 11.9%). **Vague, not quantified.** [Q1 call; CRISIL Jul-26]
- Q4 promises vs Q1 delivery:
  - Q4: "no margin deterioration", "operating leverage… from FY27". Q1: standalone EBITDA margin down 230 bps YoY to 11.2%. **Missed.** [Q4 call; Q1 call]
  - Q4: Middle East — "we have not seen any impact in the last quarter". Q1: international −11%, logistics blocked; normalisation "3 to 6 months". **Missed; new timeline.** [Q4 call; Q1 call]
  - Q4: NPCIL ₹798 cr order — billing "has not started yet", revenue from FY27, completion by 2029. Q1: still no revenue; "some" this year. **On schedule but not yet visible.** [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26 p.31]
  - Q4: data centres — "slowly making our presence in hyper-scalers". Q1: first hyperscale order, 192 MW (96 × 2,500 kVA OptiPrime Dual Core units for HyperNext), genset revenue in FY27 plus 5–6 year O&M. **Delivered.** [Q1 call; News: Indian Chemical News, 23 Jun 2026]
  - Q4 (EBITDA): "committed to EBITDA delivery for the full year" (first said Q1). **New commitment, no number.** [Q1 call]
- No FY27 revenue or margin guidance on either call. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- **Phase 1:** ₹700 cr, +50,000 engines a year through new and upgraded lines inside the existing Kagal campus, "operational by April 2027". Not yet in the gross block at Mar-26. [AR FY26 p.32, p.85; Q4 call]
- **Phase 2:** ₹1,400 cr over two years (capitalisation over ~3 years), +20,000 high-horsepower and specialised engines in a new building at Kagal; "fully new capex, nothing is for replacement". The high cost per engine (₹7 lakh vs ₹1.4 lakh in Phase 1) reflects bigger engines plus a new building. [Q4 call; AR FY26 p.85]
- Payback: management expects asset turns of 4–5× on the new block; "at an asset turn of 4… close to ₹5,000–6,000 cr" of peak revenue from Phase 2. Gross block roughly doubles from ~₹2,000 cr to ~₹4,000 cr. [Q4 call]
- FY26 capex spent ₹320 cr; capital commitments outstanding ₹391 cr at Mar-26 (₹119 cr a year earlier). CWIP ₹149 cr consolidated. [AR FY26 p.85, standalone note 5.3; Screener]
- Funding: CRISIL puts total medium-term capex at ₹1,800–2,100 cr, "funded largely through accrual", term-debt repayment only ₹30–40 cr a year. [CRISIL Jul-26]
- R&D ~2% of sales on the P&L and ~2.5% on the capex side. [Q4 call]
- My view on the capex: ₹2,100 cr against FY26 standalone EBITDA of ₹735 cr is a big bet that HHP/data-centre/export demand arrives on time; Phase 2 only earns from FY29–FY30, just when the $2 bn target is due.

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Standalone net cash (after debt, incl. treasury) ₹485 cr at Jun-26 vs ₹552 cr at Mar-26. CRISIL: liquid surplus >₹700 cr at Mar-26; adjusted gearing 0.14×; interest cover ~38×. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.13; CRISIL Jul-26]
- Consolidated borrowings ₹5,374 cr (Mar-26) are almost all Arka's lending book (Arka debt ₹5,033 cr at Jun-26). KOEL has invested ₹1,053 cr in Arka; CRISIL does not expect "material additional support". [Screener; IP Q1FY27 p.9; CRISIL Jul-26]
- Working capital (standalone, trailing-quarter days): inventory 56 → 48 (Mar-26) → 64 (Jun-26); receivables 42 → 41 → 42; payables ~60–81; cash conversion cycle 25 days at Jun-26. [IP Q1FY27 p.13; Q1 call]
- Cash flow: consolidated operating cash flow was negative FY22–FY25 (Arka's loan growth) and turned +₹932 cr in FY26, free cash flow +₹595 cr; standalone business generates cash every year. [Screener]
- Dividend payout ~18–22%; yield 0.33%. No buyback. [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Scale and share: ~50,000 gensets in FY26 in a ~1.79 lakh unit market (about 28%), grew 41% vs market 18% — clear share gain, mainly below 750 kVA; the sub-30 kVA range was "fundamentally redesigned" to win back India's largest genset segment. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Emission-rule barriers: CPCB IV+ (gensets, July 2024) and CEV Stage V (construction equipment, Jan 2025) require re-engineering and 18+ month customer type-approvals; KOEL's construction engine sales grew 44% in Q4 FY26 after the switch — evidence of winning customers during the transition. [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Qualification-heavy niches: 10 × 6.3 MW nuclear gensets for NPCIL (₹798 cr), Navy main-propulsion engine prototype (Project Daksha, Make-1), railway power cars (+47% in FY26), Army power systems. These are long approval cycles with spares annuities. [AR FY26 p.31, MD&A]
- Aftermarket: 12 straight quarters of double-digit growth; 44 dealers, ~500 service touchpoints; 2-hour response guarantees on critical sites. [Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Weak spot: pricing power is limited — Q1 showed costs rising faster than price, and in HHP data-centre gensets Cummins holds an estimated 80%+ share. [Q1 call; News: Sahi/Indmoney, Jun 2026]

## Risks and red flags
- **Margin pressure is real, not only timing:** employee cost +39% YoY; management calls part of it "structural" and would not disclose the ESOP charge. [Q1 call]
- **Arka lending arm:** ₹5,000 cr+ of borrowed money at 3.7× leverage inside a manufacturing company; profit fell to ₹6.9 cr in Q1; the long-term plan is a hive-off/listing but "it takes time". Arka's own auditor listed IT-controls risk as a key audit matter. [IP Q1FY27 p.9; Q1 call; AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report]
- **Loss-making US arm:** Kirloskar Americas (incl. Wildcat Power Gen) had FY26 turnover ₹69.8 cr and a loss of ₹19.9 cr; liabilities (₹132 cr) exceed assets (₹82 cr). Management: "nothing meaningful or significant to discuss" in the US. [AR FY26 Form AOC-1; Q4 call]
- **Related-party acquisition:** KOEL's UAE subsidiary is buying Kirloskar Trading SA (South Africa), described in the AR as "a Promoter Group Company of KOEL" (~₹8 cr total injection into KIME). Small, but worth watching. [AR FY26 standalone note 5.4.2]
- **Tax disputes:** CARO lists ₹106.8 cr of income-tax demands under dispute and not deposited (plus smaller sales-tax/GST items), while contingent liabilities show only ₹5.8 cr of income-tax demands and ₹52 cr of claims not acknowledged as debt — the gap is not explained. [AR FY26 CARO annexure; note 5.1]
- Auditor (G. D. Apte & Co.): unmodified opinion, no emphasis of matter; key audit matters are revenue recognition and ₹190 cr goodwill (mostly KFD ₹184.5 cr). No fraud, no whistle-blower complaints, audit trail intact. [AR FY26 auditor's reports]
- Exceptional: ₹29.7 cr labour-code charge (standalone) in FY26. [AR FY26 note 39]
- Customer risk on the 192 MW order: analysts questioned HyperNext's track record; management only said "we conduct our checks and balances". [Q1 call]
- Export concentration in the Middle East during the West Asia conflict. [Q1 call]
- Promoter holding 41.06% (steady, tiny drift down); no pledges disclosed. [Screener shareholding]

## My read
The core engine business is genuinely improving: KOEL is taking share in gensets (41% volume growth vs 18% market), has entered HHP and hyperscale data centres, and has qualification-led niches (nuclear, Navy, railways) that competitors cannot enter quickly. But Q1 FY27 showed the price of this build-out — standalone margin fell to 11.2% because employee costs jumped 39% and price increases lag, and exports fell. At about 68 times standalone earnings the stock already assumes the $2 bn (~₹16,000–16,600 cr) FY30 target is met with margins moving up; that needs ~20% a year revenue growth and the ₹2,100 cr Kagal capex filling at 4× asset turns. The view improves if margins recover to 13%+ in H2 FY27 as management promises and the NPCIL and HyperNext orders bill on time; it worsens if the Middle East stays shut beyond the "3–6 months", if the 192 MW customer slips, or if Arka needs fresh capital.
