# KSB Limited — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code KSB. Price ₹819, market cap ₹14,256 cr, consolidated P/E 54.4, ROCE 24.7%, ROE 18.4%, dividend yield 0.54% [Screener, 28-Sep-26]. **KSB's financial year is the calendar year (Jan–Dec).** In this file "CY25" = Jan–Dec 2025 (the annual report calls it "FY 2025"); the Apr–Jun 2026 quarter is Q2 CY26. The two stored "calls" are investor meets: 17 Mar 2026 (full-year CY25) and 19 Aug 2026 (H1 CY26).

## Business and segments
- Indian arm of Germany's KSB SE (promoter holding 69.8%). Makes pumps (standard, engineered, nuclear, solar) and industrial valves, plus aftermarket ("SupremeServ": spares, service, mechanical seals). Six plants (Pimpri, Chinchwad, Sinnar/Nashik, Shirwal energy plant, Vambori foundry, Coimbatore valves), 800+ dealers, 350+ service centres. [IP Aug-26; Aug-26 meet]
- CY25 segment results (consolidated): pumps revenue ₹2,215 cr, segment profit ₹285 cr (12.9%, up from 11.0%); valves ₹481 cr, profit ₹70 cr (14.4%, down from 15.2%). Sales mix CY25: pumps 67%, valves 18%, SupremeServ 15%; domestic 83%, exports 17%. [AR CY25 consolidated note 33; IP Aug-26 p.21]
- Order intake mix H1 CY26: standard pumps 51%, engineered 14%, SupremeServ 16%, valves 19% (CY25: 48/19/15/18). [Aug-26 meet; Mar-26 meet]
- Markets: Indian centrifugal pump market ~₹28,000 cr and industrial valves ~₹32,000 cr; KSB says it is among the top two in both. [IP Aug-26 p.17]
- Nuclear: supplier of primary (reactor) coolant pumps to every Indian pressurised heavy-water reactor since Narora; first pump maker with ISO 19443 nuclear quality certification. Nuclear order book ₹1,235 cr (Gorakhpur-Haryana 1&2, Kaiga 5&6, Kudankulam safety package). [Aug-26 meet]
- Capacity: Shirwal (115,000 sq m) runs at 80–90% utilisation, ~1,200–1,300 pumps a year across energy, oil & gas and nuclear; a new 7,000 sq m shed adds ~20%. Up to six nuclear coolant pumps a year can be tested/dispatched. [Aug-26 meet]
- Solar pumps: revenue ₹189 cr (CY24) → ₹245 cr (CY25); KSB now makes ~30–35% of the package (pump, DC motor, controller; not panels); margin "three to four points below" the rest. [Mar-26 meet]

- Order book: ₹2,145 cr (Dec-23) → ₹2,250 cr (Dec-24) → ₹2,585 cr (Dec-25, of which nuclear ₹1,282 cr) → ₹2,745 cr (Jun-26, nuclear ₹1,235 cr). Non-nuclear order intake H1 CY26 ₹1,531 cr; CY25 average ₹249 cr a month. [Mar-26 meet; Aug-26 meet; IP Aug-26 p.16]

  - Solar H1 ₹50–60 cr, "much below" plan as PM-KUSUM 2.0 slipped to Q4; solar only 5–7% of the order book vs 10–12% target. **Missed.** [Aug-26 meet]

  - Exports: ready pumps could not be shipped to the Middle East; "not yet" back on track by late August. **Missed.** [Aug-26 meet]
- Long term: KSB India's 2030 aspiration is to "sustain 12 to 13% ROS (return on sales — a profit-margin measure; the group does not define it in the deck) with aggressive growth" and raise SupremeServ's share (target 25–30% of revenue excluding agri pumps; currently ~15%, growing ~15% a year). Exports target 20–25% of sales (now ~15–17%). [IP Aug-26 p.32; Aug-26 meet; Mar-26 meet]

## Capex and capacity
- Capex ₹115 cr in CY25 on capacity, modernisation and digitisation. Guidance: ₹80–120 cr a year ("sustenance and growth"). Capital commitments ₹39.6 cr at Dec-25. CWIP rose to ₹102 cr (Jun-26) from ₹55 cr (Dec-24). [AR CY25 opening message, note 30(b); Aug-26 meet; Screener]
- Shirwal: 15,000 sq m of land bought and a ~7,000 sq m shed built in 2026 → ~20% more energy/nuclear capacity. A new Mazak machine (~₹7 cr) for mechanical seals. [Aug-26 meet; Mar-26 meet]
- Payback: not disclosed. Capex is ~4% of sales, self-funded; the constraint on growth is NPCIL's test bed and order timing, not factory space.
- Coming cost: KSB group's move to SAP S/4HANA in 2027 will be charged to KSB India as operating cost; amount "not able to say". [Aug-26 meet; IP Aug-26 p.30]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Effectively debt-free (borrowings ₹6 cr); "net financial position" ₹283 cr at Dec-25. [Screener; Mar-26 meet]
- Working capital is heavy and getting heavier: debtor days 94 → 118, inventory 165 → 197 days, cash conversion cycle 163 → 204 days (CY24 → CY25). Reasons: slow-paying solar state agencies (especially Maharashtra), export pumps stuck for the Middle East, FGD (flue-gas) inventory after that market stopped. [Screener; Aug-26 meet]
- Operating cash flow fell to ₹93 cr in CY25 (₹187 cr in CY24); free cash flow ~₹0. [Screener]
- Dividend 220% (₹4.40 per ₹2 share), payout ~28%. [Mar-26 meet; Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Nuclear incumbency: every PHWR coolant pump since 1978; completely localised pump now in test; ISO 19443; access to the German parent's designs for any new reactor type. But NPCIL "is looking for alternative suppliers" and has given development orders; an analyst named Kirloskar Brothers and Flowserve. [Aug-26 meet; Mar-26 meet]
- Thermal: first to localise 700 MW boiler-feed pumps (2021), first localised boiler circulation pump order (2026, previously imported); claims ~50% share of boiler-feed and condensate pumps in new thermal plants, zero in cooling-water pumps. [Mar-26 meet; Aug-26 meet]
- Aftermarket: gross margins "2 times, 3 times" new-pump business; growing ~15% a year; reverse-engineering replacements for Japanese descaling pumps in steel. [Aug-26 meet]
- Distribution: 800+ dealers; standard business is "50% of the business" with stable margins and fast turnover. [Mar-26 meet]
- Pricing power is limited in projects: engineered refinery and thermal pumps face "very high competition… price pressure"; foundries got 12–15% increases while KSB passed on only single-digit increases. [Mar-26 meet; Aug-26 meet]

## Risks and red flags
- **Nuclear timing is outside KSB's control:** the order book's biggest piece (45%) depends on NPCIL's test bed, which failed in April–May 2026. [Aug-26 meet]
- **Contingent liabilities ₹212 cr** at Dec-25 (₹192 cr a year earlier): income tax ₹54.9 cr, GST/excise/service tax ₹130.7 cr (input-credit and classification disputes), labour and other ₹26.4 cr — about 12% of net worth. [AR CY25 standalone note 30(a)]
- **Related-party dependence:** exports go 70–80% through KSB group companies; technology comes under licence with royalty to KSB SE (₹31–37 cr a year in CY24–CY25 per note 32) plus trademark fees; transfer pricing is set by an Advance Pricing Agreement — renewal for Apr-2023 to Mar-2028 was still awaiting approval at Dec-25, and the MD said in Aug-26 it "was signed in this year". Group S/4HANA costs will be added. [Mar-26 meet; AR CY25 notes 32, 41; Aug-26 meet]
- Auditor (Price Waterhouse Chartered Accountants LLP): unmodified opinion; key audit matter is revenue recognition timing on project contracts (point-in-time vs over-time, right to payment). No emphasis of matter found. [AR CY25 auditor's report]
- Cash conversion is weak (see above) while earnings are flat — a pattern to watch. [Screener]
- Margin pressure from fixed-price project orders without price-variation clauses; foundry gas shortages during the West Asia war. [Aug-26 meet]
- Solar dependence on state subsidy schemes (PM-KUSUM) and slow government payment. [Mar-26 meet; Aug-26 meet]
- Credit rating: ICRA rationale (9 Sep 2025) exists but the page would not load; not used.

## My read
KSB's long-term position is strong — nuclear incumbency, localised thermal pumps, a growing aftermarket, a parent that feeds it technology and export orders — and the order book (₹2,745 cr, about a year of sales) keeps rising. But delivery has been poor: sales grew only 6% in CY25 and 2–3% in H1 CY26, margins dipped, cash conversion collapsed and every dated promise from March (nuclear test, solar, 15–20% growth) slipped. At roughly 54 times trailing earnings the stock prices in the nuclear ramp arriving; that needs the NPCIL test to finish in Q4 CY26 and pumps to bill at about one a quarter from CY27. The view improves if H2 CY26 shows double-digit growth with margins back at 13%+ and receivable days falling; it worsens if the test bed slips again, a second coolant-pump vendor wins the next eight-reactor tender, or S/4HANA charges eat the margin recovery.
