# Windlas Biotech (WINDLAS) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Price ₹1,103, market cap ₹2,275 cr, trailing P/E 34.2, ROCE 15.9%, ROE 12.2% (Screener, including the large cash pile; company's ex-cash ROE 29%, ROCE 32%) [Screener; IP Q1FY27 p.3].

## Business and segments
- A Dehradun-based domestic formulation contract maker (tablets, capsules, liquids, sachets; injectables since 2024) for Indian pharma marketing companies, plus its own trade generics and institutional supplies, and small exports to semi-regulated markets [IP Q1FY27].
- Q1 FY27 revenue ₹248 cr (+18%): Generic Formulations CDMO ₹207 cr (84%, +29%), Trade Generics & Institutional (TGI) ₹30 cr (12%, –32%), Exports ₹11 cr (4%, +79%) [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.7].
- FY26 revenue ₹904 cr (+19%): CDMO ₹664 cr (+20%), TGI ₹195 cr (+13%), Exports ₹46 cr (+40%) [Q4 call].
- Diversified clients: 926 customers, 5,644 brands; top customer 6.5% of FY26 revenue, top 10 ~32%, top 20 ~40–41%; 99% of products use Windlas-owned formulation IP [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Capacity: 5 WHO-GMP plants; 8,522 mn tablets/capsules, 61 mn liquid bottles, 54 mn pouches/sachets; utilisation 66% in FY26 (59% in FY25); injectables plant (Plant 5, 2024) ~₹100 cr revenue capacity; Plant 6 (oral solids) mechanically complete, commercial by end-H1 FY27 [IP Q1FY27 p.22; Q1 call].
- Revenue capacity with Plant 6: ~₹1,000 cr ex-injectables + ~₹100 cr injectables = ₹1,100 cr, with 10–15% more from debottlenecking (and "a further ₹100–150 cr in Plant 6") [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Approvals: one EU-GMP unit; Philippines GMP for Plants 4 and 5 (FY26); EU-GMP for injectables being pursued, no timeline [Q4 call].

## Guidance history
- Company gives no numerical revenue/margin guidance [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Q4 FY26 call (22 May 2026): Plant 6 commercial in H1 FY27; no organic capex in FY27 other than maintenance; next expansion when utilisation nears ~60%, maybe FY28; TGI to recover from codeine loss (~₹50–60 cr/yr product) with alternates; injectables "catching up"; working capital to stay within ±10–15 days [Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27 call (11 Aug 2026): Plant 6 at end-Q2 (±15 days), full depreciation (~₹3 cr/quarter) from Q3; TGI hit acknowledged as lasting "a few quarters"; ₹1,100 cr capacity called "an easy milestone"; maintenance capex ₹12–15 cr/yr; brownfield oral-solid expansion takes ~1 year [Q1 call].
- Delivery: 14th straight record-revenue quarter; CDMO +29%; exports +79%; TGI –32% — the codeine gap is not yet filled [IP Q1FY27].

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ₹73 cr (mostly Plant 6); gross block ₹426 cr; CWIP ₹60 cr at Mar-26 [IP Q1FY27 p.22; Screener].
- Plant 6 is an oral-solid expansion of the core (not US/EU-grade); new depreciation ~₹3 cr/quarter in year one [Q1 call].
- Injectables: utilities and area ready for a second line; adding machinery takes 6–8 months once decided [Q1 call].
- Asset turn: revenue ₹904 cr on ₹426 cr gross block ≈ 2.1x [IP Q1FY27; my arithmetic].
- New dosage forms not yet present: ointments, steroids, beta-lactams, softgels, hormones, eye drops — no firm plans [Q4 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net liquidity ₹251 cr, debt-free on a net basis (Mar-26); operating cash flow ₹105 cr in FY26 (115% of operating profit); free cash flow ₹37 cr after Plant 6 capex [Q4 call; Screener].
- Working-capital days 14 (FY26), cash conversion cycle –24 days (payables 156 days exceed receivables 92 + inventory 40) [Screener].
- Buyback ₹47 cr (4.7 lakh shares at ₹1,000, ~2.23% of equity, tender route; promoters did not participate) completed Q1 FY27; FY26 dividend ₹13 cr (₹6.30/share), policy ~20% payout [IP Q1FY27 p.12].
- Rating ICRA A+/Stable, A1 (reaffirmed 7 Apr 2025) [AR FY26 Directors' report; IP Q1FY27]. The ICRA rationale page was not retrievable.

## Moat evidence
- Scale and breadth in domestic oral-solid contract manufacturing; 70–80 customer audits a year; cost-plus pricing with open cost sheets (API swings passed on) [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Low concentration (top customer 6.5%) and 99% formulation IP ownership make it a sticky partner [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Revised Schedule M raises compliance costs for small unorganised makers — management sees this as a differentiator [Q4 call].
- Pricing power limited: EBITDA ~12–14% for five years; management refuses giant plants because idle capacity invites low-margin deals [Screener; Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **ESOP concentration**: of 5,70,500 RSUs granted in Sep 2025 at ₹5 (face value) under ESOP Plan 2025, 3,15,000 (55%) went to CEO & CFO Komal Gupta (~₹35 cr at today's price); her FY26 salary rose to ₹2.66 cr from ₹0.90 cr [AR FY26 Directors' report ESOP annexure; note 41].
- Auditor: unmodified; key audit matter revenue recognition; audit trail at database level of SaaS software not available [AR FY26 auditor's report].
- No contingent liabilities disclosed; related-party dealings small (rent to promoters ~₹0.16 cr each, promoter salaries ₹1.6–2.4 cr) [AR FY26 notes 41, 46].
- Codeine cough-syrup ban removed a ~₹50–60 cr/yr product from TGI; the vertical fell 32% YoY in Q1 [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Uttarakhand retrospective minimum-wage hike lifted employee costs (+17% ex-ESOP) [Q1 call].
- Institutional (government) sales are lumpy [Q4 call].
- Windlas Inc., USA (wholly owned) being dissolved [AR FY26 Directors' report].
- Promoter holding rose to 63.31% after the buyback (promoters didn't tender) [Screener].

## My read
Windlas is a clean, cash-rich, well-run domestic contract maker: revenue has compounded ~20% for three years, working capital is negative, the balance sheet has ₹251 cr net cash, and it returned ₹60 cr in buybacks and dividends this year. The limits are a structural ~12–14% margin, a trade-generics arm still recovering from the codeine ban, and heavy ESOP costs (mostly to the CEO) that keep reported profit growth flat in FY27. At about 34x trailing earnings the price needs roughly 15% a year earnings growth, which looks achievable once ESOP charges fade in FY28 and Plant 6 fills. I would turn more positive if TGI returns to growth and margins hold near 13–14% adjusted with Plant 6 depreciation; negative if CDMO growth drops back below 15%.
