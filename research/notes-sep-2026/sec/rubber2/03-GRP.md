## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# GRP

## Executive Summary
GRP exists to close the loop in the global polymer and rubber supply chain, transforming end-of-life tires and waste plastics into high-value reclaimed materials. The company solves a dual mandate for global tire manufacturers and auto OEMs: reducing reliance on volatile fossil fuels and fulfilling increasingly stringent Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) regulations. 

GRP is a **Category Leader** in India's organized reclaim rubber space, having successfully transitioned from a commoditized recycler into an integrated circular materials platform. While legacy operations faced a cyclical crucible in FY26 due to US trade policies and raw material inflation, a massive Q1 FY27 turnaround proves the business has structurally evolved. With US tariff headwinds fully resolved, management is reinvesting regulatory windfalls (EPR credits) into next-generation pyrolysis and recovered carbon black (RCB), cementing an unassailable moat bolstered by their status as the first reclaim manufacturer globally to earn ISCC Plus certification.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
*   **What do they sell?** Reclaim rubber, next-gen silane-treated rubber, recycled polyolefins (PP/PE)/Nylon, custom die forms, pyrolysis oil, and upcoming Recovered Carbon Black (RCB). A highly lucrative secondary revenue stream is the generation and sale of EPR credits.
*   **Who pays them?** Global and domestic tire manufacturers, auto OEMs, packaging converters, and road surfacing contractors (for pyrolysis fuel oil). 
*   **How do they make money?** Through a mix of spot and short-term contracts for reclaimed materials. Additionally, they monetize EPR credits—generated exclusively via domestic raw material sourcing—by selling them to corporate buyers at floor prices (₹2.5–3.0) via B2B transactions. The company has formally regrouped its business into two highly synergistic segments: Rubber Recycling (Reclaim + Pyrova) and Others (Plastics & Wind).

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
*   **Widening Structural Synergies.**
*   **Evidence:** GRP is no longer just selling individual recycled products; it is offering top-tier global tire manufacturers a unified, sticky ecosystem (Reclaim + RCB + EPR Credits). The structural integration between Reclaim Rubber and Pyrova Energy (sharing feedstock, renewable energy, and customer acquisition channels) creates a margin floor that standalone recyclers cannot replicate. Imminent auto EPR norms (mandating recycled content by FY28) further create a captive domestic market.
*   **The Bear Case:** The moat remains vulnerable to global commodity price distortion. When virgin polypropylene prices crash or China dumps cheap materials, the economic spread for converters collapses, potentially nullifying the commercial incentive to buy GRP's recycled plastics if sustainability mandates are weakly enforced.

## Growth Vectors
*   **Where is the next $100M coming from?** The "Pyrova Energy" platform. GRP has outlined highly explicit targets: scaling Pyrova to 45,000 tons in FY27, adding another 30,000 tons by FY28, and reaching peak revenues of ₹250-300 Cr. The real margin inflection (targeting 18-20% segment EBITDA) relies on Phase 1B: the commissioning of the 12,000-ton Recovered Carbon Black (RCB) facility slated for October 2026, with customer approvals expected by Q4 FY27.
*   **Management Execution:** Management is demonstrating ruthless operational discipline. Despite severe raw material inflation in Q1 FY27, they successfully flexed their pricing power to expand consolidated EBITDA margins by 233 bps to 11%. Working capital improved by 8 days (to 86 days), allowing GRP to self-fund its ₹90-100 Cr FY27 capex program strictly through internal accruals and existing DFI lines without immediate equity dilution.

## Key Risks & Governance
*   **Forensic/Governance:** No major red flags. Management is highly transparent, delineating between structural improvements (synergies) and cyclical factors. While the balance sheet levered up previously to fund Pyrova, cash flow generation is significantly accelerating, and multi-year margin/revenue guidance has been explicitly provided, signaling peak internal visibility.
*   **Business Risks:** 
    *   **Execution Risk on RCB:** With US tariff risks now resolved, the primary risk has shifted internally. If top-tier tire manufacturers delay or reject RCB validations in FY27/FY28, Pyrova's output will be trapped in the low-margin industrial fuel segment.
    *   **Raw Material Inflation:** Input costs remain elevated. While GRP passed through costs effectively in Q1 FY27, hyper-inflation could eventually outpace pricing power and suppress the targeted 10-14% Reclaim margins. 
    *   **EPR Subsidization:** Net profitability is still insulated by EPR credits, though strong underlying Q1 FY27 core revenue growth (+26% YoY) indicates the operating business is becoming self-sustaining.

## Investment Thesis
*   **The Bull Case:** The cyclical inflection has officially materialized. Q1 FY27 delivered a massive 60% YoY EBITDA surge as US export volumes snapped back and the bleeding composite business was shuttered. GRP is structurally re-rating from a commoditized auto-ancillary into a high-multiple Circular Economy platform. As Pyrova scales to ₹300 Cr in revenue and RCB commercializes by Q4 FY27, investors are buying into a business where shared feedstock and renewable energy synergies will permanently lift the margin floor to 15%+ levels.
*   **The Bear Case:** The company's core operations remain captive to external validation and pricing dynamics. If virgin crude/polymer prices remain permanently depressed, or if the critical October 2026 physical commissioning of the RCB plant encounters technological delays, GRP’s heavily levered new capacity will struggle to generate a return on capital greater than its debt servicing costs.
