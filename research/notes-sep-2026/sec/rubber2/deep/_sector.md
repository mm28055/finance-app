# Rubber (synthetic latex/NBR, reclaim, crumb/recycling) — sector research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Covers the three listed names in this note: **Apcotex** (synthetic latex and nitrile rubber), **Tinna Rubber** (crumb rubber, rubberised bitumen, MRP, reclaim, EPR) and **GRP** (reclaim rubber, pyrolysis/rCB). All three sell mainly to tyre makers, rubber-goods makers, glove makers and road builders.

## Market size and growth
- India is the world's 3rd-largest natural rubber (NR) producer and 2nd-largest consumer; ~6,000 rubber-goods units; the automotive tyre sector takes ~50% of rubber consumption [Infomerics, "Rubber Industry" 2025, citing DSIR/Rubber Board].
- NR: production 8.57 lakh t in FY24 [Infomerics 2025]; ~9.05 lakh t in FY26 (+3.4%) [News: Helixtap, 2 Jun 2026, citing Rubber Board]. Consumption ~14.2 lakh t a year (FY24), so ~35–40% is imported [Infomerics 2025]. Compounded-rubber imports rose 42% in FY26 while direct NR imports fell [Helixtap, Jun 2026].
- Synthetic rubber (SR): India's SR output was 2.74 lakh t in H1 FY25 (+3.2%) against consumption of 4.29 lakh t (+10.5%) — roughly a third of SR is imported [Infomerics 2025]. For NBR specifically, Apcotex (21,000 t capacity, full) holds ~30% of Indian demand; the rest is imported — implying an Indian NBR market near 70,000 t (my calculation) [Apcotex Q1 FY27 call].
- Tyres: industry revenue ~₹1.36 lakh cr in FY26 [CRISIL Ratings, 24 Aug 2026]; revenues of seven large tyre makers grew 12.5% in 9M FY26 [ICRA, Mar 2026].
- Reclaim rubber: Indian reclaim consumption grew ~7% in CY2025 (+15% in tyre use) vs ~4% for all rubber [GRP AR FY26]. GRP claims ~21% of the domestic reclaim market and ~35% of Indian reclaim exports [GRP IP Q1 FY27].
- Waste tyres / EPR: cumulative EPR obligations of tyre producers in 2022–2025 ~6.20 mn t; certificates generated ~6.64 mn t from domestic tyres and ~2.56 mn t from imported tyres; >500 recyclers registered; by March 2026 registered recyclers had processed ~12.23 lakh t with ~11.69 lakh t of certificates generated [Tinna AR FY26 MD&A, citing CPCB portal]. Tinna projects formal ELT recycling could reach ~4 mn t a year by FY2030 (company view) [Tinna AR FY26].
- Roads: NHAI used 2.77 lakh t of crumb-rubber-modified bitumen (CRMB) in FY25, up from 1.69 lakh t in FY24 and 1.14 lakh t in FY23; PMB (polymer-modified) 2.04 lakh t in FY25 [MoEFCC TRC minutes, 14 Jul 2025]. India makes ~5.5 mn t of bitumen a year [same] and imports 3–3.5 mn t (Tinna's estimate) [Tinna Q4 FY26 call].
- Emulsion polymers (Apcotex's market): Indian market forecast to grow >8% CAGR over the next decade (Future Market Insights, cited) [Apcotex AR FY26 MD&A].

## Demand drivers
- **Tyres** (main buyer for all three): ICRA estimates domestic tyre demand +5–7% (units) in FY27, replacement +5–7%, OEM +4–6%; exports under pressure from the West Asia conflict (West Asia = 8–10% of India's tyre exports) [ICRA, Mar 2026]. CRISIL (Aug 2026) is lower: FY27 volumes +4–5%, exports +3–4% [CRISIL Ratings, 24 Aug 2026].
- High virgin prices push substitution: virgin rubber ~₹240–260/kg vs recycled rubber ~₹50/kg [Tinna Q4 FY26 call]; reclaim is "looked at as a fairly strong alternative" when SR/carbon black are tight [GRP Q1 FY27 call].
- **Gloves** (nitrile latex, ~8–20% of Apcotex revenue): US tariffs on China-made gloves rose to 50% from 1 Jan 2025 and 100% from 1 Jan 2026 [News: The Edge Malaysia, Feb 2025; Apcotex Q1 FY27 call]; this shifts orders to Malaysia/Thailand/Vietnam — Apcotex's customers. New Chinese glove capacity in Southeast Asia from 2026 is a supply risk [News: Bernama, May 2025]; a new Malaysian nitrile-latex plant was starting in July–August 2026 [Apcotex Q1 call].
- **Roads**: bitumen shortage and prices "almost doubled" after the West Asia war made rubberised bitumen the cheaper option in Q1 FY27 [Tinna Q4 and Q1 FY27 calls].
- **ESG / circularity**: global tyre makers seek recycled content; ISCC Plus certification (GRP) and life-cycle CO2 data (Tinna: 10.37 mn kg CO2 avoided) are sales tools [GRP Q1 call; Tinna Q1 call]. Auto recycled-plastics rules help GRP's plastics [GRP Q1 call].

## Policy
- **Waste-tyre EPR** (Hazardous Waste Rules, Schedule IX, notified July 2022): tyre producers/importers must buy certificates equal to 100% of the weight of tyres made/imported two years earlier (Y-2) from FY2025-26 on; certificates generated by registered recyclers on the CPCB portal [CPCB/PIB via search; Tinna AR FY26]. Certificates trade near the floor price — ~₹2,500 per unit (Tinna) / ~₹2.52/kg (GRP) [Tinna Q1 call; GRP Q4 call]. EPR income is material: ~₹29 cr a year for Tinna (~40% of FY26 PBT) and ₹20 cr for GRP in FY26 (more than its PBT) [company calls; Screener].
- **Import rule**: imported waste tyres may not be used to make pyrolysis oil or char; EPR credits are not available on products from imported tyres [CPCB via PIB search result; Tinna Q4 FY26 call]. CPCB revised the pyrolysis SOP in January 2025 [PIB via search]. Down To Earth (Nov 2025) says loopholes persist — all tyre forms share one customs code, and illegal pyrolysis units continue [Down To Earth, 3 Nov 2025].
- **CRMB mandate (proposed)**: the MoEFCC Technical Review Committee recommended that refiners be given a minimum crumb-rubber-modifier use of **3% of bitumen output in FY27, 4% in FY28 and 5% from FY29**, subject to consultation with MoRTH and MoPNG — **a recommendation, not yet law** [MoEFCC TRC minutes, 14 Jul 2025]. The committee also noted domestic waste tyres are insufficient, so a mandate would raise waste-tyre imports [same]. At 5.5 mn t bitumen, 3–5% implies 1.7–2.8 lakh t of crumb rubber a year (my calculation).
- **Anti-dumping on NBR**: DGTR investigated NBR from China, EU, Korea and Russia on Apcotex's application (initiated 26 Sep 2024; final findings 25 Sep 2025) [DGTR case page]; the Finance Ministry did not notify the recommended duty [Apcotex Q4 FY26 call; ICRA on Apcotex, Aug 2026]. Apcotex is now doubling NBR without duty support [Apcotex Q1 call].
- **Plastics EPR** (GRP, Tinna PCMB): tightening; Tinna registered as a plastics recycler from 1 Apr 2026 [Tinna Q1 call; GRP Q1 call].

## Cost drivers
- NR price: ~₹220/kg average in FY26 → ~₹275/kg in June 2026; key tyre inputs up 35–40% [CRISIL Ratings, 24 Aug 2026]. RSS-4 peaked at ~US$2,740/t in May 2026 [Helixtap, Jun 2026].
- Crude/petrochemicals: crude up ~70% in a couple of months from March 2026 [Apcotex Q4 call]; styrene, butadiene, acrylonitrile drive Apcotex's costs (formula pass-through with a lag) [ICRA on Apcotex, Aug 2026].
- Scrap-tyre feedstock and freight: Tinna imports ~55% of raw material [CARE on Tinna, Oct 2025]; GRP saw a 43% rise in one raw-material SKU in FY26 [GRP Q4 call].
- Energy: dual-fuel plants (Apcotex) and captive solar (Tinna 4.48 MW, GRP 48% renewable) cut exposure [company calls].

## Competitive structure and share
- **NBR / nitrile latex / HSR**: Apcotex is the only Indian maker; competes with imports (Korea, China, Russia) [ICRA on Apcotex, Aug 2026]. Synthetic latex: few imports into India; global expansions now "staggered" [Apcotex Q1 call].
- **Reclaim rubber**: GRP is India's largest and a global top-three maker [CRISIL on GRP, Dec 2025]; the rest is fragmented.
- **Crumb / rubberised bitumen / MRP**: Tinna is the largest organised player (1.85 lakh t crushing capacity) among >500 registered recyclers [Tinna Q1 call; Tinna AR FY26]. IOCL makes most of India's CRMB; BPCL makes none [MoEFCC TRC, Jul 2025]. Entry barriers in basic crumb are low (management admits this) [Tinna Q4 call].
- **Pyrolysis / rCB**: many small, often non-compliant pyrolysis units [Down To Earth, Nov 2025]; organised entrants (Tinna, GRP) are only now commissioning rCB; tyre-maker approval takes 2–5 years [Tinna Q4 call; GRP Q1 call].

## Cycle position
- Synthetic latex/nitrile: coming out of a post-COVID glut (FY24–FY25 margins 8–10% at Apcotex) into a war-driven spike (Q1 FY27 margin 22%); management's through-cycle view is 15–16% [Apcotex Q1 call; Screener].
- Tyres: CRISIL calls FY27 margin compression (to 11.5–12% from 14.2% in FY26) a "cost-pass-through lag rather than a structural profitability reset", with recovery to 13–13.5% in FY28 [CRISIL Ratings, 24 Aug 2026]. Tyre makers under margin pressure will push harder for cheaper reclaim/crumb but resist input price rises.
- Recycling: mid-investment cycle — Tinna and GRP both have pyrolysis/rCB plants 1–8 months behind schedule, with free cash flow negative for three years at both [company files].

## Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecaster named)
- Tyre demand: +5–7% units in FY27 (ICRA, Mar 2026); +4–5% volume in FY27, margins 11.5–12% then 13–13.5% in FY28 (CRISIL Ratings, Aug 2026).
- CRMB: if the TRC recommendation is notified, a 3%/4%/5% refinery CRM mandate for FY27/FY28/FY29 (MoEFCC TRC, Jul 2025) — the biggest single demand lever for crumb rubber.
- Reclaim: GRP revenue growth 9–11% a year after FY26, EBITDA margin 9–11% medium term (CRISIL on GRP, Dec 2025); GRP itself targets reclaim volume mid-teens growth and 10–14% margin (GRP Q1 call).
- Tyre recycling: Tinna targets ₹1,000 cr revenue by FY29 (company); ELT formal recycling ~4 mn t by FY2030 (Tinna AR, company view).
- Emulsion polymers: >8% CAGR in India over the decade (Future Market Insights, cited in Apcotex AR); Apcotex volume +~40% after FY28 capacity (company).
- Gloves: MARGMA guided to stronger 2026 demand (~398 bn pieces, as reported via search summary — not verified at source).

## Key sector findings (for the note)
1. **EPR is the profit hinge for recyclers**: certificates trade at the regulatory floor and made up ~40% of Tinna's FY26 PBT and more than all of GRP's FY26 PBT; any change in the Y-2 obligation formula, floor price or fake-certificate enforcement moves their earnings directly.
2. **Roads could become the growth engine for crumb rubber**: NHAI's CRMB use rose 2.4x in two years (1.14 → 2.77 lakh t), and the proposed 3–5% refinery CRM mandate for FY27–FY29 would add 1.7–2.8 lakh t of demand — but it is still a recommendation, and domestic scrap is short.
3. **Synthetic rubber is import-substitution without tariff help**: India imports roughly a third of its SR and ~70% of its NBR; the Finance Ministry declined to notify NBR anti-dumping duty, so Apcotex's cheap debottlenecking (₹130–140 cr to nearly double NBR) is the only route to share gain. Raw-material-driven margin spikes (Q1 FY27) should be treated as windfalls, with CRISIL expecting tyre customers' margins to compress in FY27.
