# GRP Ltd — deep research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Screener code GRPLTD (consolidated: GRP + GRP Circular Solutions Ltd (GCSL, polyolefins) + Gripsurya Recycling LLP (GSPL)). Price ₹1,999, market cap ₹1,066 cr, P/E 156 on TTM PAT of ~₹6 cr, ROCE 6.9%, ROE 2.5%, dividend yield 0.18% [Screener, 27 Sep 2026]. Promoters (Gandhi family) hold 40.05%; institutions almost nil [Screener].

## Business and segments
- India's largest and one of the world's top three reclaim-rubber makers (reclaim = de-vulcanised rubber from scrap tyres/tubes that tyre makers blend back in) [CRISIL Dec-25]. Claims ~21% of the Indian market and ~35% of India's reclaim exports; sells to 8 of the top 10 global tyre makers; ~50% of revenue from exports; 120,000+ MTPA capacity across five locations (Ankleshwar, Panoli, Solapur and others) [IP Q1FY27].
- Other lines: custom die forms (rubber sheets, exported mainly to the US), engineering plastics (recycled nylon for auto parts), repurposed polyolefins via subsidiary GCSL, windmills; polymer composites (US contract manufacturing) shut in Q3 FY26 because of US tariffs [Q4 call; AR FY26 Directors' report].
- New: **Pyrova Energy** — tyre pyrolysis at Solapur: India's largest single-line continuous reactor (15,000 TPA, commissioned Oct 2025) plus crumb-rubber plant; recovered carbon black (rCB) plant under commissioning [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- Segments were regrouped in Q1 FY27 into Rubber Recycling (reclaim + die forms + Pyrova) and Others (engineering plastics, windmill, residual composites) [Q1 call]. Q1 FY27 standalone: Rubber Recycling ₹143.7 cr (+34% YoY); Others ₹11.9 cr (−1%); total ₹155.7 cr split ~51% domestic / 49% exports [IP Q1FY27].
- FY26 standalone segment note (old grouping): Reclaim Rubber net revenue ₹464.4 cr (FY25 ₹471.8 cr), segment result ₹53.9 cr (₹77.9 cr); Others ₹61.7 cr, result ₹1.8 cr (₹6.6 cr); unallocated costs −₹26.3 cr [AR FY26 segment note].
- Exports to the US and Europe were ~60% of export revenue in FY25 and H1 FY26 [CRISIL Dec-25].

Domestic reclaim margins are 200–250 bp below export margins [Q4 call]. EPR certificates trade near the floor of ~₹2.52/kg; ~85% sold under quarterly contracts with tyre makers [Q4 call].

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (18 May 2026): no numeric guidance; reclaim margins to recover through FY27 as US volumes return; rCB "in the second half of the year"; Phase 1B (rCB + more pyrolysis lines) complete "by February 2027 in phases"; FY27 capex ₹90–100 cr; second new-technology reclaim line by end-Q1 (350 → ~700 t/month); pyrolysis EPR approvals expected within weeks, retrospective accrual "definitely by H1" [Q4 call].
- Q1 call (27 Jul 2026): first explicit outlook — FY27 revenue growth ~20%+; reclaim volume "close to 20%" (later in the call "double-digit"); reclaim volume mid-teens growth for three years with EBITDA margin 10–14% (from ~9–10%); Pyrova to 45,000 TPA, +30,000 TPA in FY28, margin 18–20% (also stated as 15–20%) once rCB is approved; Pyrova revenue ₹250–300 cr at maturity; plastics >20% growth in FY27, mid-teens to FY30, margin 10–15% (also stated 12–15%) [Q1 call].
- Q1 delivery vs Q4 hopes: margin recovery delivered (11% vs 6–8% run-rate); US direct volumes back; indirect volumes (Thailand/Vietnam/Indonesia tyre plants supplying the US) "not fully recovered" [Q1 call]. The Q4 promises on the second new-tech reclaim line and pyrolysis EPR accrual were **not updated** on the Q1 call — EPR income of ₹4.9 cr shows no sign of a retrospective pyrolysis accrual [Q1 call].
- Timeline slippage: CRISIL (Dec 2025) expected Phase 1B commissioning in May–June 2026 with some cost overrun [CRISIL Dec-25]; now rCB commissioning is October 2026, meaningful sales in Q4 FY27, tyre-industry approval "several quarters" after that and maturity in FY28 — **a slip of about four to eight months** [Q1 call].
- ₹250 cr overall investment plan (announced ~18 months earlier) kept; ~₹100 cr still to be committed, possibly spilling into FY29 [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- FY24–FY26 consolidated capex ~₹170 cr: ~50% Pyrova, 30–35% reclaim (capacity + new technology), ~15% other (captive solar/wind SPVs, plastics debottlenecking) [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Pyrova cumulative investment ₹79 cr (March 2026) → ₹91 cr (June 2026) [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- FY27 capex ₹90–100 cr: two more pyrolysis lines (to 45,000 TPA), 12,000 TPA rCB plant, reclaim debottlenecking [Q1 call; Q4 call]. Crumb-rubber capacity ~65,000 TPA after this phase [Q4 call].
- Phase 3: integrated crumb/TPO/rCB unit in Gujarat (Dahej), +30,000 TPA pyrolysis and 12,000 TPA rCB, "through Oct 2027"; FY28 capex decision in H2 FY27 [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- Reclaim capacity to go from ~88 KTA to ~110 KTA and crumb/TPO/rCB from ~39 KTA to ~130 KTA over FY27–FY30 [IP Q1FY27]. 38% of reclaim capacity moved to the new process in FY26, saving ~5% on cost [Q4 call].
- Energy SPVs: 26.43% stake in BECIS Solar 5 (8 MW captive solar in Gujarat), ₹2.60 cr rights subscription completed 10 Aug 2026; the company calls it a related-party transaction at arm's length [BSE filing, 12 Aug 2026].
- Payback (my maths): ₹250–300 cr Pyrova revenue at 15–20% margin = ₹40–60 cr EBITDA on ~₹180–200 cr of cumulative Pyrova capex — roughly a 4-year payback **only after** rCB approval; until then TPO sells as low-grade furnace-oil substitute to road builders [Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹208 cr at March 2026 (₹147 cr March 2025, ₹113 cr March 2024) [Screener]; debt/equity 1.15 (0.76) [Q4 call]. Includes a EUR 12 mn (~₹126 cr) ECB sanctioned by Proparco (French DFI), ~₹75 cr drawn by September 2025 [CRISIL Dec-25; Q4 call].
- Cash ~₹24 cr (September 2025); working-capital limits ~90% utilised over 12 months [CRISIL Dec-25].
- FY26 operating cash flow ₹30 cr, investing −₹56 cr, free cash flow −₹16 cr; FCF negative three years running (−₹30 cr, −₹18 cr, −₹16 cr) [Screener].
- FY26 debtor days 75, inventory days 69, payable days 38, cash conversion cycle 105 days [Screener]; improved by 8 days to 86 days by June 2026 (company measure) [IP Q1FY27].
- Consolidated equity fell from ₹191.6 cr to ₹178.4 cr in FY26 despite a profit, because other comprehensive income was −₹7.0 cr, mostly a ₹13.3 cr pre-tax loss on the cash-flow hedge reserve (the euro loan hedge) [AR FY26 consolidated P&L; IP Q1FY27].
- Dividend ₹3.5/share for FY26 (payout 58% of a tiny profit) [Q4 call; Screener].
- Rating: **CRISIL downgraded GRP to BBB+/Stable/A2 from A-/A2+ on 10 Dec 2025**, citing weaker operating performance and debt metrics [CRISIL Dec-25].
- A planned QIP lapsed without issue in FY26 [Q4 call]; management says deleveraging timing is "not entirely in our hands" and faster Gujarat expansion needs deleveraging [Q1 call].

## Moat evidence
- Scale and customer approvals: 8 of top 10 global tyre makers; 55+ countries; 400+ customers; first reclaim maker to get ISCC Plus certification [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- Market share: ~21% domestic reclaim; domestic share up ~1 point in FY26 [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]. Indian reclaim consumption grew 7% in CY2025 (15% in tyre applications) vs 4% for all rubber [AR FY26 Directors' report].
- Pricing: contracts reset quarterly, half-yearly or annually, so cost pass-through lags; a 43% jump in one raw-material SKU hurt FY26 margins until April 2026 price resets [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Weaknesses: US tariff shock showed customers can move volumes to other suppliers; composites business closed; plastics hit by cheap Chinese virgin polypropylene [Q4 call; AR FY26].

## Risks and red flags
- **Profit quality**: FY26 core business barely broke even before EPR; FY25 profit was flattered by ₹21.4 cr of prior-period EPR accruals [Q4 call; AR FY26 Directors' report].
- **Balance sheet stretch**: rising debt, negative FCF, 90% working-capital utilisation, CRISIL downgrade, lapsed QIP [CRISIL Dec-25; Screener; Q4 call].
- **Execution**: rCB is a first for GRP; tyre-maker approval takes "several quarters"; Phase 1B already late [Q1 call; CRISIL Dec-25].
- Auditor (Rajendra & Co.): unqualified opinion; **key audit matter on contingent liabilities** from uncertain tax positions [AR FY26 auditor's report]. Standalone contingent items at March 2026: GST ₹6.65 cr, income tax ₹3.14 cr, sales tax ₹0.71 cr, excise/service tax ₹0.20 cr, other claims ₹1.68 cr, bank guarantees ₹8.37 cr, guarantee for subsidiary loans ₹6.53 cr [AR FY26 note 39].
- Related parties: mainly subsidiaries — loan to subsidiary ₹8.5 cr outstanding, purchases from GCSL ₹9.9 cr, ₹5 cr equity into GCSL; KMP pay ₹5.2 cr vs standalone PAT of ₹8.9 cr; professional fees ₹0.68 cr paid to KMP-side [AR FY26 note 41]. Management itself says it is reassessing the operating model of the polyolefins subsidiary [AR FY26 Directors' report].
- Governance/disclosure: Executive Chairman Rajendra Gandhi ceased to be KMP in July 2025; company secretary left April 2026 [AR FY26 note 41]. The Directors' report says FY26 PAT "took hit of 5%" when it fell 76% (₹37.9 cr → ₹8.9 cr standalone) [AR FY26 Directors' report] — careless drafting.
- EPR rule risk: AR itself flags non-compliant recyclers and demand-supply imbalance in certificates [AR FY26 risk section].
- Forex: ECB in euros (hedged, but hedge losses hit equity) [AR FY26].

## My read
GRP's core reclaim franchise is real and Q1 FY27 showed a genuine margin recovery once US tariffs eased and prices were reset. But the group is mid-way through a debt-funded move into pyrolysis and rCB that is late, still loss-making and months away from tyre-maker approval, while EPR income has been carrying reported profit. At ~156 times trailing earnings the price needs about 39% a year earnings growth for a decade — it is pricing the FY28–FY29 targets (reclaim 10–14%, Pyrova 18–20%) as if already delivered. I would change my view on evidence of rCB approvals by a major tyre maker and FCF turning positive; a further rating cut or another rCB delay would make it worse.
