# MV Electrosystems (MVELECTRO) — deep research, Sep 2026

Price ₹814 (52-week range ₹510–948; IPO price ₹425), market cap ₹2,220 cr, loss-making (no P/E), ROE −31%, ROCE −15% [Screener, 27-Sep-26]. Listed on NSE and BSE on 6-Aug-26 after a ₹290 cr fresh-issue IPO (68.2 lakh shares at ₹425) [AR FY26 Directors' Report].

## Business and segments
- Two businesses: **rail propulsion** — indigenous IGBT-based 3-phase propulsion (traction and auxiliary converters, vehicle control units, driver consoles) for 6,000 HP-class electric locomotives, with MEMU/EMU propulsion in development; and **rail electrification and cable products** — switchgear panels, cable protection/management, and an automatic fault locator (AFL) for 25 kV lines via a tie-up with PNC of South Korea [IP Q1FY27; Official Q1 transcript; AR FY26].
- Approval history: 50,000 km trial completed and CLW prototype clearance received in September 2025; now one of the approved suppliers alongside Siemens, Alstom, ABB, Medha and BHEL [Official Q1 transcript; IP Q1FY27].
- Customers: Indian Railways production units (CLW, MCF Raebareli); ~25–26% of the last locomotive-propulsion tender quantity won (L1 in one tender, L2 in two) [Official Q1 transcript].
- Order book 30-Jun-26: 564 locomotive propulsion systems worth ₹989.32 cr (₹921.64 cr supply + ₹67.68 cr three-year AMC) — ~₹1.63 cr supply value a set (management: ₹1.67 cr); developmental orders ₹94.7 cr incl. 6 MEMU propulsion sets (₹91.2 cr) and a composite-converter order; other orders ~₹20 cr [IP Q1FY27 slide "Strong Order Visibility"]. Total ≈ ₹1,100 cr, 22x FY26 revenue.
- Unit values quoted: ~₹16 cr of electrics per 12-car EMU; ~₹40 cr per 16-car Vande Bharat; AFL ₹12–36 lakh per unit (~₹1 cr per substation section) [Official Q1 transcript].
- Capacity: certified 114 propulsion systems a year (Unit 1) plus 171 (Unit 2, June 2026) — 285 a year combined; management says it can make 55 a month but will run at ~40 [IP Q1FY27; Official Q1 transcript]. R&D team 45 people; R&D spend ₹7.90 cr in FY26 (16% of revenue), expensed not capitalised [IP Q1FY27; Official Q1 transcript].

## Key numbers
- Quarterly sales (₹ cr): Jun-25 13.45 → Mar-26 14.93 → Jun-26 12.77. EBITDA −3.5 → −5.9 → −5.4 (margin −26% → −39% → −42%). Net profit −5.7 → −2.9 → −6.9 [Screener; IP Q1FY27].
- Annual: FY24 revenue ₹50.0 cr, EBITDA ₹5.8 cr (11.6%), PAT ₹0.6 cr; FY25 ₹62.6 cr, ₹6.9 cr (11.0%), ₹1.4 cr; FY26 ₹49.4 cr (−21%), EBITDA −₹10.3 cr, PAT −₹12.6 cr; gross margin collapsed 47.6% → 40.5% → 29.4% (Q1 FY27: 8.8%) [IP Q1FY27 annual table; Screener].
- Why: manufacturing staff hired ahead of propulsion production, high material prices, R&D (~₹2 cr in Q1), and only small pilot batches shipped [IP Q1FY27; Official Q1 transcript].
- No propulsion deliveries in July–August 2026; first 10 sets planned for September [Official Q1 transcript].

## Guidance history (first call as a listed company — no earlier call to compare)
- **FY27 revenue ₹400 cr** — "we will achieve it definitely" [Official Q1 transcript]. Delivery plan: 10 sets in September, 25 in November, 40 a month from January; ~70 sets by end-Q3 FY27; balance of the 564 in FY28 [Official Q1 transcript; IP Q1FY27]. My arithmetic at ~₹1.63 cr a set: ~₹115 cr from the first 70 sets and ~₹195 cr from 120 sets in Q4, plus ~₹12–15 cr a quarter of other products ≈ ₹350–360 cr — so ₹400 cr needs a faster ramp than the plan shown.
- **Margins:** PAT "10% plus" once at 40 sets a month (from about January); "profitable in the next 2–3 months" [Official Q1 transcript].
- **Run-rate FY28:** ~40 sets a month ≈ ₹70 cr a month of revenue [Official Q1 transcript].
- **Contract timing:** DRHP-stated completion concern (December 2026) — management says supply runs to March 2027 and partly FY28, and that Railways grants extensions [Official Q1 transcript].
- **MEMU:** design in 8–10 months, approval ~15–16 months in total; 24-month supply window [Official Q1 transcript].
- **New orders:** CLW tender for 748 locomotive propulsion sets open; BLW and PLW to follow; ~1,600–1,700 a year in total; ICF EMU and train-set tenders open — none bid yet [Official Q1 transcript].

## Capex and capacity
- Unit 2 set up (body fabrication/assembly started end-August 2026); no further machinery capex needed; three more test set-ups ordered, installation by December 2026 [Official Q1 transcript; IP Q1FY27].
- FY26 cash capex ~₹18 cr (investing cash flow); fixed assets ₹23 cr → ₹37 cr [Screener].
- Working capital need at full run-rate ~₹200 cr (management) — that is where the IPO money goes [Official Q1 transcript].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- FY26 operating cash flow −₹58 cr; FCF −₹76 cr; funded by ₹59.6 cr pre-IPO private placements (10.9 lakh shares at ₹547, pre-split) and higher bank lines (cash credit raised from ₹4.6 cr to ₹18 cr, plus ₹5.4 cr insta-loan and ₹7 cr machinery loan) [Screener; AR FY26 auditor's report CARO (xvii)].
- Mar-26: borrowings ~₹50 cr (₹40.4 cr current + ₹9.5 cr non-current), debt/equity 0.80; inventory ₹67.5 cr (₹31.8 cr) — about 1.4x FY26 revenue; inventory days 731, cash cycle 571 days [IP Q1FY27 balance sheet; AR FY26 note 43; Screener].
- Material for ~30 propulsion sets in stock; long-lead parts (IGBTs, gate drivers, capacitors) ordered post-IPO [IP Q1FY27].
- Cash loss FY26 ₹13.7 cr [AR FY26 note 42.14]. Post-IPO net cash should be large (₹290 cr raised in August); not yet reported.
- Management claims recent dispatches were paid in ~15 days and a full cycle of ~85 days (prospectus: 105 days) [Official Q1 transcript].
- Contingent liabilities small: claims ₹3.1 cr, bank guarantees ₹0.5 cr [AR FY26 note 34].

## Moat evidence
- Indigenous IP for locomotive propulsion recognised by DSIR; one of only a handful of approved suppliers globally/nationally; Railways tends to spread quantities so all 7 winners of the last tender got some quantity — "very, very safe business" per management [Official Q1 transcript].
- Won ~25–26% of the last tender's quantity as a new entrant [Official Q1 transcript].
- Limits: prices are fixed with no escalation clause (analyst's point, not denied); electronics costs rose; bids were "very close" among 6–7 suppliers; distributed-power (EMU/Vande Bharat) products need new approvals; AFL depends on Korean technology [Official Q1 transcript].

## Risks and red flags
- **Auditor's warnings (FY26):** emphasis of matter — no provision for interest on delayed payments to micro and small suppliers (₹4.9 cr owed to MSMEs of ₹21.9 cr payables); under CARO the auditor said persistent losses "warrant attention… with respect to the Company's medium and long-term financial sustainability", noted a cash loss, and concluded no material going-concern uncertainty only because of the equity infusion and bigger bank lines [AR FY26 auditor's report; note 42.13].
- **Related parties:** Quadrant Future Tek Ltd (another listed railway-signalling company) is a disclosed related party — MV sold it ₹13.7 cr in FY25 (≈22% of FY25 revenue) and ₹1.8 cr in FY26, and bought ₹2.6 cr from it in FY26; promoters (Mohit Vohra, Dhawan family) lent and were repaid ₹2.6 cr in FY26; MV Mobility, Prime Electronics and IBoard India are other promoter-linked counterparties [AR FY26 note 33].
- **Valuation:** market cap ₹2,220 cr vs FY26 revenue ₹49 cr and losses — the price discounts FY28 run-rate revenue of ~₹800 cr at 10% PAT almost fully.
- **Execution:** zero deliveries in July–August; ramp from 10 to 40 sets a month in four months with thousands of parts and 7-day to 6-month lead times [Official Q1 transcript].
- **Management awareness:** asked about a cancelled PLW tender (~127 sets), management said "none of us knows about any order which has got cancelled" [Official Q1 transcript].
- Single customer (Indian Railways); fixed prices; new competitors (e.g. Hirect as a developmental source) [Official Q1 transcript].

## My read
MV has done the hard part technically — an approved indigenous locomotive propulsion system and a ~₹1,000 cr order book — but it is a ₹50 cr-revenue, loss-making company whose auditor flagged sustainability before the IPO, and whose ramp had not begun at the time of the call. At ₹2,220 cr market cap the price already assumes the ramp to 40 sets a month works, margins reach 10% and new tenders keep the plant full. I would wait for evidence: Q2 and Q3 dispatch counts (10 in September, 70 cumulative by December), a positive EBITDA quarter, the CLW 748-set tender result, and clean handling of related-party sales to Quadrant Future Tek; slippage on any of these would matter a lot at this valuation.
