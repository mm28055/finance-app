# Titagarh Rail Systems (TITAGARH) — deep research, Sep 2026

Price ₹823, market cap ₹11,078 cr, P/E 57.0, book value ₹182, ROE 6.8%, ROCE 10.9%, dividend yield 0.12% [Screener, 27-Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- Three businesses: **Freight Rail Systems (FRS)** — wagons, bogies, couplers, castings, loco shells; **Passenger Rail Systems (PRS)** — metro cars, Vande Bharat sleeper trains (with BHEL), EMU/MEMU propulsion, traction motors; **Shipbuilding** — moved into a wholly owned subsidiary, Titagarh Naval Systems Ltd (TNSL), by slump sale for ₹114.88 cr effective 1-Jan-26 [AR FY26 note 36].
- FY26 consolidated segment split: FRS revenue ₹2,604 cr, segment profit ₹318 cr (12.2%); PRS revenue ₹539 cr, profit ₹77 cr (14.3%); Shipbuilding revenue ₹42 cr, loss ₹21 cr. Total revenue ₹3,186 cr [AR FY26 note 41].
- Q1 FY27 standalone segments: FRS ₹505 cr (68.7%), EBIT ₹61.7 cr (12.2%); PRS ₹230 cr (31.3%), EBIT ₹33.7 cr (14.7%). A year earlier PRS was ₹77 cr (11.5% of revenue) at 11.3% EBIT; Q4 FY26 PRS was ₹173 cr at 18.8% EBIT [IP Q1FY27 slide 14].
- Customers: Indian Railways (wagons, Vande Bharat), state metro corporations (Pune, Gujarat, Bangalore, Mumbai), private freight owners (JSW, Balmer Lawrie lease), Indian Navy (ships). Almost entirely domestic after the Firema (Italy) exit [Q4 call].
- Capacity: 1,000 wagons a month (12,000 a year; CRISIL confirms 12,000/yr) [Q1 call; CRISIL Mar-26]. Coach capacity being built toward 850 cars a year by FY29–FY30 [Q1 call]. FY26 wagon dispatches 7,019 (about 59% of capacity) [AR FY26 MD&A]; Q1 FY27 dispatches 1,284 wagons (about 43% of capacity) [IP Q1FY27].
- Order book 30-Jun-26: standalone incl. TNSL ~₹13,335 cr — PRS ~₹10,395 cr (81%), FRS ~₹2,470 cr (19%), shipbuilding ~₹470 cr. Plus share of JVs: forged-wheel JV ~₹6,300 cr and Vande Bharat maintenance JV with BHEL ~₹7,000 cr; total ~₹26,635 cr [IP Q1FY27 slide 7]. Physical backlog: ~5,300 wagons (all due in FY27); 491 metro cars and 1,280 Vande Bharat cars; 74 propulsion sets and 72 traction motors [IP Q1FY27 slide 9].

## Key numbers
- Quarterly sales (consolidated, ₹ cr): Jun-25 679 → Sep-25 799 → Dec-25 832 → Mar-26 875 → Jun-26 765. Operating profit 64 → 74 → 91 → 92 → 94; margin 9% → 9% → 11% → 11% → 12%. Net profit 31 → 37 → 45 → 54 → 53 [Screener].
- Q1 FY27 vs Q1 FY26: sales +13%, operating profit +47%, net profit +71%. Vs Q4 FY26 sales fell 13% because wagon output was cut deliberately [Screener; Q1 call].
- Annual: FY24 sales ₹3,853 cr, net profit ₹286 cr; FY25 ₹3,868 cr, ₹87 cr (after the Firema restatement); FY26 ₹3,186 cr (−18%), ₹123 cr. Operating margin 12% → 9% → 10%. TTM sales ₹3,272 cr, net profit ₹188 cr [Screener].
- 3-year sales growth is 5% a year and TTM sales are down 10% — the company is in a transition year, not a growth year yet [Screener].
- Unit economics: a metro car is worth about ₹10 cr; on Vande Bharat only ~51% of car value comes to Titagarh because it is a consortium with BHEL [Q1 call]. Wagon revenue potential at 1,000/month is ₹4,000–4,500 cr a year, i.e. roughly ₹33–38 lakh a wagon [Q1 call]. A forged wheel is ~₹1 lakh [Q1 call].
- Coach dispatches: 12 cars FY25 → 63 cars FY26 → 30 in Q1 FY27 (+400% YoY, +43% QoQ) [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].

## Guidance history
- **FY26 promise missed:** the strategic plan promised 100–120 coaches in FY26; actual was 63 [Q4 call].
- **FY27 coaches:** Q4 call — "extremely confident" of 200 cars in FY27. Q1 call — target "intact", ±10%, with a 45–50 cars/quarter run-rate. Arithmetic: 30 in Q1 plus 50 a quarter gives 180, the low end of the band. **Kept, but on the edge.**
- **Wagons:** Q4 call — FY27 run-rate "higher than last year" (FY26 was 7,019, ~585/month). Q1 call — cut to 600–650 a month until a big Railways tender arrives; Q1 actual 1,284 (428/month). **Effectively cut.** Management says no further cut is planned because 600/month keeps them slightly ahead of delivery dates [Q1 call].
- **Vande Bharat:** first train by end-Q3/start-Q4 FY27; 2 trains in FY27 [Q4 call]. Q1 call repeated the same window. **Kept.** It was originally due earlier; management blames the Railways' switch from 16 to 24 cars [Q4 call].
- **Wheel JV:** Q4 call — production to start "in June". Q1 call — hot trials under way, samples in August, invoicing "within this year". **Slipped 2–3 months.**
- **Shipyard (Falta):** Q4 call — production to start "within the current financial year". Q1 call — fully running "between Q1 and Q2 of the next financial year". **Slipped about two quarters.**
- **Metro:** Gujarat (34 trains/102 cars) to finish in FY27, maybe spilling one quarter; Bangalore in FY27 with possible 1–2 quarter spillover (depends on free-issue material from the client); Mumbai first train Q3–Q4 FY27 [Q4 call]. Q1 call: Mumbai prototype Q4 FY27 — **kept**.
- **Margins:** long-term PRS target about 15% (similar to or above freight's ~12%); Q4's 19% was flattered by the Bangalore contract where the client supplies material free [Q4 call]. Q1 PRS EBIT came in at 14.7% — consistent with that warning.
- Longer term: 850 cars a year by FY29–FY30 (~₹8,500–9,000 cr revenue potential) and ~₹4,000–4,500 cr from freight at full output — management stressed these are capacity, not forecasts [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- Group capex plan about ₹1,000 cr (CRISIL) [CRISIL Mar-26]. FY26 cash capex implied by Screener: CFO ₹322 cr less FCF −₹45 cr ≈ ₹367 cr. Fixed assets rose from ₹992 cr to ₹1,274 cr and CWIP to ₹192 cr [Screener].
- Coach capacity from 300 to 850 a year (CRISIL says by March 2027; management now says FY29–FY30) [CRISIL Mar-26; Q1 call].
- Aluminium coach line (extrusion to finished car, replacing flat-packs from Firema) to be commissioned by Q1 FY28; serves the 12-train Pune repeat order due Q3–Q4 FY28 and positions for high-speed trains [Q1 call].
- Falta shipyard: ~₹600–610 cr project, ₹169 cr approved under the Shipbuilding Financial Assistance Scheme (about 25%); balance via debt and equity in TNSL, with outside strategic investors sought and a possible separate listing later [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 18].
- Forged-wheel JV with Ramkrishna Forgings (49% Titagarh): ~₹2,000 cr project, 228,000 wheels a year; ₹500 cr equity put in by both partners; Railways buy 80,000 wheels a year for 20 years (~40% of capacity) [IP Q1FY27 slide 19; Q1 call]. At ₹1 lakh/wheel full capacity is ~₹2,280 cr revenue a year; Titagarh books only its 49% share of profit.
- Foundries converted to resin moulding so castings can support 1,000 wagons a month; power upgrades wait for order visibility [Q4 call].
- Promoter warrants: 21.1 lakh warrants at ₹947 (₹200 cr), 25% paid; ₹150 cr due before May 2027; promoter stake to rise to 41.44% on conversion [AR FY26; IP Q1FY27 slide 21; CRISIL Mar-26]. With the price at ₹823, conversion at ₹947 is below water today.

## Cash, debt and working capital
- 31-Mar-26 consolidated: borrowings ₹522 cr, cash ₹162 cr, net debt ₹360 cr, net debt/equity 0.15 (0.22 a year earlier) [AR FY26 note 45]. Screener shows borrowings of ₹623 cr (likely includes lease liabilities) [Screener].
- Cash flow: CFO +₹322 cr in FY26 after −₹97 cr in FY25; free cash flow −₹45 cr after capex [Screener]. Management said FY26 "free cash flow" was ₹380 cr [Q4 call] — this does not match Screener's definition; treat the management figure as operating cash before capex.
- Working capital (FY26): debtor days 72, inventory days 92, payable days 52, cash conversion cycle 113 days — worse than FY24 (75 days) [Screener]. Standalone debtor days 75 (59) and inventory days 84 (66) [AR FY26 ratios]. Management's normal target: freight ~30 net working-capital days, passenger ~75 days once scale is reached by end-FY27; public-sector payments slowed at year end [Q4 call].
- Contingent: financial undertaking to fund cash shortfalls of the wheel JV's loans — ₹504 cr at year end for Titagarh's share (₹170 cr a year earlier) [AR FY26 note 5.2(b)/7(d)].
- Dividend: ₹0.50/share paid for FY25; payout ~11–15% [AR FY26; Screener].
- CRISIL AA-/Stable/A1+ reaffirmed 30-Mar-26; liquidity "strong"; cash ~₹530 cr (as of CRISIL's review); ~55% average use of working-capital lines [CRISIL Mar-26].

## Moat evidence
- Share: 32% (24,177 wagons) of Indian Railways' wagon orders [CRISIL Mar-26].
- Only Indian maker with both stainless-steel and aluminium metro cars; Pune's first 34 trains are through their defect-liability period and won a 12-train repeat order [Q4 call]. This is the strongest proof of acceptance a new entrant can get.
- Propulsion in-house: two EMU rakes supplied; MEMU propulsion due by Q2–Q3 FY27; 263 traction motors dispatched in Q1 [Q1 call].
- 150 people in design engineering and R&D in PRS alone — a fixed-cost base that also acts as a barrier [Q4 call].
- 20-year Railways offtake for wheels via the JV; 35-year maintenance of 80 Vande Bharat trainsets via the 50:50 BHEL JV [IP Q1FY27 slides 19–20].
- Limits: freight margin is capped by Railways' price-variation clauses; CRISIL flags steel price exposure and Railways' bargaining power [CRISIL Mar-26]. The MRVC (Mumbai suburban) tender was cancelled and moved to ICF, a Railways factory — policy can shrink the private share [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Auditor qualified the internal-controls opinion (consolidated and standalone):** "material weaknesses" in reviewing impairment of investments in an associate and a JV, loss allowance on a loan to a JV, and provision for collateral given for an associate's loan. The financial statements themselves are unqualified [AR FY26 IFC report paras 8–10, p.214].
- **Emphasis of matter — restated FY25:** Firema's audited FY25 loss came in at €104.7 m (₹948 cr) versus €16.9 m in management accounts used earlier. Titagarh restated FY25: ₹112.7 cr impairment of investment plus ₹157.5 cr provision for collateral (total exceptional ₹270 cr); FY25 profit after tax dropped from a reported ₹317 cr (standalone) to ₹90.6 cr. A further ₹64.8 cr loss allowance was booked in FY26 [AR FY26 note 35A]. Management says nothing more is due [Q4 call].
- Company had pledged 3 million Firema shares for Firema's Bank of Baroda loan; Firema's business went to FS Group on 4-Mar-26 with nothing paid to shareholders [AR FY26 notes 35A, 5].
- Freight air-pocket: only ~5,300 wagons left, all due in FY27; a reported ₹40,000 cr, one-lakh-wagon Railways tender (35,000–40,000 wagons a year over 3–4 years) was still at the consultation stage in May and had not been floated by the Q1 call in August [IP Q1FY27; News: Swarajya, 26-May-26; Q1 call].
- Execution slips: FY26 coach target missed by ~45%; wheel JV and shipyard dates slipped between calls.
- Contingent liabilities: ₹187.5 cr of tax and legal claims (flat YoY) plus Gwalior municipal tax demand ₹7.2 cr [AR FY26 note 40].
- Customer concentration on Indian Railways; working capital cycle lengthening (113 days).
- ROE only 6.8% and 3-year profit growth 7% a year — the valuation assumes a steep ramp that has not yet shown in numbers [Screener].
- CARE, as monitoring agency for the warrant money, reported ₹150 cr allocated (₹50 cr working-capital repayment, ₹50 cr general purposes, ₹50 cr capex reimbursement), noted no general-purpose spending had taken place, and found no material deviation [News: Whalesbook, 14-May-26].
- Governance positives: no promoter share pledge disclosed; promoter holding flat at 40.46% for two years [Screener].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone: Q4 was upbeat (Firema closed, record PRS year, "cruising altitude" freight). Q1 was defensive — wagon output cut to 600–650/month, time spent on why the MRVC cancellation does not shrink the market.
- Numbers: wagon backlog ~6,500 (31-Mar) → ~5,300 (30-Jun); metro cars 522 → 491; total order book ₹27,540 cr → ₹26,635 cr — no large new orders in the quarter.
- PRS revenue ₹173 cr → ₹230 cr; PRS EBIT margin 18.8% → 14.7% (as flagged on Q4).
- New issues: MRVC tender cancelled; West Asia war cited as delaying Railways' priorities; hyperloop collaboration with TuTr (IIT Madras start-up) mentioned in the deck.

## My read
The business is changing for the better in mix — passenger work is now 81% of the standalone order book and 31% of revenue at a 14–15% EBIT margin — but the reported numbers have not caught up: FY26 sales fell 18% and FY27 depends on a freight tender that keeps slipping. The price (P/E 57, ROE under 7%) already pays for the 850-car, ₹8,500 cr passenger ambition; to earn it Titagarh must deliver roughly 180–200 coaches this year, the first Vande Bharat train by Q4 FY27 and a Railways wagon order by early FY28. The Firema restatement and the qualified internal-controls report are a governance mark that the market has largely ignored. I would turn more positive on a large Railways wagon award plus two quarters of 50+ coaches; more negative if Q2 coach dispatches stay near 30 or the Vande Bharat prototype slips into FY28.
