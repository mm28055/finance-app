# Jupiter Wagons (JWL) — deep research, Sep 2026

Price ₹227 (near the 52-week low of ₹224; high ₹358), market cap ₹9,690 cr, P/E 54.5, book value ₹69.7, ROE 6.4%, ROCE 9.1%, dividend yield 0.44% [Screener, 27-Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- Products: freight wagons (Indian Railways and private owners), commercial-vehicle load bodies and containers, CMS track crossings, wheels/axles/wheelsets (subsidiary Jupiter Tatravagonka Railwheel Factory, JTRWF), freight brakes (Stone India, 75% owned), passenger brakes (JV with DAKO-CZ), brake discs (JV with Kovis), and batteries/BESS plus a small electric truck (Jupiter Electric Mobility, JEM) [IP Q1FY27 slides 13–14; AR FY26].
- Revenue mix (CRISIL): about 75% wagons, 10% wheelsets, 10% CV bodies, 5% other components [CRISIL Apr-26].
- Order book 30-Jun-26: ~₹4,550 cr (deck). Split given on the call: wagons ~₹3,000 cr (about 7,000 wagons, ~80% private), wheelsets ~₹700 cr, CV bodies ~₹500 cr, BESS ~₹500 cr [IP Q1FY27 slide 9; Q1 call]. The book was ₹6,304 cr in Mar-25, ₹5,041 cr in Dec-25 and ₹4,675 cr in Mar-26 — shrinking for five quarters [CRISIL Apr-26; Q4 call].
- Volumes (Q1 FY27 / Q4 FY26 / Q1 FY26 / FY26): wagons 1,141 / 1,347 / 826 / 5,498; CV bodies 2,597 / 3,343 / 2,182 / 10,909; containers 311 / 279 / 339 / 1,460; axles 1,008 / 720 / 744 / 3,798; wheelsets 1,282 / 5,290 / 4,811 / 19,939; brake discs 5,160 / 6,356 / 5,199 / 26,267 [IP Q1FY27 slide 9, read from the table columns]. Wheelset output fell about 76% QoQ in Q1 — not explained on the call.
- JTRWF (wheels) FY26: revenue ₹522 cr (₹343 cr in FY25), EBITDA margin ~17% (12% in FY25), profit after tax ₹56.6 cr [Q4 call; AR FY26 AOC-1].
- Stone India FY26: turnover ₹36.3 cr, loss before tax ₹29.7 cr; RDSO approval received, commercial production from July 2026 [AR FY26 AOC-1; Q1 call].
- Wagon realisation about ₹41.5 lakh a wagon in Q1 FY27 vs ~₹38 lakh a year earlier [Q1 call].

## Key numbers
- Quarterly sales (consolidated, ₹ cr): Jun-25 459 → Sep-25 786 → Dec-25 890 → Mar-26 780 → Jun-26 671. Operating profit 59 → 103 → 113 → 79 → 65; margin 13% → 13% → 13% → 10% → 10%. Net profit 31 → 45 → 62 → 27 → 26 [Screener].
- Q1 FY27: revenue +46% YoY (against a Q1 FY26 hit by the wheelset shortage) but −14% QoQ; EBITDA ₹64.9 cr (9.7%, down 330 bps YoY); net profit ₹26.2 cr, −16% YoY [IP Q1FY27 slide 5]. Standalone EBITDA ₹65.7 cr is higher than consolidated — subsidiaries lost money at EBITDA level [IP Q1FY27 slide 8].
- Annual: FY24 sales ₹3,644 cr, profit ₹331 cr; FY25 ₹3,963 cr, ₹380 cr; FY26 ₹2,916 cr (−26%), ₹166 cr (−56%). Operating margin 13% → 14% → 12%. TTM sales ₹3,127 cr, profit ₹161 cr [Screener].
- 9M FY26 wagon dispatches were 4,151 vs 6,343 a year earlier (−35%) because of the wheelset shortage [CRISIL Apr-26].
- Return on net worth fell from 17.2% (FY25) to 6.4% (FY26); interest cover from 8.6x to 5.0x [AR FY26 MD&A ratios].

## Guidance history
- **FY27 revenue:** Q4 call — "much better than the previous financial year"; wagon execution better than FY26 (5,498 wagons) but a big jump only when a Railways order arrives [Q4 call]. Q1 delivered 1,141 wagons (4,564 annualised, below FY26) and revenue down 14% QoQ; management blamed a switch to new private wagon designs needing prototype approval and promised a pick-up from Q2 [Q1 call]. **Kept in words; behind in numbers.**
- **Wagon tender:** Q4 call — Railways order expected "within the next 2 quarters", covering ~3 years of needs. Q1 call — "cannot give you any information". **Softened.**
- **Inventory:** Q4 call — inventory had jumped from ₹769 cr to ₹1,079 cr; normal "from second quarter onwards" of FY27 [Q4 call]. Not yet testable.
- **Odisha wheel plant:** interim (axle line) March 2027, full (wheel line) March 2028 — interim already slipped two quarters because of shipping delays [Q4 call]. Q1 call repeated the same dates. **Kept since Q4.**
- **Stone India:** production from July 2026 [Q4 call] — **met** (production started July) [Q1 call]. Profitable from Q3 FY27; ~15% EBITDA from FY28 [Q1 call].
- **BESS (JEM):** Q4 call — ₹1,000 cr *revenue* business in 3–4 years. Q1 call — ₹1,000 cr *order book* by FY27 and ₹5,000 cr of orders over three years; JEM EBITDA-positive only from FY28. These are different measures; the revenue goal was not raised.
- **Passenger entry:** Q4 call — partnership "very shortly", entry in FY27. Q1 call — "very advanced stage", announcement "before the end of the year". **Slipping.**
- **Containers:** revenue to double in FY27 [Q4 call].
- **Long term:** ₹10,000 cr revenue by 2030 with at least 15% EBITDA [Q4 call] — roughly 3.2x TTM sales in 3.5 years.

## Capex and capacity
- Odisha greenfield forged wheel plant: capacity 1 lakh wheelsets plus 20,000 loose wheels a year; the annual report says investment of about ₹3,000 cr, the Q1 call says ₹2,600 cr [AR FY26; Q1 call]. CRISIL counts ₹2,500 cr capex over FY26–FY28, funded 65:35 debt to equity; term loans repay in 40 quarterly instalments from June 2029 [CRISIL Apr-26].
- Funding change: Lucchini RS (Italy) and SIMEST (Italian state finance arm) to put ~₹290 cr into JTRWF for 25%, cutting Jupiter's own equity need from ₹900 cr to ₹600 cr; Jupiter says its share is already infused [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 12]. The deal values JTRWF's equity at about ₹1,160 cr post-money (290 ÷ 25%).
- Revenue potential at full use ₹2,500–3,000 cr with EBITDA "at least 15%"; 50% captive/domestic, 50% export (Tatravagonka offtake of 20–30 thousand wheels a year; Lucchini handles export marketing) [Q1 call].
- Capital commitments not yet spent: ₹1,506 cr at 31-Mar-26, up from ₹705 cr [AR FY26 note 43B].
- Cash capex FY26 about ₹533 cr (CFO ₹9 cr less FCF −₹524 cr); CWIP up from ₹66 cr to ₹270 cr [Screener].
- BESS: 100 MW/400 MWh West Bengal projects (~₹400 cr) on a 15-year build-own-operate basis; funding (debt or internal cash) "yet to be ascertained"; project IRR "15% plus" [Q1 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹996 cr at Mar-26, double the ₹502 cr of Mar-25; debt/equity still low at 0.16 [Screener; AR FY26 ratios].
- Cash flow: operating cash only ₹9 cr in FY26 on ₹354 cr operating profit; free cash flow −₹524 cr (FY25 −₹403 cr, FY24 −₹143 cr) — three years of cash burn [Screener].
- Working capital (FY26): debtor days 95 (75), inventory days 188 (94), payable days 69, cash conversion cycle 213 days vs 119 a year earlier [Screener]. CRISIL puts gross current asset days at 181–208 [CRISIL Apr-26].
- Liquidity: cash and mutual funds ₹557 cr (Sep-25); bank limits ~60% used; CRISIL AA-/Stable/A1+ reaffirmed 16-Apr-26 on ₹2,241.5 cr of facilities [CRISIL Apr-26].
- Dividend payout 25% of FY26 profit [Screener].
- QIP money: ₹43.64 cr of December-2023 QIP funds earmarked for a foundry has sat idle over two years; the board (14-Aug-26) seeks to switch it to general corporate purposes — the second change to that object after an April-2025 variation [AR FY26 AGM notice item 14].

## Moat evidence
- One of the three large private wagon builders with RDSO and AAR (M-1003) approvals [AR FY26].
- Private-customer franchise: ~80% of the wagon book is private (JSW Rail and Port Logistics, CWC, Orissa Alloy Steel) — Q1 orders ₹264 cr + ₹211 cr [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Backward integration is real: in-house wheelsets (19,939 made in FY26), brake discs, CMS crossings, and now freight brakes (100% of own needs by end-2026) [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- Railways orders for 9,000 LHB axles and an LoI for 5,376 Vande Bharat wheelsets [Q4 call] — proof of quality acceptance.
- Partners: Tatravagonka (promoter and wheel buyer), Lucchini RS (largest global wheel maker), DAKO-CZ, Kovis, Talleres Alegria [IP Q1FY27 slide 14].
- Limits: private orders are mostly fixed-price, so steel price moves hit margins [CRISIL Apr-26]; a competitor (Titagarh–Ramkrishna JV) is building 228,000 wheels a year of capacity [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- Cash burn and working capital: CCC doubled to 213 days, operating cash near zero, debt doubled, while a ₹2,500–3,000 cr capex programme is only half done.
- Order book has shrunk five quarters in a row; wagon output in Q1 was below FY26's run-rate; Railways tender timing unknown.
- Diversification sprawl: wagons, wheels, brakes, containers, e-trucks, BESS (15-year build-own-operate) and a planned passenger entry — each needs capital and management time; subsidiaries dragged Q1 EBITDA.
- Related parties: FY25 capital goods worth ₹45.3 cr bought from Log9 Material Scientific, an entity where the company or KMP has significant influence; small consultancy/advance dealings with KMP-linked Karisma Goods [AR FY26 note 46]. Tatravagonka is both a promoter and a key customer/partner.
- Contingent liabilities (tax/excise): ₹54.4 cr, unchanged [AR FY26 note 43A].
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; key audit matter is revenue recognition; no emphasis of matter found [AR FY26 auditor's report p.283]. CARO: no loans raised on pledge of subsidiary shares [AR FY26 CARO].
- Promoter holding 68.3%, stable; no pledge shown [Screener].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone: Q4 was defensive (LPG and consumable shortages, wheelset crisis); Q1 was promotional (Lucchini deal, BESS wins) while core wagon output fell.
- Numbers: order book ₹4,675 cr → ~₹4,550 cr; wagons 1,347 → 1,141; EBITDA margin 10.7% → 9.7%; wagon realisation ₹41.5 lakh vs ₹38 lakh.
- New: Lucchini/SIMEST 25% stake in JTRWF; 100% ownership of JTRWF bought (last 1.94%); West Bengal BESS win; Stone India production started. Dropped: the "tender in 2 quarters" line.

## My read
The core wagon business is stuck between a finished Railways order cycle and a new one that has not been placed, and Jupiter is spending heavily (₹1,500 cr of commitments) while operating cash flow has dried up. The long-term pieces are good — a Lucchini-backed wheel plant, in-house brakes and a private-customer franchise — but none earns meaningfully before FY28. At P/E ~55 on depressed FY26 profit, the price needs profit to roughly triple by FY29, which needs both the Railways tender and Odisha on time. I would get more positive on a large Railways order plus inventory days falling back toward 100; more negative if Q2 wagons stay near 1,100 or the BESS projects are debt-funded.
