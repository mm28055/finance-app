# Indian Construction and EPC: Sector View (September 2026)

*Research date: 25 Sep 2026. It draws on the 32 company files in this folder (cited as [file NN]) and on outside sources from the last 12 months (rating agencies, the Union Budget and PRS analysis, and press). All figures are in ₹ unless stated. Where a number comes only from management commentary, it is labelled as such.*

## 1. Summary

- Indian construction is in the second year of a slowdown driven by awarding.
  - ICRA estimates industry revenue growth of only **2–4% in FY26** after a flat FY25, recovering to **6–8% in FY27**. [ICRA Mar-26]
  - Operating margins are expected to slip to **10.1–10.6% in FY27**, against 13–14% in FY21. [ICRA Mar-26]
- Roads have been hit hardest.
  - National-highway awards fell from a peak of 12,731 km (FY22) to about 7,000 km (FY26). Construction pace is expected to fall to **about 21 km/day in FY27** from a peak of 36.8 km/day in FY21. [CareEdge Sep-26 via Tribune]
- Money has not run out. The FY27 Union Budget raised central capex 11.5% to about ₹12.2 lakh crore, with roads at ₹3.10 lakh crore and railways at ₹2.81 lakh crore of ministry allocation. [PRS Feb-26] [News: Business Standard/Newsonair, Feb-26]
- The bottleneck is project readiness and land. Road awarding shifted deliberately to projects that already have land and approvals, and the West Asia war added a cost shock in 2026: bitumen roughly doubled, and steel, aluminium, freight and labour all rose. [News: Autocar Pro, 31-Jul-26] [file 16] [file 29]
- Profits have moved away from road EPC towards four areas:
  - diversified and urban EPC (metro, tunnels, water, power T&D);
  - energy-transition EPC (solar and battery storage);
  - steel-building makers serving factory and data-centre capex;
  - a few asset-light PSU project-management firms.
- ICRA explicitly expects diversified EPC firms to grow 8–10% in FY27, against continuing pressure on road-focused contractors. [ICRA Mar-26]
- Governance and legal overhangs are unusually dense in this group. Across the 32 files:
  - NHAI debarment or suspension of a developer (PNC, Ashoka);
  - CBI or other agency cases under auditor emphasis of matter (GR Infra, Ashoka, HG Infra);
  - income-tax searches (Interarch, GR Infra);
  - a qualified audit opinion (Capacit'e);
  - a qualified internal-controls opinion (Ceigall);
  - going-concern flags at subsidiaries (Patel);
  - rating withdrawals or Negative outlooks (Ashoka, Afcons, HG, Sterling and Wilson).
- These are material to the risk of owning the sector, not footnotes.

## 2. Market size and growth

- **Industry revenue:** ICRA's sample of 19 listed construction companies had combined revenue of about ₹1.28 trillion. Growth ran at 20%+ in FY22–FY24, was roughly flat in FY25 and 2–4% (estimated) in FY26, and is forecast at 6–8% for FY27. [ICRA Mar-26]
- **Order inflows:** ICRA expects about 10% growth in FY27, led by road awards and Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) work. [ICRA Mar-26]
  - India Ratings (Ind-Ra) projected about 10% FY27 EPC revenue growth, against a 20% average in FY22–24. It has since flagged that the sector will stay under "credit stress through FY27". [News: BW Businessworld, 2026]
- **Road network and spending:** road length grew from 52.3 lakh km (FY16) to 63.73 lakh km (FY26). Government plus private road capex rose from ₹0.94 lakh crore to ₹2.62 lakh crore over the same period. [CareEdge Sep-26 via Tribune]
- **What the listed companies show:** order books are rebuilding faster than revenue.
  - J Kumar: order book ₹22,246 cr, but FY26 revenue flat at ₹5,723 cr. [file 17]
  - Ahluwalia: order book ₹20,664 cr, revenue +11%. [file 16]
  - Capacit'e: order book ₹13,535 cr, Q1 FY27 revenue +7%. [file 28]
  - Across the road names, revenue fell sharply in FY26: KNR standalone −38% per CRISIL, PNC standalone −9%, Ashoka standalone −17%. HG Infra's consolidated PAT fell 35%. [file 19] [file 18] [file 20] [file 22]

## 3. Demand drivers

### 3.1 Central government capex and the budget

- FY27 Budget Estimates (BE) versus FY26 Revised Estimates (RE):
  - Total central capex +11.5%, to about ₹12.2 lakh crore.
  - Road Transport and Highways ₹3,09,875 crore (+7.9%).
  - Railways ₹2,81,377 crore (+10.1%).
  - Defence capital outlay ₹2,19,306 crore (+17.6%).
  - Housing and Urban Affairs ₹85,522 crore (+49.5%).
  - Jal Shakti ₹94,808 crore, up from an RE of only ₹41,437 crore after about ₹50,000 crore of JJM underspending in FY26.
  - Special capex loans to states ₹1.85 lakh crore.
  - Fiscal deficit target 4.3% of GDP. [PRS Feb-26]
- The FY26 underspend is the key lesson. JJM (₹50,000 cr unspent) and PMAY (about ₹40,000 cr unspent) budgets were not converted into cash for contractors. That fed straight into contractor receivables (NCC, PNC). [PRS Feb-26] [file 09] [file 18]

### 3.2 Roads (NHAI and MoRTH)

- **Awards:**
  - ICRA: 7,538 km in FY25, 7,250–7,750 km projected for FY26, and only 1,951 km in 8M FY26 (−24%). [ICRA Feb-26 via EPC World]
  - Nuvama (cited by Autocar Pro): NHAI awarded only 3,124 km in FY26 (−22%). [News: Autocar Pro, 31-Jul-26]
  - Companies told us NHAI awarded only about 107 km in Q1 FY27. [file 18] [file 19]
- **Execution:** ICRA sees MoRTH road construction at 9,000–9,500 km in FY27 (10,660 km in FY25). CareEdge sees about 7,750 km (21 km/day). [ICRA Feb-26 via EPC World] [CareEdge Sep-26 via Tribune]
- **Pipeline (management):** NHAI launched 54 projects of about ₹1.8 lakh crore for bidding in FY27 (26 EPC, 21 HAM, 7 BOT-toll). The government is targeting about ₹75,000 crore of BOT-toll. [file 18] [file 19] [file 20]
- **States:** CareEdge expects state road capex to outpace central capex in FY27. [CareEdge Sep-26 via Tribune]
  - Maharashtra is the stand-out, with a pipeline of ₹50,000 crore to ₹1 lakh crore around Mumbai per J Kumar's management, including Uttan–Virar and metro lines 5, 10, 13 and 14. [file 17]

### 3.3 Railways and metros

- The Railways ministry allocation is ₹2.81 lakh crore, and railway capex budgetary support is about ₹2.77–2.78 lakh crore. [PRS Feb-26] [News: Business Standard/Newsonair, Feb-26]
- Seven high-speed rail corridors were announced in the budget. [News: Business Standard, Feb-26]
- Companies cite a record ₹2.93 lakh crore rail capex (management figure, which appears to include extra-budgetary resources). [file 20] [file 19]
- Beneficiaries in our sample: RITES and IRCON (PSU), GPT (bridges and, via Alcon, signalling), Ashoka and HG (rail diversification), and J Kumar and Afcons (metro and tunnels). [file 06] [file 07] [file 30] [file 17] [file 08]

### 3.4 Water (JJM, irrigation, river-linking)

- JJM was extended to December 2028 and restructured as JJM 2.0 with higher outlay. ICRA sees this reviving water order inflows. [ICRA Mar-26] [file 18] [file 31]
- Payment stress is severe, per press reports:
  - Contractors report delays of 6–20 months.
  - Maharashtra had about ₹5,183 crore of bills pending at district level at end-May 2026, and allotted ₹22,898 crore from its own funds to finish projects.
  - The first JJM 2.0 central tranche (April 2026) was only ₹1,561 crore across five states. [News: The Core, 2026] [News: NewKerala, 2026]
- Irrigation is dominated by state budgets. KNR's Telangana (Kaleswaram) dues of about ₹1,450 cr and PNC's AP canal dues show the risk. [file 19] [file 18]
- SPML tracks ₹98,725 crore of water and irrigation projects across 11 states. [file 31]

### 3.5 Urban, buildings and real estate

- Institutional and government buildings have been strong demand sources: Central Vista, IIT Bombay, CSMT, AIIMS-type hospitals and redevelopment (MHADA BDD, Dharavi). [file 16] [file 28] [file 21]
- Residential construction is a large but slowing driver.
  - ANAROCK: housing sales in the top 7 cities fell 6% YoY to about 90,715 units in Q2 2026 (the lowest since January 2023), while launches rose 7% to 1.06 lakh units. [News: Business Standard, 29-Jun-26]
  - Contractors see this directly: Ahluwalia's management reports developers "slowed down the launches". [file 16]
  - Capacit'e and PSP depend on developers such as Mahindra Lifespace, DLF, Godrej, Signature Global and Adani (Dharavi). [file 16] [file 28] [file 21]

### 3.6 Private capex: factories, energy and data centres

- The strongest private demand comes from energy transition and manufacturing plants: solar modules and cells, batteries, EVs, semiconductors, transformers and cables, and warehousing. This shows up in the PEB makers' order books:
  - Interarch: ₹1,864 cr, capacity 245,000 t. [file 23]
  - EPack: ₹1,380 cr. [file 26]
  - M&B: ₹1,053 cr. [file 29]
  - Pennar: PEB India ₹1,008 cr. [file 27]
- **Data centres:** India had about 1.6 GW installed by mid-2026. JLL expects 6 GW by 2029, needing about US$110 billion. [JLL mid-2026]
  - Contractors are positioning for it: Interarch (heavy structures), EPack (a data-centre subsidiary), M&B (a heavy-steel line), and PSP and Ahluwalia (buildings).
  - Management estimates steel structure at about 8–10% of data-centre capex (M&B). [file 23] [file 26] [file 29]
- **Solar and BESS:**
  - Sterling and Wilson sees 25 GW of utility solar plus about 35 GWh of BESS ordering in H2 FY27 (management). [file 15]
  - GK Energy depends on PM-KUSUM 2.0, which is still awaited. [file 25]
  - Solarworld and SPML are building BESS manufacturing. [file 32] [file 31]
- **Transmission:** a ₹6 lakh crore, five-year capex plan (PNC management) attracts road contractors diversifying into it (PNC, HG, Ashoka, KNR). [file 18] [file 22] [file 20]

### 3.7 Defence infrastructure

- The defence capital outlay rose 17.6% to ₹2.19 lakh crore. [PRS Feb-26]
- In our sample, defence infrastructure is a minor theme: Pennar divested its 51% defence subsidiary in FY26. No company cited defence construction as a material FY27 driver (not disclosed). [file 27]

## 4. Policy

- **Award only when ready.** MoRTH and NHAI now award mainly after land acquisition and approvals are largely in place. CRISIL describes the slowdown as "largely structural". [News: CRISIL via Bizzbuzz]
  - Ind-Ra says delayed Q4 FY26 awards pushed revenue recovery into FY27. [News: BW Businessworld, 2026]
- **HAM vs EPC vs BOT:** see section 5.
- **Price adjustment:** MoRTH rebased bitumen from 1 April 2026 and moved from quarterly to monthly price adjustment. Contractors could choose either force majeure extensions or price adjustment for the bitumen spike, not both. [file 18] [file 19] [News: Autocar Pro, 31-Jul-26]
- **Enforcement on quality and integrity:**
  - NHAI extended the Awadh Expressway concessionaire's debarment to PNC, blocking it from MoRTH/NHAI bidding for three years (September 2026). [file 18]
  - NHAI suspended Ashoka from bidding in November 2025; the suspension was stayed by the Delhi HC. [file 20]
  - A CBI chargesheet names Ashoka over NHAI work in Bihar. [file 20]
  - CBI/ACB Patna arrested HG Infra employees over railway projects. [file 22]
  - GR Infra has a CBI case and an income-tax search. [file 10]
- The regulatory risk of bidding for NHAI work is clearly higher than two years ago.
- **Asset monetisation:** NHAI monetised about ₹28,000 crore through InvITs/TOT in FY26 (KNR management). Private InvITs (Vertis/KKR, Indus, Neo, EAAA) bought HAM portfolios from PNC, KNR, HG and Ashoka. This recycling underpins road developers' balance sheets. [file 19] [file 18] [file 22] [file 20]
- **Domestic-content rules:** DCR/ALMM rules for solar raise module costs (premiums of 130–160% per an analyst on Sterling and Wilson's call), while backing domestic manufacturers. [file 15] [file 32]

## 5. HAM vs EPC vs BOT

- **EPC** (the government funds the project and bears traffic risk) has been the most competitive format. ICRA says about **71% of NHAI/MoRTH EPC projects** in the last three years went at **more than 20% below base price**. [ICRA Feb-26 via EPC World]
  - Contractors told the press many bids were 30–35% below base price. [News: Autocar Pro, 31-Jul-26]
- **HAM** (40% government construction support, 60% annuity) also saw deep discounts: −16% in FY25 and −21% in 10M FY26 against base project cost. [ICRA Feb-26 via EPC World]
  - HAM ties up developer equity (for example, HG ₹3,019 cr committed; PNC ₹1,623 cr) and delays cash until milestones are hit, since NHAI moved back from monthly to milestone payments. [file 22] [file 18]
  - Well-built HAM portfolios sell to InvITs at about 1.5–2.5x invested equity (for example, KNR Guruvayur ₹485.86 cr against ₹193.32 cr invested). [file 19]
- **BOT-toll** is being revived (a revised framework and about ₹75,000 cr targeted), but developers remain cautious. Ashoka's management would bid only where "we are sure about traffic and execution". [CareEdge Sep-26 via Tribune] [file 20]
  - Toll collections are expected to grow 6–8% in FY27. [ICRA Feb-26 via EPC World]
- Our read: HAM equity plus working capital was the main cause of balance-sheet strain for mid-sized road names (HG, PNC, KNR). Monetisation has been the release valve, and it depends on getting COD/PCOD on time. HAM-related payment and COD delays show up in all three files. [file 22] [file 18] [file 19]

## 6. State payment delays, bank guarantees and working capital

- ICRA says the cash conversion cycle **lengthened in FY25** after the Atmanirbhar Bharat relief measures expired (including reduced retention and faster payments), and worsened in FY26 because of JJM payment delays.
  - Interest coverage is expected to fall to **3.2–3.5x in FY26 and 3.1–3.4x in FY27**. [ICRA Mar-26]
- Company evidence of state payment stress:
  - Telangana irrigation: KNR has about ₹1,450 cr due, including ₹1,363 cr on Kaleswaram Package 4 under an auditor emphasis of matter. [file 19]
  - JJM water receivables at PNC of ₹925 cr (of which ₹741 cr is pending from the government). [file 18]
  - NCC's JJM receivables. [file 09]
  - AP irrigation dues at PNC; Assam payments delayed around elections (Ahluwalia). [file 18] [file 16]
  - A central-university project that has gone over budget and left Ahluwalia unpaid for five months. [file 16]
  - Central agencies (NHAI, DMRC, MMRDA, BMC) are generally described as prompt payers. [file 17]
- **Bank guarantees and surety bonds are becoming a binding constraint** as order books grow:
  - J Kumar's bank guarantees and surety bonds rose 48% to ₹4,364 cr, with non-fund lines 65% used. [file 17]
  - PNC's non-fund lines are about 66% used. [file 18]
  - Ahluwalia's surety and indemnity bonds rose to ₹574 cr from ₹224 cr. [file 16]
  - Sterling and Wilson has raised more than ₹3,200 cr of new lines. [file 15]
  - Contractors increasingly use insurance surety bonds (SPML, Patel). [file 31] [file 24]
- **Mobilisation advances** remain a funding source, but more are now interest-bearing (J Kumar ₹470 cr at 8.5–10%; Ahluwalia's Central Vista advance). [file 17] [file 16]

## 7. Cost drivers: steel, cement, bitumen, labour

- **Bitumen:** the biggest 2026 shock for road contractors.
  - Prices went from about ₹30,000/t (Feb 2026) to ₹70,000–75,000/t (Jul 2026). [News: Autocar Pro, 31-Jul-26]
  - India imports 30–40% of its needs. Asphalt road work stalled, and equipment makers cut FY27 growth expectations to single digits. [News: Autocar Pro, 31-Jul-26]
  - ICRA cites bitumen pressure as a reason for its FY27 margin cut. [ICRA Mar-26]
- **Steel:** companies reported domestic steel up 12–20% in the months after the war began. [file 26] [file 29]
  - The effect split by contract type:
    - Fixed-price PEB makers absorbed about 100bp (EPack) or had a 200bp PEB margin lag (Pennar). [file 26] [file 27]
    - Contractors with index-linked escalation (J Kumar, Ahluwalia on government jobs) were largely covered. [file 17] [file 16]
    - Capacit'e took ₹20 cr of provisions because aluminium (+35–40%) and electrical items (+18–20%) outran WPI-based escalation. [file 28]
- **Cement:** Ind-Ra expects mid-single-digit demand growth in FY27, about 50 mnt of capacity additions, and utilisation of 68–69%.
  - Cement makers can pass on only low-to-mid single-digit price rises against a needed 5–7%. This suggests cement costs for contractors stay contained, as the sector is oversupplied. [Ind-Ra Jun-26 via EPC World]
- **Labour:** a rising structural cost.
  - Haryana and UP minimum wages rose 35–40%, cutting about 1.5 points from Ahluwalia's EBITDA. NCR sites ran at 40–50% labour strength. [file 16]
  - Election-related shortages (West Bengal, Assam) hurt GPT and Capacit'e, and LPG shortages drove labour off sites. [file 30] [file 28] [file 16]
  - Rising labour cost is pushing mechanisation capex (Ahluwalia) and off-site steel construction (the PEB names). [file 16] [file 23]
- **Freight and forex:** US-bound PEB exports face doubled container freight (M&B) but lower US Section 232 duties (25%, from 50%). [file 29]

## 8. Competitive structure and bid margins

- The sector is fragmented, and competition is intense in every segment. ICRA says competition in metro and water has also increased "with new entrants trying to diversify their order book". [ICRA Mar-26]
- **Roads:** very aggressive bidding (see section 5).
  - HG has said "Intensifying competition and lower margins, as always". PNC calls it "very unhealthy competition for the last two to three years". [file 22] [file 18]
  - The result is falling margins at pure road EPC firms: KNR from about 19% EBITDA to about 9%, and HG from about 14.5% to about 8.5%. [file 19] [file 22]
- **Specialist niches hold margins better:**
  - J Kumar (urban tunnels and elevated corridors): 14–15% EBITDA.
  - Patel (Himalayan hydro and tunnels): 13–14%.
  - GPT (bridges plus signalling): 14–15%.
  - Capacit'e (high-rise towers): about 16%. [file 17] [file 24] [file 30] [file 28]
  - Their weaknesses are dependence on a few clients or cities (Mumbai agencies, hydro PSUs), and a record of slow conversion.
- **Captive and group work:**
  - Cemindia: Adani group is about 53% of the order book.
  - PSP: Adani about 70%.
  - Sterling and Wilson: Reliance is its parent; the order is still awaited.
  - This gives visibility but carries related-party risk, and it does not necessarily bring higher margins (PSP at about 6% EBITDA). [file 02] [file 21] [file 15]
- **Solar EPC:** Sterling and Wilson cites "new entrants [who] have gone aggressive". Solar EPC gross margins are 8–10%. [file 15]
- **PEB:** at least four listed players are adding capacity together: Interarch 245,000 t, EPack three new plants, M&B plus 20,000 t, and Pennar. Margins are about 9–12% EBITDA, and a 2026 steel spike showed limited pricing power. [file 23] [file 26] [file 29] [file 27]

## 9. Where the profit sits

In our sample, economic profit is concentrated in five places:

1. **Asset-light PSU project managers and consultants:** NBCC, RITES and EIL have net cash and high ROE. Their margins depend on the mix: turnkey work is about 1.5% margin at RITES. [file 01] [file 07] [file 04]
2. **Specialist urban and underground contractors:** J Kumar, Patel, Afcons (under pressure in FY26) and GPT. [file 17] [file 24] [file 08] [file 30]
3. **Net-cash building contractors:** Ahluwalia's balance sheet is strong, but its FY27 margin is under 10%. [file 16]
4. **Asset-light energy EPC:** GK Energy (ROE about 37%, PAT margin in double digits) and Solarworld (EPC PAT about 10.5% per management). Both depend heavily on schemes and PSUs, and their cash conversion is weak. [file 25] [file 32]
5. **HAM asset recyclers:** capital gains on InvIT sales have boosted reported profits at KNR, PNC, HG and Ashoka. For example, Ashoka's Dec-25 quarter gain was ₹2,416 cr of other income. This is not recurring operating profit. [file 20] [file 19]

- Profit does not sit in pure road EPC, where FY27 standalone margins are guided at 8–12%. It also does not sit in commodity-steel legacy businesses (Pennar's steel and railways are being run down). [file 19] [file 22] [file 18] [file 27]

## 10. Cycle position and FY27–FY29 outlook

- **Named forecasts:**
  - **ICRA (Mar-26):** revenue +6–8% in FY27 (diversified EPC +8–10%); order inflows +10%; OPM 10.1–10.6%; interest coverage 3.1–3.4x. Sector outlook Stable, but road-focused credit profiles under pressure. [ICRA Mar-26]
  - **ICRA (Feb-26):** road construction 9,000–9,500 km in FY27; awards subdued; toll growth 6–8%. [ICRA Feb-26 via EPC World]
  - **CareEdge (Sep-26):** about 21 km/day (about 7,750 km) in FY27; states to lead road capex; a revised BOT framework may help FY27 awards. [CareEdge Sep-26 via Tribune]
  - **Ind-Ra:** about 10% FY27 EPC revenue growth, with credit stress persisting through FY27. The best opportunities are power T&D, energy storage, urban infrastructure and mining. [News: BW Businessworld, 2026]
  - **CRISIL:** road-EPC revenue growth moderating as NH awarding weakens, with the slowdown described as structural. It expects margins to hold at 13–14% because of lower steel and bitumen prices, a view overtaken by the 2026 bitumen spike. [News: CRISIL via Bizzbuzz]
- **Our synthesis:**
  - **FY27 is a trough-to-early-recovery year.** Order books are rebuilding, but revenue conversion is back-ended into H2 at almost every company we covered: Ahluwalia, J Kumar, HG, GK Energy, GPT, Capacit'e, Sterling and Wilson, PNC and KNR. [files 15–31]
  - H2 FY27 guidance looks aggressive across the board. We expect more guidance cuts in the Q2 results (Oct–Nov 2026), especially where land or appointed-date delays drive the ramp (HG, PNC, KNR, J Kumar) or where a scheme is still pending (GK Energy's PM-KUSUM 2.0; SPML's NTPC BESS approvals).
  - **Margins:** FY27 will be the low point for road EPC as bitumen and labour costs flow through. FY28 could recover 50–150bp if escalation indices catch up and bids now priced at 2026 costs replace old low-margin work (inference). ICRA's forecast implies no recovery in FY27. [ICRA Mar-26]
  - **FY28–FY29:** the growth mix shifts towards railways and high-speed rail, metros, state water and irrigation (if JJM 2.0 cash arrives), power T&D and BESS, data centres and high-rises. NHAI awards recover only gradually. Balance sheets are generally sound (ICRA calls leverage "comfortable"), but working capital and guarantee limits will cap the growth of weaker names. [ICRA Mar-26]
- **Key swing factors:**
  1. The pace of NHAI awards under the "ready project" policy.
  2. Timing of JJM 2.0 and state payments.
  3. How long the West Asia war keeps oil and bitumen high.
  4. Enforcement actions (debarments and CBI cases) spreading to other developers.
  5. Private capex in data centres and energy manufacturing.

## 12. Suggested buckets

I suggest grouping the 32 companies into five business-model buckets. Each bucket shares its main drivers, cash-flow profile and risks.

1. **PSU project managers and PSU EPC: NBCC, Engineers India, RITES, IRCON.** These are government-owned, largely asset-light firms paid fees or cost-plus, with net cash. Their earnings depend on the government's project mix rather than bid margins, and turnkey work dilutes margins. [file 01] [file 04] [file 07] [file 06]

They face 20%+ bid discounts and HAM equity plus InvIT monetisation cycles. They also carry the highest regulatory and enforcement risk: debarments, CBI cases and concession terminations. They depend on state and PSU payments and on converting large, slow order books. ICRA expects this group to grow fastest.
