# Patel Engineering (NSE: PATELENG)

*Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Consolidated figures in ₹ crore unless stated otherwise.*

## Business and segments
- Patel Engineering is a Mumbai-based heavy-civil contractor specialising in hydropower (dams, tunnels, powerhouses), irrigation tunnels and canals, general tunnelling, and roads and urban infrastructure. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- Its record includes more than 350 projects, about 15,000 MW of hydro capacity, 87 dams and 300 km of tunnels. [Infomerics Jun-26]
- Order book at 30 Jun 2026: **₹14,636 cr** (about 3x revenue).
  - By segment: hydropower 62%, irrigation 17%, tunnelling 4%, roads and urban infrastructure 17%. [Q1 call]
  - Central PSUs (NEEPCO, NTPC, NHPC, SJVN, IRCON) make up about 62% of the book and state governments about 30%. The top ten projects are 63.2% of the book. [Infomerics Jun-26]
- Q1 FY27 revenue mix: hydro 68%, irrigation 15%, tunnelling 13%, roads and other 4%. [Q1 call]
- Key projects:
  - Subansiri Lower HEP (2,000 MW): four units operating; all eight expected this financial year.
  - Kiru and Kwar HEPs (J&K): Kwar dam concreting past 50%.
  - Parnai HEP.
  - Dorjilung HEP in Bhutan: work has started.
  - Sleemanabad irrigation tunnel (11.95 km, India's longest): TBM breakthrough achieved. [Q1 call]
- Non-core assets: land banks (including about 400 acres left near Hyderabad) and the Smondo Gachibowli residential project, whose occupation certificate is pending because of litigation. [Q1 call] [AR FY26]
- **Promoter holding is 31.49%, with 86.6% of it pledged.** Promoter holding fell from 36.11% to 31.48% in Dec-25 after the rights issue. [Screener]

## Key numbers
- Quarterly revenue, Mar-25 → Jun-26 (₹ cr): 1,612, 1,233, 1,208, 1,239, 1,421, 1,281. [Screener]
- Quarterly operating margin (OPM), same quarters: 14%, 13%, 13%, 12%, 15%, 14%. [Screener]
- Quarterly net profit, same quarters (₹ cr): 38, 81, 73, 72, 44, 98. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27: revenue ₹1,281 cr (+4%), operating EBITDA margin 14.02% (13.4%), PAT ₹93.5 cr (+24.5%, helped by about ₹10 cr lower finance cost). [Q1 call]
- FY26: revenue ₹5,103 cr (flat); EBITDA ₹659 cr (about 13%, down from 14.4%); the call cites PAT of ₹319 cr (the extracted text does not make clear whether this is standalone or consolidated; Screener's consolidated figure is ₹269 cr).
  - Exceptional items included about ₹56 cr impairment of hydro subsidiaries and ₹30 cr on an SPV stake sale.
  - The questioner cited a total "debt and advance return" of ₹153 cr in FY26, against ₹270–280 cr in FY25. [Q4 call] [Screener]
- Revenue, FY21–FY26 (₹ cr): 1,995, 3,380, 3,891, 4,544, 5,093, 5,103. Interest cost fell from ₹420 cr in FY22 to ₹296 cr in FY26. [Screener]
- Market data:
  - Market cap ₹2,741 cr; price ₹27.6 against a 52-week range of ₹22–38.5.
  - Price/book 0.62x; P/E 7.0. No dividend is paid. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- **FY26:** guided to no growth and delivered flat revenue. [Q4 call]
- **Q4 FY26 call (14 May 2026):**
  - FY27 revenue growth of about 10%, weighted to H2.
  - An L1 position of ₹1,600 cr at the start of FY27. [Q4 call]
- **Q1 FY27 call (11 Aug 2026):**
  - FY27 revenue about +10% and FY28 about +15%.
  - EBITDA 13–14%; the CFO would not commit to 15%.
  - New orders of about ₹8,000 cr targeted for FY27, with about ₹9,000 cr of bids under evaluation and a ₹60,000 cr pipeline.
  - Non-core monetisation of ₹150–200 cr (land plus arbitration awards).
  - Pledged promoter shares expected to fall by "at least 15%-20%".
  - Buybacks only after debt is repaid, in "maybe two years, three years". [Q1 call]
- **Infomerics' view:** 7–10% revenue growth in FY27 and about 13% EBITDA margin. [Infomerics Jun-26]

## Capex and capacity
- Capital intensity is low. The main assets are TBMs and tunnelling equipment. FY26 fixed assets were ₹1,338 cr and capital work in progress ₹126 cr. [Screener]
- FY26 order inflow was about ₹4,400 cr. The company lost the roughly ₹16,000–17,000 cr Dibang hydro package to a much lower bid. Its win rate is 25–30% excluding Dibang and 12–13% including it. [Q4 call]
- Pipeline highlights:
  - Kalai-II (1,200 MW, about ₹14,000 cr) and Kamla (1,720 MW, about ₹26,000 cr) hydro projects approved.
  - 100 GW pumped-storage roadmap to 2035.
  - Himalayan and northeast tunnels.
  - MMR urban projects of about ₹22,000 cr.
  - The proposed Gulf of Khambhat dam (about ₹1.2 lakh cr). [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- A **rights issue of about ₹400 cr in Dec 2025** plus about ₹185 cr of non-core monetisation cut consolidated gross debt from ₹1,645 cr to ₹1,187 cr (Mar-26). Debt rose again to about ₹1,293 cr by June 2026 (₹969 cr working capital, ₹324 cr term debt). [Q4 call] [Q1 call] [Infomerics Jun-26]
- Debt/equity is about 0.28 and client advances ₹615 cr. Net working capital days are about 137 (120 at March). Receivable days are 40–45. [Q1 call]
- Infomerics-adjusted total debt, including interest-bearing contract advances, fell from ₹2,267 cr to ₹1,809 cr, and TOL/ATNW improved from 1.68x to 1.22x. DSCR is 1.47x. [Infomerics Jun-26]
- **Ratings upgraded:** Infomerics upgraded to IVR A/Stable and A1 (from A-/A2) on 5 Jun 2026. Ratings from India Ratings (2 Jul 2026) and Acuité also exist but were not read. [Infomerics Jun-26] [Screener]
- Contingent liabilities are ₹2,995 cr per Screener (breakdown not extracted). [Screener]
- The FY26 operating cash flow of ₹560 cr covered interest, and free cash flow was ₹452 cr. [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- It is one of very few Indian contractors with deep hydropower and underground-works capability, including Himalayan geology, TBMs and large dams. This is a genuine barrier to entry. [Infomerics Jun-26] [Q1 call]
- Management cites an execution turnaround: Subansiri (2,000 MW) is nearing completion within about 5–6 years of active execution, which "is unheard of". [Q1 call]
- Management points to faster approvals, with land and MoEF clearances now obtained before award. It says this lowers the risk of stalled projects. [Q1 call]
- Its weakness is bidding: pricing discipline lost it Dibang, and "competition ... has increased". [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Input costs
- EBITDA fell to 13.41% in FY26 from 14.40% because of project mix and changes in labour laws. [Infomerics Jun-26]
- Most PSU contracts carry price-variation clauses (details not disclosed in the calls). Management's main cost lever is process optimisation. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Promoter pledge:** about 86.6% of the 31.5% promoter stake is pledged, with only a hoped-for 15–20% reduction. That leaves a low-control, high-pledge structure. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- **Subsidiary going-concern doubts, under auditor emphasis of matter (consolidated):** material uncertainty over going concern at Shreeanant Construction, West Kameng Energy, Digin Hydropower and Meyong Hydropower. [AR FY26]
- **Repeated exceptional write-downs:** about ₹270–280 cr in FY25 and ₹153 cr in FY26 (Vivad-se-Vishwas settlements and investment write-downs). Management says nothing more is expected. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Concentration:** hydro is 62% of the book and the top ten projects 63%, all in geologically risky locations (Arunachal, J&K, Bhutan). [Infomerics Jun-26] [Q1 call]
- **Dilution:** the rights issue raised the share count (equity capital from ₹84 cr to ₹99 cr). With a ₹1 face value and a large retail base (460k shareholders), the stock is heavily retail-owned. [Screener]
- **Non-core assets:** a 27-acre Telangana sale at about ₹1 cr an acre was questioned by investors as cheap. Smondo still has no occupation certificate after homebuyers took possession for fit-outs. [Q1 call]
- The stock carries a zero dividend payout despite about ₹290 cr of annual PAT. [Screener]

## My read
Patel Engineering has a rare niche in Himalayan hydro and tunnelling at a time when India plans 100 GW of pumped storage and large hydro awards (Kalai-II, Kamla). The balance sheet has visibly repaired: a ₹400 cr rights issue, debt down about ₹450 cr, and an upgrade to A/A1. But the equity story is held back by a promoter group owning only 31.5%, with about 87% of that pledged, recurring exceptional write-downs, going-concern flags at four subsidiaries, and single-digit ROE. At 0.6x book, the market is pricing in governance risk more than execution risk. Watch for a real reduction in the pledge, the ₹150–200 cr monetisation, and hydro order wins at sensible margins.
