# Ashoka Buildcon (NSE: ASHOKA)

*Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Figures in ₹ crore. "SA" means standalone EPC; "consol" includes Ashoka Concessions (ACL) and the SPVs.*

## Business and segments
- Ashoka is a Nashik-based EPC contractor that has evolved from BOT/HAM roads into multi-sector EPC. Its segments are road EPC and HAM, power transmission and distribution (T&D), railways, buildings, and a growing international book. It also makes ready-mix concrete (RMC) and bitumen. [CRISIL Jun-26] [Q1 call]
- Order book at 30 Jun 2026: ₹15,251 cr, excluding about ₹450 cr won after June.
  - Road and railway ₹9,648 cr (63.3%), made up of HAM about ₹1,500 cr, road EPC about ₹6,780 cr and rail ₹1,346 cr.
  - Power T&D ₹5,066 cr (33.2%); buildings ₹536 cr (3.5%). [Q1 call]
- Q1 FY27 standalone revenue mix: road EPC 49.3%, HAM 10.2%, T&D 18.4%, rail 11.5%, other 10.7%. [Q1 call]
- International work spans 7 countries, with plans for about 10:
  - Guyana: the Versailles–Parika highway, US$35.42 m.
  - Liberia road: about ₹430 cr.
  - Angola distribution networks: US$72 m (about ₹690 cr).
  - Saudi Arabia: Diriyah hotel package, Ashoka's share ₹900 cr (consolidated only). [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Domestic additions: a Gems & Jewellery Park PPP in Raipur (51% JV, about ₹450 cr EPC) and a Gandak river bridge in Bihar (Ashoka's share ₹242 cr). [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Monetisation history:
  - 5 BOT assets sold for ₹1,814 cr and 5 HAM assets for ₹1,146 cr.
  - SBI Macquarie's stake in ACL was bought out for about ₹1,550 cr.
  - 6 more HAM SPVs are being sold to EAAA/Infrastructure Yield Plus for about ₹1,150 cr. [CRISIL Jun-26] [BSE 15-Sep-26]
- Promoters hold 54.47%. FIIs have fallen from 7.48% to 4.39% in five quarters. [Screener]

## Key numbers
- Consolidated quarterly revenue, Mar-25 → Jun-26 (₹ cr): 2,694, 1,887, 1,851, 1,827, 1,954, 1,500. [Screener]
- Consolidated quarterly net profit, same quarters (₹ cr): 452, 227, 91, 2,111, 147, 127. Dec-25 included other income of ₹2,416 cr from the monetisation gain. [Screener]
- Standalone FY26: total income ₹5,952 cr (−17%); EBITDA ₹636 cr (10.7% including other income, about 8.5% on a CRISIL-adjusted basis); PAT ₹320 cr. [Q4 call] [CRISIL Jun-26]
- Standalone Q1 FY27: total income ₹1,320 cr (flat); EBITDA ₹126 cr (9.5%, −17% YoY); PAT ₹31.5 cr. **Excluding other income, EBITDA margin was 7.2%.** [Q1 call]
- Consolidated Q1 FY27: income ₹1,534 cr (−21%), EBITDA ₹292 cr (19.0%), PAT ₹127 cr. [Q1 call]
- Jaora–Nayagaon toll: ₹75 cr in Q1 FY27, up 8%. [Q1 call]
- Consolidated revenue, FY22–FY26 (₹ cr): 5,999, 8,100, 9,798, 10,037, 7,520. FY26 PAT was ₹2,576 cr, mostly from the gain on sale. [Screener]
- Market data:
  - Market cap ₹3,177 cr; price ₹113 against a 52-week range of ₹101–214, down 40% in one year.
  - Price/book 0.48x. No dividend has been paid. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- **Q4 FY26 call (22 May 2026):**
  - FY27 revenue growth of 20%.
  - Order inflow ₹8,000–10,000 cr.
  - EBITDA "around 2 digits" at the start of the year (as the CFO recalled on the Q1 call).
  - 4 of the 6 HAM SPVs to be sold by June 2026 (about ₹750 cr+), and 2 more by December (about ₹400 cr). [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Q1 FY27 call (12 Aug 2026):**
  - Revenue growth **cut to 10–15%**, citing a flat Q1 and supply-chain problems.
  - Order inflow ₹6,000–8,000 cr, plus the ₹780 cr won in Q1 and ₹1,800 cr of L1 positions.
  - EBITDA 9–9.5%, about 0.5 points below the start-of-year plan. FY28 margin guided to 10.5–11%.
  - Monetisation of 4 SPVs pushed to September/early October and 2 to December–Q4. [Q1 call]
- **After the Q1 call:** a 15 Sep 2026 filing extended the three nearly-complete SPVs (TS-1/2/3) to 31 Oct 2026, with the others to "follow thereafter". [BSE 15-Sep-26]
- **Earlier monetisation targets:** "4 assets by March '26 and 2 assets by June '26" (analyst recap on the Q4 call). The timeline has slipped at every update since. [Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity

- HAM equity:
  - ₹638 cr invested up to June in six HAM projects.
  - Still due: ₹179 cr in FY27 (including Bowaichandi) and ₹72 cr in each of FY28 and FY29. [Q1 call]
- Gems & Jewellery Park: a total project of about ₹1,000 cr over 5 years on about 9 acres, recovered through long-lease sales. Ashoka's investment is ₹145 cr (a correction to the figure stated on the call). [Q1 call]

- Bid pipeline:
  - Bids submitted but not yet opened: about ₹8,000 cr.
  - Opportunities: NHAI/MoRTH about ₹1 lakh cr, states about ₹25,000 cr, railways about ₹50,000 cr. [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Debt at 31 Mar 2026:
  - Consolidated ₹2,778 cr.
  - Standalone ₹1,127 cr: equipment loans ₹70 cr, NCDs ₹300 cr, working capital ₹757 cr. [Q4 call]
- CRISIL-adjusted debt, including interest-bearing acceptances, was ₹2,847 cr, "despite the receipt of around Rs 2,900 crore through the asset monetisation programme". Interest coverage is 2.03x. [CRISIL Jun-26]
- About ₹2,100 cr of standalone debt at Jun-26 includes loans from subsidiaries. After monetisation, management targets third-party debt of about ₹500–700 cr, or about ₹1,100–1,200 cr including subsidiary loans. [Q1 call]
- Working capital: about ₹250 cr went into new projects in Q1. Power T&D receivables and unbilled revenue are elevated and expected to normalise by Q4 FY27. Screener's working capital days rose to 142 in FY26 from 3. [Q1 call] [Screener]
- Q4 FY26 included an expected credit loss provision of ₹28 cr. [Q4 call]
- **Ratings:**
  - CRISIL reaffirmed AA-/**Negative** and A1+ on 12 Jun 2026 and **withdrew them at the company's request**, along with the ₹200 cr commercial paper rating.
  - Acuité reaffirmed AA/Stable and A1+ (per the Q4 call; the rationale dated 24 Feb 2026 was not read). [CRISIL Jun-26] [Q4 call]
- Contingent liabilities (standalone, Mar-26):
  - Taxes about ₹223 cr: income tax ₹77.8 cr (from the 2016 search), sales tax ₹95.6 cr, GST ₹49.9 cr.
  - Funding commitments to group companies ₹179 cr. [AR FY26]
- MSME dues outstanding rose to ₹161 cr from ₹114 cr. [AR FY26]

## Moat evidence
- A 30-year road record, including one of the earliest BOT wins (1997). It has in-house EPC plus RMC and bitumen manufacture. [CRISIL Jun-26]
- It has diversified into T&D (15+ years), railways (5+ years) and international markets. T&D, rail and buildings now make up about 45% of the order book, which reduces dependence on NHAI. [CRISIL Jun-26] [Q1 call]
- Asset recycling has worked (about ₹2,960 cr realised from 10 assets). But the proceeds went largely into the ACL buyout and working-capital debt, not into net cash. [CRISIL Jun-26]
- Management claims larger NHAI packages "will definitely rationalize the competition". [Q1 call]

## Input costs
- Margin pressure has come from project prolongation, fixed-cost under-absorption, and a budgeted 0.5–1% price-escalation cushion. [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 was hurt by supply-chain disruption and start-up costs for new verticals: "mobilized a lot of new administrative and employees". [Q1 call]
- There are bitumen and steel escalation clauses on government contracts. No specific numbers were disclosed (not disclosed).

## Risks and red flags
- **CBI case, under auditor emphasis of matter:**
  - The CBI Special Court in Bihar filed a chargesheet (received 28 Feb 2025) arraigning the company over a bribery FIR involving NHAI officials. The alleged issues are "non-completion / deviation in the executed work and minor irregularities in quality" during Apr 2021–Aug 2022.
  - A writ petition to quash was filed in Patna HC on 11 May 2026. [AR FY26]
- **NHAI suspension:** on 27 Nov 2025 NHAI temporarily suspended Ashoka from bidding. The Delhi HC held the suspension in abeyance (8 Dec and 23 Dec 2025) and directed a reasoned order. The final outcome is pending. [CRISIL Jun-26]
- **Rating withdrawal while on Negative outlook:** CRISIL flagged a 17% revenue decline, 8.5% margins, 2.03x interest coverage and monetisation delays. The company then asked CRISIL to withdraw the rating. [CRISIL Jun-26]
- **Serial delays in monetisation:** the target has moved from March to June, then September/October, then 31 Oct 2026 and later. [Q4 call] [Q1 call] [BSE 15-Sep-26]
- **Guidance cuts:** revenue growth went from 20% to 10–15%, and EBITDA from about 10% to 9–9.5%, within 12 weeks. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **International execution risk:** Guyana, Liberia, Angola and Saudi Arabia are new geographies with sovereign-payment and forex exposure. [Q1 call]
- The stake in Ashoka Purestudy was diluted from 59% to 39.33% through a preferential allotment. [Q1 call]

## My read
Ashoka has used a decade of BOT/HAM asset sales to buy out its PE partner and cut working-capital debt. But the core EPC engine is weak: standalone revenue fell 17% in FY26, and Q1 FY27 margins excluding other income were about 7%. Governance and regulatory overhangs are real: an auditor's emphasis of matter on a CBI chargesheet, a stayed NHAI suspension, and a CRISIL rating withdrawn while on Negative outlook. The remaining six-SPV sale (about ₹1,150 cr) keeps slipping, and it is the main lever to cut debt below about ₹1,200 cr. The move into T&D, rail and overseas work helps order intake, but at 0.5x book I'd want the October 2026 SPV closing and H2 margins at about 9.5% before trusting a recovery.
