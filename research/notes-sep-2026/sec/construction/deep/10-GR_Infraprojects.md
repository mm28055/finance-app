# G R Infraprojects Ltd (GRINFRA, ISIN INE201P01022)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Figures are consolidated (Screener) unless marked "standalone". ₹ cr = rupees crore.

## Business and segments
- Udaipur-based road EPC and HAM/BOT developer (founded 1995; promoters hold 74.69%). It is diversifying into power transmission (and now makes its own towers), oil & gas pipelines, tunnels/hydro, metro and railways, battery storage (BESS), BharatNet telecom, ropeways, and logistics/warehousing. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Order book ~₹25,300 cr at 1 Jul 2026 (₹26,470 cr at Q4). A further ~₹32,000 cr of bids are awaiting opening (~₹28,000 cr transport including metro; ~₹4,000 cr hydro/tunnels). [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Three projects worth ₹7,250 cr are awaiting appointed dates. NHAI declared the appointed date for the ₹4,262.78 cr Agra–Gwalior DBFOT on 7 Sep 2026; two HAM projects are expected by around December. [Q1 call] [BSE filing, 18 Sep 2026]
- Q1 FY27 segment contributions: transmission ~₹110 cr; oil & gas ₹270 cr (FY27 target ₹1,000–1,200 cr); roads the bulk. BharatNet FY27 revenue ~₹400 cr (₹300 cr capex, from H2, plus O&M). [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- It recycles assets into its sponsored InvIT (Indus Infra Trust). Four HAM assets were sold in FY26 for ₹321 cr, with an exceptional gain of ₹253 cr, at ~1.18–1.2x book; 3–4 more are to be transferred in FY27. GR holds ~₹2,000–2,400 cr of InvIT units. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- **FY26 Q4 revenue ~₹3,000 cr (guided at Q3):** actual standalone ₹2,521 cr. **Missed** because of a delayed appointed date, the March geopolitical shock and slow oil & gas billing (~₹400 cr against ₹600 cr expected). [Q4 call]
- **FY27 revenue:** Q4 said "in the range of 15%" (15–20% on questioning). Q1 kept 15–20% after +33% standalone in Q1; 25% is possible if appointed dates arrive on time. FY28: ~20% growth to ~₹11,000–12,000 cr. **Kept, with upside.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Margins:** Q4 said 10.5–11%, with margins exposed if the geopolitical disruption persists. Q1 said 10–11%. **Kept (lower end widened).** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **FY27 order inflow:** Q4 said ₹20,000–22,000 cr (roads ₹12,000–14,000 cr, transmission ₹5,000 cr, tunnels/hydro ₹2,000–3,000 cr, oil & gas ₹2,000–3,000 cr, others ₹1,000–2,000 cr). Q1 said around ₹20,000 cr ±10%, roads ~₹14,000 cr. **Kept.** Q1 wins were negligible (order book fell). [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Capex:** Q4 said ₹300–350 cr; Q1 said ~₹300 cr for FY27 and ₹200–250 cr for FY28. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **HAM/BOT equity:** ₹3,346 cr still to be invested (Jun-26), of which ₹900–1,000 cr in FY27, and ~₹3,300 cr over three years. Logistics/warehousing equity of ₹450–500 cr in FY27 (₹130 cr invested so far). [Q1 call]
- **Execution rate:** "we have to assume at same level. I don't think it is going to increase" (CFO, Q4), because piecemeal land handover slows work. [Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Own fleet net block ~₹1,019 cr. Capex has risen from ~₹100 cr a year to ₹300–400 cr for transmission and tunnel equipment. A transmission-tower manufacturing unit has been set up and is being expanded because tower supply was delaying projects. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Warehousing/logistics: the Indore multimodal logistics park, plus land in progress at Guwahati and Sambhajinagar, and 15 leased dark stores. Equity of ₹600–700 cr is planned over three years. [Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Standalone borrowings are only ₹239 cr (D/E 0.03). Consolidated borrowings ₹5,286 cr (D/E 0.55), mostly HAM/BOT project debt. [Q1 call]
- Working capital was 148 days at Jun-26 (128 at Mar-26, 117 at Mar-25). Standalone trade receivables ₹2,655 cr, including ₹1,784 cr of HAM receivables (from its own SPVs); inventory ₹863 cr. Oil & gas receivables are released only at project completion (May 2027). [Q1 call]
- Consolidated operating cash flow has been negative for four years: FY23 −363, FY24 −1,590, FY25 −2,042, FY26 −2,811 (₹ cr). This reflects HAM construction funded inside SPVs (HAM receivables sit in operating assets). [Screener]
- InvIT distributions ~₹200–250 cr a year in cash (₹70 cr in Q1). Dividend payout is low (3% in FY26). [Q4 call] [Q1 call] [Screener]
- CARE AA+/Stable (reaffirmed 5 Jan 2026), citing low leverage and flexibility from InvIT units. [CARE Jan-26]

## Moat evidence
- Integrated road builder with its own large equipment fleet and, now, in-house transmission-tower manufacturing. Historically industry-leading margins (standalone EBITDA ~14% in FY25, 11% in FY26). [Q4 call] [Screener]
- Balance-sheet strength (standalone net debt near zero) lets it bid BOT/HAM, where competition is thinner. Management expects NHAI's BOT shift and stricter concession rules to "limit participation" to strong balance sheets. [Q1 call]
- Pricing pressure: NHAI road bids now come in 30–40% below authority estimates, against ~20% above five years ago. This is why margins compressed. [Q4 call]

## Input costs
- About 40% of road cost is crude-linked (fuel ~15%, bitumen 20–25%). The government's 2026 bitumen circular compensates bitumen directly, but not diesel. Transmission projects (aluminium, copper) have no escalation, and BharatNet optical fibre cable is fixed-price with no escalation, though its vendor contract is also fixed. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Legal and regulatory:**
  - Income-tax search on 9 Oct 2025 at company offices and the homes of promoters and the CFO; no demand yet.
  - Auditor's emphasis of matter on the 2022 CBI case in which three employees were arrested with NHAI officials (Guwahati) under the Prevention of Corruption Act; the matter is stayed by the Gauhati High Court.
  - GST show-cause notice of ₹321.6 cr for FY2020-21 (MP), challenged by writ petition.
  
  [AR FY26] [BSE filings, 26 Aug and 25 Sep 2026]
- **NTPC BESS dispute:** on 16 Sep 2026 GR terminated its Mouda BESS EPC contracts with NTPC (citing force majeure and war risk). NTPC invoked a ₹49.53 cr bank guarantee and ₹41.34 cr of surety bonds on 17 Sep. The financial impact is "being assessed". [BSE filings, 16–17 Sep 2026]
- **Contingent liabilities:** ₹2,558 cr per Screener, mostly bank guarantees (₹2,449 cr at Mar-26). Tax and other claims ₹110 cr (including GST on HAM annuities of ₹61 cr at SPVs). [Screener] [AR FY26]
- **Execution dependence on NHAI:** appointed dates have been delayed for over a year (Agra–Gwalior), land comes piecemeal, and road awarding has been weak for 2–3 years. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Diversification risk:** new areas (oil & gas, BESS, BharatNet, warehousing) with uncertain margins, and one BESS contract already terminated. [Q1 call]
- **Related parties:** transfers to the sponsored InvIT (price set by independent valuer) and HAM receivables from its own SPVs. No promoter pledge is flagged on Screener. [Q4 call] [Screener]

## What changed between the two calls
- Revenue momentum improved sharply (Q1 standalone +33% against a 15% guide), but order intake stalled in Q1 and the order book fell.
- The Agra–Gwalior appointed date arrived (7 Sep), after the Q1 call.
- New negatives after the Q1 call: the NTPC BESS termination and bank-guarantee invocation, and the ₹321.6 cr GST notice.

## My read
GR Infra has a strong standalone balance sheet and trades below book value. Q1 FY27 showed a sharp execution rebound (+33–40%) as oil & gas, transmission and new HAMs ramped up. But road margins have structurally compressed from ~14% to ~11% because of aggressive bidding. Consolidated cash flow is deeply negative as it funds HAM equity (~₹3,300 cr more to come). A cluster of legal and regulatory issues (IT search, CBI case, GST notice, NTPC termination) weighs on governance perception. The view would improve with FY27 order wins near ₹20,000 cr, especially profitable BOT and transmission, steady InvIT monetisation, and margins holding at 11%. It would weaken if the NTPC dispute or tax matters crystallise into large cash outflows, or if road awarding stays weak.
