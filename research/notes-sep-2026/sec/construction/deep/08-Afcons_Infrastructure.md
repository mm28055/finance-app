# Afcons Infrastructure Ltd (AFCONS, ISIN INE101I01011)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Figures are consolidated (Screener) unless marked. ₹ cr = rupees crore.

## Business and segments
- Afcons is a heavy-engineering EPC contractor of the Shapoorji Pallonji (SP) group, listed in Nov 2024. It builds marine structures, urban infrastructure (metros, elevated corridors), hydro and underground works (tunnels, dams, water), surface transport (road and rail) and oil & gas/industrial projects. Marquee projects: Atal Tunnel, Chenab rail bridge, and the cable-stayed bridge on the Mumbai–Pune Missing Link (inaugurated 1 May 2026). [CRISIL Aug-26] [Q1 call]
- Order book ₹43,290 cr at 30 Jun 2026 (₹45,767 cr including later wins), up from ~₹32,000 cr at Mar-26. About 75% is in India (14 states) and the rest in 10 countries. [Q1 call] [CRISIL Aug-26]
- Q1 FY27 inflow ₹13,219 cr (₹15,700 cr year to date). It includes a Croatia railway rehabilitation contract (the largest single order in Afcons' history, ~₹7,800 cr according to an analyst on the Q4 call) and Vadhvan port breakwater (~₹5,300 cr per the same analyst; "the world's second largest breakwater"). No L1 positions are left to convert. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Revenue mix: overseas was ~30% of FY26 revenue but only 16% in Q1 FY27. The overseas share of the order book is back to ~25%. Operations span Bangladesh (roads, rail), Maldives (bridge), Tanzania, Benin, Ivory Coast, Gabon and others. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Slow-moving work is about 11% of the book: ~5.65% generally slow-moving, ~2% Bangladesh and ~3% Jal Jeevan Mission. JJM orders total ₹1,221 cr (UP ₹510 cr, MP ₹414 cr, Rajasthan ₹297 cr). [Q1 call]

## Key numbers
- Quarterly revenue (₹ cr): Jun-25 3,370 → Sep-25 2,988 → Dec-25 2,976 → Mar-26 2,614 → Jun-26 2,671 (−21% YoY). [Screener]
- Quarterly operating profit (₹ cr) / OPM: 435 / 13% → 329 / 11% → 410 / 14% → 43 / 1.6% → 251 / 9%. The company's EBITDA definition includes other income and treats BG commission as an operating cost: Q1 ₹263 cr (9.6%), Q4 ₹170 cr. [Screener] [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Quarterly net profit (₹ cr): 137 → 105 → 97 → −89 → 30. Q4 FY26 was the first quarterly loss since Afcons began publishing quarterly results in 2010. It included ~₹260–265 cr of one-offs: a move to an ECL provisioning matrix, a provision after an adverse arbitration development, and extra marine-spread cost. Q4 provisions totalled ~₹160 cr; FY26 provisions ~₹325 cr. [Screener] [Q4 call]
- Annual revenue (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 11,019 → 12,637 → 13,268 → 12,548 → 11,948. OPM: 9% → 11% → 10% → 11% → 10%. Net profit: 358 → 411 → 450 → 487 → 251. FY26 PAT includes a ₹76 cr labour-code charge. Interest rose from ₹425 cr (FY22) to ₹674 cr (FY26). [Screener] [Q4 call]
- ROCE 14% and ROE 5.6% (FY26; the company says ~12% and ~5%). Valuation (25 Sep 2026): market cap ₹9,287 cr, price ₹252 (52-week range ₹225–479), P/E 45.3, price/book 1.7x. The stock is down 43% over one year. [Screener] [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- **FY26 revenue:** at Q3 (Feb 2026) the company expected ~5% growth. Actual was −5% (total income ₹12,322 cr); Q4 fell 18–19%. **Missed badly.** Reasons given: stalled client payments (the company chose liquidity over execution), the March 2026 war-related fuel and logistics shock at overseas sites, fast-track private projects that did not ramp up, and L1s not converting. [Q4 call]
- **FY26 order inflow:** only ₹4,125 cr of new orders (plus ~₹3,800 cr of variations), far below plan. The backlog was completed in Q1 FY27. **Missed, then caught up.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **FY27 order inflow ₹30,000 cr:** set at Q4 and reaffirmed at Q1 ("very confident of booking at a minimum"), with ₹15,700 cr done. The bid pipeline is ₹1.5 lakh cr for the remaining nine months and ₹3.96 lakh cr over two years. **Kept.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **FY27 revenue and margin:** no guidance at Q4 or Q1 because of "uncertainty around… war". H1 is typically 40–45% of annual revenue and H2 55–60%. Q3–Q4 FY27 are expected to "bounce back", with a "strong FY28 and FY29". **No guidance.** The historic ~11% EBITDA target is suspended. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Working capital:** Q4 promised stuck receivables "before June" and net working capital (NWC) back to ~120 days from 143. At Q1, collections had "not moved to a level where we would have expected", and NWC was "marginally" higher than in March. **Missed.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Debt:** Q1 target is FY27-end gross debt of ~₹3,500 cr and net debt of ₹2,700–2,800 cr, despite ₹700–800 cr of capex. Net debt/equity was 0.49x at Mar-26 and ~0.68x at Jun-26. **New target, at risk.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **High-speed rail C2 (Mumbai–Ahmedabad):** Q4 expected tunnelling to start by end-Q1 FY27. Both TBMs started initial drives on schedule; main drives are expected from about November. A force-majeure settlement is expected in Q2. **Kept.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex was ₹1,069 cr (₹700 cr in Q4, mostly TBMs for high-speed rail); CWIP ₹901 cr at Mar-26. FY27 capex is guided at ₹700–800 cr (~₹725 cr at Q4) and FY28 at ₹600–650 cr. [Q4 call] [Q1 call] [Screener]
- The fleet is strategic (marine spreads, TBMs). Management rejects a shift to asset-light because African rental costs recover equipment value in ~9 months. [Q1 call]
- Accelerated TBM depreciation: of FY26 depreciation of ₹454 cr, only ~₹289 cr was normal depreciation. [Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Gross debt ₹3,538 cr and net debt ₹2,653 cr (Mar-26); total debt was ₹2,230 cr a year earlier. Cash ~₹900 cr; ₹1,200 cr of undrawn limits (Jun-26). The company keeps a minimum of ₹500–600 cr in unencumbered cash. [Q4 call] [CRISIL Aug-26]
- Operating cash flow (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 610 → 1,215 → 707 → −132 → −127; free cash flow 259 → 306 → 25 → −470 → −491. [Screener]
- NWC rose from <90 days (FY24) to 117 (FY25) and 139 (FY26), per CRISIL (143 per the company). Unbilled work is building up because certification is delayed. 38% of client advances are interest-bearing (up from ~20% in Sep-24). [CRISIL Aug-26] [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- CRISIL revised the outlook to Negative (AA-) on 19 Aug 2026 and warned of a downgrade if debtor days and operating cash flow do not improve. India Ratings affirmed the A1+ commercial paper rating on 25 Sep 2026. [CRISIL Aug-26] [BSE filing, 25 Sep 2026]
- Dividend payout 29% (FY26). [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Complexity niche: ENR ranks Afcons 8th globally in marine and 12th in international bridges. It won Croatia rail (EU market entry) and Vadhvan breakwater in competitive tenders. Overseas margins run 200–300 bps above domestic. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Margins of 10–11% (company definition) are above most domestic EPC peers. [CRISIL Aug-26]
- Marine fleet and TBM ownership are a pre-qualification advantage. [Q1 call]

## Input costs
- Domestic contracts have pass-through clauses. Overseas contracts are often fixed-price, with contingency buffers. Croatia absorbs the first 10% of cost overruns, with pass-through beyond that. Energy, transport and logistics costs rose in 2026. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Promoter pledge:** Screener shows promoters have pledged 100% of their 50.17% holding (SP group debt). CRISIL notes limited linkages, non-recourse promoter debt and no material support to group entities, but any group stress could weigh on the stock. [Screener] [CRISIL Aug-26]
- **Collections and working capital:** UP JJM withholds payment until connections are 100% complete, contrary to the contract (receivable plus unbilled ~₹400 cr, "similar level"). Bangladesh and Gabon are problem contracts; a Gabon bank guarantee was invoked and paid. Payment stretch is "widespread". [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- **Auditor emphasis of matter (FY26):**
  - uncertainty over arbitration and High Court outcomes on variation claims recognised as receivables and contract assets;
  - at the Transtonnelstroy–Afcons JV, arbitration, High Court and Supreme Court proceedings on earlier variations;
  - at the Dahej standby jetty JV, a High Court appeal against an unfavourable award on liquidated damages.
  
  [AR FY26]
- **Earnings volatility:** profit recognition starts only once a project crosses ~10% completion, and TBM depreciation is accelerated, so quarters swing. The Q1 tax rate was ~40% because of JV losses. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Disputes, including wins:** an arbitration tribunal awarded Afcons ₹147.1 cr against a ₹359.7 cr claim on Ircon's T-74R-A package (Jun 2026); Ircon will challenge it. [Ircon BSE filing, 23 Sep 2026]
- **Disclosure:** management declined to give Q1 gross debt or working-capital days (unaudited). Revenue guidance has been withdrawn. [Q1 call]
- **Cancellations:** two Croatia road L1s were cancelled for budget reasons. The Pune Ring Road and Nagpur–Gondiya L1s are going to rebid. [Q4 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- From apology to recovery: Q4 opened with an apology for the first-ever quarterly loss. Q1 showed ₹13,219 cr of wins and a return to profit (₹30 cr), but revenue fell 21%.
- The liquidity message worsened. The receivables unlock promised "before June" did not happen, and NWC and debt rose further. CRISIL then moved the outlook to Negative.
- High-speed rail tunnelling started on schedule. Vadhvan (protests, approach road) and Croatia will contribute little revenue in FY27.
- The Executive Chairman (S. Krishnamurthy) joined the Q1 call. Management repeatedly pointed to FY28 as the growth year.

## My read
Afcons' engineering franchise and order wins are real: the book rose ~40% in a quarter to a record ₹43,000–46,000 cr. But the financial trajectory is weak. Revenue has been flat to down for three years, profit has halved, operating cash flow has been negative for two years, and debt and working capital keep rising. The liquidity turnaround promised on the Q4 call has not arrived, which is why CRISIL turned Negative. The 100% promoter pledge is a governance overhang. The view would improve with visible H2 FY27 revenue recovery, a drop in NWC towards 120 days, the high-speed rail force-majeure settlement, and the JJM and Bangladesh receivables being collected. It would weaken with a rating downgrade or another provisioning quarter.
