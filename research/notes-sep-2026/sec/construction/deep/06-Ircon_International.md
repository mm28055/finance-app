# Ircon International Ltd (IRCON, ISIN INE962Y01021)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Figures are consolidated (Screener) unless marked "standalone". ₹ cr = rupees crore.

This file uses the Q4 FY26 call (25 May 2026) and the Q1 FY27 presentation.

## Business and segments
- Ircon is a Navratna PSU under the Ministry of Railways; the Government of India owns 65.17%. It is an EPC contractor for railways (new lines, doubling, electrification, signalling, Kavach, tunnels, bridges) and highways, and also invests in PPP projects. [IP Q1FY27] [Screener]
- Order book ₹23,366 cr (30 Jun 2026), down from ₹24,984 cr at Mar-26:
  - by sector: railways ₹17,989 cr (77%), highways ₹3,747 cr (16%), others ₹1,630 cr (7%);
  - by geography: domestic 91%, international 9%;
  - by award route: competitive 55%, nomination 45%.
  
  The Railway Ministry is no longer awarding new nomination work; nomination increases are scope and cost escalations on cost-plus jobs. [IP Q1FY27] [Q4 call]
- 11 subsidiaries: two BOT toll roads, seven HAM roads, a 76% renewable-power company, and Ircon Infrastructure & Services. Seven JVs: coal-connectivity railways CERL, CEWRL, MCRL, JCRL and Bastar Railway (26% each); IRSDC (being wound up); Ircon-Soma Tollway (concession ended 14 May 2026; accounts on a liquidation basis). [IP Q1FY27] [AR FY26]
- International: Algeria, Myanmar, Nepal (two projects), Sri Lanka, Malaysia; the Bangladesh project was just completed. International margins are higher and aided by foreign exchange. [Q4 call]

- Quarterly operating profit (₹ cr) / OPM: 200 / 11% → 141 / 7% → 158 / 7% → 267 / 8% → 192 / 10%. [Screener]
- Quarterly net profit (₹ cr): 164 → 137 → 100 → 191 → 92 (Q1 −44% YoY). Finance cost rose to ₹107 cr (vs ₹75 cr) as HAM/BOT SPV debt grew. Standalone Q1 PAT was ₹164 cr (+9%), so subsidiaries and JVs dragged the consolidated number. [Screener] [IP Q1FY27]
- Standalone core EBITDA margin (excluding other income): Q1 FY27 5.3%; FY26 4.2% (vs 4.7% in FY25). About 60% of standalone pre-tax profit comes from other income (₹500 cr in FY26). [IP Q1FY27]
- Annual revenue (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 7,380 → 10,368 → 12,514 → 10,760 → 9,071. OPM: 8% → 7% → 9% → 8% → 8%. Net profit: 592 → 765 → 930 → 728 → 592. Revenue has shrunk two years running. [Screener]
- ROCE 9%, ROE 8%. Valuation (25 Sep 2026): market cap ₹9,960 cr, price ₹106, near its 52-week low of ₹105 (high ₹186), P/E 19.0, price/book 1.5x, dividend yield 1.8%. The stock is down 39% over one year. [Screener]
- Bidding: 107 bids worth ~₹48,000 cr were under evaluation at Q4. Hit rate by value: 5.7% (FY24), 6% (FY25), 10% (FY26). FY26 order wins were ~₹5,000 cr. [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- **FY26 revenue:** guided ₹10,000–11,000 cr from the Q2 call; actual ₹9,071 cr (operating). **Missed** because of low order inflow, land and clearance delays, and intense competition. [Q4 call]
- **FY27 revenue:** "similar levels as FY26" (~₹9,000 cr), with the order book ~2x revenue. Q1 was +9.5% YoY. **No update (no Q1 call).** [Q4 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Margins:** standalone core EBITDA 4.0–4.5% ("some of the bids are going much below the estimate"). Consolidated core EBITDA ~9%. Consolidated PAT margin 6.1–6.3%. Q1 FY27 delivered standalone core EBITDA 5.3%, consolidated core 9.8%, but a consolidated PAT margin of only 4.5%. **PAT guide at risk.** [Q4 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Investment in SPVs:** ₹700–800 cr requirement, ₹400–600 cr of it in FY27. About ₹3,000 cr is already invested in subsidiaries and JVs. Company capex ₹50–60 cr. [Q4 call]
- **JV profit share:** ~₹70–80 cr a year. The CERL coal-rail loss is shrinking, with break-even in about two years. Ircon-Soma income ends after the concession. [Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Capital goes into PPP roads (HAM and BOT), coal-connectivity rail JVs and renewable power, funded by project debt at 70:30–80:20 debt:equity. Consolidated fixed assets rose to ₹3,524 cr (Mar-26) from ₹1,736 cr (Mar-24). [Q4 call] [Screener]
- Standalone capex is small (machinery, ~₹50–60 cr). [Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Consolidated borrowings rose to ₹5,726 cr (Mar-26) from ₹1,399 cr (Mar-22); almost all is SPV project debt. Standalone debt is minimal (a ₹103 cr working-capital loan taken for delayed receivables, half repaid). [Screener] [Q4 call]
- Reported cash ~₹4,200 cr, but "our own cash is about ₹950 crores". The rest is project-specific or client advances. [Q4 call]
- Operating cash flow (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 1,414 → −278 → −79 → −1,110 → −618; free cash flow was negative for four straight years (FY26 −₹1,148 cr). Debtor days rose to 56 from 23 (FY24). [Screener]
- Dividend: ₹1.90/share for FY26 (interim ₹1.20 + final ₹0.70); payout 30%. [Q4 call] [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Rail pedigree: 5,858 track-km, 10,762 route-km electrified, 167 km of tunnels, 408 domestic and 131 international projects completed. Ranked 239th among ENR's top international contractors (2025). [IP Q1FY27]
- The moat has thinned: nomination work has dried up. Competitive bids are won at prices "much below the estimate", and the standalone core margin fell to ~4%. [Q4 call]
- The IVR AAA/Stable rating (Infomerics, Mar 2026) rests on government linkages. The only downgrade trigger listed is the government stake falling below 51%. [Infomerics Mar-26]

## Input costs
- Contracts carry standard price-variation formulas, whether competitive or nomination. These do not fully cover extraordinary shocks such as the 2026 fuel and bitumen spike; MoRTH and NHAI issued relief on bitumen. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Shrinking scale and margins:** revenue has fallen from ₹12,514 cr (FY24) to ₹9,071 cr (FY26). The order book slipped to ₹23,366 cr. Low-margin competitive work is replacing nomination work. [Screener] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Leverage and cash burn at group level:** SPV debt is up ~4x in four years, and consolidated interest cost rose from ₹62 cr (FY22) to ₹352 cr (FY26). [Screener]
- **Management change:** CMD Hari Mohan Gupta retired on 30 Jun 2026. RVNL's CMD Saleem Ahmad holds additional charge for up to one year. Management said at Q4 it had no official information on media reports of an Ircon–RVNL merger. [News: People Matters/Indian PSU, Jun–Jul 2026] [Q4 call]
- **Disputes:** in an Afcons arbitration over package T-74R-A (USBRL), the majority tribunal awarded ₹147.1 cr against a claim of ₹359.7 cr on 29 Jun 2026. Ircon will challenge it. [BSE filing, 23 Sep 2026]
- **Contingent liabilities (standalone, Mar-26):** legal cases ~₹643 cr (after ₹208 cr of additions and ₹93 cr settled), indirect-tax disputes ₹165 cr, direct tax ₹26 cr, plus guarantees on behalf of subsidiaries. [AR FY26]
- **Auditor:** unmodified. Emphasis of matter: the Ircon-Soma Tollway and IRSDC JVs are prepared on a liquidation basis, with no impairment expected. Key audit matter: revenue recognition under Ind AS 115. [AR FY26]
- **Pledges/promoter:** no pledge. The government holding is 65.17%, down ~8 points over three years after offer-for-sale. [Screener]
- **Disclosure gap:** no Q1 FY27 earnings call was held (or none was filed). [Ircon investor page]

## My read
Ircon is a steady but shrinking railway EPC PSU. Revenue has fallen 28% from its FY24 peak, standalone core margins are only 4–5%, and most standalone profit comes from treasury income. Consolidated profits are being diluted by interest on its growing HAM/BOT portfolio, and group cash flow has been negative for four years. Leadership is interim, and the 107 bids pending (~₹48,000 cr) are the main hope. The view would improve with large railway wins (new DFC corridors, high-speed corridors) that lift the order book above ~₹30,000 cr, and with the coal-rail JVs breaking even. It would weaken if revenue stays near ₹9,000 cr while SPV equity calls continue.
