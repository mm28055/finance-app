# NCC Ltd (NCC, ISIN INE868B01028)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Figures are consolidated (Screener) unless marked "standalone". ₹ cr = rupees crore.

## Business and segments
- NCC is a Hyderabad-based diversified EPC contractor, active for about 48 years, working mainly for state governments and PSUs. It has seven divisions. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Order book ₹81,214 cr at 30 Jun 2026 (record ₹83,004 cr at Mar-26):
  - buildings ₹22,357 cr (28%);
  - transportation ₹16,344 cr (20%);
  - mining ₹13,400 cr (16%; includes 3-year look-ahead volumes of the Pachhwara coal-mining contract);
  - electrical T&D ₹13,312 cr (16%, including smart meters and BharatNet);
  - water & railways ₹10,994 cr (14%);
  - irrigation ₹4,806 cr (6%).
  
  Standalone ₹71,312 cr; subsidiaries ₹9,902 cr. [Q1 call]
- Client mix: PSUs and state government entities 60%, state governments 19%, central government 14%, multilateral-funded (ADB/AIIB) 5%, private 4%. [Q1 call]
- Notable projects:
  - Ken–Betwa river interlinking (₹3,390 cr; only ₹116 cr executed);
  - BharatNet optical fibre (₹6,500 cr balance, fixed price);
  - smart meters: ~7–8 million meters across two Maharashtra SPVs and one Bihar contract, ~45% installed, capex to finish by Mar 2027, then O&M annuity (~₹1 cr a year per 100,000 meters);
  - UP Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM).
  
  [Q1 call]
- Bid pipeline ~₹2.5 lakh cr. [Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- **FY26:** guidance was withheld from the Q2/Q3 calls. Revenue fell 6%. [Q4 call]
- **FY27 at Q4 (May 2026):** "no guidance for all these parameters", citing too much uncertainty. Management promised to revisit after Q1, and analysts pushed back hard. [Q4 call]
- **FY27 at Q1 (Aug 2026):** guidance given for the first time in several quarters. Revenue growth 8–10%, EBITDA margin 8.5–9%, order inflow ₹22,000–25,000 cr. **New.** Q1 was already running at +12% growth and a 9.4% margin, so the guidance looks conservative. [Q1 call]
- **Debt:** Q1 said standalone debt at Mar-27 would be "more or less" flat at ~₹2,250 cr, depending on JJM collections. [Q1 call]
- **Capex:** ₹500 cr for FY27 (₹170 cr in Q1). FY26 capex was ₹912 cr against a budget of ₹1,050 cr, including ~₹320 cr of TBM work in progress. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- **Receivables:** Vizag Urban (a group loan) of ₹271 cr to be recovered by Dec 2026 (Q4 had said ~₹250 cr in FY27). AP Capital City old dues of ₹142 cr expected by Q2 or early Q3. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Equipment capex has been rising: fixed assets ₹2,056 cr and CWIP ₹351 cr at Mar-26, including TBMs. TBM depreciation starts from Q3 FY27. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- Smart-meter SPVs: equity of ~₹460 cr is fully invested. Project debt was ₹1,461 cr at Jun-26, raised at 9.15%. The target is an 18% IRR on total capital. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Consolidated gross debt ₹4,020 cr and net debt ₹3,513 cr at Jun-26 (net ₹1,574 cr a year earlier). ₹1,461 cr of this is smart-meter project debt. Standalone gross debt ₹2,410 cr, net ₹2,008 cr, debt/equity 0.31. [Q1 call]
- Working capital (standalone, excluding cash) ₹5,334 cr, 95 days (97 at Mar-26). Receivables ₹3,055 cr (68 days). Unbilled revenue ₹7,414 cr (38% of revenue), up from ₹6,442 cr a year earlier. Retention ₹2,396 cr. Mobilisation advances ₹3,163 cr, 60% interest-bearing at ~9.1%. [Q1 call]
- UP JJM: receivables plus unbilled ₹1,043 cr at Jun-26 (UP only); all-JJM unbilled ₹2,771 cr. Collections were ~₹1,000 cr in Q4 FY26, ₹610 cr in Q1 FY27 and ₹413 cr in July. JJM order book ₹5,881 cr. [Q1 call] [Q4 call] [CARE Aug-26]
- Operating cash flow (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 1,416 → 1,100 → 1,359 → 742 → −459; free cash flow −₹1,401 cr in FY26. [Screener]
- CARE AA-/Stable reaffirmed on 27 Aug 2026. Debt/PBILDT was 4.90x in FY26, improving to 3.87x in Q1 FY27; TOL/TNW 2.02x. [CARE Aug-26]
- Dividend ₹2.20/share for FY26 (payout 20%). [BSE filing, 28 Aug 2026] [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Scale and diversification (a ~₹81,000 cr book across seven verticals and many states) and a long record. Margins are consistently ~9% even in bad years. About 81% of contracts carry price-escalation clauses. [Q1 call] [Screener]
- Growth areas are building up: smart metering (annuity), mining MDO (Pachhwara), river interlinking. Private-sector orders are only 4%. [Q1 call]

## Input costs
- Steel and cement are stable. Petroleum-linked inputs, aluminium, copper and optical fibre cable rose in 2026. The BharatNet contract is fixed-price on fibre, which "may not be resulting into a loss, but it will result into a lesser profit". NCC is lobbying the client for relief. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **State payment risk:** 79% of the book is with state-linked clients. The UP JJM receivables crisis dented FY26, and collections are improving but not yet complete. Telangana Mission Bhagiratha dues of ~₹180 cr are sub judice (₹50 cr received). [Q1 call]
- **Rising leverage and unbilled revenue:** consolidated net debt more than doubled year on year, and unbilled revenue keeps growing. The auditor made receivables and contract assets (₹3,336 cr + ₹9,552 cr, 56% of standalone assets) a key audit matter. [Q1 call] [AR FY26]
- **Contingent liabilities (group, Mar-26):** tax disputes ~₹350 cr (sales tax ₹137 cr, GST ₹155 cr, service tax ₹35 cr); NHAI disputes ₹512 cr. [AR FY26]
- **Related-party exposure:** loans to group companies ₹290 cr and investments ₹867 cr; the old Vizag Urban real-estate loan is still being recovered. An Oman investment was impaired by ₹21.3 cr in FY26. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- **Promoter holding is low** at 23.09% (rising slowly through buying). No pledge is flagged on Screener. [Screener]
- **Project risk:** agitation over Ken–Betwa land (management says it is 20–30 km from its site); BharatNet fibre price and availability. [Q1 call]
- **Disclosure:** guidance was withheld for three quarters before Q1. The auditor's report found no emphasis of matter in the extracts reviewed. [Q4 call] [AR FY26]

## What changed between the two calls
- Guidance went from none (May) to explicit FY27 numbers (Aug). Q1 beat them on revenue and margin.
- JJM cash flow kept improving (₹1,000 cr in Q4, ₹610 cr in Q1, ₹413 cr in July). Debt rose further on smart-meter drawdowns.
- New worries: fibre-cable prices for BharatNet, the Ken–Betwa protests, and the sub judice Telangana dues.

## My read
NCC has a very large, executable order book (~3.5–4x revenue), steady ~9% margins, and a cheap valuation (~1x book). Q1 FY27 showed growth returning (+12%) as UP JJM money started to flow. The weak points are cash conversion (FY26 operating cash flow negative, rising unbilled revenue), consolidated debt rising with smart-meter capex, and heavy dependence on state governments. The view would improve if JJM receivables fall below ₹1,000 cr, unbilled revenue drops as BharatNet billing starts, and consolidated debt stabilises. It would weaken if state payments stall again or BharatNet fibre costs start eating margins.
