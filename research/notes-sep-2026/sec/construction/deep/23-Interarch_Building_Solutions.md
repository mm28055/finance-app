# Interarch Building Solutions (NSE: INTERARCH)

*Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Standalone figures in ₹ crore (Screener has no consolidated page).*

## Business and segments
- Interarch is a Noida-based pre-engineered building (PEB) and steel-structure company. It does design, engineering, in-house fabrication, delivery and on-site erection, sold as a lump-sum "one building, one date, one price" package. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- End-markets: factories and warehouses (for example IndoSpace, LOGOS, Horizon, Lodha Industrial), electric vehicles, lithium batteries, semiconductors (Micron Sanand, Tata Electronics Assam), renewables, data centres, and some airports and hospitals. [Q4 call]
- CRISIL describes it as the second-largest integrated PEB capacity in India. Key repeat customers include Reliance, Tata group and Aditya Birla group. [CRISIL Apr-26]
- Installed capacity:
  - 200,000 t in FY26, 221,000 t after the Kheda (Gujarat) Phase 1 plant started on 9 Jul 2026, and **245,000 t** after the Andhra Pradesh heavy-structural-steel Unit 2 Phase 1 began commercial production on 24 Sep 2026. [Q1 call] [BSE 24-Sep-26]
  - FY26 volume was 162,000 t (about 80% of installed and about 88% of "utilisable" capacity). Q1 FY27 volume was 38,499 t. [Q1 call]
- Exports: about ₹40 cr of orders in 12 months (Africa, Canada, Myanmar). A JV/MOU with Canada's ER Steel will set up a 100%-export open-web-joist plant: 15,000 t at full capacity (about US$20–23 m of sales, targeted 20% EBITDA), starting around July 2027. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Promoters hold 59.44%. The company raised about ₹180 cr in its IPO, per management. [Screener] [Q1 call]

## Key numbers
- Quarterly revenue, Mar-25 → Jun-26 (₹ cr): 464, 381, 491, 523, 504, 460. [Screener]
- Quarterly operating margin (OPM), same quarters: 11%, 8%, 8%, 10%, 10%, 9%. [Screener]
- Quarterly net profit, same quarters (₹ cr): 39, 28, 32, 37, 37, 28. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27: revenue ₹460 cr (+20.7%), EBITDA ₹39 cr (8.6%), PAT ₹28 cr (flat, as other income fell). [Q1 call]
- FY26: revenue ₹1,898 cr (+30.6%), EBITDA ₹184 cr (about 10%), PAT ₹135 cr. [Screener] [Q4 call]
- FY21–FY26 history (₹ cr):
  - Revenue: 576, 835, 1,124, 1,293, 1,454, 1,898.
  - PAT: 6, 17, 81, 86, 108, 135. [Screener]
- Market data:
  - Market cap ₹2,893 cr; price ₹1,720 against a 52-week range of ₹1,600–2,763, down 14% in one year.
  - P/E 21.2; ROCE 23.7%; ROE 16.8%. [Screener]
- Debt is negligible (₹18 cr of borrowings); interest cost ₹1 cr a quarter. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- **Q4 FY26 call (14 May 2026):**
  - FY27 revenue of ₹2,150–2,200 cr.
  - Execution of ₹550–600 cr a quarter from a ₹1,700 cr order book "fulfilled in the next 9 months".
  - FY26 projections were described as "crossed". [Q4 call]
- **Q1 FY27 call (7 Aug 2026):**
  - FY27 revenue of ₹2,150–2,200 cr maintained. This needs about ₹1,700 cr (about ₹570 cr a quarter) over the next nine months.
  - **FY28 revenue target raised to ₹2,700 cr from ₹2,500 cr.**
  - EBITDA of 9.5–10% for FY28, with FY27 "remaining as we had projected".
  - The MD was "not very sure that it will show immediate results this year" on margin expansion, because new plants carry costs ahead of revenue. [Q1 call]
- **CRISIL's view:** revenue compound growth of 13–14% and operating margin of 9–10% over the medium term. [CRISIL Apr-26]
- **Track record:** revenue guidance has broadly been met; FY26 grew 30.6%. Margins have stayed in a narrow 8–11% band. [Screener] [Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- The company has spent about ₹230–240 cr on capex since April 2024, more than its IPO proceeds. [Q1 call]
- A planned **QIP of about ₹250 cr** (up from a ₹100 cr approval) will fund:
  - Andhra heavy-structure Phases 2 and 3 (about ₹140–150 cr), targeted for Mar-27 and Dec-27, taking the plant to 75,000–80,000 t.
  - A second Gujarat PEB plant (₹50–60 cr; land already bought).
  - The Canada open-web-joist export unit (₹50–60 cr). [Q1 call]
- FY26 fixed assets rose to ₹287 cr and capital work in progress (CWIP) to ₹58 cr. [Screener]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- The company is almost debt-free (borrowings ₹18 cr). Cash and treasury are being drawn down for capex, so other income is falling. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- FY26 operating cash flow was **−₹19 cr** and free cash flow −₹138 cr. Management cited three reasons:
  - large projects billed but not yet collected;
  - extra steel stocking ahead of price rises;
  - advance payments to steel suppliers.
  - Management said "we are back to positive now" in Q1. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- Working capital days rose from 21 (FY24) to 56 (FY26); the cash conversion cycle is 73 days. [Screener]
- Fixed deposits held as margin for guarantees and letters of credit rose to ₹5.1 cr from ₹0.4 cr. [AR FY26]
- CRISIL A/Stable/A1 was reaffirmed on 28 Apr 2026 on ₹600 cr of bank lines (enhanced from ₹495 cr). Gearing is expected below 0.05x and interest coverage above 16x. [CRISIL Apr-26]

## Moat evidence
- Scale and integration: design, detailing, fabrication across five-plus plants, and erection. The company is described as the second-largest integrated PEB capacity in India. [CRISIL Apr-26] [Q1 call]
- Repeat blue-chip clients and "preferred partner" status on complex, fast-track projects (semiconductor fabs, EV and battery plants, data centres). [Q4 call] [CRISIL Apr-26]
- Structural tailwind: steel off-site construction saves "about 50%-60% time". Labour scarcity at sites favours PEB (management claim). [Q1 call]
- Capacity-constrained order intake: management books orders only to what it can deliver on time. [Q1 call]

## Input costs
- Steel is the main raw material. Rising steel prices can hurt gross margin on orders being executed because of the gap between bidding and execution. Mitigation comes through bulk procurement. [CRISIL Apr-26]
- In Q4 FY26 the company paid advances to steel suppliers to secure supply as prices rose. [Q1 call]
- Q4 FY26 EBITDA absorbed labour-code costs, export-certification professional fees and outsourced high-rise engineering. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Income-tax search, under auditor emphasis of matter:**
  - The search and survey under sections 132/133A ran from 18 to 22 Aug 2025. It covered offices, plants and residences of certain employees and KMPs. [AR FY26]
  - On 15 Sep 2026 the company received a section 142(1) notice for the **block assessment period 1 Apr 2019 – 17 Oct 2025**. The notice seeks issue-wise explanations and questionnaires for AY2020-21 to AY2025-26. [BSE 15-Sep-26]
  - No demand has been raised yet, and the impact "cannot be ascertained at this stage".
- **Cash conversion:** negative operating cash flow in FY26 despite a record PAT. Capex and a QIP are both needed to fund growth. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- **Margin ceiling:** EBITDA has stayed stuck at about 8.5–10.5%. New plants add fixed cost ahead of revenue. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- **Execution risk in new areas:** heavy structures for high-rises and data centres, and exports to the US and Canada. These are new capabilities. [Q1 call]
- **Cyclicality:** private industrial capex drives demand. CRISIL flags end-user cyclicality and raw-material volatility. [CRISIL Apr-26]
- **Seasonality:** Q1 and Q2 revenue are weaker because of site clearances and the monsoon. [Q1 call]

## My read
Interarch is the cleanest way in this list to play private industrial capex (factories, EVs, semiconductors, data centres). It has net cash, CRISIL A, 24% ROCE and 25–30% volume-led growth. Capacity has risen about 23% to 245,000 t this year, with more heavy-structure capacity funded by a planned QIP. Exports offer a higher-margin option. The weak spots are a margin ceiling of about 9–10% tied to steel prices, negative operating cash flow in FY26, and an unresolved income-tax block assessment after the August 2025 search, which the auditor flagged as an emphasis of matter. At about 21x earnings, delivery on the ₹2,150–2,200 cr FY27 target and a clean end to the tax assessment are the key checks.
