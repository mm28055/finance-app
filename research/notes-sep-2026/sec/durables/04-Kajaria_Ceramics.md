## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Kajaria Ceramics

## Executive Summary
Kajaria Ceramics is the undisputed Category Leader in the Indian tile industry, commanding a dominant share of the organized market. The company exists to provide comprehensive tiling and bathroom solutions, solving the consumer need for branded, reliable, and aesthetically superior home improvement products in a market historically fragmented by unorganized players. While Kajaria previously navigated a "clean-up" phase dubbed "Kajaria 2.0"—characterized by flattish revenues, strategic dealer unification, SKU rationalization, and the closure of its plywood division—the company has definitively pivoted back to high growth. Driven by a structural tailwind where unorganized players face crippling fuel inflation, Kajaria has executed a massive return to volume and revenue growth (jumping 20% YoY in Q1 FY27). Leveraging a multi-locational manufacturing advantage, the company is aggressively defending and expanding its margins (printing an exceptional 19.60%). The investment narrative now centers on a multi-year compound earnings growth cycle as the company accelerates in-house capex and pushes into adjacent verticals like bathware and adhesives. 

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
*   **Core Product:** Manufacturing and trading of Ceramic Wall/Floor tiles, Glazed Vitrified Tiles (GVT), and Polished Vitrified Tiles (PVT). 
*   **Adjacencies:** Sanitaryware and Faucets (Bathware), and Tile Adhesives/Grouts. The company recently gained 100% control of its Bathware subsidiary (Kerovit) by acquiring the remaining 15% stake from a PE investor.
*   **Customer Base:** Primarily Retail homeowners (approx. 70% of mix) and an aggressively expanding Institutional/Project client base (30%), marked by recent breakthrough contracts with mega-builders.
*   **Monetization:** Revenue is generated through a vast dealer network of roughly 1,800 dealers. The company successfully shifted from product-specific dealers to a "unified" dealer model to encourage cross-selling, a transition now considered fully resolved.

**Geographic Note:** Domestic sales heavily dominate (exports are less than 1% of turnover). With global trade uncertainty and steep domestic demand, Kajaria remains laser-focused on the Indian consumption story.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
*   **Moat Status: Widening and Durable.**
    *   **Brand & Distribution:** Kajaria possesses immense pricing power, successfully executing a 10-14% price hike in Q1 FY27 across its portfolio without sacrificing volume growth. This operational leverage pushed EBITDA margins to 19.60% (+288 bps YoY).
    *   **Multi-Locational Scale:** With over 82 MSM of current capacity and geographically dispersed manufacturing, Kajaria holds a structural cost advantage over single-cluster peers. By relying on GAIL and Southern CGD companies, Kajaria avoids the severe gas price shocks hitting the unorganized sector.
    *   **Bear Case on Moat Neutralized:** The historical "Morbi Threat" (unorganized cluster in Gujarat) has structurally deteriorated. Because Morbi relies exclusively on GSPC for gas, spiking spot rates have forced unorganized players to hike prices by 40-45% merely to survive. Consequently, the historical price gap between unorganized and branded tiles has halved from 40% to 20%, decisively shifting consumer preference toward Kajaria.

## Growth Vectors

### Efficiency over Expansion
While the previous phase of Kajaria 2.0 explicitly favored internal efficiency over capacity expansion, the collapse of Morbi's price advantage has triggered a massive strategic pivot. 
*   **In-House Capex Acceleration:** Realizing that Morbi's fuel volatility is a permanent supply chain risk, management has stepped away from its asset-light outsourcing targets. The company is injecting Rs ~400 Cr in FY27 to add 21 MSM of new capacity across Srikalahasti (South) and Gailpur/Jaipur (North).
*   **Value and ROCE Migration:** The new capex utilizes highly efficient 340-meter kilns. These state-of-the-art large-format tile facilities are expected to generate 2x asset turnover with significantly lower operating expenses, structurally elevating return on capital once commissioned in April 2027.

### New Revenue Sources
*   **Institutional & Project Sales:** Moving beyond its traditional retail stronghold, Kajaria is aggressively targeting B2B real estate demand, recently securing major volume commitments from top-tier national builders. 
*   **Bathware & Adhesives:** With Adhesives growing 80% YoY and Bathware growing 33%, these segments are rapidly transitioning into standalone pillars, reducing reliance on the pure tile cycle.
*   **Network Expansion:** The company plans to add 100 new dealers this year (50 of which will be exclusive), further densifying its pan-India footprint.

## Key Risks & Governance

### Governance Red Flag: The Kerovit Fraud
A historical risk factor materialized in FY26 with the detection of financial irregularities at the step-down subsidiary, *Kerovit Global Pvt. Ltd.*
*   **The Incident:** A senior employee (8-year tenure) siphoned funds via vendor fraud and capex manipulation.
*   **Management Response:** Following an EY forensic audit, management overhauled signatory powers and vendor onboarding processes. Furthermore, to support corporate profitability and signal alignment with shareholders during this growth phase, the promoters have re-confirmed they will draw zero salary for the current financial year.
*   **Investor Caution:** While transparency has been excellent and Kajaria has since bought out the remaining 15% PE stake in Kerovit, the past event still warrants monitoring to ensure new subsidiary control systems remain robust.

### Business & Macro Risks
*   **Energy Volatility:** Fuel is a major cost component. While Kajaria currently enjoys massive margin arbitrage (blended fuel cost of Rs 71/scm vs Morbi's Rs 85/scm), any severe macroeconomic shock that crashes global gas prices could allow Morbi players to slash tile prices and widen the competitive gap again.
*   **Capacity Timing:** Kajaria's Rs 400 Cr capacity expansion is slated to come online in early FY28. If the domestic real estate cycle cools concurrently, the company could face near-term underutilization.
*   **Working Capital:** Previously a risk due to dealer liquidity stress, working capital management has seen a sharp turnaround, with cycle days improving dramatically to 46 days (from a peak of 64 in late 2025). 

## Investment Thesis

### The Bull Case
Kajaria Ceramics is transitioning from a "quality in distress" play to a powerful structural compounder. The unorganized sector in Morbi has been permanently crippled by structural gas inflation, halving the price gap and driving a massive consumer migration toward branded tiles. With tremendous pricing power, reinstated double-digit volume guidance, and a target of crossing Rs 1,000 Cr in absolute operating EBITDA this year, Kajaria is firing on all cylinders. As 21 MSM of highly efficient, low-opex new capacity comes online in April 2027, the company is primed to push margins past the 20% mark, driving a multi-year earnings growth cycle insulated by a multi-locational manufacturing moat. 

### The Bear Case
The primary risk to the growth story is a sudden collapse in global spot gas prices. If fuel costs normalize for single-cluster unorganized players, Morbi could swiftly reverse its 40-45% price hikes, renewing a price war at the lower end of the ceramic portfolio. Furthermore, the company's aggressive Rs 400 Cr pivot back to heavy in-house manufacturing abandons its prior asset-light strategy. Should the new Srikalahasti and Gailpur capacities come online just as macroeconomic headwinds cool the domestic real estate market, Kajaria may experience margin dilution from underutilized assets and heightened depreciation, testing the limits of its premiumization narrative.
