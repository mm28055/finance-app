# Stove Kraft (Pigeon) — deep research, Sep 2026

Note on numbers: Screener's *consolidated* page for STOVEKRAFT is stale (stops at FY21 — the company had no subsidiaries until the 2026 China entities). All figures below use the **standalone** Screener page, which matches the company's "consolidated" results. Price ₹814, market cap ₹2,696 cr (Screener, 27 Sep 2026).

## Business and segments
- Makes and sells kitchen products under **Pigeon** (value), **Gilma** (chimneys/hobs, sold through franchise outlets) and licensed **Black+Decker**; one reported segment, "Kitchen and Home appliances" — no segment margins are disclosed. [AR FY26 note 41]
- Q1 FY27 product mix: induction cooktops 27% of revenue (+315.9% YoY), pressure cookers +41.3%, non-stick cookware 21% of revenue (+21.8%), small appliances –2.2%, gas cooktops –6.9% (small-appliance lines were diverted to induction). [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Channel mix Q1 FY27: e-commerce 31.8%, general trade 28.6%, OEM exports 15.3%, modern retail 13.0%, own retail stores 8.7%; general trade grew 56.2% (best in three years), own retail +86.3%. FY26 mix: e-com 35.9%, GT 29.7%, MT 12.4%, exports 10.7%, retail 7.5%, corporate 3.8%. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Geography: South India is "around 50%" of sales; North/East are the growth white space. Exports go to Walmart (US/Mexico), a large UK retailer and, from Q2 FY27, IKEA's global network. [Q1 call; ICRA Dec-25]
- Plants: Bengaluru (~53 acres, heavily backward-integrated — makes its own lids, gaskets, die-cast parts, PCBs assembly, coils) and Baddi (inner-lid cookers); a 1.8 lakh sq ft IKEA plant at Harohalli; a cast-iron foundry at 2.2 million pieces/yr (designed for 4.4 million); chimneys made in-house since Q1 FY26. Deck claims capability of ~70 million units; in-house share of production is 97–98%. [AR FY26 MD&A p.37; IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Retail: 346 Pigeon stores in 160 cities at June 2026 (180 COCO, 78 COFO, 88 FOFO — franchise share rising); average sale ₹4.36 lakh per store per month vs break-even ₹2.5 lakh; target 500 stores by end-2027. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]

## Key numbers (Screener, standalone, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly revenue / operating profit / net profit: Jun-25 340 / 36 / 10; Sep-25 474 / 57 / 21; Dec-25 378 / 34 / 4; Mar-26 415 / 39 / 6; Jun-26 481 / 54 / 17. Operating margin moved 10% → 12% → 9% → 10% → 11%. [Screener]
- Annual: FY24 sales 1,361, OP 121 (9%), PAT 34; FY25 1,448, 151 (10%), 39; FY26 1,605, 157 (10%), 42. TTM sales 1,748, PAT 49. Five-year sales CAGR 13%, three-year 8%; three-year average ROE 9%, FY26 ROCE 11%. [Screener]
- The drag below operating profit is heavy: FY26 depreciation ₹80 cr (FY24 ₹49 cr) and interest ₹30 cr eat most of the ₹157 cr operating profit, leaving a 2.6% net margin. This is the cost of five years of capex and store roll-out; the whole bull case is operating leverage on that fixed base. [Screener; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Company's Q1 FY27 view: revenue ₹480.6 cr (+41.3%), gross margin 39.6% (+127 bps YoY), EBITDA ₹53.8 cr (11.2%), PAT ₹17.1 cr (3.5%). Other expenses jumped to 18% of sales: job-work (outsourced manufacturing) +1.2 pts, marketing +1.2 pts, franchise commission +1 pt, advance CSR. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Unit economics: Q4 FY26 induction value +89.4% on volume +67.3%; small appliances value +12.7% on volume +97.7% (cheaper items drove volume). Price hikes of about 10% were taken in Q1 FY27; exporters accepted higher prices from 1 June 2026. [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (13 May 2026): "upwards of 15%" revenue growth in FY27; "protect 11%" EBITDA margin and grow from there; gross margin +1 pt a year to ~42% in 2–3 years; IKEA first line to bill "from this quarter" (Q1), second line by Q3, third by Q4, **₹40–50 cr** in FY27 and ₹200–250 cr at full use of three lines; FY27 capex about ₹40 cr including retail; working capital "below 30 days"; peak revenue ₹2,500–3,000 cr from existing plants. [Q4 call, official transcript]
- Q1 FY27 delivery: revenue +41.3% (well ahead), EBITDA margin 11.2% (met), gross margin +127 bps YoY (met). **IKEA missed Q1** — no supply in the quarter, started in Q2. Working capital rose to 45 days from 23 days (missed the "below 30" line, explained as festive stocking). [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 FY27 call raised the ambition: 15–20% revenue CAGR ("same CAGR as the last 10 years"), non-induction categories ~20%, EBITDA margin 14–15% and PAT margin ~7% in 2–3 years, induction revenue "2x of last year" in FY27 and ≥20% of FY27 revenue, exports incl. IKEA ~15% of revenue within two years; Q2 FY27 to grow on last year's ₹474 cr base. IKEA at full scale capped at "5–6% of our business". [Q1 call]
- Verdict: guidance was **raised** on growth and margins; IKEA timing **slipped one quarter**; no dropped targets. [Q1 call vs Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Five-year build: fixed assets rose from ₹221 cr (FY21) to ₹583 cr + ₹15 cr CWIP (FY26); management says over ₹500 cr was invested and that plants are "designed for ₹3,000 cr" vs ₹1,605 cr today — i.e. roughly 50–55% utilisation by value. Asset turn on net block is about 2.7x now; at ₹2,500–3,000 cr it would be 4–5x. [Screener; Q4 call]
- FY26 cash capex: cash-flow statement shows ₹109–111 cr, but part was funded by suppliers' credit; CFO said net cash out was ₹70–75 cr. FY26 guidance had been ₹50 cr — an overrun. ICRA had been told ₹70–80 cr. [Q4 call; ICRA Dec-25]
- FY27 guidance ~₹40 cr, yet the balance sheet shows ₹52.9 cr of capital commitments still to be executed at 31 Mar 2026 (₹58.2 cr a year earlier), and the Q1 call added new projects: a fully automated pressure-cooker line, a triply cookware line "ready by end of third quarter", added induction and small-appliance lines at Baddi and Bengaluru, and 25–30 new stores a quarter. Expect FY27 capex above ₹40 cr. [AR FY26 note 43(b); Q1 call; Q4 call]
- China: board approved (25 May 2026) a wholly-owned trading subsidiary, Stove Kraft Guangzhou Import Export, and a 50:50 JV with Ningbo Wochi New Materials to make triply sheets/circles; machines being installed, commercial output targeted before December 2026. Investment amount not disclosed. [News: ScanX, May 2026; Q1 call]
- Induction output: ~2 million units made in FY26; run-rate now 4–5 million with no "meaningful" capex. Payback rule stated: "whatever we invest, we should get back in maximum 3 years" — not met on the past ₹500 cr (cumulative PBT FY22–26 about ₹256 cr, an analyst challenged this on the call). [Q4 call; Screener]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Operating cash flow jumped to ₹258 cr in FY26 (₹130 cr FY25) and free cash flow to ₹150 cr — mainly working-capital release, not profit. Cash conversion cycle fell from 73 to 27 days: debtor days 33 → 26, inventory days 147 → 133, payable days 107 → 133. [Screener]
- How the cycle was cut: channel financing on receivables, payables pushed onto TReDS/payable-finance (suppliers are paid in 90 days by a financier; MSME limit is 45 days), and supplier's credit on capex. These are real but partly *financing* moves, not pure efficiency. [Q4 call; AR FY26 notes 17–18]
- True net debt at 31 Mar 2026: **₹171 cr** (cash ₹22 cr; lease liabilities ₹60 cr; suppliers' credit ₹55 cr; bank borrowings ₹2 cr; **sale-and-leaseback liability ₹76 cr**). Company quotes ₹27 cr by excluding leases and the leaseback. Year earlier ₹368 cr. Screener's "borrowings" (₹117 cr) leaves out the leaseback. [AR FY26 note 36; AR FY26 MD&A p.38]
- The Q4 FY26 plant sale-and-leaseback (accounted as a loan, note 22) raised finance cost even as bank debt fell; FY26 cash interest paid rose from ₹19 cr to ₹25 cr. Lease liabilities also fell from ₹168 cr to ₹60 cr because store-lease life was cut from 9 to 3 years — an accounting change, not repayment. [Q4 call; AR FY26 note 22; ICRA Dec-25]
- Working capital reversed in Q1 FY27: 45 days (inventory 140 days) vs 23 days at March, on festive and induction stock build. [IP Q1FY27]
- Dividend: 35% (₹3.50/share) proposed for FY26, payout ~28%. [AR FY26 directors' report; Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Scale and cost: claims volume leadership in pressure cookers and induction on both big e-commerce sites; "by volume numbers the largest" in pressure cookers at current run-rate. No third-party share figure disclosed (analysts asked for one). [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Backward integration proved itself in March–June 2026: when LPG shortages (Iran war) spiked induction demand and import-dependent assemblers ran dry, Stove Kraft doubled induction output using in-house PCBs and coils; only precision glass and magnets are imported (33–40% of input value). [Q4 call]
- Pricing: took ~10% hikes in Q1 FY27 and still expanded gross margin 127 bps; describes itself as "cost-plus" with quarterly pass-through. That is pass-through, not pricing power — it works because competitors face the same costs. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Distribution: ICRA upgraded the rating to [ICRA]A (Stable)/A1 in Dec 2025, citing brand recall, distribution, and backward integration; 346 own stores and quick-commerce reach are growing faster than the market. [ICRA Dec-25; IP Q1FY27]
- Weakness: value positioning in a crowded market (TTK Prestige, Hawkins, Butterfly and many new D2C brands); ICRA says competition "constrains pricing flexibility". Switching costs are nil. [ICRA Dec-25]

## Risks and red flags
- **Auditor emphasis of matter** on the November 2023 Income Tax search: assessment orders for several years with ₹2.45 cr of extra tax demanded; appeals pending. Management says no material impact. [AR FY26 auditor's report; note 50]
- **Bank stock statements do not match books**: receivables reported to lenders exceeded books by ₹19–35 cr every quarter of FY26 (March 2026: ₹145 cr reported vs ₹115 cr in books); inventory reported below books by ₹16–27 cr. Explained as credit-note accruals and provisions, but it is a recurring CARO disclosure. [AR FY26 CARO (ii)(b)]
- **Audit trail gap**: one accounting software did not log direct database changes. [AR FY26 auditor's report 16(h)(vi)]
- **Promoter pledge**: Rajendra Gandhi pledged a further 2 lakh shares to Bajaj Finance in March 2026, taking encumbered shares to 29 lakh (8.76% of capital, roughly 16% of the promoter holding); ICRA noted 14.62% of promoter shares pledged. The loan financed his March 2022 share purchase. [News: ScanX, Mar 2026; ICRA Dec-25]
- Contingent liabilities are small: ₹18 cr (indirect tax ₹14.7 cr, direct tax ₹2.2 cr, PF ₹0.65 cr) plus ₹7.1 cr interest. Related-party dealings are limited to KMP pay (MD ₹1.58 cr) and small balances with promoter entities. [AR FY26 notes 42–43]
- Demand-spike risk: Q1 FY27 was flattered by the LPG-shortage induction rush and the GST cut to 5%; analysts on the call openly doubted sustainability. [Q1 call]
- China dependence rises with the Guangzhou trading entity and triply JV (50:50, equal board rights) — capital amount undisclosed. [News: ScanX, May 2026]
- CFO change: Ramakrishna Pendyala handed over to Subhadeep Pal on 16 May 2026. [Q4 call]
- Low returns: ROE 8–9% for three years; three-year payback promise unmet. [Screener; Q4 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "recovery" (US tariffs cut from ~25% to ~13%, exports normalising, IKEA starting) to "structural shift" (induction adoption, premiumisation, 15–20% CAGR, 14–15% margin). Exports/tariffs barely came up in Q1. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- New issues: supply bottlenecks forcing job-work outsourcing (after claiming 97–98% in-house at Q4), higher franchise commission as store mix shifts, the China JV, and working capital doubling from 23 to 45 days. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- IKEA quietly moved from Q1 to Q2; management also downplayed it (5–6% of business at full scale). [Q1 call]

## Best management quotes
- "We are very confident of the current margin levels... improve at least 1% on gross margin year-on-year." — R. Gandhi [Q1 call]
- "Though we are a brand company, we still are a cost plus." — R. Gandhi [Q1 call]
- "We have moved from 800 crores in revenue to 1,600 crores, but the capex is designed for 3,000 crores." — R. Gandhi [Q4 call]

## My read
The business is clearly improving: FY26 cash flow was the best in its history, net debt more than halved, and Q1 FY27 delivered 41% growth with an 11% margin. But much of the cash gain came from supplier and channel financing and an accounting change on leases, and Q1 demand was lifted by a one-off LPG scare; the Q2–Q3 prints (festive quarters against a ₹474 cr Sep-25 base) are the real test. At about 55 times trailing earnings the price already assumes the 15–20% growth and a rise toward 14–15% margins actually happen; history (10–11% margins, 8–9% ROE, capex overruns) says be sceptical. The view improves if H1 FY27 EBITDA margin holds above 11% with working capital back under 30 days and IKEA billing visibly; it worsens if induction demand fades and job-work costs stick.
