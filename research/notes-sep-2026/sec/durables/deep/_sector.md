# Indian consumer durables and home products — sector research (Phase A2), 27 Sep 2026

Scope: the 42 companies in `sec/durables/` — room ACs and cooling, fans/lighting/electricals, kitchen and houseware, paints, tiles/sanitaryware/bathware, plywood/laminates/MDF, footwear, mattresses, water purifiers, batteries, personal/home-care brands.
Source tags: [ICRA RAC Sep-25], [ICRA Tiles Sep-25], [ICICI Sec GST Sep-25], [Anarock Q2-26], [Morbi exports Aug-26], [BT Morbi Mar-26], [IBEF/PLI Jan-26], [RBI Apr-26], [Copper Sep-26], [Paints Sep-26], [CARE Greenpanel Aug-26]; company facts carry the company and document, most from the companies' own Q4 FY26 / Q1 FY27 calls, presentations and FY26 annual reports gathered in this research round (files in `deep/`).

## 1. The big picture in five findings
1. **FY27 started with a price shock, not a demand boom.** The West Asia war (from March 2026) pushed up gas, crude-linked chemicals, copper and freight at once. Almost every sub-sector raised prices 5–20% in March–May 2026; Q1 FY27 revenue growth of 15–45% at many companies is mostly price, with volume growth in single digits outside cooling. [company Q1 calls — Somany, Orient Bell, Cera, Hindware, Kansai, TTK, Cello, Voltas]
2. **Weather still decides the cooling year.** Unseasonal rain cut room-AC sales 15–20% in April–July 2025 and FY26 industry volumes fell 10–15% (ICRA); a hot 2026 summer then lifted Voltas's RAC volume ~45% in Q1 FY27 — but industry capacity is now far above demand, so price discipline is weak. [ICRA RAC Sep-25; Voltas Q1 call; Blue Star Q1 call]
3. **Oversupply is the main profit risk in three categories** — room ACs (capacity heading to 35–38 mn units vs ~11–14 mn demand), MDF (capacity 4–5 mn CBM vs demand ~3–3.7 mn CBM) and decorative paints (Birla Opus, JSW, Haisha doubling capacity). In each, Q1 FY27 showed price rises being eroded or rolled back. [ICRA RAC Sep-25; Greenpanel Q1 call; Greenply AR FY26; Paints Sep-26]
4. **Policy has turned supportive but costly.** GST 2.0 (22 Sep 2025) cut ACs/dishwashers/large TVs from 28% to 18%, footwear ₹1,000–2,500 from 12% to 5%, soaps/shampoo/toothpaste from 18% to 5%; income-tax relief lifted urban demand; but BIS quality-control orders (QCOs), the January 2026 BEE energy-label reset and compressor import caps raise costs and favour scale players. [ICICI Sec GST Sep-25; ICRA RAC Sep-25; Voltas AR FY26; Whirlpool Q1 call]
5. **Organised players are gaining share from disruption, not from demand.** Morbi (≈80–90% of India's tile output) shut for ~6 weeks and its exports fell 70%; unorganised plywood makers ran part-time on cash-only resin; small appliance assemblers ran out of imported parts. Share gains are real but may partly reverse when gas and freight normalise. [BT Morbi Mar-26; Morbi exports Aug-26; Greenply Q4 call; Stove Kraft Q1 call]

## 2. Demand drivers
- **Summer seasonality.** Cooling (ACs, coolers, fans) earns most of its profit in February–June. ICRA: unseasonal rain cut RAC sales 15–20% in April–July 2025 vs 40–50% growth a year earlier; channel inventory doubled to ~2.5 mn units by July 2025; FY26 volume forecast 11.0–11.5 mn units (–10–15%) after a record 12.5–13.0 mn in FY25. [ICRA RAC Sep-25] Voltas puts FY26 primary industry at 14.3 mn units (down 10–12%) on its own basis. [Voltas Q4 call]
- **Summer 2026 was hot**: Voltas RAC volume +45% in Q1 FY27; Orient Electric revenue +23.5% on fans; Symphony's trade stock normalised by June. [Voltas Q1 call; Orient Electric IP Q1FY27; Symphony Q1 call]
- **Housing and real estate.** Tiles, sanitaryware, paints, panels and pipes follow completions 2–3 years after launches. Anarock: Q2 2026 housing sales in the top 7 cities fell 6% y/y to ~90,715 units (–11% q/q) while launches rose 7% to ~1.06 lakh; affordable housing only 6% of launches; chairman Anuj Puri blamed the "Middle East war's disruptions" and IT-sector uncertainty. [Anarock Q2-26] ICRA (tiles) estimated top-7-city launches grew 4–7% in FY26 after a 14% CAGR FY18–FY25 — the pipeline supports finishing-material demand into FY27–FY28. [ICRA Tiles Sep-25]
- **Premiumisation beats volume.** New launches skew to ₹80 lakh+ homes (74% of Q2 2026 launches were above ₹80 lakh) — this helps premium tiles, faucets, modular kitchens and large appliances, and hurts mass brands. [Anarock Q2-26; Hindware Q4 call]
- **Rural vs urban.** Urban demand improved after income-tax cuts and GST rationalisation (RBI); rural was mixed — TTK's MFI-led rural sales dragged, while Relaxo/Bata's sub-₹1,000 footwear stabilised after the GST cut. [RBI Apr-26 context; TTK AR FY26; Bata Q4 call]
- **Macro.** RBI (8 Apr 2026) held repo at 5.25%, projected FY27 GDP 6.9% and CPI 4.6%, and warned the West Asia conflict "will adversely impact growth" through energy prices. [RBI Apr-26]
- **War-specific demand spikes.** LPG shortages in March–June 2026 drove induction-cooktop demand: TTK appliances +20% in Q4 FY26 and group growth +34% in Q1; Stove Kraft doubled induction output. [TTK IP; Stove Kraft Q1 call]

## 3. Policy: GST, PLI, BIS/QCO and energy labels
- **GST 2.0 (effective 22 Sep 2025):** ACs, TVs >32", monitors, projectors, dishwashers 28% → 18%; footwear and apparel up to ₹2,500 now 5% (the ₹1,000–2,500 band was 12%); hair oil, shampoo, soap, shaving cream, toothpaste, toothbrush 18% → 5%; cement 28% → 18%. [ICICI Sec GST Sep-25] ICRA estimated the AC cut lowers prices 6–8% (₹2,000–3,000 a unit), more than offsetting the new star-label cost. [ICRA RAC Sep-25] Relaxo blames the 2022 footwear GST rise for "90%" of its slump; the cut reversed it. [Relaxo Q2 call]
- **PLI for white goods (ACs and LED components):** outlay ₹6,238 cr; 85 companies selected with committed investment ₹11,198 cr and lifetime production ₹1.90 lakh cr; 4th round (Jan 2026) added 5 AC-component makers (₹863 cr); aim to raise domestic value addition from 20–25% to 75–80%. [IBEF/PLI Jan-26] ICRA expects RAC indigenisation to reach 70–75% by FY2028 from 50–60%. [ICRA RAC Sep-25] PLI accruals can be clawed back: EPACK reversed ₹32 cr of FY26 PLI in Q4 and has ~₹56 cr (FY26) and ~₹60 cr (FY27) unbooked. [EPACK Q4 call]
- **BIS / QCOs:** RAC and component QCOs phased in over 12 months from 2025 [ICRA RAC Sep-25]; compressor imports capped at 30% of FY25 volumes — Voltas formed a compressor JV with Atomberg for supply security [Voltas Q1 call]; MDF/plywood BIS from Feb 2025 cut cheap imports (only 8–10 foreign MDF plants certified) [CARE Greenpanel Aug-26; Rushil Q1 call]; footwear BIS licence renewals for ASEAN factories are "erratic", slowing Metro's Foot Locker/Fila roll-out [Metro Q1 call]; robotic vacuums QCO due Sep 2026 [Eureka Q4 call]; hardware BIS enforcement weak [Greenply Q1 call].
- **Energy labels (BEE):** new star tables for ACs, refrigerators and ceiling fans from 1 Jan 2026 — Voltas says the new table added 7–8% to AC cost this season; ICRA estimated ₹500–2,500 a unit; the next reset is due about January 2029. [Voltas Q1 call; ICRA RAC Sep-25; Whirlpool Q1 call; Orient Electric AR FY26]
- **Tax enforcement is rising:** income-tax searches at Greenlam, Greenply and Greenpanel (26 Feb–2 Mar 2026; outcomes pending); GST intelligence search at Greenpanel (₹4.8 cr paid); large new GST/income-tax demands at Voltas, Cera, Cello, EPACK, Bajaj Electricals. [company ARs FY26; Voltas BSE filing Sep-26]

## 4. Input costs
- **Copper** ≈ US$6.70/lb (~US$14,800/t) on 25 Sep 2026, up 42% y/y and near its all-time high — the main cost in ACs, fans, motors, wires and cookware bases. [Copper Sep-26]
- **Gas for kilns (tiles, sanitaryware):** gas is ~40% of Morbi tile cost; prices roughly doubled from ₹48/SCM to ₹79–90 [Morbi exports Aug-26]; propane ₹55/kg → ₹100–120 [BT Morbi Mar-26]; organised players pay ₹60–70/SCM (Orient Bell ₹44–45 → ₹60–62; Somany blended ~₹68). ICRA says fuel is 22–25% of tile production cost and was the key margin lever before the shock. [ICRA Tiles Sep-25; Orient Bell Q1 call; Somany Q1 call]
- **Crude derivatives:** MDF/plywood resins (chemicals ~40% of MDF raw material) rose 35–45% [Greenpanel Q4 call; Rushil Q1 call]; paints' raw materials (TiO2, monomers, solvents) are 55–60% of sales for Kansai [Kansai deep file, CRISIL Aug-26]; polymers for houseware/plastic furniture spiked 40–50% [All Time Plastics Q1 call]; PVC resin for pipes ₹68 → ₹114/kg then ~₹80–85 [Hindware Q4 call].
- **Brass** up ~40% y/y — faucet prices rose ~20% in 12 months. [Hindware Q1 update]
- **Timber** stable; MDF makers cut cost 6–7% by switching species. [Greenpanel Q1 call]
- **Freight:** Gulf container rates rose 5–10x (MDF to the Middle East $400–500 → $5,500–6,000 per container; UAE tiles $300–400 → $2,500). [Greenpanel Q1 call; Morbi exports Aug-26]
- **Pass-through record, Q1 FY27:** tiles 16–20% held (Morbi raised 30–35%); faucets +15–20%, sanitaryware +6–13%; paints ~5% + 3% more in Q2; MDF +15% rolled back to ~+1–2% at Greenpanel but held at Rushil; laminates +10%; cookware +5–8%; houseware 12–13%; ACs — cost +10–12%, most passed on except 1–2 points at Voltas. [company calls]

## 5. Import competition and China
- **Localisation is advancing:** RAC indigenisation 50–60% → 70–75% by FY2028 (ICRA); Hindware cut Chinese sourcing to "very low single digits"; Bajaj/Stove Kraft localise PCBs and coils. [ICRA RAC Sep-25; Hindware Q3 call; Stove Kraft Q1 call]
- **But China still sets prices in houseware and glass:** Cello says no further price rises are possible "because Chinese imports have not risen"; glassware and opalware face dumping and new capacity; Stove Kraft opened a Guangzhou trading arm and a triply JV with a Chinese partner. [Cello Q1 call; Stove Kraft Q4 call]
- **China+1 export wins:** All Time Plastics (IKEA 57% of sales; US share up to 19%) and Carysil (quartz sinks) sell into global retail; US tariffs are a swing factor. [All Time Plastics AR FY26; Q1 call]
- **Import barriers help panels:** BIS removed cheap MDF imports; Vietnam/Thailand supply the Gulf instead. [Rushil Q1 call; CARE Greenpanel Aug-26]

## 6. Channel shifts
- **E-commerce and quick commerce** are now 10–35% of sales for small-ticket brands: Stove Kraft e-com 31.8%; Cello online (incl. QC) 16.3% (10.4% a year ago); Jyothy MT/e-com/QC +25–30%; Bajaj e-com 15–18% with QC 8–10% of that; Orient Electric QC ~10% of digital; Relaxo "new channels" ~12%; Metro e-com ~13%. [company IP/calls]
- **D2C brands** (Honasa, Wakefit, Campus's own stores) compete on digital acquisition; incumbents answer with own-store roll-outs (Bata KRO outlets 3,472 → 4,000+; Campus 90–100 EBOs). [company calls]
- **Sell-out support replaces channel stuffing** in building materials: Orient Bell's company-generated orders were ~40% of dealer purchases; dealers hold hand-to-mouth stock when prices swing. [Orient Bell Q1 call; Greenpanel Q1 call]

## 7. Competition and structure
- **Room ACs:** ~60 brands; top-7 OEMs hold ~80% of capacity; contract makers (EPACK, Dixon, Amber) 30–35% of capacity; capacity 24–26 mn units rising 40–50% in two years with ₹4,500–5,000 cr capex [ICRA RAC Sep-25]; Blue Star says capacity is "almost double the market size" and industry margin may settle at 7–7.5% [Blue Star Q1 call].
- **Paints:** Asian Paints 55–60% of decorative, Berger ~17–18%; Birla Opus, JSW (now owner of JSW Dulux) and Pidilite's Haisha keep "intense" competition; Q1 FY27 volume growth Asian 9%, Berger 8.5%, JSW Dulux 25% (after price cuts). Asian Paints guides 8–10% FY27 volume. [Paints Sep-26; JSW Dulux Q1 call] CRISIL expects Kansai's revenue to grow 6–8% a year medium-term. [Kansai deep file, CRISIL Aug-26]
- **Tiles:** Morbi ≈80–90% of output; organised players (Kajaria, Somany, Orient Bell, Asian Granito) gained in Q1 FY27; 10–17% of Morbi capacity may not restart. ICRA's 8-company sample: revenue +4–6% and margin 11–11.5% in FY26, limited new capacity (20–25 msm). [ICRA Tiles Sep-25; Somany Q4 call]
- **MDF:** capacity 4.0–4.5 mn CBM (Greenpanel) or 5.1 mn CBM vs demand 3.7 mn CBM in FY26 (Greenply AR); demand grows 10–20% a year; new lines (Action Tesa south, Greenply MP) arrive in FY27; Century overtook Greenpanel in domestic volume. CARE: "capacity surpassing demand", sub-70% utilisation. [Greenpanel Q4/Q1 calls; Greenply AR FY26; CARE Greenpanel Aug-26]
- **Footwear:** unorganised ~65% of the market (ICRA via Relaxo); Campus ~17% of scaled sports-and-athleisure; industry grew 7–8% in FY26 (Campus estimate). [Relaxo deep file; Campus Q4 call]
- **Sanitaryware/faucets:** organised ~60%; five or six national players; market ~₹7,000–8,000 cr sanitaryware and ~₹13,000–14,000 cr faucets (Hindware estimate). [Cera AR FY26; Hindware Q4 call]

## 8. Capex cycle
- **Cooling:** ₹4,500–5,000 cr RAC capex over 2–3 years (ICRA); Voltas-Atomberg compressor JV (spend FY28–FY29); Blue Star Sri City modules; EPACK's ₹470 cr programme (now slowed). [ICRA RAC Sep-25; Voltas Q1 call; EPACK IP Q1FY27]
- **Panels:** Greenpanel (₹613 cr AP line), Rushil (₹90 cr Jumbo laminates), Greenply MDF — all now in "sweat the assets" mode; next 18–24 months maintenance only. [company calls]
- **Tiles:** debottlenecking only (Somany 3–5 msm); Orient Bell weighing an expansion. [Somany Q1 call; Orient Bell Q1 call]
- **Paints:** Kansai ~₹600 cr industrial capacity; JSW Dulux promises "serious" capex, unsized; new entrants still commissioning. [Kansai IP; JSW Dulux Q4 call]
- **Houseware/contract:** All Time Plastics 41,000 t + 1,500 t ordered; ₹90 cr of IPO money unspent. [All Time Plastics AR FY26]
- Read-across: FY24–FY26 was the build-out; FY27–FY28 is the utilisation phase, so operating leverage (or price wars) will drive earnings more than new capacity.

## 9. Where profit sits
- Highest and most durable: branded consumer franchises with low capital needs — Metro (EBITDA ~30%), Campus (17–19% band), Symphony (gross margin ~46–50%), Kansai ~14% and JSW Dulux ~12% in Q1 FY27, Jyothy/Honasa. [company calls]
- Middle: tiles (Somany ~11–12%, Kajaria ~18–19% per Somany call), sanitaryware/bath (Hindware bathware ~12% in Q1), kitchen brands.
- Thin: room-AC makers (Voltas UCP 5.3% in its best quarter; Blue Star product margin 2.9% in Q1), contract makers (EPACK ~6%), MDF (8–10%), pipes (3%). [company IP/calls]

- My synthesis: FY27 revenue growth for the listed set should be strong in nominal terms (price-led, 12–18%) but margins will be tested in H2 FY27 as (a) gas and resin ease and price rises unwind (tiles, MDF), (b) new AC/paint/MDF capacity chases volume, and (c) copper stays near records. FY28–FY29 depend on the housing pipeline (launches still rising) and on whether West Asia freight normalises (exports of tiles, MDF, laminates, houseware).

## 12. Suggested buckets (42 companies, 5 tabs)
1. **Cooling & electrical appliances (12)** — Voltas, Blue Star, Whirlpool, Symphony, IFB Industries, EPACK Durable, Crompton Greaves Consumer, V-Guard, Bajaj Electricals, Orient Electric, Eureka Forbes, Eveready. *Rationale: summer-driven, copper/aluminium-heavy, BEE/PLI/QCO-governed products sold through dealers and e-com; profits swing with weather and capacity (EPACK is the contract maker behind several brands).*
2. **Kitchen & houseware (6)** — TTK Prestige, Stove Kraft, Cello World, Borosil, All Time Plastics, Carysil. *Rationale: small-ticket, non-seasonal home products where China sets the price, e-commerce/quick commerce is the fastest channel, and two are China+1 exporters to global retailers.*
3. **Paints & coatings (4)** — Kansai Nerolac, JSW Dulux (ex-Akzo Nobel), Indigo Paints, Sirca Paints. *Rationale: distribution- and brand-led, crude-linked raw materials, and all face the same new-entrant price war from Birla Opus/JSW/Haisha.*
4. **Tiles, bath & wood panels — housing surfaces (11)** — Kajaria, Somany, Orient Bell, Cera Sanitaryware, Hindware Home Innovation, Century Plyboards, Greenply, Greenpanel, Rushil Decor, Greenlam, Stylam. *Rationale: finishing materials bought 2–3 years after housing launches; energy (gas) or chemical (resin) costs dominate; Morbi and MDF oversupply decide pricing.*
5. **Lifestyle consumer brands — footwear, personal care, sleep (9)** — Metro Brands, Bata, Relaxo, Campus Activewear, Mayur Uniquoters, Honasa Consumer, Jyothy Labs, Sheela Foam, Wakefit. *Rationale: brand-and-retail businesses (own stores, D2C, FMCG distribution) that benefited from GST cuts, with low capital needs and returns driven by brand strength rather than capacity (Mayur supplies synthetic leather to footwear/auto makers).*
