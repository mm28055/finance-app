# Borosil Ltd (BOROLTD) — deep research, Phase A1

**Identity warning:** this note is about **Borosil Ltd** (consumer glassware, Larah opalware, Hydra bottles, appliances — BSE 543212). The dashboard's headline metrics for "Borosil" (mcap ₹6,698 cr, price ₹455, 52-week ₹374–721, P/E 16.7, ROCE 26.6%, TTM sales ₹1,608 cr) belong to a different listed company, **Borosil Renewables** (solar glass, BORORENEW: Screener mcap ₹6,699 cr, price ₹455, 52-week ₹374–721, ROCE 25.3%). Screener for Borosil Ltd today: **mcap ₹3,167 cr, price ₹265, 52-week ₹214–364, P/E 43.4, ROCE 11.1%, ROE 8.8%**. Any valuation built on the DB figures is wrong.

## Business and segments
- Kitchen and dining brand: glassware (borosilicate microwavable ware, tumblers, lunch boxes, storage), opalware (Larah), and non-glassware (Hydra vacuum-insulated steel bottles/flasks, small appliances, cookware). ~250 distributors, 24,000+ retailers, e-commerce/quick commerce, B2B/exports; first two exclusive brand stores opened (Pune, Gurugram; ₹40–50 lakh each). [Q1 call; ICRA Mar-26]
- FY26 revenue ₹1,195.9 cr (+8%): Larah opalware ₹411.9 cr (+7.3%), glassware ₹295.5 cr (+17.3%), non-glassware ₹463.7 cr (+2.4%). Q1 FY27: opalware ₹83.6 cr (+9.8%), glassware ₹65.6 cr (+16.8%), non-glassware ₹98.1 cr (+4.2%); all volume-led. [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Capacity: opalware furnaces 84 TPD (tonnes/day) at ~90–95% utilisation; borosilicate pressware furnace 25 TPD (lit Jan/Mar 2024) at ~90%; ~two-thirds of glassware sales self-made, rest outsourced (partly from Borosil Scientific's Bharuch plant). New Hydra plant (subsidiary Stylenest India, Rajasthan): 2 double-wall lines producing from 30 Jun 2026, 3rd line in Q2 FY27; ~3.6 mn units/year; ₹65 cr capex. [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Market (company estimates): opalware ~₹1,350 cr with Borosil ~one-third share; borosilicate glassware ~₹415 cr; insulated steel bottles ₹2,000+ cr. Borosil is the **only Indian producer** of borosilicate table/kitchen glass (confirmed by DGTR). [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27; DGTR disclosure Sep-26]

- Q1 FY27: revenue +9%; operating EBITDA ₹35.9 cr (14.2%) vs ₹40.2 cr (17.8%); West Asia conflict net cost ~₹10 cr (fuel 5.8% of sales vs 2.9%); PBT ₹17.4 cr vs ₹23.5 cr (last year had a ₹7.2 cr one-off reversal); PAT ₹12.8 cr vs ₹17.4 cr. [Q1 call]
- Annual (Screener): sales FY24 ₹934 cr → FY25 ₹1,090 cr → FY26 ₹1,175 cr; OPM 15% → 16% → 14%; PAT ₹66 cr → ₹74 cr → ₹75 cr. TTM PAT ₹70 cr (–11%). ROCE 14% → 12% → 11%; ROE ~9%. Company "operational ROCE" 11.5% (FY25), 10.7% (FY26). [Screener; Q1 call]
- Company operating EBITDA FY26 ₹176.7 cr (15.1%) vs ₹177.7 cr (16.3%) — flat, mainly because ~₹100 cr of Hydra sales were lost to the BIS quality-control order on imported steel bottles. [Q4 call]
- Unit economics: fuel ~4% of sales in opalware and ~5.5% in glassware at normal gas prices; gas bill rose from ~₹20 cr to ~₹50 cr a year at the peak. Price increases of 5–7% (8–10% in some categories) announced in April, effective from mid-May; only ~₹2 cr realised in Q1 against ~₹12 cr of cost impact. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (May 2026): Hydra lines to start before end-Q1 (third by end-Q2); 20 MWp solar in Q1, saving ~₹28 cr EBITDA in FY27; FY27 capex ~₹110 cr (₹90 cr+ for two glassware projects plus ₹20–25 cr maintenance); opalware growth "muted" for about a year; medium-term revenue growth 15–20% a year and EBITDA "closer to 20-odd percent". [Q4 call]
- Q1 delivery: Hydra lines started 30 Jun (**delivered**); solar commissioned, 61% of power (**delivered**); opalware +9.8% (**better** than "muted"); margin 14.2% (**missed** the 18–20% path; West Asia costs). [Q1 call]
- Q1 call (Aug 2026): FY27 EBITDA "about 18%" — then clarified as 18% "in a steady-state business without the West Asia impact", then again "for this year, we wish to achieve". Reaching 18% for the year needs ~19–20% for Q2–Q4 against a 14–15% history — management itself conceded the history. Capex **raised** to ₹125–150 cr (adds opal furnace rebuild and more solar); depreciation ~₹92 cr; ROCE aspiration 20–24% "medium term". [Q1 call]
- Outside view: ICRA (Mar 2026) expected mid-to-high single-digit growth and noted margins of 14–15%; "commensurate returns from capex" is its key monitorable. [ICRA Mar-26]

## Capex and capacity
- Capex history: ₹197 cr (FY24), ₹97 cr (FY25), ₹136 cr (FY26); FY27 ₹125–150 cr. CWIP ₹105 cr at Mar-26 (₹18 cr a year earlier). [Q1 call; Screener]
- Projects: (1) Hydra plant, ₹65 cr, lines 1–2 live, line 3 in Q2 FY27; management expects gross margin at least 10 points higher than trading, but only after 6–12 months of stabilisation. (2) Bharuch glassware (jugs, jars, bottles, drinking glasses), ₹42 cr, by end-Q3 FY27 — replaces ~₹100 cr of sales now sourced from Borosil Scientific. (3) Borosilicate furnace 25 → 32 TPD with a third forming line, ₹50 cr, by end-Q4 FY28 at the scheduled rebuild. (4) Solar: ~₹130 cr invested in three plants (8.6 + 7.2 + 20 MWp with battery storage), ~₹30 cr a year EBITDA saving; 6.5 MWp + 3–4 MWp more under evaluation. [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Funding: mix of internal cash and debt; Stylenest has ICRA AA- rated term loans (₹49 cr) and cash credit. [ICRA Mar-26; Q4 call]
- Payback: management gives no project-level returns; ICRA flags returns on past capex as the main risk. Asset turn is low (FY26 sales ₹1,175 cr on ₹531 cr net block + ₹105 cr CWIP ≈ 1.8x) and inventory ties up more capital. [Screener; ICRA Mar-26]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net debt ₹49.7 cr (Mar-26) → **₹99 cr** (Jun-26): debt ₹155.2 cr vs cash/investments ₹56.2 cr. Screener borrowings ₹148 cr (Mar-26). ICRA: gearing ~0.1x, debt/OPBDITA 0.6x, interest cover 12x (FY25); downgrade trigger debt/OPBDITA above 1.5x. [Q1 call; Q4 call; Screener; ICRA Mar-26]
- Operating cash flow ₹48 cr (FY24) → –₹19 cr (FY25) → ₹119 cr (FY26); **free cash flow negative for four straight years** (–₹77 cr, –₹126 cr, –₹103 cr, –₹17 cr). A ₹150 cr QIP in June 2024 repaid debt. [Screener; ICRA Mar-26]
- **Inventory is the weak spot**: inventory days 243 (FY24) → 299 (FY25) → 282 (FY26); cash conversion cycle ~259 days. Causes: advance imports of Hydra and appliances before BIS deadlines, and the borosilicate furnace producing more than it sells (minimum efficient size). Management expects a reversal from FY28. [Screener; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- No dividend. Promoters 64.7% (down from 70.2% in 2023 after the QIP). [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Brand: "Borosil" is the generic name for heat-resistant glass in India; Larah holds ~one-third of opalware. [Q4 call]
- Manufacturing position: sole Indian borosilicate glassware producer; 84 TPD opalware among India's largest; BIS-certified Hydra plant now shields it from import bans that hit rivals. [IP Q1FY27; DGTR disclosure Sep-26]
- **Trade protection coming**: on 24 Sep 2026 DGTR issued final findings recommending anti-dumping duty for 5 years on Chinese borosilicate table and kitchen glassware, finding dumping "materially retarded the establishment of the domestic industry"; the Finance Ministry notification is still pending. Management had said Chinese dumping continued despite a weaker rupee. [DGTR disclosure Sep-26; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Weak points: opalware growth limited by a lack of new products ("unless we come up with something exciting"); brands sourcing from China undercut Borosil glass; pricing lags costs by 1–2 quarters. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Valuation/data risk**: the dashboard uses Borosil Renewables' metrics for this company (see top). On Screener, Borosil Ltd trades on ~43x trailing earnings with ROCE ~11% — far more demanding than the DB's 16.7x.
- **Capital efficiency**: ROCE ~11% for four years despite heavy capex; free cash flow negative every year since FY23; inventory ~280 days. ICRA lists working-capital intensity and returns on capex as key weaknesses. [Screener; ICRA Mar-26]
- **Margin guidance credibility**: the 18% FY27 target was re-explained three times in one call; Q1 was 14.2%. [Q1 call]
- **Related party — Borosil Scientific (BSL)**: Borosil Ltd buys some glassware made on BSL's Bharuch assets at "cost plus 5%" (roughly), approved by shareholders of both companies; under 10% of Borosil Ltd sales. The new ₹42 cr Bharuch plant will move ~₹100 cr of that sourcing in-house. Other related parties include Borosil Renewables and Kheruka family entities (shared services, reimbursements). [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26 related-party note]
- **Management change**: Rituraj Sharma (President) appointed CEO from May 2026; promoter Shreevar Kheruka re-designated from MD & CEO (remuneration cap up to ₹10 cr a year approved). [AR FY26 Directors' Report; Q1 call]
- Regulatory: appliance QCO (BIS) deadline 1 Oct 2026 — company built advance inventory; says it can switch to local sourcing. [Q4 call]
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; key audit matter is revenue recognition (discounts/rebates and cut-off). Contingent liabilities negligible (entry tax ₹0.18 cr, income tax ₹0.48 cr, workers' welfare cess ₹0.15 cr; bank guarantees ₹1.1 cr). Inventory write-down ₹1.45 cr in FY26. [AR FY26 auditor's report; AR FY26 notes 12.1, 38]

## My read
Borosil Ltd is a strong brand with a poor capital record: sales have compounded ~17% over three years, but profit has barely moved (₹66 cr → ₹75 cr), ROCE sits near 11% and free cash flow has been negative for four years because of capex and bloated inventory. FY27 has three real positives — in-house Hydra supply, ~₹28 cr of solar savings and a likely anti-dumping duty on Chinese borosilicate glass — so margins should recover from Q1's 14%. At ~43x trailing earnings on Screener, the price needs earnings to roughly double over 3–4 years (18%+ margins plus inventory release). The view improves if the duty is notified, Q2–Q3 margins reach 17–18% and inventory days fall; it worsens if margins stay near 14–15% while capex keeps rising.
