# All Time Plastics (ALLTIME) — deep research, 27 Sep 2026

Figures from Screener consolidated page (only FY25–FY26 annuals; listed August 2025) unless tagged. Share price ₹201, market cap ₹1,317 cr, 52-week range ₹185–315 [Screener].

## Business and segments
- India's largest exporter of plastic houseware and plastic furniture: injection-moulded kitchen, storage, bath, cleaning and kids' products, designed in-house; exports to 29 countries [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Customers are big-box retailers: IKEA (27-year relationship), Tesco, Asda, Michaels; domestic modern trade (Spencer's) and its own "All Time" brand [Crisil Mar-26].
- **Customer concentration is extreme**: the largest customer (IKEA) bought ₹346.6 cr in FY26 = ~57% of revenue (₹314.8 cr, ~56%, in FY25); IKEA was 55% of Q4 FY26 sales; the second customer ₹47.7 cr [AR FY26 segment note; Q4 call]. Crisil puts IKEA at ~60% and notes no single product is over 15% of IKEA revenue [Crisil Mar-26].
- Geography Q1 FY27: Europe ~52%, US ~19% (12% in FY26), UK ~11%, India ~16% [Q1 call]. FY26: Europe 58%, UK 12%, US 12%, India 17% [Q4 call]. Exports 84% of Q1 revenue [IP Q1FY27].
- Plants: Daman (domestic + export), Silvassa and Khatalwada/Manekpur (100% export-oriented units) in Gujarat; 170+ all-electric moulding machines; pilot bamboo unit at Guwahati [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call].
- Capacity 39,000 t at March 2026 → ~41,000 t after 2,000 t installed in Q1 FY27; utilisation 89.7% (Q1 FY26, smaller base) → 51.9% (Q4 FY26) → 64.9% (Q1 FY27) [Q1 call].
- Domestic brand business is ~12–13% of revenue; management wants B2C to reach 22–25% in 1–1.5 years [Q1 call; Q4 call].

## Key numbers
- Quarterly revenue (₹ cr): Jun-25 158, Sep-25 147, Dec-25 159, Mar-26 146, Jun-26 162; operating margin 18%, 11%, 15%, 15%, 14%; net profit 13, 4, 9, 9, 12 [Screener].
- Annual: FY25 revenue ₹558 cr, margin 18%, profit ₹47 cr; FY26 ₹611 cr (+9.4%), margin 15%, profit ₹35 cr [Screener]. Company: FY26 EBITDA ₹90.1 cr (14.8%), PAT ₹35.6 cr [Q4 call].
- Volume flat: polymer processed 26,300 t in FY26 vs 26,230 t in FY25 — FY26 growth was price/mix, not volume. Recycled polymer 8,022 t (7,136 t) [Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27: revenue ₹161 cr (+2% y/y, +10.5% q/q); gross margin 39.5% (41.9% Q4, 39.3% a year ago); EBITDA ₹23 cr (14.3% vs 18.2%); PAT ₹12 cr (-5.5%) [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]. Sales volume 6,090 t vs 5,831 t in Q4 [Q1 call].
- About ₹5.5 cr of shipments slipped from June into July (containers, port congestion) [Q1 call].
- Unit economics: revenue ~₹2.6 lakh per tonne sold (₹161 cr / 6,090 t); EBITDA ~₹38,000/t at 14.3% (my calculation). Raw material is ~52–53% of product cost, so a 40% polymer rise needs ~20% price rise [Q1 call].
- Costs stepped up with the new plant: employee cost ₹17.1 cr in Q1 vs ₹13.8 cr a year ago (+24%); management says Q1 is the run-rate [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (25 May 2026): FY27 utilisation 70–75%; margins "better than FY26", back to 18–19% "in the second half if things remain settled"; 6,000 t more capacity "on track" but on hold pending clarity; bamboo "good volumes" by H2 FY27; B2C to 22–25% of sales [Q4 call].
- Q1 call (6 Aug 2026): revenue growth 15–20% for FY27 ("at least"); utilisation ~75% on 41,000 t; domestic growth 30–35%; 18–19% margin only "once we achieve 80% utilisation"; FY27 margin "better than last year" but would not confirm 16–17% [Q1 call].
- Delivery vs Q4 promises: Q1 utilisation 65% (on track for 70–75% only if H2 is strong); margin fell to 14.3% (not better yet); domestic revenue flat at ~₹26 cr (vs 30–35% target) because the company halted domestic supply ~8 weeks to force a full price increase [Q1 call]. Bamboo slipped from "H2" to "Q4 FY27". Verdict: revenue guidance firmed; margin timeline pushed out; capacity guidance kept.
- Earlier miss: Crisil in March 2026 expected FY26 revenue to grow over 20%; actual growth was 9.4% [Crisil Mar-26; Screener].

## Capex and capacity
- IPO (August 2025) raised ₹280 cr for the company: ₹143 cr repaid debt, ₹113.7 cr earmarked for machinery and automated warehouse at the Manekpur (Khatalwada) plant — only ₹23.3 cr spent by March 2026, ₹90.4 cr unspent in fixed deposits [AR FY26 note 19].
- Plan in the prospectus: capacity to 52,500 t. Status: 41,000 t now; 14 machines (+1,500 t) ordered for Q4 FY27; a further ~4,000 t to be ordered in Q3 once customer programmes are fixed [Q1 call]. The slow spend means the ₹90 cr is a timing risk, not a cash problem.
- Bamboo: subsidiary All Time Bamboo; 75,000 sq ft leased plant at Madanpur, Guwahati; 3,000 CBM a year phase 1; capex ~₹15 cr; revenue potential ~₹60 cr at full use; margin "slightly higher" than plastics; machines shipped from China, installation by end-September 2026, first revenue Q4 FY27 [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Payback: at 75% use of 41,000 t and current ₹2.6 lakh/t, revenue capacity is ~₹800 cr — about 30% above FY26 — without new capex (my estimate). ROCE fell from 20% (Q1 FY26 annualised) to ~9% on IPO cash and new capacity [IP Q1FY27].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹86 cr at March 2026 (₹223 cr a year earlier); debt/equity 0.14x; IPO cash in deposits [Screener; Q1 call; AR FY26].
- Operating cash flow ₹86 cr in FY26 (₹52 cr FY25 per Screener; company says ₹39.4 cr); free cash flow -₹46 cr after capex [Screener; Q4 call].
- Net working capital 74 days (FY25) → 57 days (FY26) → 60 days (Q1 FY27): inventory 44, receivables 50, payables 34 [Q1 call]. Screener's cash cycle is 82 days on its own method.
- ~90% of exports are FOB/FCA, so buyers bear freight; IKEA and domestic billing are in rupees; only ~18–20% of revenue earns dollars [Q4 call].
- No dividend [Screener].

## Moat evidence
- Sticky programmes: 27 years with IKEA, 17 with Tesco, 14 with Asda; long product cycles and SKU refreshes; in-house mould design [Crisil Mar-26; IP Q1FY27].
- Pricing: domestic price rise passed 100%; IKEA has a formula pass-through with an ~8-week lag; 15–20% of revenue had only half the rise passed [Q1 call]. Gross margin held at 39–42% through a 40–50% polymer spike.
- Cost/compliance: all-electric machines, >25% recycled input, Higg/EcoVadis/amfori/ISCC certification — needed by European retailers [IP Q1FY27].
- The weak spot: switching costs protect it with IKEA, but IKEA's scale also gives it the bargaining power; the "third customer grew 225%" in FY26 shows diversification is starting [Q4 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Concentration**: ~57% of revenue from one customer (IKEA) [AR FY26].
- **Rating trigger close**: Crisil's downgrade trigger is operating margin sustained below 14%; Q1 FY27 was 14.3% [Crisil Mar-26; Q1 call].
- Promoter-family pay: three promoter-directors drew ₹5.9 cr and relatives on payroll ₹1.4 cr in FY26 — ~₹7.3 cr against PAT of ₹35.6 cr; rent ₹0.5 cr to a family firm [AR FY26 note 39].
- Auditor: clean opinion; key audit matter is revenue timing on export shipping terms [AR FY26 auditor's report]. Contingent liabilities nil (₹0.6 cr customs claim closed) [AR FY26 note 41].
- Logistics and polymer prices tied to the West Asia conflict; container shortages delay shipments [Q1 call].
- US tariff exposure as the US share rises to ~19% [Q1 call].
- IPO money partly idle (₹90 cr) — watch for delays or change of use [AR FY26].

## What changed between the two calls
- May: defensive — Q4 revenue fell 1.6% y/y, capacity addition paused, customers absorbing only 10–12% of a 22–25% cost rise.
- August: more confident — price pass-through largely agreed, utilisation up to 65%, new machines ordered, US share up to 19%, hint of a large new US programme, explicit 15–20% FY27 growth.
- But the margin target moved from "H2 FY27" to "at 80% utilisation", and bamboo slipped one quarter.

## My read
A good, clean, cash-rich manufacturer whose recent numbers look worse than the business because a big new plant arrived just as polymer prices and shipping blew up. Volumes were flat in FY26, margins have fallen from 18% to 14%, and the story now depends on filling 41,000 t of capacity — every 10 points of utilisation is roughly ₹100 cr of revenue at high incremental margin. At ₹201 the price needs FY27–28 growth near the 15–20% guided plus a margin recovery toward 17–18%; that is achievable but unproven, and IKEA at ~57% of sales caps how much the market will pay. The view improves with utilisation above 75% and margin above 16% in H2 FY27, or a signed large US programme; it worsens if margin stays under 14% (Crisil trigger) or IKEA volumes slip.
