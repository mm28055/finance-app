## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Unimech Aerospace

## Executive Summary
Unimech Aerospace is rapidly evolving from a niche Indian precision-machining vendor into a highly integrated, global Tier-1 partner for complex electromechanical assemblies. The company solves a critical supply chain bottleneck for Western OEMs in aerospace, power generation, and nuclear sectors: the need for heavily qualified, high-tolerance engineering at a fraction of Western manufacturing costs. 

Historically a Challenger in the Aerotooling space, Unimech is aggressively pivoting toward Category Leadership in specialized sub-assemblies. This transition is punctuated by the recent acquisition of Hobel Bellows, which instantly vaults them up the value chain into proprietary metallic bellows and cooling components feeding the booming hyperscale data center and nuclear markets. Armed with a newly fully operational Free Trade Warehousing Zone (FTWZ) to neutralize geopolitical tariff friction, an expanding ₹280 Cr order book, and a landmark 5-year commercial agreement with FACC Austria, Unimech is structurally repositioning itself from a vulnerable, cyclical components supplier to an embedded, high-margin subsystems partner with durable switching costs.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** Historically, the company sold Aerotooling equipment and precision-machined friction components. The portfolio now aggressively includes complex sub-assemblies: metallic bellows, flexible tubing, exhaust manifolds, and drone support equipment.
- **Who pays them?** Global OEMs in aerospace, US/EU aviation players, nuclear authorities (NPCIL pipeline), and industrial power generation giants. 
- **How do they make money?** The model relies on long-cycle qualification frameworks (often taking 18-24 months) culminating in high-visibility Long-Term Agreements (LTAs) and drop-shipment orders. This model's success was validated in Q1 FY27 by a 5-year, $7.5M recurring supply contract with Tier-1 supplier FACC Austria.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
- **Is the moat Durable or Fleeting?** The moat is **Durable and Deepening Rapidly**. 
- **Evidence:** Unimech benefits from extreme switching costs characterized by grueling First Article Inspection (FAI) and Source Appointment Inspection (SAI) processes. Management completed a staggering 165 FAIs in Q1 FY27 alone, creating a massive barrier to entry. The Hobel Bellows acquisition acts as a major moat-widener: 70% of Hobel’s components are single-sourced with proprietary drawings. 
- **The Bear Case:** The moat is geographically exposed. As evidenced in Q3 FY26, the business is highly sensitive to US trade policy. However, this risk is now structurally mitigated by the fully operational FTWZ, transitioning clients to a duty-free inventory-building model. 

## Growth Vectors
- **The Hobel Bellows Integration:** This ₹450 Cr all-cash acquisition is successfully contributing to the P&L (₹22 Cr in just two months of Q1 FY27). It provides backdoor access to hyperscale AI infrastructure and nuclear markets. Management is now targeting AS9100 aerospace certification for the Vizag facility by Q4 FY27 to cross-sell Hobel's components to aviation OEMs.
- **FTWZ Operationalization:** Fully live as of Q1 FY27, this strategic facility transitions Unimech's US-bound client procurement from volatile, drop-ship ordering to duty-free inventory building. It effectively neutralizes the primary logistics and tariff friction points.
- **Middle East Localization (Saudi JV) & Deep-Tech:** A 51%-owned JV with the Kanoo Group targeting the Saudi Vision 2030 localization mandate, with a $10M deployment executing in Q1/Q2 FY27. Additionally, the company is advancing indigenous micro gas turbines via Dheya Engineering, recently showcased to the French President.
- **Capacity & Management Execution:** Management is demonstrating ruthless commercial execution, converting capabilities into multi-year recurring revenue (e.g., FACC Austria). To meet accelerating Tier-1 demand, they are advancing capex, expecting the gross block to double by the end of FY27. 

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Forensic & Dilution Red Flags:** The ₹450 Cr Hobel Bellows acquisition utilized a Compulsorily Convertible Debenture (CCD) structure. More pressingly, the Board recently approved an enabling QIP resolution for up to ₹750 Cr. While management asserts this is primarily for Minimum Public Shareholding (MPS) compliance and capacity war-chesting over the next 18 months, it introduces potential equity dilution risk.
- **Business Risks:** 
  - **Working Capital Intensity:** The strategic shift toward long-cycle precision aerospace components and nuclear programs requires heavy inventory commitments. Management has proactively flagged that receivable days will stretch from 130 to 160+ days by year-end, which could drag on free cash flow.
  - **Tariff & Trade Volatility:** Despite the operational FTWZ, a macro pivot toward extreme protectionism in the US or EU remains an existential threat to an export-heavy entity.
  - **Capacity Execution:** With fixed assets set to double and current capacity utilization at ~58% (with 10% blocked purely for FAI prove-outs), the company must smoothly convert its 165 new FAIs into serial production to justify the soaring depreciation overhead.
- **What could derail the thesis?** A permanent depression of free cash flow due to structural working capital bloat, execution failure in the ₹750 Cr QIP at poor valuations, or delays in H2 FY27 NPCIL nuclear order conversions.

## Investment Thesis
- **The Bull Case:** Unimech is firing on all cylinders and successfully making the leap from one-off tooling purchase orders to multi-year contractual revenue. Q1 FY27 proved the core business has violently rebounded (₹108 Cr revenue, +71% YoY), while maintaining exceptional pricing power (36.5% EBITDA margins) even during a heavy FAI investment phase. With upgraded margin guidance of 34%-35% for FY27, a robust ₹280 Cr order book, and embedded operating leverage as utilization scales through the live FTWZ, Unimech is cementing its status as a globally competitive precision engineering compounder.
- **The Bear Case:** The company is fighting a multi-front, capital-intensive war: integrating a major acquisition, doubling its gross block, scaling a Greenfield joint venture in Saudi Arabia, and absorbing a structurally longer working capital cycle (160+ days). If the newly passed ₹750 Cr QIP is executed poorly, it could dilute retail investors right as the company’s heavy fixed-asset investments temporarily compress return on capital. Stay away if you lack conviction in management's ability to manage severe working capital stretches.
