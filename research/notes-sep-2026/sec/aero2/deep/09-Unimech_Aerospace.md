## Business and segments
- Two offerings: **Aero-tooling** (engine and airframe assembly/MRO tools, ground support equipment, engine stands for LEAP, Rolls-Royce, Airbus/Boeing programmes; sold mainly to Tier-1 engine-tool licensees) and **Precision Components & Assemblies (PCA)** — aerospace parts, semiconductor-equipment parts, nuclear sub-systems, and now **Hobel Bellows** (metallic bellows, flexible tubing, exhaust manifolds for locomotives/gensets/power). Reported as one segment. [AR FY26 MD&A, note 49; IP Q1FY27]
- Mix: FY26 ~90% tooling / ~10% PCA; **Q1 FY27 76% tooling / 24% PCA, with Hobel alone 21% of revenue (~₹22 cr for two months)**. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q1 call]
- Geography: **~96% exports** (Q1 FY27); FY26 revenue ₹240.5 cr of which US ₹181.9 cr (**76%**), India ₹25.4 cr, rest UK/Canada/Israel/Germany. ~35 customers. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 note 24.1; MD&A]
- **Customer concentration: top customer 44.5% of FY26 revenue (66.5% in FY25); top three 72.4% (87.6% in FY25).** [AR FY26 note 49(c)]
- Capacity: three Bengaluru plants (incl. 150,000 sq ft tooling plant), 150+ CNC machines, gross block >₹212 cr at FY26; Hobel adds ~200,000 sq ft at Duvada SEZ, Visakhapatnam; Free Trade Warehouse (FTWZ) operational May 2026. **Utilisation ~58% in Q1 FY27** (plus ~10% of capacity tied up in qualification work), from ~50–55% in FY26. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q1 call]
- Qualified SKUs ~6,300; 200+ first-article inspections (FAIs) in FY26, **165 FAIs in Q1 FY27**; ~80% of PCA qualified parts move into serial production. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Associates/JVs: **Dheya Engineering** (micro gas turbines for UAVs; exclusive manufacturing rights to Unimech) — stake raised from ~16% to ~30% in FY26 and to **35.07% in Sep 2026**; **Kanoo–Unimech JV** in Saudi Arabia (51%), total planned investment $30 mn over ~3 years, break-even in ~3 years. [AR FY26 MD&A; NSE filing 7 Sep 2026; Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- **Q4 call (29 May 2026):** Q1 FY27 revenue to exceed Q4's ₹82 cr; FY27 EBITDA margin "better than FY26" (31%) but no number ("similar" profile as the Saudi JV may dilute); no significant core capex in FY27, only Saudi JV capex; working capital to drift from 120–125 days toward 150–160 days "over time"; FTWZ in final stage; AS9100 for Hobel in 6–9 months; FAIs to double from ~200. Admitted "FY26 may not have been the high growth year we initially envisaged". [Q4 call]
- **Q1 delivery:** revenue ₹108 cr — beat the Q4 promise, including organically (~₹86 cr). EBITDA margin 36.5%. FTWZ live. [Q1 call]
- **Q1 call guidance:** FY27 EBITDA margin **34–35%** (long-run 30–32%+) — **raised**; Q2 "stronger" with full Hobel quarter; gross margin ~65% sustainable; Hobel growth 15–20%; **working capital 130 days → 160+ days by FY27-end** — worse and sooner; **capex advanced** — gross block to roughly **double by FY27-end**, mostly the Saudi JV (~$10 mn infusion in Aug); ROCE ~15–16% this year, 20–21% as utilisation rises; asset turns ~2.5x medium term. No revenue number given. [Q1 call]
- Verdict: **delivering / raised on margins**, but capital intensity rising (capex pulled forward, working capital up, QIP planned). [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- IPO (Dec 2024): ₹250 cr fresh issue at ₹785; net ₹230.9 cr. **Objects changed by postal ballot (17 Dec 2025)**: ₹61.3 cr re-allocated to "Merger & Acquisition, JV and Greenfield Projects"; ₹94.5 cr unutilised at Mar-26 (incl. that ₹61.3 cr, ₹11.8 cr of machinery capex and ₹21.4 cr of working capital). [AR FY26 note 48; board's report]
- **Hobel Bellows**: up to **₹450 cr** paid through Unimech (24%) and wholly owned Innomech (76%) into Hobel Bellows Pvt Ltd, structured as equity, debt and CCDs; completed 27 Apr 2026; consolidated from 1 May. Hobel FY26: revenue ~₹129 cr, EBITDA margin >50%, pre-deal ROCE >50%, debt-free, ~90% exports; price ≈3.5x revenue / 6–7x EBITDA; guided growth 15–17% a year for 3–4 years. **Two OEM groups give ~93% of Hobel's revenue** (an analyst put Cummins at 70–80%; management did not name it). Group ROCE drops to 10–16% after the deal. [AR FY26 notes 50/51; investor-meet transcript 28 Apr 2026; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Saudi JV: $30 mn total, Unimech 51% (~$15 mn) over three years; Dheya: ₹3 cr for 5.08% in Sep 2026 (implies ~₹59 cr value for a company with ₹2.06 cr turnover). [Q4 call; NSE filing 7 Sep 2026]
- Capital commitments ₹11.5 cr (Mar-26). [AR FY26 note 46]
- **₹750 cr QIP enabling resolution** (board 3 Aug 2026, subject to AGM): for minimum public shareholding (promoters 79.82%; must reach ≤75% within ~18 months), capacity and possible M&A; "not an immediate fundraise". [NSE filing 3 Aug 2026; Q1 call]

## Moat evidence
- Qualification-led: ~6,300 qualified SKUs, 200+ FAIs a year; customers must re-qualify a new source, so switching costs are high once a part is approved. [Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Pricing: gross margins 65–73% and EBITDA margins 36–43% in normal quarters; margins held even through the tariff year (FY26 31%). [Q4 call; Q1 call; Screener]
- Wins: $4 mn (~₹35 cr) LEAP ground-support-equipment order; **FACC (Austria) 5-year LTA, initial $7.5 mn** — first recurring aerostructure supply deal; ~₹80–87 cr nuclear refurbishment orders (Tarapur/Madras EMCCR); >10 nuclear sub-assemblies qualified. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Cost position: Indian cost base serving US/EU OEMs; FTWZ lets US customers hold stock duty-deferred in India. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Concentration:** one customer 44.5% of FY26 revenue; US 76% of revenue; tariffs already cut FY26 revenue (Q3 FY26 sales ₹34 cr, margin 4.6%). [AR FY26 note 49; Screener]
- **Disclosure lag on management exits:** on 7 Sep 2026 the company disclosed that five senior managers (Head/VP Technology & IT, AGM Business Development, AVP SBU-2, a BU head, SCM head) had ceased — resignations dated **Jul 2025–Jan 2026**, i.e. 8–14 months late. [NSE filing 7 Sep 2026]
- **Related party:** Dheya is a related party (CMD Anil Kumar Puttan and WTD Rajanikanth Balaraman are on its board); Unimech keeps buying stakes (latest secondary purchase at a rich implied value). [NSE filing 7 Sep 2026]
- **Change in IPO objects** within a year of listing; ₹61 cr moved to M&A/JV/greenfield. [AR FY26 note 48]
- Earnings quality in FY26 leaned on treasury income (57% of PBT); that support is now gone, so FY27 profit must come from operations. [Screener; Q1 call]
- **Dilution:** ₹750 cr QIP plus MPS requirement (promoter 79.82%). [NSE filing; Screener]
- Hobel's own concentration: two OEM groups ≈93% of its revenue. [Investor-meet transcript 28 Apr 2026]
- Forex: foreign-currency borrowings produced a ₹9.6 cr loss in Q4 FY26. [Q4 call]
- Execution load: integrating Hobel, doubling gross block, Saudi greenfield, Dheya deep-tech bets — while working capital rises to 160+ days. [Q1 call]
- Auditor: unmodified; key audit matter = revenue recognition timing; CARO clean on loans (regular repayment). No contingent liabilities at Mar-26 (a ₹3.8 cr GST demand was closed). [AR FY26 auditor's report; note 46]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone shifted from defending FY26 (tariffs, Hobel price) to growth: Q1 record revenue, FACC LTA, margin guide raised to 34–35%. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Capex stance reversed: "no significant core capex in FY27" (Q4) → capacity investment "advanced earlier than originally planned", gross block to double (Q1). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- New: ₹750 cr QIP, working capital 160+ days by year-end, Hobel AS9100 by Q4 FY27, engine Tier-1 LTA "at an advanced stage". [Q1 call]
- Order book slipped from ₹314 cr (26 May) to ₹280 cr (30 Jun) on fast execution and customer pull-ins. [Q1 call]

## My read
The core tooling franchise recovered fast once tariffs eased: Q1 organic growth ~37% and 36.5% EBITDA margins show real pricing power and operating leverage from a half-used plant. Hobel adds a steady, high-return (pre-deal) business but at a full price, and FY26 profit was flattered by treasury income that has now gone. The price needs ~35%+ growth for several years plus the PCA/nuclear/aerostructure pipeline to scale while working capital and capex rise. The view improves if the engine Tier-1 LTA closes and utilisation passes ~70% with ROCE heading to 20%; it worsens if US tariffs return, the top customer cuts orders, or the QIP is used for more expensive M&A.
