## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Aequs

## Executive Summary
Aequs exists to solve one of the most complex bottlenecks in global hardware: the geographic concentration and fragmentation of precision manufacturing. By creating a fully vertically integrated manufacturing ecosystem—from forging and machining to surface treatment and assembly—within a single Special Economic Zone in India, Aequs allows global aerospace and consumer OEMs to consolidate their supply chains and derisk from China and Europe. 

The company operates as a definitive **Category Challenger** transitioning into a domestic Category Leader. In aerospace, they have successfully transcended commodity component machining to become a strategic, Tier-1 aligned partner for the likes of Airbus, Boeing, and Safran. However, their aggressive foray into consumer electronics and toys is currently testing the limits of their capital allocation, masking the core aerospace profitability under a heavy burden of growth capex and depreciation.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** Highly engineered precision components and sub-assemblies. In aerospace, this includes critical aerostructures, actuation systems, engine components, landing gear, and fully assembled wheels. In the consumer vertical, they manufacture mechanical enclosures for consumer electronics, toys, and premium cookware. 
- **Who pays them?** Global commercial aerospace OEMs (Airbus, Boeing), Tier-1 aerospace suppliers, and multinational consumer brands (such as Mattel). 
- **How do they make money?** Aerospace revenue is secured through long-term, multi-year contracts rooted in rigorous 15-year qualification cycles, providing immense visibility (current order book stands at a record $1.004 billion, offering 5-7 years of revenue visibility). Consumer revenue operates on slightly shorter cycles but is anchored by dedicated capacity build-outs and strategic vendor agreements. Material costs in aerospace are largely insulated via back-to-back long-term agreements.

### Segment & Geographic Mix
Approximately 93-94% of the company's overall revenue is USD-denominated (including deemed exports), providing a natural hedge and underscoring their true export-driven model.

Notably, a steep sequential drop in consolidated *reported* EBITDA was heavily skewed by a one-off FX gain in the prior quarter; *operational* EBITDA actually expanded 3.5x sequentially.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
- **Is the moat Durable or Fleeting?** The aerospace moat is highly **Durable** and actively widening, while the consumer moat remains **Unproven**.
- **Evidence:** The aerospace business benefits from extreme switching costs. Parts require Nadcap and Prime special process certifications. Aequs’ competitive advantage lies in its Belagavi SEZ—a singular ecosystem housing forging, machining, and surface treatments, which drastically cuts cycle times and logistics costs compared to fragmented global peers. The moat recently widened with a landmark 15-year contract from Safran to deliver fully assembled Airbus A320 wheels (a "Make in India" first), signaling a leap from component supplier to critical sub-assembly partner. Their portfolio of 5,740 approved aerospace SKUs represents a massive barrier to entry.
- **The Bear Case:** The moat in the consumer segment is decidedly fragile. Past abrupt exits of clients like Hasbro highlight the lower switching costs and inherent lack of loyalty in the global toy supply chain. If Aequs cannot steadily scale utilization beyond 22%, their consumer assets risk becoming stranded.

## Growth Vectors
- **Where is the next $100M coming from?** 
  1. **Consumer Operating Leverage:** Management is pushing consumer facility utilization toward a target of 50% by Q4 FY27, which is slated to be the segment's EBITDA breakeven point. Despite utilization dipping to 22% in Q1, revenue grew 16% sequentially driven by improved product mix and enhanced throughput.
  2. **Aerospace Value-Chain Ascent:** Aequs is aggressively migrating from simple aerostructures to higher-margin, higher-complexity fully integrated sub-assemblies (like the A320 wheels) and engine components. This is backed by a ₹1,900 crore investment to build India's first fully integrated aero-engine and landing gear ecosystem in Hosur, Tamil Nadu, with initial revenues expected in FY29.
- **Management Execution:** Management is delivering on aerospace execution, shattering targets by pushing the order book past the $1 billion mark. Crucially, they are showing renewed capital discipline, signaling a willingness to walk back the aggressive ₹500 crore consumer capex budget and reallocate those funds toward surging aerospace demand if consumer utilization does not justify the investment.

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Forensic Red Flags:** The sudden resignation of the Chief Financial Officer (Dinesh Iyer) effective June 2026, mere months after the company's IPO, remains a governance flag. An interim internal replacement is currently leading the function, though a permanent search is progressing. 
- **Business Risks:** 
  - **Structural Supply Chain Vulnerability:** Management recently disclosed that 99% of raw materials are imported. Amidst escalating global tariff wars and geopolitical fragmentation, this extreme import reliance exposes Aequs to severe supply chain disruptions and input cost volatility.
  - **Working Capital Bloat:** While net working capital days improved slightly to 125 days in Q1 (down from 151 at the end of FY26), operating cash flow plunged to a negative ₹41.4 crore due to the heavy working capital requirements needed to feed the aggressive production ramp-up.
  - **Execution Risk on Mega-Capex:** The company is managing complex MoUs across Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. Developing the fully integrated Hosur ecosystem introduces immense operational risk while simultaneously attempting to turn around the bleeding consumer unit.
- **What could derail the thesis?** Geopolitical tensions disrupting the 99% imported raw material supply chain, or an inability to scale consumer utilization past 30%, which would leave Aequs suffocating under its own fixed-cost base.

## Investment Thesis
- **The Bull Case:** Aequs is a premier, scale-play on the structural shift of global aerospace manufacturing to India. The core aerospace business is a cash-printing juggernaut yielding >20% segment margins with an unparalleled $1.004 billion backlog and breakthrough sub-assembly contracts (Safran A320 wheels). The current consolidated losses are a temporal illusion caused by upfront consumer capex and one-off FX base effects. With consumer operational losses already narrowing 24% sequentially, embedded operational leverage will trigger a violent, non-linear inflection in consolidated earnings once consumer hits breakeven in late FY27.
- **The Bear Case:** The company is suffering from "empire-building" syndrome. The pristine aerospace business is diluted by an ₹800+ crore gross block investment in a commoditized consumer segment that is struggling to push utilization past 22%. Furthermore, the revelation that 99% of raw materials are imported introduces a massive geopolitical tail-risk that undermines the "Make in India" thesis. With negative operating cash flows and heavy quarterly depreciation (₹45.3 crore), the transition to a disciplined public company remains under stress. Avoid until the consumer segment definitively crosses the breakeven threshold.
