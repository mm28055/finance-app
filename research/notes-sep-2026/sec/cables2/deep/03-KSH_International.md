# KSH International — deep research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Screener code KSHINTL (standalone only; no consolidated page). Listed Dec 2025 (IPO ~₹420 cr). Price ₹1,036, market cap ₹7,016 cr, 52-week range ₹330–1,198 [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].

## Business and segments
- Makes magnet winding wires — the copper conductors wound inside transformers, motors and compressors. One reported segment [AR FY26 Note 32].
- Two product families: Specialised wires (75.3% of FY26 revenue — continuously transposed conductors (CTC), paper-insulated and rectangular wires for power and traction transformers, HVDC, 765 kV) and Standard wires (24.7% — round enamelled wire for motors, AC compressors, EV traction motors, DG-set alternators). FY26 specialised revenue ₹2,187 cr, standard ₹717 cr [IP Q1FY27 slides 8–9].
- Specialised earns ~3× the EBITDA per ton of standard wire; CTC is 50–70% of specialised; 765 kV + HVDC >25% of CTC volume [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Claims: India's leading specialised winding-wire maker, largest winding-wire exporter, and "only Indian supplier approved for HVDC transformers" [IP Q1FY27 slide 2].
- Customers: 120+ OEMs (Hitachi Energy, CG Power, BHEL, GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Bharat Bijlee, Nidec, Suzlon); 97% repeat revenue; top-10 customers 51.15% of FY26 revenue [IP Q1FY27 slides 2–3; AR FY26 Risks p.35].
- Geography: exports ₹823 cr in FY26 (26.5% of revenue), all to transformer makers in 24 countries; Middle East, Europe and US each 8–11% of revenue; target to return to the historical peak of ~40% exports [AR FY26 segment geography note; Q1 call].
- Capacity: 29,045 t (FY25) → 43,445 t after Supa Phase 1 (Oct 2025) → 59,045 t when Phase 2 finishes by March 2027. Plants: Taloja, two in Chakan, Supa. ~65% of 43,445 t is specialised capacity; insulation lines are not interchangeable. MPCB consent to operate at Supa raised to 28,800 t a year on 9 Sep 2026 [IP Q1FY27 slide 6; Q4 call; BSE filing 10-Sep-26].
- Utilisation: 80.3% FY25, 64.8% FY26 (new capacity), 70% Q4 FY26, 73.5% Q1 FY27; practical peak ~85%, reached 2–3 years after a new plant starts [IP Q1FY27 slide 6; Q4 call; Q1 call].

## Key numbers
- Quarterly (Screener standalone; revenue / operating profit / OPM / PAT, ₹ cr): Q1 FY26 559 / 40 / 7% / 23; Q2 712 / 46 / 6% / 30; Q3 818 / 49 / 6% / 23; Q4 1,018 / 56 / 6% / 35; Q1 FY27 1,164 / 74 / 6% / 42 [Screener].
- Annual: revenue FY24 ₹1,383 cr → FY25 ₹1,928 cr → FY26 ₹3,107 cr (+61%); operating profit ₹72 → ₹123 → ₹193 cr; PAT ₹37 → ₹68 → ₹110 cr (+62%); TTM revenue ₹3,712 cr, PAT ₹130 cr. ROCE 21%, ROE 20% [Screener].
- Volumes: FY24 21,495 t; FY25 23,324 t; FY26 28,168 t (+21%); Q1 FY27 7,969 t (+30% y/y, +5% q/q) [IP Q1FY27 slide 6; Q1 call].
- Unit economics (the right lens, because copper is passed through and swamps revenue): EBITDA per ton ₹52,500 (FY25) → ₹67,625 (FY26) → ₹65,885 (Q1 FY26), ~₹64,500 (Q3), ~₹74,000 (Q4) → ₹93,325 (Q1 FY27). Realisation ~₹14–14.6 lakh/t, mostly copper [IP Q1FY27 slides 3, 12; Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Q1 FY27 drivers of the ₹93k/t jump: record CTC share (front-loaded Phase 1 capacity), exports up 76%, new customers at market-linked (higher) value-addition rates, utilisation 73.5%, weaker rupee. Management declined to split out inventory gains but said "nothing played an outsized role" beyond mix [Q1 call].
- Interest cost rising with working capital: Q1 FY27 ₹17 cr vs ₹7 cr a year ago; FY26 ₹45 cr (incl. ₹2.7 cr one-off on Supa debt) [Screener; IP Q1FY27 slide 15].

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (26 May 2026): FY27 volume growth "at the very least" FY26's 21%; EBITDA/ton — CFO said ₹65–70k sustainable, MD said ₹67–74k; working-capital days (65–68 in FY26) to trend down, payables to rise above 25 days (toward ~30 by year-end); Phase 2 next capacity in Q2 FY27; upcast copper backward integration in H2 FY27; weighing multi-year OEM contracts; no near-term capacity beyond Phase 2 but 10,000 t of room at Supa [Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27 delivery (11 Aug 2026): volume +30% (beat 21%); EBITDA/t ₹93k (well above both ranges); WC days 60 (from 65), payables +5 days; upcast plant (5,000 t) commissioned early August — ahead of "H2"; five-year framework agreement with Hitachi Energy signed (but no quantity or price yet) [Q1 call].
- Q1 FY27 new targets: FY27 volume growth ~26% (trailing rate), EBITDA/ton "comfortable" at ~₹75,000 for FY27 — explicitly "not guidance"; WC days 30–35 in "a multi-quarter, not multi-year" time; exports back to ~40% over time; board authorised evaluating 10 more acres at Supa [Q1 call].
- Net: guidance raised on volume and unit profit; working-capital and backward-integration promises delivered on time.

## Capex and capacity
- Supa Phase 1 (14,400 t, commissioned Sep–Oct 2025) funded mainly by a ₹177 cr term loan, completed "without cost overrun" per CARE [CARE Feb-26].
- Supa Phase 2 (15,600 t; 14,400 of the 30,000 t Supa plan done by Q1) costs ~₹150–160 cr, largely from IPO money; FY27 cash capex "higher than ₹50 cr"; completion by March 2027, next wave in Q2 FY27; includes EV thin-insulation and PEEK wires (PEEK line "end of Q2", small, meaningful in 1.5–2 years; auto 5–10% of 59,045 t) [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- IPO use to 31 Mar 2026: ₹226 cr debt repaid; ₹87 cr earmarked for Supa machinery of which ₹28.6 cr spent (₹58.4 cr left); ₹8.8 cr rooftop solar (₹6.0 cr spent); monitoring agency (CARE) reported no deviation [AR FY26 notes — IPO proceeds table; AR FY26 Board's report].
- Capital commitments ₹41.5 cr at Mar-26 (₹11.1 cr a year earlier) [AR FY26 Note 28].
- Further room: 10,000–12,000 t more at Supa without new land; plus the 10-acre plot under evaluation. Next expansion to start once utilisation nears 85% [Q1 call].
- Payback (my arithmetic): 15,600 t at 85% × ~₹75,000/t ≈ ₹100 cr of annual EBITDA for ~₹155 cr of capex — under two years at full utilisation, before extra working capital (roughly ₹14 lakh/t × 60 days ≈ ₹300 cr+ of working capital to fund the added volume).

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Debt Mar-26: borrowings ₹316 cr (₹290 cr working-capital, ₹26 cr long-term) vs cash ₹72 cr + bank balances ₹12 cr → net debt ~₹232 cr; debt/equity 0.39 (1.21 FY25). Q1 FY27 total debt ₹481 cr, D/E 0.57 [AR FY26 Note 33; IP Q1FY27 slide 13].
- Cash flow: operating cash flow negative in four of the last five years — ₹−17 cr FY24, ₹−9 cr FY25, ₹−65 cr FY26; free cash flow ₹−228 cr in FY26, funded by the IPO [Screener].
- Days: debtors 39, inventory 56, payables 14 (Screener FY26); company's average net working-capital days 71 (Q1 FY26) → 65 (Q4) → 60 (Q1 FY27) [Screener; IP Q1FY27 slide 13].
- Pledged assets: ₹1,117 cr of assets pledged to lenders (all current assets and most fixed assets), normal for working-capital lines [AR FY26 Note 38].
- No dividend [Screener].
- Rating: CARE upgraded to A/Stable and A1 (from A-/A2) on 24 Feb 2026; bank limits enhanced to ₹330 cr; average limit use ~74% in 2025 [CARE Feb-26].

## Moat evidence
- Approvals: first to make CTC in India (~20 years); new CTC entrants take 5–7 years to qualify up to 765 kV; each utility approves separately [Q4 call].
- Turnaround 15–20 days per order on self-specified machines [Q4 call].
- Pricing: year-long value-addition (fabrication fee) matrices with 80–85% of customers; copper is passed through at order date, so EBITDA/ton is steady while margin % swings [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 slide 10].
- Demand pull: global transformer OEMs sit on 3–5-year order books; Hitachi Energy signed a five-year framework to supply its Indian and some global plants [Q1 call].
- Import competition weak: CTC imports (~1,000 t a month earlier) now dearer due to 10% duty and rupee; BIS rules are shifting standard wire to Indian makers [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Market sizing (management): India CTC demand ~40,000 t in FY25, ~70–75k t by FY27–28, 100–120k t by 2030–32 [Q4 call].
- Weak spots: standard wire is commoditised; value-addition rates for established customers are fixed yearly; margins are thin (EBITDA 6% of sales) and CARE lists "modest profitability" as a weakness [CARE Feb-26].

## Risks and red flags
- Management turnover: CEO Sandesh Bhagwat resigned 31 Mar 2026 (new CEO Hukumchand Lakhotiya from 2 Apr 2026); company secretary also changed; three directors including promoter Pushpa Hegde left the board on 3 Mar 2025, pre-IPO; Rohit Hegde stepped down as whole-time director from 1 Jan 2026 [AR FY26 Note 31]. Neither call discussed the CEO change.
- Customer concentration: top-10 = 51% of revenue; power sector dominates end-use (CARE) [AR FY26 p.35; CARE Feb-26].
- Cash burn: growth consumes working capital; CFO negative; interest cost more than doubled y/y in Q1 [Screener].
- EPCG export obligation: ₹201.8 cr of exports to be met from April 2026 against ₹33.6 cr of duty saved; failure would mean paying duty with interest [AR FY26 Note 28].
- Contingent liabilities small: bank guarantees ₹8.5 cr [AR FY26 Note 28].
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; key audit matters = revenue recognition and capitalisation of the large capex; audit trail operated all year [AR FY26 auditor's report p.176; AR FY26 additional regulatory disclosures].
- Related parties: small purchases from promoter firms (Kushal Electricals, Waterloo Motors, KSH Distriparks ≈ ₹0.3 cr); KMP remuneration ₹13.5 cr in FY26 — about 12% of PAT, high for the company's size [AR FY26 Note 31].
- Customer-side delays: some transformer OEMs are late in collecting orders while their own plants ramp [Q1 call].
- Fixed costs from Phase 2 will weigh until utilisation rises; management expects operating leverage only after Q4 FY27 [Q4 call].
- Promoter holding 74.58%; no pledge disclosed [Screener].

## My read
KSH is growing fast in a genuinely tight market: volume +30%, EBITDA per ton up ~40% in a year, and capacity rising 36% more by March 2027, with a qualification moat in high-voltage CTC that takes rivals 5–7 years to cross. The weak points are cash (operating cash flow has been negative for years and interest costs are climbing), thin percentage margins, concentration in a few transformer OEMs, and an unexplained CEO change right after listing. At a trailing P/E in the 50s, the price needs FY27 volumes near 26% with EBITDA/ton holding ~₹75k and utilisation of the new 59,000 t heading to 80%+ by FY29. I would get more confident if working-capital days fall toward 35–40 and operating cash flow turns positive in FY27; a fall back below ₹65k/t or OEM order slippage would change the view.
