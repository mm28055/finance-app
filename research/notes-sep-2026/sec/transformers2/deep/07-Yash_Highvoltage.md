# Yash Highvoltage (BSE SME 544310) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

This is a BSE SME stock, so it reports **half-yearly**. The two latest calls are H2 FY26 (14 May 2026) and H1 FY26 (Oct 2025). The input file held only the H2 FY26 transcript, so I read the official H1 FY26 transcript from BSE myself. Sources read: Screener standalone page (raw rows; consolidated is almost identical because the subsidiaries are tiny), the official H2 FY26 and H1 FY26 transcripts, the FY26 investor presentation (13 May 2026), the Annual Report FY26 (Board's report, MD&A, auditor's report and CARO, AOC-2, notes 37 and segment), the 14 Sep 2026 press release, and the AGM outcome. No credit-rating rationale was found on Screener or BSE.

## Business and segments
- It is a pure-play maker of **condenser-graded transformer bushings**, the insulated entry and exit points on every transformer. A transformer needs about 7 of them, and bushings are about 5% of transformer cost. Products: OIP (oil-impregnated paper), RIP/RIS (resin-impregnated, "dry"), high-current, wall and oil-to-oil bushings, plus retrofit services. The company says it is one of fewer than 12 independent makers worldwide and has 1,000+ customers in 60+ countries. [IP FY26] [H2 call]
- One reportable segment. **FY26 sales: India ₹217.0 cr, outside India ₹17.2 cr (about 7%)**, against ₹7.7 cr (5%) in FY25. **One customer bought ₹44.2 cr, about 19% of sales**; in FY25 the top two were ₹15.9 cr and ₹15.2 cr. [AR FY26 segment note p.145]
- **Mix:** about 82–85% of revenue and order book is RIP. RIP is currently assembled on **imported capacitive cores**, supplied under the former Swiss partner MGC Moser-Glaser's agreement, which runs to mid-2027. OIP plus high-current make up 15–18%. Retrofits were ₹16.0 cr (+60%) and high-current ₹10 cr (66% exported, including orders for a US tech company's data centres). [H2 call] [AR FY26 Board's report p.63–64]
- **Capacity:** the existing Vadodara plant was expanded from about 6,000 to about 10,000 units a year in FY26. About 7,000 bushings were made in FY26 (75–80% utilisation), split roughly 3,500 OIP and 3,500 RIP. Once Jarod and brownfield come on stream, total capacity is about 15,000 units with a revenue potential of about ₹700 cr (about ₹100 cr of it OIP). [H2 call] [AR FY26 p.63]
- **Group:** Yash HV USA Inc. (wholly owned, Texas, Apr 2025). Yash HV Power Component Pvt Ltd (wholly owned). **Sukrut Electric** (50% joint venture with Quality Power; Yash paid about ₹5.2–5.35 cr; revenue about ₹25–26 cr in FY26). [AR FY26 p.65, 3636] [H2 call]

- **Half-years:** Sep-24 57, Mar-25 93, Sep-25 100, Mar-26 136. OPM: 19%, 23%, 21%, 26%. PAT: 6, 15, 14, 24. [Screener]
- **H2 FY26 (company):** revenue ₹135.5 cr (+46% YoY), EBITDA ₹37.2 cr (27.4%), PAT ₹23.7 cr (17.5%). Gross margin 45.9% (H2 FY25: 42.0%). **FY26:** revenue ₹235.1 cr (+57%), EBITDA ₹60.4 cr (25.7%), PAT ₹37.4 cr (+75%), EPS ₹13.08. [IP FY26] [H2 call]
- The company's 25.7% EBITDA margin is higher than Screener's 24% OPM. The gap is how other income and some costs are classified. [Screener] [IP FY26]

- **Unit economics:** ₹235 cr over about 7,000 units is about ₹3.4 lakh per bushing on average. Prices range from about ₹30,000 (24 kV OIP) to about ₹40 lakh (400 kV). Management admits realisations "definitely" rose over 2–3 years. [H2 call]
- ROCE FY26 29% (FY24 45%, diluted by the IPO cash and capex). ROE 24%. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- **H1 FY26 call (Oct 2025):** "minimum CAGR of 35%" for five years. EBITDA of 21–22% to be kept, with a "steep" margin rise from FY28 once cores are localised. Greenfield commissioning in H2 FY27. Sukrut revenue to grow "8–10 times". Claimed about 30–35% share of India's addressable bushing market. [H1 call]
- **H2 FY26 call (May 2026):** growth raised to **"40–42% for the next four or five years minimum"**. For FY27, both "40–45% growth" (about ₹330–340 cr) and an invoicing target of **₹360–400 cr** (+53–70%) were given on the same call, which is internally inconsistent. EBITDA "24–25%" in FY27, with expansion from FY28. Order inflow target ≥₹500 cr. Export share target of 20%+ within 2–3 years. The Sukrut target was cut to "5–6x" (₹150–160 cr in 4–5 years). [H2 call]
- **Delivery so far:** H2 FY26 beat the run-rate: +46% revenue and a 27.4% margin, above the 21–22% guided in October. The order book went from about ₹150 cr to ₹400+ cr in one year. H1 FY27 results (due about Nov 2026) are not out yet, so FY27 guidance is untested. [AR FY26 p.64] [IP FY26]
- **Watch:** H1 has historically been 35–40% of the year ("60–65% of revenue comes in the second half"). So an H1 FY27 of about ₹140–150 cr would be on track for ₹360 cr. [H1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- **Jarod greenfield (20 km from Vadodara): ₹153 cr** for RIP/RIS core localisation. Design range was raised from 220 kV to 550 kV, adding 5,000–7,000 units a year. About ₹80 cr had been spent by May 2026. Plan: testing from July 2026; in-house RIP core commercial output from **Oct/Nov 2026** after type tests; ramp-up over "the next few years". Funded from the IPO (₹93.5 cr gross; ₹70.3 cr allotted to the factory; ₹14.3 cr unspent at Mar-26) and accruals. CWIP rose from ₹8 cr to ₹52 cr in FY26. Capital commitments were ₹35.3 cr at Mar-26 (Mar-25: ₹59.3 cr). [H2 call] [AR FY26 IPO note, note 37] [Screener]
- **Preferential issue:** approved by the board on 22 Jun 2026 and at an EGM on 15 Jul, and **allotted 6 Aug 2026**. It comprised 12.62 lakh shares plus 8.32 lakh warrants at ₹721, up to ₹151 cr in total, to 13 non-promoter investors. That is about ₹91 cr now plus 25% of the ₹60 cr warrant money, with the rest due within 18 months. Uses: 550 kV RIP manufacturing and testing, OIP brownfield (the OIP autoclave is the bottleneck), and repaying about ₹28–30 cr of working-capital debt. Promoter holding went from 57.94% to 54.79%. [AR FY26 p.65] [News: scanx.trade, Aug 2026] [Screener shareholding]
- Management's reason for equity rather than debt: "I am a bit debt-averse." [H2 call]
- **Payback maths:** ₹153 cr plus about ₹100 cr of new equity supports about ₹700 cr of revenue potential, which is about 2.5–3x asset turns. If gross margin rises as imported cores are replaced, the returns are good, but this is still unproven. [H2 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings were ₹38 cr at Mar-26 (Mar-25: ₹24 cr) and debt/equity 0.17. Net worth was about ₹184 cr before the ₹91 cr+ preferential money arrived in Aug 2026. [Screener] [IP FY26]
- **Cash conversion is weak:** FY26 operating cash flow was ₹9 cr against operating profit of ₹57 cr. Free cash flow was −₹50 cr (−₹29 cr in FY25). **Inventory days rose to 201 (from 138)** and the cash conversion cycle to 187 days (from 112). Payable days fell from 93 to 62. Management calls working capital "effectively managed", but the numbers say otherwise. [Screener]
- Dividend payout is about 11%, and 11.42 lakh ESOPs are outstanding. [Screener] [AR FY26 p.65]

## Moat evidence
- **Approvals:** every Indian utility, including all nuclear utilities, has approved it, plus customers in 60+ countries. It reports a 96% repeat-order rate. Qualification cycles are multi-year and customers have zero tolerance for failure. Three large global players (management named Trench and GE among them) control about 80% of the global market, and Yash has about 1% of it. [IP FY26] [H1 call]
- **Pricing power:** gross margin went from 42.0% to 45.9% (H2 on H2). Price pass-through clauses cover commodities and currency, and transformer makers now place **24–36-month blanket orders**. [IP FY26] [H2 call]
- **Localisation edge:** India imports about 95% of RIP cores. Yash expects to be early with in-house cores, but CG Power, Hitachi India, MIM (Vadodara) and a Russian-owned unit are also building local RIP capacity. Management admits "OIPs are in shortage" while RIP supply has eased. That weakens the scarcity argument for the new RIP plant. [H2 call]
- China: Chinese bushings have never been restricted, and buyers have still not switched, which management attributes to service and terms. [H2 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Cyber fraud: ₹2.10 cr** was lost to a fake email impersonating a China-based supplier (₹1.36 cr for material partly received, ₹0.74 cr for nothing received). Nothing has been recovered, and a police complaint was filed on 9 Apr 2026. **The auditor added an emphasis of matter** (not a qualification), and CARO clause xi reports it. This is a control lapse, even though the auditor found no employee involvement. [AR FY26 auditor's report EoM ii; CARO xi]
- **The first Ind AS year** (transition date 1 Apr 2024) is also an emphasis of matter, so comparatives are restated. [AR FY26 auditor EoM i]
- **Customer concentration:** one customer is 19% of FY26 sales. [AR FY26 p.145]
- **Execution:** the whole margin story depends on in-house RIP cores passing type tests in H2 FY27. Supply of cores from the ex-partner ends mid-2027. Management says FY27 revenue does not depend on it. [H2 call]
- **Related parties:** MD Keyur Shah's pay and perks were ₹2.89 cr. He also received ₹0.42 cr in office and car lease rent and **₹0.44 cr in guarantee commission for personally guaranteeing company loans**. Director Twinkle Shah received ₹0.30 cr in rent and ₹0.22 cr in consultancy fees, and a German non-executive director received ₹0.10 cr in technical fees. Each is small, but together they are about 12% of the MD's direct pay. [AR FY26 AOC-2 p.84–85]
- **Contingent liabilities (Mar-26):** tax disputes of about ₹0.8 cr (CST/sales tax/GST, 2015–2022) plus ₹2.2 cr in FY26 GST litigation (₹0.31 cr paid under protest). These are small. [AR FY26 note 37]
- **Dilution:** the IPO was in Dec 2024 and a ₹151 cr preferential issue followed within 20 months. The share count will rise about 7% once the warrants convert. [AR FY26 p.65]
- **Supply chain and people:** named by management as the main limits on growth. It is hard to add vendors, and attrition in the niche is high. [H2 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- **Tone:** confident became more ambitious. The growth guide went from ≥35% to 40–42%, and FY27 was set at ₹360–400 cr. The margin guide rose from 21–22% to 24–25% after a 27.4% H2.
- **New items:** the design range went from 220 kV to 550 kV and the capex from the IPO plan to ₹153 cr + ₹151 cr preferential. The US subsidiary and European/UK distributors are live. The cyber fraud came to light. The Swiss technology contract has ended, with supply until mid-2027. The OIP shortage has become the bottleneck.
- **Cut:** the Sukrut ambition went from 8–10x to 5–6x.

## My read
Yash is growing fast in a real global bottleneck, with pricing power (45.9% gross margin) and an order book that nearly tripled to ₹400 cr+. Management's FY27 target of ₹360–400 cr looks reachable given blanket orders. The investment case now rests on one event: in-house RIP cores passing type tests and replacing imports in H2 FY27–FY28. That is the source of the promised margin rise, and rivals are chasing the same localisation. Cash conversion is poor (₹9 cr of operating cash on ₹57 cr of profit, 201 inventory days), and equity has been raised twice in 20 months. The price, well above peers on earnings, assumes 40%+ growth and a margin step-up, so any slip in the Jarod type tests or a weak H1 FY27 would hurt. I would turn more positive on proof of commercial in-house RIP output and an operating cash flow above 50% of EBITDA.
