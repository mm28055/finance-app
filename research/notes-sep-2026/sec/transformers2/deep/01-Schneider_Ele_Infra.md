# Schneider Electric Infrastructure (SCHNEIDER) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Figures: Screener standalone page (the company has no subsidiaries), company filings. Price ₹1,210, market cap ₹28,919 cr, 52-week range ₹572–1,548 [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].

## Business and segments
- Makes medium-voltage power-distribution kit up to 33 kV: dry-type and oil transformers, air- and gas-insulated switchgear, ring main units (RMUs), vacuum interrupters and circuit breakers, relay panels, plus grid software (SCADA, ADMS, "One Digital Grid"). It does **not** make 400 kV+ transmission transformers or conductors, so it misses most transmission (evacuation) spending [Q1 call].
- Buyers: state discoms, EPC contractors, data centres, semiconductor fabs, metals and mining, railways/metro, private utilities. A large share goes through EPC firms, which caused the Q4 FY26 dispatch holds [Q4 call].
- The company reports one segment. There is no audited product split. Management gave only rough numbers: services (spares, recurring, modernisation) rose from about 12% to about 15% of revenue in FY26 [Q4 call]. Data centres are about 10–12% of the backlog [Q4 call]. "Emerging segments" (data centres plus semiconductors) are "more than 1/5" of the backlog [Q1 call]. Power & Grid is about 40% of the backlog (the analyst's figure; management did not dispute it) [Q1 call].
- Exports were 11% of FY26 turnover [AR FY26, BRSR], and 10–12% of revenue per the CFO. Imports are 10–15% of costs, so exports roughly hedge them [Q1 call].
- Plants: four sites, two in Vadodara (MV panels/RMU; transformers) and two in Kolkata (the SLW breaker factory, and the new KMVC vacuum-interrupter plant, which started in Jan 2026) [AR FY26 BRSR].
- Group dealings are large. FY26 sales to Schneider group companies were ₹512.7 cr, about 18% of revenue. Purchases from the group were ₹522.1 cr [AR FY26 note 35].

## Key numbers
- Quarterly sales / operating profit / margin / net profit (₹ cr) [Screener]:
  Q1 FY26 622 / 69 / 11% / 41; Q2 650 / 84 / 13% / 52; Q3 1,029 / 173 / 17% / 97; Q4 590 / 45 / 8% / 22; **Q1 FY27 651 / 34 / 5% / 12**.
- Company-reported Q1 FY27: EBITDA ₹41.0 cr (−44% YoY), EBIT ₹32.1 cr (−52%), PAT ₹12.4 cr (−70%). Gross margin was ₹237.5 cr, about 36.5% of sales, down 1.6 points. Material cost rose 9.4% and staff cost 14.8% [News: Investing.com, 17 Aug 2026; Q1 call]. The share fell about 11.5% on the result.
- Annual [Screener]: FY24 sales ₹2,207 cr, operating margin 13%, PAT ₹172 cr. FY25 ₹2,637 cr, 15%, ₹268 cr. FY26 ₹2,891 cr (+9.6%), 13%, ₹213 cr (−20.6%). TTM: sales ₹2,920 cr, PAT ₹184 cr. FY26 PAT includes a ₹14 cr exceptional labour-code cost, against an ₹18 cr tax-interest reversal gain in FY25 [Q4 call; AR FY26 p.25].
- Gross margin: 39.1% in FY25, 37.5% in FY26, 36.6% in Q4 FY26 (analyst figures that management accepted) [Q4 call]. Management blames about 1 point of the FY26 fall on commodities and the rest on mix: modernisation jobs are won in lowest-price tenders [Q4 call].
- Orders: FY26 intake ₹3,430 cr (+27.4%). Closing FY26 backlog ₹1,911 cr (+50.1%) [AR FY26 p.25, MD&A p.93]. Q1 FY27 intake ₹915 cr, the highest quarter ever but only +0.5% YoY. Backlog ₹2,169 cr (+32.7%) [Q1 call; News: Investing.com].
- Unit economics are not disclosed: no volumes, prices or per-unit profit.
- ROCE 41% (FY25) → 30% (FY26); ROE 38% [Screener].

## Guidance history
- Company policy is to give no numeric revenue or margin guidance. This held on both calls [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Q4 FY26 (29 May 2026): management said the fall in Q4 sales was customer-led deferrals, mostly EPCs, and that "a couple of major orders" moved out. It hoped for "recovery in Q1" ("fingers crossed"). It said it had taken only orders with price-variation clauses in Q4, and that 50–60% of commodity exposure is hedged [Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27 delivery: sales +5% YoY, so the deferrals partly came back. Margin fell again, to 5% operating margin (Screener) against 11% a year earlier. This was a **miss** on the implied recovery [Screener; Q1 call].
- New in Q1: the blame moved to "legacy" fixed-price orders booked before December 2025 (about 6-month cycle), negative operating leverage and an 8% rupee fall. Price-variation clauses are now mandatory in contracts, except utility tenders that do not allow them. Only 20–25% of the backlog carries such clauses. Management says the "remaining 3 quarters of the year will be good" [Q1 call].
- Verdict: guidance was never numeric. The qualitative promise of a Q1 recovery was not met. The tone moved from defensive to "confident in H2", with no numbers behind it.

## Capex and capacity
- Capex plan as of 31 Jul 2026 [AR FY26 p.14]:
  - Kolkata vacuum interrupters and breakers (KMVC): ₹184 cr. Capacity 80,000 → 2,50,000 VIs.
  - Kolkata breakers: ₹107.2 cr. 5,000 → 45,000 breakers.
  - Vadodara MV panels: ₹156.4 cr. 8,000 → 14,000 panels.
  - Vadodara RMU: ₹15.8 cr. 25,600 → 32,000 functions.
  - Vadodara transformers: ₹13.6 cr. 5,500 → 7,000 MVA.
  - Total about ₹477 cr, matching the CEO's "roughly ₹500 crores in our 3 plants over 3 years" [Q1 call].
- Timing: KMVC began in Jan 2026, and depreciation rose from Q4 FY26 (₹10 cr a quarter against ₹7 cr a year earlier) [Screener; Q1 call]. The CEO said new capacity arrives in H2 FY27, with "many things" in calendar 2027 and "a few" completed by 2028, then a ramp-up [Q1 call]. Unfinished capital contracts stood at ₹27.95 cr at March 2026 [AR FY26 note 34B].
- Funding: internal cash. CWIP was ₹102 cr at March 2026. Cash spent on investing was ₹63 cr in FY25 and ₹90 cr in FY26 [Screener]. No payback or asset-turn target was given.
- Purpose: the Kolkata plants are meant mainly for **exports** and to replace imports from other Schneider plants. Export ramp-up is "just starting" [Q1 call]. The transformer expansion is small (+27% MVA), so this is mainly a switchgear/breaker bet, not a transformer one.

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Gross debt ₹548 cr at March 2026 [AR FY26 MD&A p.94]. All of it is owed to group companies [AR FY26 note 12]:
  - an inter-corporate deposit from Schneider Electric IT Business India of about ₹242 cr, floating 5.9–6.3%, due June 2028;
  - 8% cumulative redeemable preference shares of about ₹201 cr, held by the parent EGATS and SE IT Business, redeemable after 20 years (2035–36).
  - Screener shows borrowings of ₹633 cr, including leases.
- Cash and equivalents ₹358 cr at March 2026, up from ₹258 cr [AR FY26 cash-flow note]. Net debt is therefore about ₹190 cr excluding leases.
- Interest is about ₹51–55 cr a year. ₹31.4 cr of it is paid to group companies [Screener; AR FY26 note 35].
- Cash flow: operating cash ₹308 cr (FY25) and ₹224 cr (FY26). Free cash ₹245 cr and ₹120 cr. Operating cash was 85% of operating profit in FY26 [Screener].
- Working capital (FY26): debtor days 103 (92 in FY25), inventory days 84, payable days 126, cash-conversion cycle 61 days [Screener]. Trade receivables including retention were ₹812.7 cr, a key audit matter [AR FY26 p.222]. The CFO said the company holds deliveries to protect cash [Q4 call].
- No dividend for FY26 despite ₹213 cr PAT. The accumulated losses from 2015–21 were only cleared recently: reserves turned positive in FY23 [AR FY26 Board's report; Screener].

## Moat evidence
- It has the global Schneider product range and brand, including T-Dry (Trihal design, about 170,000 units sold in 100 countries), SF6-free RMUs and EcoStruxure software. It won the "largest front-end fab order for the semicon segment" and "significant" data-centre wins [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call].
- It is a first mover in discom grid software: its first grid-modernisation system has run in a southern state since 2012, and there is a Tata Power Odisha case study [Q4 call]. Software attach-on orders show some switching cost [Q1 call].
- Evidence against pricing power:
  - gross margin fell 2.5 points over two years;
  - modernisation work goes to the lowest (L1) bidder;
  - only 20–25% of the backlog has price-variation clauses;
  - an analyst pointed out that competitors raised prices sooner, and management declined to compare [Q1 call; Q4 call].
- Share: management refused to give market-share targets and said it avoids some segments to protect margin [Q4 call].
- Cost: it pays the group trademark fees of ₹44.6 cr (about 1.5% of sales), data-management charges of ₹33.2 cr and management-support charges of ₹40.4 cr (up from ₹21.8 cr). Together that is about ₹118 cr, more than half of FY26 PAT [AR FY26 note 35]. This is a structural cost handicap compared with Indian peers.

## Risks and red flags
- **Inventory shortage / fraud:** management found missing inventory worth ₹2.18 cr at a warehouse. It commissioned a forensic review and filed a police complaint, and police enquiries were continuing at the report date. The loss is provided for [AR FY26 CARO clause xi].
- **Group charges and related parties:** trademark, data-management and management-support fees total about ₹118 cr and are rising, plus ₹31 cr of interest to the group. The auditor confirms that related-party transactions comply with sections 177/188 [AR FY26 note 35, CARO xiii]. Minority shareholders bear the cost of fees set by the parent.
- **Tax disputes:** contingent liabilities are income tax ₹39.9 cr (₹22.6 cr in FY25), indirect-tax cases shared with GE Vernova T&D ₹12.7 cr, and other indirect tax ₹64.4 cr. Transfer-pricing disallowances are pending before the tax tribunal (AY21–23) [AR FY26 note 34; CARO vii]. Tax litigation is a key audit matter.
- The audit opinion is unmodified. The key audit matters are revenue recognition, trade receivables and tax litigation [AR FY26 p.221–222; CG report].
- **CFO change:** Suparna Banerjee Bhattacharyya left on 15 Sep 2025, and Omkar Prasad took over on 16 Sep 2025. Several directors changed in FY26 [AR FY26 note 35].
- **Commodity and rupee:** copper and aluminum rose more than 30% in FY26 and steel about 10% [Q4 call]. The rupee fell about 8% in 2026 [Q1 call]. Legacy fixed-price orders are still being executed.
- **EPC dependence and deferrals:** customers can hold dispatches, as happened in Q4 FY26 [Q4 call].
- **Disclosure gaps:** no segment, product, volume or margin mix. The product mix was promised "from next quarter" [Q1 call].
- Bank guarantees outstanding: ₹89.4 cr [AR FY26 note 34B].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from defensive (deferrals, geopolitics, "fingers crossed") to confident on demand ("healthy pipeline"; "remaining 3 quarters… good").
- The blame for weak margins moved from one-off deferrals and commodities to legacy fixed-price orders, operating leverage and foreign exchange.
- Numbers: backlog growth slowed from +50% (March) to +33% (June). Order intake growth slowed from +27% (FY26) to +0.5% (Q1). Margin worsened from 8% to 5%.
- New: mandatory price-variation clauses, a first figure for clause coverage (20–25%), and the Kolkata export plan confirmed but "just starting".

## My read
The order book is real and growing: a ₹2,169 cr backlog is about 0.75 years of sales, and the data-centre and semiconductor share is rising. The ₹477 cr capex should lift switchgear and breaker capacity 2–9 times by 2027–28. But profit is going the wrong way. Operating margin fell from 15% (FY25) to 13% (FY26) to 5% in Q1 FY27, and only 20–25% of orders are protected against cost increases. Group fees of about ₹118 cr and ₹31 cr of group interest take a large slice of profit away from minority holders. At about 149 times trailing earnings, the price needs about 38% earnings growth a year for a decade (the note's formula). That requires margins back at 14–15% and fast sales growth together. H2 FY27 margins must recover visibly (operating margin above 12% in Q2/Q3) before the valuation has any support. Evidence that would change the view: numeric margin guidance, the promised mix disclosure, or Kolkata export orders.
