# Shilchar Technologies (SHILCTECH) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Sources read: Screener standalone page (raw HTML table rows; the company has no subsidiary, so standalone = full company), official Q1 FY27 transcript (BSE, 19 Aug 2026), official Q4 FY26 transcript (BSE), Q1 FY27 investor presentation (BSE, 12 Aug 2026), Annual Report FY26 (BSE; MD&A, auditor's report + CARO, AOC-2 related parties, notes 34 and 39), CARE rating press release 7 Jul 2026, and the stored input file.

## Business and segments
- Makes custom power and distribution transformers up to 50 MVA / 132 kV at one site in Gavasad, Vadodara. Distribution transformers run from 5 kVA to 3,000 kVA and power transformers from 3,000 kVA to 50,000 kVA. Its niche is renewable-energy transformers (inverter-duty units for solar parks, wind) and industrial/private utilities. The company says it does **not sell to state utilities**, by policy. [CARE Jul-26] [Q1 call]
- One reported segment ("Transformers & Parts"). The geography split is the useful cut. **FY26 revenue from products was ₹651.9 cr**: India ₹337.0 cr (52%), Europe and Middle East ₹195.1 cr (30%), North America ₹99.3 cr (15%), others ₹20.5 cr (3%). In FY25, EME was only ₹99.8 cr and "others" ₹89.6 cr. So the Middle East share doubled in one year, and that is the exposure that hurt Q4 and Q1. [AR FY26 note 23/39, p.133, 145]
- Exports were 48% of revenue in FY26 (FY25: 43%). Export revenue rose 16% to ₹315 cr. No customer was above 10% of revenue in FY26; three were in FY25. The top five domestic customers make up 57% of domestic sales. [CARE Jul-26] [AR FY26 p.133]
- Capacity is 7,500 MVA a year (it was raised from 4,000 MVA in Aug 2024). Utilisation, measured on dispatches: FY25 77%, FY26 79% (about 6,000 MVA). **Q1 FY27 utilisation was only 60–65%.** Realisation works out to roughly ₹11 lakh per MVA (analyst's estimate, which management called a "thumb rule"). [IP Q1FY27 p.13] [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Management says export margins run about 10 percentage points above domestic margins. The pre-crisis 50:50 domestic/export mix is what produced the roughly 30% EBITDA margin. [Q1 call]

## Key numbers (Screener, ₹ cr; OP = operating profit before other income)
- **Annual:** FY24 sales 397 / OP 113 (29%) / PAT 92 → FY25 623 / 185 (30%) / 147 → FY26 652 / 190 (29%) / 158. FY26 growth was only 5%, against 57% in FY25. [Screener]
- **Quarterly sales:** Jun-25 159, Sep-25 171, Dec-25 170, Mar-26 152, Jun-26 135. **OPM:** 33%, 31%, 31%, 21%, 16%. **PAT:** 41, 46, 42, 28, 21. [Screener]
- **Q1 FY27 vs Q1 FY26:** revenue ₹134.6 cr vs ₹158.8 cr (−15%). EBITDA excluding other income ₹22.1 cr (16.4%) vs ₹52.4 cr (33.0%). PAT ₹20.9 cr vs ₹36.3 cr (−42%). EPS ₹18.24. [IP Q1FY27 p.5]
- The "EBITDA ₹29.23 cr" quoted on the call **includes about ₹7 cr of other income** (treasury income on ₹200 cr+ of liquid funds). Core EBITDA margin is 16.4%, not 21.7%. [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27 p.5] [Screener]
- Other income is growing and flatters profit: FY26 ₹26 cr (FY25 ₹16 cr), and TTM ₹29 cr. FY26 also carried a **₹12.1 cr forex gain** from the weak rupee (FY25 ₹4.0 cr). CARE's 30.9% FY26 PBILDT margin includes that gain; Screener's OPM is 29%. [CARE Jul-26] [Screener]
- TTM (to Jun-26): sales ₹628 cr (−7%), PAT ₹138 cr (−16%). ROCE FY26 51%, ROE 38%. [Screener]
- **Unit economics:** about 6,000 MVA dispatched in FY26 for ₹652 cr, which is about ₹10.9 lakh per MVA. Transformer oil is 8–12% of cost. It doubled between Feb and May 2026, while other commodities rose 10–25%. [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- **Q3 FY26 call (per an analyst on the Q4 call):** FY27 revenue of ₹850–900 cr was talked about. **Q4 call (5 May 2026):** this was cut to "₹800 cr, conservatively ₹800–850 cr". Other Q4-call promises: FY27 margin "same 29–31% or even increase"; volume of about 7,000 MVA; Q1 would be "a normal quarter" for exports; dispatches to the Middle East "resumed in April"; price hikes being passed on. [Q4 call]
- **Q1 delivery (14 Aug 2026):** Q1 was not normal. Revenue fell 15% YoY, core margin was 16%, and utilisation was 60–65%. Shipping costs rose 3–5x from late April, and customers deferred offtake. Only **50–60% of the raw-material rise was passed on** for orders already in hand. [Q1 call]
- **Q1 call guidance:** the ₹800 cr revenue target was **kept** ("we don't anticipate any problem"). Margin guidance was **softened**: the 30% margin returns only "if exports come back online"; otherwise "a slight dip". Q2–Q3 margins are expected to be above Q1. Q2 is "fully booked". [Q1 call]
- **Arithmetic check:** ₹800 cr needs about ₹665 cr in Q2–Q4, or ₹222 cr a quarter. The best quarter ever was ₹232 cr (Mar-25), and the existing plant is capped at 7,500 MVA (about ₹825 cr at ₹11 lakh/MVA). **The target looks very stretched.** A miss of ₹80–120 cr is the likely outcome unless exports normalise quickly. [Screener] [Q4 call]
- **Longer term:** after Phase III, revenue could reach about ₹1,500 cr "once fully utilised", which management places in **FY29–FY30**. The AR says capacity could eventually reach 25,000–30,000 MVA. [Q4 call] [AR FY26 MD&A p.57]
- **Credibility note:** the FY26 annual report (circulated in July) spoke of "export disruptions behind us". Management admitted on the Q1 call that the text was written in early June, when a ceasefire looked likely. Two analysts pressed on this. [Q1 call] [AR FY26 p.57]

## Capex and capacity
- **Gavasad Expansion #3:** +6,500 MVA to reach 14,000 MVA, at a cost of about ₹120 cr, all from internal accruals. Commissioning is due in **April 2027** (possibly "slightly earlier"). Status: civil foundations done, PEB erection and utilities under way, all major equipment ordered. The expansion adds 1.1 lakh sq ft to the existing 1.6 lakh sq ft. [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27 p.11,15] [CARE Jul-26]
- **Product step-up:** the new bay can make transformers up to **160 MVA / 220 kV**. Prototyping will take about 3–4 months after production starts. PGCIL and customer approvals, audits and type tests come after that. In the meantime, the new capacity will make the existing range (solar/wind units). Management admits initial 220 kV margins will be lower "to create references". [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Asset turns:** ₹120 cr for 6,500 MVA against about ₹700 cr of potential revenue at today's realisation, i.e. about 5–6x gross asset turns. This is exceptional and explains the 50%+ ROCE. Capital commitments were ₹21.3 cr at Mar-26. Screener shows CWIP of only ₹6 cr, so most of the spend falls in FY27. [AR FY26 note 34] [Screener]
- **Phase IV:** a further 4.5 acres of adjoining land has been bought. Product and size are undisclosed. [Q1 call]
- **Bushings:** supplied by **Yash Highvoltage** (Vadodara), with Chinese imports planned. Lead times are long but manageable with inventory. [Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- **Debt-free.** Cash and equivalents were ₹246 cr at Mar-26 (company figure). CARE puts free liquidity at ₹227 cr (₹25 cr cash + ₹202 cr liquid investments). Screener shows investments of ₹202 cr and zero borrowings. Fund-based bank limits were unused; non-fund limits (bank guarantees/LCs) were 62% used. [Q4 call] [CARE Jul-26] [Screener]
- **Cash flow:** FY26 operating cash flow was ₹192 cr (Screener; CARE says ₹182 cr), up from ₹40 cr in FY25, helped by lower receivables. Free cash flow was ₹169 cr. CFO/OP was 134% in FY26 and 45% in FY25, so conversion is lumpy. [Screener] [CARE Jul-26]
- **Working capital (FY26):** debtor days 86 (FY25 134), inventory days 83, payable days 64, cash conversion cycle 105 days (FY25 120). Inventory at Mar-26 was about ₹100 cr, and was kept low by pausing conversion of work-in-progress in March. [Screener] [Q4 call]
- Management declined to give Q1 FY27 receivable and inventory days on the call ("will provide by email"). This is a disclosure gap in a quarter when stock should have built up. [Q1 call]
- **Dividend:** final dividend of ₹12.50/share proposed for FY26. The payout is about 9%, and the company's policy is to retain cash for capex. There is no buyback. [AR FY26 Chairman's letter] [Screener]
- CARE expects net cash accruals of ₹160–260 cr a year over FY27–FY29 with no debt repayments. [CARE Jul-26]

## Moat evidence
- **Returns:** ROCE was 54% in FY23, 75% in FY24, 71% in FY25 and 51% in FY26, with a 29–30% operating margin for three years. That is far above typical Indian transformer makers, who earn 12–20%. CARE credits this to "niche segment" presence. [Screener] [CARE Jul-26]
- **Lead time as a selling point:** 12–16 weeks for the power transformers it makes. Global lead times for larger units run into years. Customers cite "quality, service and shorter lead time". [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Export references:** it has exported to 25+ countries over about 19 years and has US/Canada utility and developer customers. North America revenue grew 22% in FY26 despite the 25% US tariff episode (the tariff has since fallen to 10%). [IP Q1FY27 p.8] [AR FY26 p.133] [Q4 call]
- **Limits of the moat:** products top out at 132 kV / 50 MVA, a crowded bracket. CARE calls the industry "highly fragmented". The Q1 collapse showed that customers can simply stop lifting goods. The margin premium looks like a product of the global shortage and export mix, not of technology. It has no PGCIL approval yet for 220 kV. [CARE Jul-26] [Q1 call]
- **Pricing:** it could pass on only 50–60% of the cost rise on old orders. New orders are priced at current costs, and some have price-variation (PV) clauses. Management says domestic pricing is "normal", with no pressure. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Export/logistics concentration:** the Middle East was about 30% of FY26 revenue. Container costs rose 3–5x. About ₹30–35 cr of Q1 revenue and ₹35–40 cr of Q4 revenue were deferred, and the order book is now 70% domestic. [Q1 call] [Q4 call] [AR FY26 p.133]
- **Margin reset:** if exports stay weak, a domestic-heavy mix implies margins in the low 20s rather than 30%. Core OPM was 21% in Q4 and 16% in Q1. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- **Guidance credibility:** the ₹850–900 cr target became ₹800 cr and is now likely to miss. The upbeat annual-report text was contradicted within weeks. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Contingent liabilities (Mar-26):** a GST dispute of ₹6.43 cr (nil a year earlier), taken to the First Appellate Authority. CARO lists GST demands for FY20–FY23 of about ₹6.4 cr in total, before the Joint Commissioner. Other items: VAT ₹0.04 cr; claims not acknowledged ₹0.35 cr; LCs/bank guarantees ₹70.6 cr (FY25 ₹47.1 cr). All are small against ₹490 cr of net worth. [AR FY26 note 34, CARO para vii]
- **Auditor:** unmodified opinion, **no key audit matters**, no emphasis of matter, no fraud reported. The audit-trail feature was found operating. [AR FY26 Auditor's report, p.104–]
- **Related parties and pay:** MD Alay Shah's pay was ₹8.30 cr, about 5% of FY26 PAT. Sons Aashay (Executive Director, ₹1.52 cr) and Aatman (₹1.14 cr) are also paid, so family pay totals about ₹11 cr, or 7% of PAT. An AGM resolution sought approval for higher pay. Sales of ₹5.3 cr went to Nile Ltd (common director), at arm's length. No material RPTs. [AR FY26 AOC-2 p.58–59]
- **Promoter holding** fell from 64.01% to 62.12% in the Dec-25 quarter (reason not verified; no pledge shown). There is no pledge disclosure. [Screener shareholding]
- **Forex:** 48% of revenue is exports and there is **no active hedging policy**. The FY26 rupee fall produced a ₹12 cr gain, which will not repeat if the rupee firms. [CARE Jul-26]
- **Execution:** the step into 220 kV needs new approvals and references and starts at lower margins. Everyone else is expanding too (the AR cites industry capacity going from about 375 GVA to about 690 GVA by FY28). [AR FY26 MD&A p.55]

## What changed between the two calls
- **Tone:** confident ("temporary", "normal quarter") in May became defensive in August ("nobody expected this crisis to go on", "not a revenue loss").
- **Numbers:** the order book was ₹452 cr (30–32% export) and is now about ₹500 cr (30% export, 70% domestic). Utilisation was 79% in FY26 and 60–65% in Q1. The margin promise moved from "29–31% or higher" to "slight dip if domestic-heavy".
- **New issues:** freight costs to North America are also up; only 50–60% of cost was passed through; 220 kV will come with initial low margins; Phase IV land has been bought; Q1 working-capital data was refused on the call.
- **Unchanged:** the ₹800 cr FY27 target, the April 2027 capex date, and the "no state utility" policy.

- **The stored profile says the Gavasad site is 21 acres** (presentation), while the AR MD&A says 17 acres. This is a company inconsistency, probably 17 acres plus 4.5 acres of new land.

## My read
The business is still excellent on returns and has a clean, debt-free balance sheet. But FY27 has turned into a lost year. Q1 core margin was 16%, utilisation 60–65%, and the ₹800 cr target needs record quarters from a plant that is already capped. The real value is FY28–FY30: 14,000 MVA and a 220 kV entry, funded from cash at about 5x asset turns, which could take revenue to ₹1,100–1,500 cr if approvals come in time. The price needs the 30% margin to come back with exports. If margins settle at 20–22% on a domestic-heavy mix, earnings power is much lower than the FY25–FY26 run rate. Three things would change my view: a clear drop in Gulf freight rates and a rise in export share back above 40% (positive); or a formal cut to FY27 guidance, or slippage in 220 kV approvals into FY29 (negative).
