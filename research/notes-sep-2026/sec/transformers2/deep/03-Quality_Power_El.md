# Quality Power Electrical Equipments (QPOWER) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Figures come from Screener's consolidated page and company filings. Price ₹1,518, market cap ₹11,754 cr, 52-week range ₹581–1,570. Promoters hold 73.91% [Screener, 27 Sep 2026]. The company listed in February 2025.

**Read this first: company "revenue" and "EBITDA" include other income.** The Q1 FY27 presentation says so directly ("EBITDA includes other income"; "Total income"). Screener shows revenue from operations and operating profit before other income. That gap explains most of the mismatch between the call numbers and Screener [IP Q1FY27; Screener].

## Business and segments
- The company is a group of three businesses:
  - **Quality Power standalone (Sangli):** reactors, coils, line traps and special transformers up to 765 kV, used in HVDC links (long-distance DC power lines), FACTS equipment (which steadies grid voltage) and data centres.
  - **Mehru Electrical (51% owned):** instrument transformers up to 500 kV. Management says Mehru supplies "1 in 2" high-voltage instrument transformers in India.
  - **Endoks Enerji, Turkey (51% owned through a subsidiary):** STATCOM/SVC grid-stabilising systems, automation, and battery-storage power-conversion systems (PCS) [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 Board's report; Q1 call].
  - Also: Sukrut Electric (a 50:50 JV, components) and S&S Transformers (100% owned) [AR FY26].
- **FY26 revenue by entity:** QP equipment and projects about ₹246 cr, Mehru ₹318 cr, Endoks ₹468 cr [Q4 call].
- **FY26 EBIT margins:** QP 33%, QP projects 21%, Mehru 15%, Endoks 23% (29–30% before the Ind AS 29 charge) [Q4 call].
- **Q1 FY27 revenue:** QP about ₹69 cr (₹37 cr a year earlier), Mehru about ₹83 cr (about ₹60 cr), Endoks about ₹107 cr [Q1 call].
- **Minority shareholders take a large share of profit.** Non-controlling interests (outside owners of Mehru and Endoks) received ₹64.2 cr, **34.6% of FY26 consolidated profit** of ₹185.5 cr. Endoks alone produced 51% of consolidated profit [AR FY26 consolidated note on entity-wise share]. So shareholders' own profit was about ₹121 cr (EPS ₹15.67).
- **Customers:** Hitachi Energy, Siemens and GE (as buyers of components), PowerGrid, Adani, renewable developers, and US data centres. The top 10 customers made up about 35% of sales at the time of the IPO document (DRHP) [Q4 call].
- **Geography:** about half of revenue is from Turkey or exports. Endoks sells to Europe and Japan, and Mehru has started supplying the grids of Denmark and Sweden [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].

## Guidance history
- **FY26:** the starting guide was ₹800 cr revenue, later raised to ₹900 cr at about 22% margin. The company delivered ₹1,007 cr (total income) at 23.5% [Q4 call]. **Beaten.** Both figures are total-income based.
- **FY27 revenue growth:**
  - Q4 call: about 15–20% growth, called a "muted" year while the new factory clears audits [Q4 call].
  - Q1 call: "we have guided to 20%… maybe in Q3 we can look at revising" [Q1 call].
  - **Kept.**
- **FY27 margin:** "Please model us at 20% or high teens EBITDA" (EBITDA here includes other income) [Q1 call].
  - Mehru's guide was raised to about 18% from 15%.
  - Coil business "stable" at about 20%.
  - A Q3 dip is flagged because the aluminium spike reaches costs after a six-month lag and Sangli's fixed costs arrive before it ramps up [Q1 call].
  - **Kept, with a caution added.**
- **FY28:** "north of 50%" growth [Q4 call], repeated as "guidance of 50%" [Q1 call].
- **Sangli coil factory:**
  - Q4 call: trial production "July end, August". Project managers were still saying December–January [Q4 call].
  - Q1 call: trial production in August subject to approvals (pollution board, factory inspector, building certificate). About 60 customer audits need about 6 months. "Trickle" of revenue in Q3, meaningful revenue in Q4 [Q1 call].
  - **Slipping.**
- **Endoks battery-storage (PCS) plant at Nigde:** targeted for December 2026 [Q4 call]; operations "during Q3" [Q1 call]. **On track.**
- **Fundraise:**
  - Q4 call: an enabling approval for up to USD 75 million, "not necessarily immediately" [Q4 call].
  - Q1 call: "less than INR 500 crores", roadshows from 20 August [Q1 call].
  - **23 Sep 2026 board meeting: a QIP of up to ₹700 cr** (a qualified institutional placement, a share sale to large investors), plus a preferential issue of swap shares for Win-Win. The shareholder meeting (EGM) is on 19 Oct 2026 [BSE board outcome 23-Sep-26].
  - **The size went up against the Q1 statement.**

## Capex and capacity
- **Sangli coil factory:** peak revenue potential of ₹1,500–1,800 cr, according to management. Capital work in progress was ₹73 cr at March 2026. It was funded largely from internal cash, and about 80% of the factory was built without drawing the ₹125 cr promoter loan line [Q4 call; Q1 call; Screener].
- **HVDC CTC magnet-wire plant** (CTC is a special copper/aluminium conductor used in transformer windings): machines are in place. It needs 3–5 months to stabilise, with full production targeted for Q4 FY27. Management's aim is ₹500 cr of aluminium wire in 2–3 years [Q1 call].
- **Endoks PCS plant (Turkey):** about USD 2 million spent, with perhaps USD 1 million more. Management puts peak revenue at USD 70–80 million. The PCS order book is about USD 60 million, with another USD 40 million expected within 12 months [Q1 call].
- **Other projects:**
  - Mehru: ₹17.2 cr for gas-insulated switchgear (GIS) parts and HV testing [Q4 call].
  - Instrument-transformer plant under construction in Turkey [IP Q1FY27].
  - GIS component prototypes: 220 kV first, then 400/765 kV over about a year [Q1 call].
- **Win-Win Speciality Insulators (Vizag, special economic zone), bought 23 Sep 2026:**
  - Deal: 100% for up to ₹272.34 cr. That is ₹148.5 cr in 10,17,123 swap shares at ₹1,460 (preferential issue) plus ₹123.84 cr cash. Management put the enterprise value at about ₹315 cr [BSE board outcome 23-Sep-26; Q1 call].
  - **Win-Win's revenue was only about ₹17.07 cr in FY26** (₹12.72 cr in FY25; ₹1.07 cr in FY24) [BSE board outcome annexure].
  - Management says only ₹50–60 cr of the price is for the business. The rest pays for 47.7 acres of land, the buildings and the sellers' debt.
  - Management's revenue claims: ₹250–300 cr a year without capex and ₹450–500 cr with about ₹50 cr of capex; margins of 15–25% until it is "under control" (about 4 quarters); a two-year payback.
  - Consolidation is expected from Q4 FY27 because SEZ approvals are needed [Q1 call].
  - Losses before closing fall on the sellers (a "lockbox" deal) [Q1 call].
- Cash used in investing: ₹100 cr (FY25) and ₹95 cr (FY26) [Screener].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- **Debt:** borrowings ₹40 cr at March 2026. Group debt was about ₹23 cr at June 2026 (subsidiary working capital) [Screener; Q1 call].
- **Cash:** cash and bank balances were ₹248 cr at March 2026 and have since fallen as capex is funded internally [Q4 call; Q1 call]. Much of the cash sits in subsidiaries [Q1 call].
- **Cash conversion is weak.**
  - Operating cash flow was ₹62 cr (FY25) and ₹80 cr (FY26), against company EBITDA of ₹236 cr. Operating cash was 64% of Screener operating profit and 0.3 times company EBITDA.
  - Free cash flow was −₹31 cr in FY26 [Screener; IP Q1FY27].
  - Management told the Q4 call that inventories rose because logistics delayed shipments [Q4 call].
- **Working capital (FY26):** debtor days 93 (149 in FY25), inventory days 74, payable days 105, cash-conversion cycle 63 days [Screener].
- **Dividends:** ₹1 final (FY26) and ₹0.25 interim (Q1 FY27). Promoters waived their dividend and salary increments ahead of the fundraise [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26].

## Moat evidence
- **Niche position:** the company sells reactors and instrument transformers to Hitachi, Siemens and GE rather than competing with them. Management says Hitachi and Siemens do not make coil products and GE has only a small plant in Brazil [Q4 call]. The main rivals are Trench (EUR 1.5 billion order book) and Arteche [Q4 call].
- **Share:** Mehru supplies "1 in 2" HV instrument transformers in India [Q1 call].
- **Pricing:** all executable orders were booked "above the margin guidance". QP coils have held about 25% gross margin for 6 quarters. Gross margin rose from 44.6% to 47.2% in Q1 FY27 [Q1 call].
- **Qualification barriers:** new plants need Hitachi, Adani and PowerGrid approvals, with about 60 audits in total [Q1 call]. This protects incumbents but also slows the company's own ramp-up.
- **Against the moat:**
  - There are no price-escalation clauses; cost increases are passed on with a lag [Q4 call].
  - Battery-storage converters start at only 10–12% margin against Chinese competition [Q4 call].
  - The company lost many US transformer bids [Q4 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Minority leakage:** 34.6% of consolidated profit belongs to minority holders. The two 51%-owned units hold 71% of the order book [AR FY26; IP Q1FY27]. Headline profit overstates what shareholders own.
- **"EBITDA" definitions:** company figures include other income (interest, a ₹16 cr foreign-exchange gain in Q4) and present "adjusted" figures that exclude Ind AS 29 losses. The Q4 FY26 charge was ₹25.7 cr and Q1 FY27 ₹7.82 cr [Q4 call; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]. The Ind AS 29 "gains" in earlier quarters were not stripped out in the same way. Management confirmed Q3 FY26 had a similar entry "which led to the bump up of the margin" [Q4 call].
- **Audit coverage:**
  - Three subsidiaries, including Endoks (audited in Turkey under international accounting standards, IFRS), were audited by other auditors. They cover ₹749.6 cr of revenue and ₹799.8 cr of assets, about three-quarters of group revenue. The group auditor relies on their reports.
  - The auditor also reports a **modification on the audit trail** (the edit log in the accounting software) for the holding company and one subsidiary. The log works at application level only.
  - The auditor is Kishor Gujar & Associates, Pimpri [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report].
- **Guarantees:** the contingent-liability note shows bank and corporate guarantees of about ₹421 cr (₹118 cr a year earlier), plus ₹51 cr [AR FY26 consolidated note B].
- **Dilution:**
  - A QIP of up to ₹700 cr is about 6% of market cap.
  - Swap shares for Win-Win: 10.17 lakh shares.
  - Both come on top of an IPO only 19 months ago [BSE board outcome 23-Sep-26].
- **Price paid for Win-Win:** ₹272 cr of equity value for a ₹17 cr-revenue business with a troubled history. Previous owners wrote off about ₹240 cr after Cyclone Hudhud, and it lost about ₹2 cr a day during the gas crisis [Q1 call; board outcome].
- **Related parties:**
  - Purchases of ₹19.9 cr from the Sukrut JV.
  - Rent paid to promoter family members.
  - Machinery rent of ₹19 cr within the group.
  - An EGM approved pay of up to ₹5 cr a year for **25 years** for a promoter relative holding an office of profit (actual pay ₹86.4 lakh) [AR FY26 Board's report, AOC-2].
  - Endoks directors own the software firm Innovitas [Q1 call].
- **Turkey:** hyperinflation accounting and India–Turkey political tension [Q4 call].
- **Execution load:** a new factory, a wire plant, a PCS plant, GIS, an acquisition turnaround and a US entry, all at once. Management itself flags "management bandwidth" [Q1 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- **Tone:** Q4 was about beating guidance and hyperinflation "optics". Q1 was about execution: plant installation, audits, integration and a group procurement head.
- **Order book:** ₹1,400 cr+ → ₹1,945 cr (+39% in a quarter).
- **Win-Win:** from a hint ("the board is deciding something on insulators") to a completed diligence and a ₹315 cr enterprise value. The share purchase agreement (SPA) was signed on 23 September.
- **Fundraise:** from "enabling only" to under ₹500 cr, and then to a ₹700 cr QIP after the Q1 call.
- **Sangli:** from "July–August trial" to "trial subject to approvals; meaningful revenue in Q4".
- **New:** a group CTO (formerly Hitachi/ABB), a group procurement head (CPO), a Mehru plant in Turkey or Vizag under evaluation, and a Q3 margin warning.

## My read
Demand is clearly strong. The order book is 1.9 times revenue, the reactor/instrument-transformer niche has real qualification barriers, and gross margins are rising. But the headline numbers flatter the shareholder: "EBITDA" includes other income, adjusted profit ignores the Ind AS 29 losses, and a third of consolidated profit belongs to minority holders. Cash conversion is poor: ₹80 cr of operating cash on ₹236 cr of EBITDA. FY27 is guided at only 20% growth while Sangli clears about 60 audits, and a ₹700 cr QIP plus a costly acquisition of a small, troubled insulator plant will dilute holders. At about 88 times trailing earnings, the price needs about 31% earnings growth a year for a decade (the note's formula), and that is on owners' profit after the minority share. Watch four things: Sangli's first revenue in Q3–Q4 FY27, the QIP price, Win-Win's first consolidated quarter, and whether operating cash flow catches up with profit.
