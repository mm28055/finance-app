# Indian transformers and grid equipment — sector research (as of 28 Sep 2026)

Scope: power and distribution transformers, their key components (bushings, CRGO steel, tanks) and small grid instruments (CTs, meters). This note covers the group Shilchar, Yash Highvoltage, Rishabh Instruments and Supreme Power, and gives context for the larger peers (CG Power, Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, T&R, Voltamp). Tags: [CEA/Outlook Sep-26] = CEA data reported by Outlook Business on 26 Sep 2026; [ICRA Jul-26]; [PowerLine May-26] = CEA transmission plan; [WoodMac]; [Co. AR/call] = company documents read for the company files.

## 1. Market size and growth
- **India transformer market:** about **US$3.25 bn in 2026**, rising to about **US$4.82 bn by 2031** (8.2% CAGR), according to **Mordor Intelligence**. Distribution transformers were about 59% of the 2025 market, and utilities about 47% of revenue. [Supreme AR FY26 MD&A citing Mordor Intelligence; Mordor web summary]
- **Global transformer market:** about **US$65 bn in 2025**, rising to about **US$88 bn by 2030** (about 6.5% CAGR), according to MarketsandMarkets. Asia-Pacific is the largest and fastest-growing region. [Shilchar AR FY26 MD&A citing MarketsandMarkets]
- **Physical demand, CEA data:** India added **113,013 MVA** of AC transformation capacity (220 kV and above) in FY26, which was **90% of the 126,007 MVA target**. The FY27 target is **158,339 MVA** (+40%). Only **35,670 MVA** was added in Apr–Jul 2026, about 23% of the target in a third of the year. The grid held about **1,486 GVA** at 220 kV+ by July 2026. [CEA/Outlook Sep-26]
- **The 2022–32 National Electricity Plan** (NEP, the government's 10-year transmission plan) covers **1,91,474 ckm** of lines and **1,274 GVA** of transformation at 220 kV+, with about **₹9.15–9.2 lakh cr** of transmission investment. Of that, about ₹4.25 lakh cr is for FY22–FY27 and ₹4.91 lakh cr for FY28–FY32. Installed 220 kV+ transformation should go from about 1,451 GVA (FY26) to **about 2,412 GVA by FY32** (about 9% CAGR). **765 kV substation capacity roughly triples.** [Shilchar AR FY26 MD&A citing CEA/NEP] [CEA/Outlook Sep-26]
- **New CEA transmission plan (released Mar 2026) for 2026-27 to 2035-36:** **1,37,500 ckm and 8,27,600 MVA** of substations at **₹7.93 lakh cr**, to connect **664 GW** of solar (509 GW) and wind (155 GW), plus 44.8 GWh of batteries. It includes 1,150 kV AC and 800 kV HVDC corridors. [PowerLine May-26] [Hitachi Energy press release Jun-26]
- **Industry order momentum:** outstanding orders and FY26 inflows for key transmission equipment makers have **more than doubled compared with FY22**. The IEEMA electrical equipment index rose about 14% YoY in H1 FY26, led by power transformers. [ICRA Jul-26] [Shilchar AR FY26 citing IEEMA]

## 2. Demand drivers
- **Transmission capex:** ICRA expects **₹5–6 trillion** of transmission capex between FY27 and FY32. That needs about **20,000 ckm of lines and 120 GVA of substation capacity every year** (Ankit Jain, VP, ICRA). About 107 GW of generation is due to connect to the inter-state grid over FY27–FY31. [ICRA Jul-26]
- **Renewable connections:** FY26 was a record year. Figures differ by source: company documents cite 43 GW (CEA, in Supreme's AR), about 55 GW (Shilchar's AR) and 65 GW (Shilchar's Q4 call). **Wind alone added a record 6.05 GW** (MNRE). By May 2026, **about 33% of the 54.8 GW of recently commissioned RE was running on temporary grid access, with 50–60% curtailment in solar hours.** Evacuation, not generation, is now the bottleneck, which is directly bullish for transformers. [ICRA Jul-26] [SolarQuarter Apr-26, MNRE] [Supreme AR FY26] [Shilchar AR FY26]
- **Distribution:** the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (about ₹3 lakh cr) keeps distribution-transformer demand steady. BEE star-labelling rules tightened from 1 Jan 2025 and push demand for better core steel. [Shilchar AR FY26] [Supreme AR FY26]
- **Data centres:** India's data-centre stock passed **about 1.7 GW in 2025** (CBRE), with about 500 MW more expected in 2026. **Wood Mackenzie (Jul 2026) sees 12 GW operational by 2030.** Data centres want 100–200 MVA units and dry-type/cast-resin transformers. Supreme and Shilchar are only now qualifying for this size. [Supreme AR FY26 citing CBRE and WoodMac] [Supreme Q1 call]
- **Replacement and higher voltage:** an ageing fleet and a shift to 400/765 kV and HVDC (converter transformers) sustain demand beyond new builds. [Shilchar AR FY26]

## 3. Export demand (US and Europe shortage)
- **US shortage (Wood Mackenzie):** in 2025 the supply deficit was **30% for power transformers and 6–10% for distribution transformers**. **In 2026 the active shortage is about 15% for power transformers and 8% for substations.** Power-transformer demand is **up about 116–119% since 2019**, and generation step-up units up 274%. **About 80% of US power-transformer supply and 50% of distribution supply is imported.** Prices have risen **+77% for power transformers and +78–95% for distribution transformers** since 2019. [WoodMac Aug-25] [pv magazine USA Sep-26 citing WoodMac]
- **US policy shock:** Executive Order 14420 (**26 Aug 2026**) restricts foreign-made bulk-power equipment (≥69 kV) from 24 sanctioned or embargoed countries. Wood Mackenzie says Chinese makers supplied the bulk of more than **$22 bn** of US bulk-power imports since 2025, so the order should widen the gap. That helps approved non-Chinese suppliers (India, Korea, Mexico), but only for **≥69 kV** products. Most small Indian exporters (Shilchar: ≤132 kV and mostly distribution/renewable units) sit partly below or at that line. [pv magazine USA Sep-26]
- **Lead times:** US lead times are 26–40 weeks for catalogue distribution units, 30–36 months for large power transformers, and up to 60 months for extra-high-voltage units. Indian domestic lead times are 11–15 months at 220 kV, about 15 months at 400 kV and 20–24 months at 765 kV. [WoodMac/DistroForge summary] [CEA/Outlook Sep-26]
- **Indian exporters' experience in 2026:** **CG Power's export bookings "more than doubled YoY"** (May 2026). Shilchar's exports rose **16% to ₹315 cr** in FY26 (48% of revenue). But the **West Asia war (from late Feb 2026) raised container freight 3–5x** and halted ex-works offtake in Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27. This is a logistics shock, not a demand collapse, but it shows export revenue is fragile. The US 25% tariff on Indian goods (Aug 2025) hit orders before it was cut to 10%. [Informist May-26, CG Power call] [CARE on Shilchar Jul-26] [Shilchar Q4/Q1 calls]
- Official FY26 export totals for Indian transformers (HS 8504) were **not verified**. Trade-portal figures found were stale or unsourced.

## 4. Policy
- **Quality control and BIS:** CRGO imports need BIS licences. **Delayed BIS licence renewals** for foreign mills have repeatedly squeezed supply. [GTRI via Business Standard/BW] [KNN]
- **Anti-dumping:** **DGTR opened an anti-dumping probe on CRGO imports from China, Japan, South Korea and Russia on 22 Jun 2026**, on a complaint by **JSW JFE Electrical Steel**. Duties would raise transformer costs because India imports about **90%** of its CRGO. [DiscoveryAlert Jun-26] [Whalesbook Jun-26]
- **Transmission bidding:** under Tariff-Based Competitive Bidding (TBCB), about ₹1.03 lakh cr of projects have been commissioned, with private developers supplying close to half the capital. But **only about 12% of TBCB projects were on time by Mar 2026** (median delay 10+ months), caused by right-of-way problems, land and equipment. [ICRA Jul-26] [Shilchar AR FY26]
- **Make in India / PLI:** supports domestic electronics, which helps EMS and meter makers such as Rishabh. [Rishabh Q4 call]

## 5. Cost drivers
- **CRGO steel** (grain-oriented electrical steel for transformer cores): **25–40% of transformer material cost**. Indian demand is about **400,000–450,000 t/yr** against about **40,000–50,000 t** of domestic capacity (JSW JFE Nashik), so about 90% is imported. About 90% of imports come from **China, Japan, South Korea and Russia**. JSW JFE plans to reach **350,000 t/yr in phases from FY28** (₹5,845 cr of capex). CRGO prices paid by small makers run **₹180–250/kg** by grade. [DiscoveryAlert Jun-26] [Machine Maker/JSW] [Supreme H2 call]
- **Copper:** LME copper hit a **record of about US$14,500–14,900/t in September 2026**, up about **47% year-to-date**, driven by US tariff speculation and mine supply. [Rio Times 13 Sep 2026] [Trading Economics/discoveryalert, Sep-26]
- **Transformer oil:** "almost doubled" between Feb and May 2026 after the West Asia war. Oil is 8–12% of transformer cost. Other commodities rose 10–25% in the same period. [Shilchar Q4 call]
- **Pass-through:** 80–85% of Supreme's orders and "much" of the industry's carry **price-variation clauses** (often IEEMA-index-linked). Fixed-price and short-delivery orders are exposed: **Shilchar recovered only 50–60%** of the Q1 FY27 cost jump on old orders, and Supreme lost 1–1.5 margin points in FY26. [Supreme Q1 call] [Shilchar Q1 call] [Shilchar AR FY26]
- **Labour:** skilled-labour shortage is the binding constraint on ramp-ups. Supreme's new plant is at 20–25% utilisation using freshers, and Yash names "supply chain and people" as its limits. [Supreme Q1 call] [Yash H2 call] [ICRA Jul-26]

## 6. Competitive structure and capacity additions
- **Structure:** a few large multinational and domestic makers dominate EHV (400/765 kV, HVDC): Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, GE Vernova T&D, CG Power, BHEL, T&R. Behind them is **a long tail of regional makers** in distribution and ≤220 kV. CARE calls the industry "highly fragmented". **The barrier in higher ratings is qualification (utility approvals and type tests), not capital.** [Supreme AR FY26] [CARE on Shilchar Jul-26]
- **Capacity boom:** industry capacity is estimated to grow from **about 375 GVA (FY25) to about 690 GVA by FY28**, per the sector report cited in Shilchar's AR (Apr 2026). Examples:
  - **CG Power:** 75,000 MVA going to about **110,000 MVA by end-2026**.
  - **Hitachi Energy:** a new ₹2,000 cr large-power-transformer plant in Karjan, Vadodara (FY28).
  - **Siemens Energy India:** ₹2,060 cr for +30,000 MVA, to about 60,000 MVA (FY30–FY32).
  - **Shilchar:** 7,500 to 14,000 MVA (Apr 2027).
  - **Supreme:** 2,500 to 9,000 MVA (Feb 2026).
  - [Shilchar AR FY26] [Informist May-26] [Hitachi Energy Jun-26] [Energetica/Mercom Feb-26] [Co. calls]
- **Bushings:** India imports about 95% of RIP capacitive cores. Yash, CG Power, Hitachi India, MIM and a Russian-owned unit are all localising. Hitachi Energy's new plant is reported to quadruple India's EHV dry-bushing capacity. So the scarcity premium in bushings could narrow over FY27–FY29. [Yash H2 call] [Hitachi Energy features, via search summary]

- **Read-across:** profit is highest at bottleneck components and at makers qualified for scarce ratings or export markets. It is thinnest in utility-tendered distribution transformers, where capacity is growing fastest. [synthesis]

## 8. Cycle position
- **High-voltage (400 kV+):** still in the **up-cycle**. Lead times are 15–24 months, CG says demand is "continuing to outstrip supply", and the FY27 CEA target is +40%. [CEA/Outlook Sep-26] [Informist May-26]
- **≤220 kV and distribution:** **mid-to-late cycle**. Capacity roughly doubles by FY28. Analysts on the Yash call flagged "overcapacity on the lower kV side (<400 kV)". Shilchar reports domestic pricing as "normal, no pressure" in Q1 FY27. There is no hard evidence of price cuts yet. [Yash H2 call] [Shilchar Q1 call]
- **Execution risk rising:** FY26 additions missed the CEA target by 10%. Apr–Jul FY27 is running behind the FY27 target. TBCB delays are the norm. Order books are fat, but conversion into revenue is slowing at the small caps (Shilchar Q1 −15%; Supreme below guidance). [CEA/Outlook Sep-26] [ICRA Jul-26] [Co. calls]
- **Cost cycle:** record copper, doubled transformer oil, and a possible CRGO anti-dumping duty are all squeezing fixed-price orders in H1 FY27. [Rio Times Sep-26] [Shilchar Q4 call] [DiscoveryAlert Jun-26]

- **My synthesis:** volume demand for FY27–FY29 is secure. The CEA's physical targets alone imply about 150–160 GVA a year of 220 kV+ additions against about 113 GVA delivered in FY26, before counting distribution, renewables and data centres. **The risk has moved from demand to supply and margins:**
  1. Capacity nearly doubles by FY28, mostly at ≤220 kV.
  2. CRGO duties and record copper raise costs for fixed-price makers.
  3. Execution and qualification (type tests, skilled labour) decide who captures growth.
  4. Export upside is real (the US gap and EO 14420), but freight and tariff shocks make it lumpy.
- **Expect** HV/EHV makers and bottleneck-component suppliers to keep margins through FY28. Distribution and ≤132 kV margins are likely to drift down from peak once new capacity is absorbed (FY28–FY29). [synthesis of the sources above]
