# Transformers & Rectifiers (India) (TARIL) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Figures come from Screener (consolidated page) and company filings. Price ₹278, market cap ₹8,334 cr, 52-week range ₹224–502. The stock is down about 44% over one year. Promoters hold 64.36%, down from 74.9% before the ₹500 cr share sale to institutions (QIP) in FY25 [Screener, 27 Sep 2026].

Watch the definitions: TARIL's "EBITDA" includes other income. The CFO confirmed this on the Q1 call. So the company's margins run about 2–3 points above Screener's operating margin [Q1 call].

## Business and segments
- **What it makes:** power transformers from 5 kV to 1,200 kV and up to 1,000 MVA, plus reactors, distribution, furnace, rectifier and special transformers [IP Q1FY27].
- **Order book by product (Q1 FY27):** power transformers and reactors 80%, distribution 10%, furnace 2%, rectifier 2%, special 1%, others 5% [IP Q1FY27].
- **Customers (FY26 order book):** EPC contractors 32%, state utilities 31%, central utilities 13%, industrial 8%, exports 9%, renewables 5% [India Ratings Jul-26]. Management says exports will stay at 10–15% of revenue [Q1 call].
- **Plants:** three sites around Ahmedabad.
  - Moraiya: 40,000 MVA, 57% used in Q1 FY27.
  - Changodar: 35,000 MVA, only 27% used while being expanded.
  - Odhav: 2,000 MVA, 100% used [IP Q1FY27].
  - India Ratings puts installed capacity at 40,200 MVA, rising to 77,200 MVA during FY27 once Changodar adds 15,000 MVA and Moraiya 22,000 MVA [India Ratings Jul-26]. The presentation's 75,000–77,000 MVA figure therefore **includes capacity not yet running**.
- **FY26 production:** a record 33,763 MVA [Q4 call].
- **Subsidiaries (7 in total):**
  - Transweld, Savas and TARIL Infrastructure: 100% owned.
  - Transpares: 51%. TARIL Switchgear: 60%.
  - Triveni Transtech: a 51% CRGO steel-processing unit bought in FY25.
  - A new UK subsidiary, Maxwell Grid Transformers (UK), set up in September 2026 [India Ratings Jul-26; AR FY26 note 51; BSE 10-Sep-26].

## Guidance history
- **FY26 revenue:** guided ₹3,500 cr at the start of the year, cut to ₹2,500–2,600 cr at Q2, and reiterated at about ₹2,600 cr at Q3. The actual was ₹2,507 cr [Q4 call]. **Missed twice.**
- **FY26 closing order book:** "very confidently" guided at ₹8,000 cr on the Q3 call; the actual was about ₹5,000 cr. Management explained the gap as "deliberately not taking orders" beyond 24 months [Q4 call]. **Missed.**
- **FY27 revenue:**
  - Q4 call: "around INR3,250 crores" and "35%, 40% growth". These two figures do not agree: ₹3,250 cr is +30% on FY26 [Q4 call].
  - Q1 call: "25% revenue growth", which means about ₹3,136 cr, with growth "from Q3 FY27 onwards" [Q1 call].
  - **Cut.**
- **Margins:**
  - Q4 call: 16.5–17% EBITDA [Q4 call].
  - Q1 call: 16% standalone EBITDA including other income, PAT margin 9–10%. Consolidated "20%, 21% or beyond" [Q1 call].
  - **Standalone guide trimmed.**
- **Changodar expansion (15,000 MVA, ₹150 cr):**
  - Originally due by end-FY26; then "from quarter 2", with 80% use by end-Q3 [Q4 call; India Ratings].
  - Q1 call: expansion finishes by August 2026 and stabilises in Q3. Only 60–65% use this year and 80–85% in FY28 [Q1 call].
  - **Delayed again, and the ramp-up target was cut.**
- **Moraiya expansion (22,000 MVA):** Q3 FY27, "on track" [Q1 call]. Moraiya itself ran at only 57% in Q1 FY27, against "about 75%" claimed on the Q4 call. On the Q1 call the CFO gave "60–65%" and "no major reason as such" for the gap [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Backward integration:** Q4 call said "impact from the starting of next financial year". Q1 call gave:
  - CTC (a special copper conductor): Q2 FY27
  - Pressboard: Q3 FY27
  - RIP bushings: Q4 FY27
  - Fabrication: Q1 FY28
  - The presentation says all facilities will be commissioned by Q1 FY28, "after considering delay of up to one quarter" [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- **Order discipline:** the Q4 call promised "no order beyond 24 months". In Q1 the company took the 30-month PGCIL order, described as "only an exception" [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- **Long-term target:** "$1 billion by FY28–29". This was redefined on the Q1 call as about ₹8,000 cr, because "rupee level was different" [Q1 call].

## Capex and capacity
- **Changodar:** about ₹150 cr. **Backward integration:** ₹900–1,000 cr [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]. The projects are:
  - CTC conductor: 8,000 tonnes a year in Phase I, 24,000 in Phase II
  - Pressboard: 5,000 then 10,000 tonnes a year
  - RIP bushings: 3,000 then 6,000 units a year
  - Fabrication: 25,000 then 50,000 tonnes a year
  - CRGO steel processing: already running
  - Aim: 80–85% of raw materials made in-house [Q1 call].
- **Funding:**
  - About ₹145 cr of QIP money is still unused.
  - About ₹500 cr of equipment will be taken on finance lease; the rest comes from internal cash and "debt if required" [Q1 call].
  - India Ratings expects lease repayments of about ₹105 cr in FY27 and ₹140 cr in FY28, with leverage (debt including leases) "elevated" for 2–3 years [India Ratings Jul-26].
  - Unfinished capital contracts rose to about ₹183 cr from ₹58 cr [AR FY26 note 45].
- **Payback claims:**
  - Backward integration should add 200–300 bp to margin from FY28. India Ratings cites 150–200 bp [Q1 call; India Ratings Jul-26].
  - Third-party sales of components could add ₹800–1,000 cr of revenue (60–65% of output used in-house) [Q1 call].
  - Existing plus expanded plants could support ₹5,000–6,000 cr of revenue [Q1 call].
- **HVDC:** the company is repairing an HVDC transformer for PowerGrid, the first Indian firm to do so. The repair needs 9 more months, then 6 months of satisfactory running before PowerGrid lists it as a supplier. Management says full HVDC manufacturing is 15–16 months away [Q1 call; Q4 call].

- **Working capital (FY26):**
  - Debtor days 129 (85 in FY25), inventory days 133, payable days 62 (109 in FY25).
  - Cash-conversion cycle 200 days (94 in FY25) [Screener].
  - Standalone receivables ₹1,057.71 cr, which is 42.3% of total assets; the auditor made this a key audit matter [AR FY26 auditor's report]. Standalone inventory ₹561 cr [Q1 call].

  - Target working capital: 120–130 days [Q1 call].

  - Consolidated debt ₹590 cr at FYE26 (₹400 cr at FYE25). Standalone debt about ₹424 cr. Screener borrowings ₹457 cr.
  - Vendor financing: ₹125 cr from banks plus ₹125 cr from others [India Ratings Jul-26; Q1 call; Screener; AR FY26 note 22].
  - Cash ₹295 cr at FYE26 (₹393 cr at FYE25).
  - Bank limits: 84% average use of fund-based and 82% of non-fund-based limits in the year to May 2026 [India Ratings Jul-26].
  - Net debt to EBITDA, including bills accepted: 0.8x. Interest coverage: 7.5x [India Ratings Jul-26].
- **Credit rating:** IND A+/Stable/A1+ affirmed in July 2026, with an extra ₹500 cr of bank limits rated [India Ratings Jul-26].
- **Dividend:** payout was 3% in FY25 and nil for FY26 per Screener [Screener].

## Moat evidence
- It is one of few Indian makers of 765 kV and 1,200 kV transformers, with a 20-year 765 kV track record in the US [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- **Technology partners:** ZTR (Ukraine) for 765 kV, Fuji Electric for 420 kV step-up transformers and reactors [India Ratings Jul-26].
- It completed dynamic short-circuit tests on 4 units in Q1 FY27. It has tested a 315 MVA, 400/220 kV unit at KEMA [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- **Evidence the moat is weak:**
  - An analyst noted that its **margins (15–16%) trail lower-voltage peers at 18–20%**. Management blamed old orders and said it now bids selectively [Q4 call].
  - All Indian tenders are decided on lowest price (L1) [Q1 call].
  - Growth is slower than peers (10% against peers' 50–60% in Q1) [Q1 call].
- **Pricing protection:** most orders carry price-variation clauses [India Ratings Jul-26; Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Promoter shares pledged for company loans.** Cash-credit limits are secured partly by a pledge of **4,22,00,000 shares held by a director**, plus 10,00,000 more for a Bandhan Bank loan, along with personal guarantees [AR FY26 notes 22, 27(a)]. That is about 14% of the company's 30.02 cr shares, or about 22% of the promoter stake.
- **Bank stock statements do not match the books.** Inventory, debtors and creditors in the quarterly statements differ from the accounts. Reasons given: stock with job-workers, goods in transit, "pending reconciliation entries", and overdue receivables left out [AR FY26 note 27(b)].
- **Cash burn:** negative operating cash flow while reporting ₹272 cr of profit. Receivables and inventory both sit at record levels [Screener].
- **Execution misses:** both FY26 guides were missed; the FY27 guide has been cut; Changodar has slipped about three quarters; Moraiya is under-used; and late-delivery penalties hit FY26 margins [Q4 call; Q1 call; India Ratings Jul-26].
- **Management churn:**
  - CEO Mukul Srivastav served only from July 2025 to January 2026.
  - CFO Chanchal Rajora left on 5 March 2026.
  - Satyen Mamtora (the promoter's son) became MD & CEO on 8 January 2026 [AR FY26 note 51].
  - An investor complained on the Q4 call that the former finance director's TV guidance did not match the calls [Q4 call].
- **World Bank (Nigeria project):** TARIL was listed as debarred and later removed. It filed its reply by the 12 January 2026 deadline. As of July 2026 there has been no further notice [India Ratings Jul-26; Q4 call].
- **Contingent liabilities:** about ₹19 cr of pending litigation. Tax and customs matters are listed separately in note 45; contingent liabilities are also a key audit matter [AR FY26 note 45; auditor's report].
- **Related parties:** promoter-controlled vendors Benchmark HR Solutions LLP, Skytrek Tours & Travels and Harsha Engineers International appear in the related-party list. TARIL also guarantees subsidiaries' bank loans [AR FY26 note 51; India Ratings Jul-26].
- **Chinese competition and CRGO:** a DGTR anti-dumping probe on CRGO steel imports is under way. Management would not comment [Q1 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- **Tone:** Q4 was defensive, answering investor criticism of missed guidance and inconsistent messaging. Q1 opened with a promise to give "only those guidance parameters that we are confident of achieving", and EY was hired to run investor relations [Q1 call].
- **Orders:** from a "selective, 24-month cap" to a record ₹2,114 cr quarter including a 30-month PGCIL order.
- **Numbers:** FY27 growth moved from 35–40% (₹3,250 cr) to 25%. Plant use moved from "75%, going to 95% this year" to Changodar 27%, Moraiya 57%, and 60–65% for the year.
- **New:** inventory stockpiling, a working capital cycle of 170 days, about ₹500 cr of finance leases, a UK subsidiary, and a hybrid HVDC design.

## My read
Demand and the order book are not the problem. At ₹6,630 cr the book is 2.6 times TTM sales. The problem is conversion and credibility:
- Revenue grew only 8–10% in Q1 while peers grew 50%.
- Two guidance cuts in a year, and plant timelines keep slipping.
- Operating cash flow turned negative (−₹105 cr) as receivables and inventory ballooned.
- Promoter shares are pledged to support company borrowing.
- Margins trail smaller peers.

After a roughly 44% fall, the price needs about 19% earnings growth a year for a decade (the note's formula). That is achievable if Changodar and Moraiya actually ramp up in H2 FY27. Watch three things: Q2/Q3 revenue above ₹750 cr a quarter, positive operating cash flow in H1 FY27, and the CTC plant starting in Q2 as promised. Another miss on any of these would confirm that execution, not demand, is the constraint.
