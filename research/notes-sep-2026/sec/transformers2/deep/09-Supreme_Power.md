# Supreme Power Equipment (NSE SME: SUPREMEPWR) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

This is an NSE Emerge (SME) stock that voluntarily reports quarterly figures and plans to move to the main board after Dec 2026. The **consolidated** numbers include Danya Electric Company, a partnership in which Supreme holds 90% and the two promoters hold the rest. Standalone revenue is *higher* than consolidated because of sales to Danya, so consolidated is the correct base. Sources read: Screener consolidated and standalone pages (raw rows), official Q1 FY27 transcript (NSE, 21 Aug 2026), official H2 FY26 transcript (NSE, 5 Jun 2026), Q1 FY27 investor presentation (NSE), Annual Report FY26 (NSE; MD&A, auditor's report + CARO, contingent liabilities, AGM notice RPT disclosures), CRISIL rationale of 20 Mar 2026, and the 21 Sep 2026 investor-meet outcome.

## Business and segments
- It makes power, distribution, inverter-duty (solar), windmill, generator, converter and isolation transformers. There is one reportable segment. Plants: **Thirumazhisai** (old; up to 25 MVA / 132 kV) and **Kannur, Tamil Nadu** (new, about ₹100 cr, commercial production from Feb 2026; up to 200 MVA / 220 kV). Combined capacity went from **2,500 MVA to about 9,000 MVA**. [AR FY26 MD&A p.31–33] [Q1 call]
- **Customers:** state utilities (TANGEDCO/TNPDCL, KSEB, KPTCL), EPC contractors (Karnataka, Telangana), renewable developers, and a Navratna PSU (₹60.9 cr inverter-duty order). The geography is southern India, expanding into Maharashtra and Delhi. Exports are "not material". [AR FY26 p.31] [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Order book ₹590.06 cr (13 Aug 2026)**, about 3.2x FY26 revenue. By product: power transformers ₹453.8 cr (76.9%), distribution ₹105.3 cr (17.8%), inverter-duty ₹31.0 cr (5.3%). By customer: government ₹177.7 cr (30.1%), non-government ₹412.5 cr (69.9%). Q1 inflow: 10 orders worth ₹195.6 cr, including a **112.5 MVA / 330 kV** (₹13.5 cr) and a **160 MVA / 220 kV** (₹23.3 cr) unit for Hyderabad customers. Both are larger than anything it has type-tested. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- **Utilisation:** the old plant was 70–78% used (FY26). Kannur is at 20–25% (Q1 FY27), with 30–50% targeted by Q4 FY27 and about 90% in 2–3 years. The new plant contributed only about ₹20–25 cr of FY26 revenue. [H2 call] [Q1 call]

- **Quarterly (consolidated):** Jun-25 35.1, Sep-25 40.1, Dec-25 35.8, **Mar-26 ≈ 70.6–71** (derived: FY26 minus first nine months), Jun-26 48.2. OPM: 18.9%, 18.7%, 14.2%, about 17%, 18.3%. [Screener]
- **Q1 FY27 (company, consolidated):** total income ₹48.31 cr (+37.3%), EBITDA ₹8.89 cr (18.4%), PAT ₹4.90 cr (+10.0%, margin 10.1%), EPS ₹1.96. Raw materials were ₹33.0 cr. Employee cost went from ₹0.81 cr to ₹1.92 cr, other expenses from ₹1.47 cr to ₹4.49 cr, finance cost from ₹0.41 cr to ₹1.30 cr, and depreciation from ₹0.17 cr to ₹0.80 cr. PAT grew only 10% on 37% revenue growth because Kannur's fixed costs arrived ahead of its output. [IP Q1FY27] [Screener]

- **Unit economics:** tanks are 5–8% of turnover (6–7% of transformer cost). CRGO steel costs ₹180–250 per kg. Raw materials are about 70% of cost (CRISIL). A prototype 160 MVA unit costs ₹14–16 cr to build. [H2 call] [CRISIL Mar-26]
- ROCE 22% (FY25 28%, FY24 36%) and ROE 19%. Returns are falling as ₹100 cr of new assets sits under-used. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- **FY26 track record (admitted by the MD on the Q1 call):** "Initially I committed to 225 crores … later … 200 crores. However, I ended at only 190 crores." Consolidated revenue came in at ₹182 cr, so **the target was missed twice**. [Q1 call]
- **H2 FY26 call (2 Jun 2026):** FY27 revenue of **₹275–300 cr**, with the MD also saying "₹250–300 cr" and "₹270–300 cr" on the same call. FY28 about +₹100 cr. PAT margin 10–12% and operating margin "same 15–16%". Q1 FY27 revenue ₹50–60 cr. Tank factory (₹22–25 cr) to be "completed in this financial year". 160 MVA CPRI type test in "December or January". [H2 call]
- **Q1 delivery:** revenue ₹48.3 cr, **below the ₹50–60 cr promised**. The EBITDA margin of 18.4% is fine. The tank factory is still waiting on 6–7 departmental approvals, with completion now "before March". [Q1 call]
- **Q1 FY27 call (17 Aug 2026):** FY27 guidance of **₹250–300 cr**, which drops the ₹275 cr lower end (though ₹250 cr had already been said in June). PAT 10–12% ("may be 1% higher or lower"; elsewhere "9–12%"). EBITDA 18–20%. FY28 "minimum 30%" growth (about ₹400 cr). Peak revenue from both plants of ₹600–650 cr (the H2 call said ₹500–550 cr), with FY29 revenue of "₹550–600 cr". Order book to stay at ₹500–600 cr. Order inflow of ₹300–500 cr expected in FY27. [Q1 call]
- **Arithmetic:** ₹250–300 cr needs ₹202–252 cr in the remaining nine months, or ₹67–84 cr a quarter, against a best-ever quarter of about ₹71 cr (Mar-26). Management says ₹377 cr is due for delivery before March but caps guidance for customer-site delays. **This is achievable only if Kannur ramps fast; the lower half of the range looks more likely.** [Screener] [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- **Kannur plant:** about ₹100 cr, capitalised in FY26. Fixed assets went from ₹15 cr to ₹92 cr and CWIP of ₹28 cr remains. It is funded by IPO money (Dec 2023), bank term loans and accruals. It sits on 6 acres, which are fully built, so no further expansion is possible there. [AR FY26 p.32] [Screener] [Q1 call]
- **Tank factory:** ₹20–22 cr (earlier ₹22–25 cr) on a 1-acre site bought about a year ago, funded by **term loans**. Status: awaiting approvals, targeted before Mar-27, and the H2 call already put it at "next financial year". [Q1 call] [H2 call]
- **Next phase:** management wants 300–500 MVA / 330 kV-plus capability on new land, but no land has been bought and there is no plan yet. 765 kV is "a vision", 2–3 years out at least. [Q1 call] [H2 call]
- **Qualification path:** a 160 MVA / 220 kV prototype must pass the CPRI type test (targeted Dec 2026–Jan 2027) before it can supply data centres and larger utility orders. KPTCL has placed two 165 MVA units. [H2 call] [Q1 call]
- **Asset turns:** peak revenue of ₹600–650 cr on about ₹130 cr of gross block is about 4.5–5x at full use. At the FY27 guide it is only about 2x. [Q1 call] [Screener]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- **Consolidated borrowings of ₹50 cr at Mar-26** (₹19 cr in FY25, ₹9 cr in FY24). Debt/equity went from 0.20 to 0.42 and debt-service coverage fell from 8.6x to 2.5x. The AR says this is because of repayments that rose about 287%. [Screener] [AR FY26 ratio table]
- **Cash flow:** FY26 operating cash flow ₹26 cr against capex of about ₹57 cr, giving free cash flow of −₹32 cr (FY24 −₹31 cr, FY25 about 0). [Screener]
- **Working capital (consolidated FY26):** debtor days 94 (FY24: 211), **inventory days 167** (FY25: 103), payable days 168, cash conversion cycle 94 days. Receivables improved as the share of private EPC customers rose, but inventory is building for the larger order book. Management needs 2–2.5 months of inventory and 80–100 debtor days, and has sought higher bank limits. [Screener] [Q1 call] [AR FY26]
- **Equity:** "no requirement for equity dilution this year", but "we may require it in FY27–28". There is no dividend. [Q1 call] [Screener]
- **Rating:** CRISIL upgraded it to **BBB/Stable, A3+** on 20 Mar 2026 (from BBB−/A3), based on a ₹300 cr+ order book, about 17% margin and 0.2x gearing. Downgrade triggers: margin at 12–13%, a stretched working-capital cycle, or **large debt-funded capex**. CRISIL noted escalation clauses covered only about 50% of the order book in Feb 2026; management now says 80–85%. [CRISIL Mar-26] [Q1 call]

## Moat evidence
- **Weak to moderate.** It is a regional supplier in a fragmented market, with state utility empanelments (TANGEDCO, KSEB, KPTCL, TNPDCL) and about 880 clients. It claims "no power transformer … has recorded a field failure to date". [AR FY26 p.31] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Qualification is the barrier**, as the AR itself says ("not capital but qualification"). Supreme does not yet hold type-test credentials above about 50–70 MVA. [AR FY26 MD&A]
- **Pricing:** 80–85% of orders now carry price-variation clauses. Short-delivery (<3–4 month) orders are fixed-price with back-to-back buying. FY26 margin still fell 1–1.5 points on oil and copper spikes. CRISIL notes that tender-based bidding means "aggressive bidding, mostly compromising on the operating margin". [H2 call] [Q1 call] [CRISIL Mar-26]
- **Lead-time edge:** power transformers of 25 MVA take 6–8 weeks and 50–100 MVA take 3–4 months, against 2+ years at the large multinationals for 300–500 MVA units. [H2 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Related party, Danya Electric Company:** a transformer partnership in which Supreme holds 90% and **MD Vee Rajmohan holds 7.5% and director K V Pradeep 2.5%**. FY26 transactions: purchases ₹25.6 cr and sales ₹24.6 cr (on a Danya turnover of ₹41.7 cr and PAT of ₹2.35 cr). Supreme has given a ₹14.7 cr **corporate guarantee** to IndusInd Bank for Danya's loans. The FY27 AGM sought approval for up to **₹125 cr** of dealings, including loans and guarantees up to ₹25 cr, which is **299% of Danya's turnover**. Danya is also listed as a source of **customer orders** in the FY26 order-inflow disclosure. The promoters take a cut of a firm that trades heavily with the listed company. [AR FY26 AGM notice items 6 and 9; note on contingent liabilities; MD&A p.32]
- **Guidance credibility:** FY26 was guided at ₹225 cr, then ₹200 cr, and delivered ₹190 cr standalone / ₹182 cr consolidated. The Q1 FY27 target of ₹50–60 cr was missed. The same call gives several different ranges. [Q1 call] [H2 call]
- **Execution:** the new plant is staffed by 250+ mostly fresher workers (only 20–25% moved from Unit 1). It has sub-30% utilisation, and higher-rating orders (160 MVA, 330 kV) need type tests it has not yet passed. [Q1 call]
- **Leverage creep:** debt/equity has doubled, the tank capex is loan-funded, and equity may be raised in FY28. This is against CRISIL's "large, debt-funded capex" downgrade trigger. [AR FY26] [CRISIL Mar-26]
- **Auditor:** unmodified opinion. **Key audit matters: revenue recognition** (cut-off, price-variation claims, liquidated damages) **and existence/valuation of inventories**. The accounts are under Indian GAAP (AS), not Ind AS. CARO: no disputed statutory dues and no defaults. Quarterly stock and debtor statements to banks differed from the books by 4–5% on debtors every quarter, explained as "provisional books". [AR FY26 auditor's report p.106; CARO vii; bank-statement reconciliation note]
- **Contingent liabilities:** only the ₹14.7 cr Danya guarantee (no tax demands). No capital commitments were recorded at Mar-26, even though the tank project has been announced. [AR FY26 contingent-liabilities note]
- **Concentration:** southern states, a few large EPC clients, and 30% government exposure (slow payers). [AR FY26 MD&A]
- **Shareholding:** promoters hold 57.16% (no pledge disclosed). DIIs fell from 11.3% (Dec-23) to 0.04%. There are 4,785 shareholders, so the stock is thinly traded. [Screener]

## What changed between the two calls
- **Guidance:** ₹275–300 cr became ₹250–300 cr (₹250 cr had already appeared once in June). Peak potential went from ₹500–550 cr to ₹600–650 cr. The EBITDA guide was restated as 18–20% (June: "15–16% same margins"; consolidated FY26 was 18.3%).
- **Order book:** stable at ₹588 cr → ₹590 cr despite about ₹48 cr executed. It is more power-transformer heavy (73% → 77%) and less government (33.6% → 30.1%).
- **New:** the customer-delay explanation (₹377 cr due, ₹250–300 cr guided); main-board migration after Dec-26; entry into Maharashtra; 330 kV and 160 MVA orders; a possible FY28 equity raise.
- **Slipped:** the tank-factory timing and the Q1 revenue.

## Stored-data errors
- **"Q4 FY26" figures (rev ₹106.75 cr, EBITDA ₹19.01 cr, PAT ₹11.03 cr) are H2 FY26 (six months).** Q4 alone was about ₹70.6–71 cr of consolidated revenue.
- **"yoy=21.78" stored for "Q4 FY26"** is the full-year FY26 growth. H2 growth was 41.66% (half on half).
- **Q3 FY26 EBITDA stored as 0 / 0.0% margin.** Screener consolidated OP for Dec-25 is ₹5.09 cr (14.2%). Stored revenue of ₹36.03 cr vs Screener's ₹35.83 cr (total income vs revenue from operations).
- **Q3 FY26 "ob=300"** looks like CRISIL's approximate Feb-26 figure, not a disclosed quarter-end order book.
- **The stored analysis says employee costs "jumped roughly 4x YoY".** Company consolidated Q1 employee cost went from ₹0.81 cr to ₹1.92 cr, **about 2.4x**. The 4x came from an analyst's question.
- **The stored analysis says "₹165 MVA prototype testing"**, which garbles the MVA rating. The prototype is **160 MVA / 220 kV**; KPTCL's order is for 165 MVA units.
- **The stored analysis says "the FY27 target was lowered from ₹275–300 cr"**. That is only partly true: ₹250–300 cr was already stated on the June call.
- **The stored profile says the new plant is "5–7x larger" and "went live in Q4 FY26"**. Commercial production started in Feb 2026, and capacity rose 3.6x (2,500 → 9,000 MVA).
- Stored market data (mcap ₹526 cr, P/E 25.2, ROCE 21.5%, TTM ₹169.51 cr) is close to Screener. Consolidated TTM sales to Jun-26 are about ₹195 cr (Sep-25 40.1 + Dec-25 35.8 + Mar-26 ≈71 + Jun-26 48.2), so **the stored TTM of ₹169.5 cr does not match Screener**.

## My read
Supreme has made a big bet, taking capacity 3.6x with a 220 kV plant, and has an order book (₹590 cr, 77% power transformers) that on paper fills FY27–FY28. But the history is one of missed revenue targets (₹225 cr became ₹200 cr and then ₹182 cr delivered, and Q1 came in below guidance). Utilisation of the new plant is still in the 20s. Debt, inventory and a promoter-linked partnership (Danya) are all growing. The price needs about ₹250 cr+ in FY27 with a steady 18% EBITDA margin and a successful CPRI test on the 160 MVA prototype. Without the test, the larger-rating orders and data-centre hopes stay out of reach. I would upgrade my view on a quarterly run-rate above ₹75 cr with stable margins and a passed type test. I would downgrade it on another guidance cut, a further rise in Danya dealings, or a dilutive equity raise.
