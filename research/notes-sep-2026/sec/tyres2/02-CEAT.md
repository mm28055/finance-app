## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# CEAT

## Executive Summary
CEAT is a leading Indian tyre manufacturer and the flagship company of the RPG Group, primarily existing to provide mobility solutions across the two-wheeler (2W), passenger car (PCR), and agricultural sectors. The company solves the critical problem of traction and durability for a price-sensitive yet aspirational demographic in India, while increasingly serving as a specialized off-highway tyre (OHT) supplier to global markets.

CEAT is definitively a **Category Leader in the 2W and EV segments** (~25% share in India’s passenger EV and 2W EV OEM markets), while operating as a **strong Challenger** in the Truck & Bus Radial (TBR) and premium PCR space. It has successfully decoupled itself from being a pure-play commodity rubber processor by pivoting toward premiumization (17-inch+ tyre volumes growing 100% YoY in replacement) and "green" mobility. As of mid-2026, the company is delivering robust quarterly revenues exceeding ₹4,300 Cr (+22.3% YoY in Q1 FY27), though profitability is currently facing a severe stress test from commodity hyperinflation. The investment thesis hinges on management's ability to successfully pass on aggressive double-digit price hikes without destroying demand, while navigating peak raw material costs, adverse forex movements, and the integration of the Camso acquisition.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
*   **Product Mix:** CEAT sells a diversified portfolio of tyres, ranging from high-volume 2W/3W tyres to complex, high-margin Off-Highway Tyres (OHT) and radial tyres for commercial vehicles. A critical emerging revenue stream is EV-specific tyres, where CEAT has secured early dominance.
*   **Customer Base:** The company is paid by a mix of Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) like Maruti Suzuki and Hero MotoCorp (long-term volume contracts), and a fragmented network of dealers and distributors in the Replacement market (spot pricing with incentives).
*   **Monetization:** Revenue is derived primarily from unit sales. Margins are a function of raw material pass-through capabilities (easier in Replacement, lagged in OEM) and operating leverage. The Camso acquisition introduces a B2B export component where CEAT acts as a strategic manufacturing partner for Michelin during the transition phase, before shifting to direct customer billing.

### Segment & Geographic Mix
Based on Q1 FY27 performance, the business reflects a heavy domestic skew with a growing, highly resilient international arm.

| Segment | Trend | Commentary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Domestic Replacement** | **Growing (Mid-Teens)** | The cash cow (~50% mix). Absorbed cumulative 7-11% price hikes smoothly so far. Strong volume execution in 2W and farm. |
| **Domestic OEM** | **Growing (Low-Teens)** | Strong uptake in premium PVs (17-inch+) and farm tyres. ~25% EV market share acts as a strategic differentiator. |
| **International** | **Surging (~30%)** | Comprises ~20% of standalone revenue (23% incl. Camso). Shook off Middle East disruptions via robust demand in EU/US OHT and PCR. |
| **Specialty (OHT)** | **High Growth** | "China+1" beneficiary. Strong execution and new SKU additions despite ongoing 50% US import duties. |

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
*   **Moat Status: Durable but Cyclical.**
*   **Brand & Distribution:** CEAT possesses a deep, defensible moat in the Indian replacement market via its distribution network and brand equity, evidenced by its rare ability to push through 11% cumulative price hikes in a single quarter without immediately cratering volumes. 
*   **Technological Lead (EVs):** The ~25% market share in Passenger and 2W EV OEMs indicates a genuine R&D edge, creating high switching costs for OEMs who require low rolling resistance and noise reduction.
*   **The Bear Case for Moat:** The "moat" is highly vulnerable to raw material shocks, breaking near-term operating leverage. With domestic rubber prices hitting 15-year highs of ₹280/kg and the Rupee depreciating to ~96.50 vs USD, the cost advantage erodes rapidly. Furthermore, the US tariff wall (29% combined on on-road tyres) proves that the export moat is subject to geopolitical regulatory capture.

## Growth Vectors
*   **Camso Integration & OHT Expansion:** The next leg of significant value creation ($120M+ annualized) relies on fully integrating Camso’s localized assets. Currently, the business is EBITDA negative due to heavy unabsorbed European infrastructure and warehousing costs. However, CEAT expects 90% of the customer interface under direct control by September 2026, targeting full value chain control by FY28 to capture the margin currently surrendered to Michelin.
*   **Domestic Capacity Scale-up:** Demonstrating long-term demand confidence despite near-term margin pressure, the Board approved a new ₹1,205 Cr capex plan for 53,000 unit/day 2W tyre capacity (staggered to FY31). This is in addition to the maintained ₹1,300-1,400 Cr FY27 base capex for passenger vehicle capacity expansions.
*   **Digital & AI Efficiency:** Management is deploying an enterprise data lake, "Agentic AI", and SAP S/4HANA to drive efficiencies. Execution is matching the rhetoric, with premium tyre sales via digital leads growing 2x YoY in Q1 FY27.

## Key Risks & Governance
*   **Forensic & Financial Risks:**
    *   **Currency Exposure & LKR Debt:** A severe vulnerability materialized in Q1 FY27 as Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR) depreciation against an $80M parent loan triggered a ₹48 Cr non-cash forex hit, wiping out consolidated profits. The Board is restructuring $24.5M of this into equity to stem further bleeding.
    *   **Raw Material Hyperinflation:** The 16-18% QoQ raw material cost spike completely overwhelmed pricing power, crashing consolidated EBITDA margins by 562 bps to 8.6%. With another 8-10% cost pressure expected in Q2, the balance sheet is straining, reflected by consolidated debt rising ₹232 Cr (Debt/EBITDA at 1.6x) due to physical inventory hoarding.
*   **Business Risks:**
    *   **Demand Destruction via Price Hikes:** Gross margins collapsed 575 bps to 33.9%. Bridging this gap requires management to push another 4-6% in price hikes (on top of the 11% already taken). Consumers may reject a cumulative 15%+ price hike, leading to severe volume degrowth.
    *   **Integration Drag:** The Camso transition is currently a financial drag, pulling consolidated profits below standalone levels. Prolonged under-utilization of new EU/UK warehousing will dilute ROIC.

## Investment Thesis

### The Bull Case
CEAT is experiencing robust structural top-line growth (>20% YoY) that validates its premiumization and market share strategies. The company is not merely riding a cycle but structurally upgrading its mix, evidenced by 100% YoY growth in 17-inch+ passenger vehicle tyres in the replacement market and sustained ~25% EV OEM dominance. If management successfully threads the needle—meaning price hikes are absorbed by consumers, rubber prices revert to the mean by H2 FY27, and Camso's customer transition unlocks hidden margins—CEAT will emerge with restored 40% gross margins and a vastly superior, premiumized product mix. 

### The Bear Case
Near-term operating leverage is fundamentally broken, and the margin expansion story has violently reversed. With natural rubber structurally settling at 15-year highs (₹280/kg) and the Rupee severely weakened (96.50 vs USD), the massive commodity headwind has wiped out bottom-line profitability (Consolidated PAT crashed to just ₹4 Cr in Q1 FY27). Aggressive base capex (₹1,400 Cr) combined with new 2W capacity approvals (₹1,200 Cr) will heavily spike depreciation and interest burdens just as margins are collapsing. If consecutive double-digit price hikes trigger consumer demand destruction, or if Camso remains EBITDA negative, the stock faces severe de-rating over the medium term.
