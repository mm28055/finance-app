# Indian tyres — sector research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

## Market size and structure
- Industry turnover about ₹1 lakh cr (manufacturing basis, ATMA); CRISIL puts FY26 revenue of the sector at ~₹1.36 lakh cr with the top six makers ~85% (different basis — includes overseas subsidiaries). [ATMA via NewsDrum, 3-Jun-26; CRISIL via Autocar Pro, 24-Aug-26]
- Volume mix: aftermarket (replacement) ~half of industry volumes, OEM and exports about a quarter each. [CRISIL, 24-Aug-26]
- Imports are small: under 5% of demand; import volumes fell 10% in FY25 and 5% in 10M FY26. [ICRA Mar-26]
- Exports: record ₹27,312 cr in FY26 (+9% from ₹25,057 cr); export-to-turnover 26–27%. Top markets: US 15% (₹4,082 cr, down from 17%), Germany 7%, Italy 5%, Brazil 5%, France 4%. West Asia takes ~8–10% of exports (~US$250–260 mn). [ATMA via NewsDrum, 3-Jun-26; ICRA Mar-26; The Core, 1-Jul-26]
- FY26 profit pool, six listed makers (Screener, consolidated; ₹ cr):

- Q1 FY27 margins (Screener): MRF 12%, Apollo 12%, CEAT 8.5%, JK 6.5%, BKT 22%, TVS Srichakra 8% — the replacement-heavy leaders (MRF, Apollo) and the export niche (BKT) held up best. [Screener]
- Share: MRF is the largest by revenue; Apollo claims >30% of truck-radial replacement and 21%+ of car-tyre replacement (internal estimates); CARE calls JK the TBR market leader (overlapping claims — no official industry share data is published). [Apollo Q1 call; CARE on JK, Aug-26]

## Demand drivers
- **GST cut (22 Sep 2025):** tyres 28% → 18% for most categories, farm tyres 18% → 5%; vehicle GST cuts too. This drove H2 FY26 and Q1 FY27 volume: Q1 FY27 industry PV +23%, CV +14%, 2/3W +14%, tractors +22% (FADA retail >15%). [CEAT Q4 call; JK Q1 call]
- **Replacement**: steady 5–7% growth expected (ICRA); replacement is the profit engine (higher prices than OEM, faster pass-through). Two-wheeler replacement is growing fastest. [ICRA Mar-26]
- **OEM**: slowing to 4–6% in FY27 on a high base (ICRA); OEM prices are indexed to raw materials with a one-quarter lag. [ICRA Mar-26; company calls]
- **Truck/bus radialisation**: TBR share of truck tyres rose from 48% (FY19) to over 55% (FY24); >70% of truck tyres are retreaded at least once (dampens replacement volumes). [ICRA via Tyre Trends, Jan-25 — older than 12 months]
- **Premiumisation / EVs**: 17-inch+ car tyres, 250cc+ bike tyres and EV fitments carry higher prices; EV tyres wear 5–10% faster (JK). [CEAT Q1 call; JK Q1 call]

## Policy
- **India import protection**: anti-dumping duty on Chinese truck tyres ran to Dec 2022; countervailing (anti-subsidy) duty since 2019; since June 2020 many new tyres need a DGFT import licence. CARE flags rising low-cost imports from Thailand as a risk to pricing discipline. [CARE on JK, Aug-26; CARE on CEAT, Jan-26]
- **EU anti-dumping on Chinese car/van tyres**: definitive duties 4.3%–45.3% from 8 Jul 2026 for five years; China held 28% of a 330 mn-unit, €18 bn EU market in 2024 (93 mn units). Anti-subsidy probe due Dec 2026. Helps Indian exporters (Apollo brand, CEAT, JK) at the lower end. [European Commission, 9-Jul-26; Apollo Q1 call]
- **US tariffs**: raised to 50% in Aug 2025, cut to 18% by Feb 2026 on the reciprocal part (ATMA); on-road tyres still face Section 232 auto-parts tariff of 25% plus ~4% base (29% total), off-highway tyres 10%. [NewsDrum/ATMA, 3-Jun-26; CEAT Q1 call]
- India–EU/UK FTAs signed; Apollo says benefit "not material". [Apollo Q1 call; JK Q1 call]
- **Kerala rubber floor price** raised to ₹250/kg from ₹200 (per analyst on CEAT call). [CEAT Q1 call]
- **CCI 2018 cartel order** (2011–12 truck-tyre pricing): penalties included Apollo ₹425.5 cr, JK ₹310 cr, CEAT ₹252 cr (MRF also penalised; amount not checked); NCLAT remanded; CCI appeal pending in the Supreme Court. [company ARs FY26]

## Cost drivers
- Raw materials are 60–65% of cost; natural rubber ~half of the basket. [CARE on JK; CRISIL, 24-Aug-26]
- **Natural rubber**: India produced 8.75 lakh t and consumed 14.1 lakh t in FY25; imports 5.51 lakh t (Côte d'Ivoire 26%, Indonesia 22%, Vietnam 21%); tyres use 67% of NR. FY25 average RSS4 ₹200/kg. Prices rose to ~₹275/kg in June 2026 (from ~₹220 FY26 average) and ~₹280/kg in July (a 15-year high; domestic at ₹15–20 premium to imports); Apollo expects Q2 average >₹260 and cooling from Q3 as monsoon supply returns. [Rubber Board May-25; CRISIL, 24-Aug-26; CEAT Q1 call; Apollo Q1 call]
- **Crude-linked inputs** (synthetic rubber, carbon black, nylon cord): Brent went above US$100 in March–May 2026 on the West Asia war, later back to ~US$80–85; Apollo's Q1 costs: synthetic rubber ₹250/kg (Q4 ₹170), carbon black ₹125 (₹110), steel cord ₹165 (₹155). Crude costs flow through with at least a quarter's lag. [Apollo Q1 and Q4 calls]
- **Rupee** weakened from ~₹88–90 to ~₹96.5/US$; ~40–50% of raw material is imported and domestic rubber prices track import parity. [CEAT Q1 call; Apollo Q1 call; CARE on JK]
- Net: key inputs up 35–40% (CRISIL); Q1 FY27 basket +16–20% QoQ with a further +8–10% in Q2 (company calls). Industry needs ~15–16% price increases; ~11% taken by August. [CRISIL; CEAT, Apollo, JK Q1 calls]
- Resorcinol (a tyre chemical) is under a new Indian anti-dumping probe (June 2026) — a small cost-up risk. [News: The News Minute, Jun-26, search result only]

## Competitive structure and where profit sits
- Oligopoly of five national players (MRF, Apollo, CEAT, JK, plus BKT in export OHT) with smaller TVS Srichakra, Goodyear India, Bridgestone and Michelin. Price moves are followed "in similar magnitude" (Apollo), which is why the CCI case exists.
- Profit sits in replacement, two-wheeler and premium car tyres, and OHT exports (BKT 21–24% margins). Truck tyres are the most price-sensitive; OEM supply is low-margin but seeds future replacement demand.
- Balance sheets: MRF borrowings ₹3,207 cr on ₹31,149 cr sales (near net cash); Apollo net debt ~₹1,700 cr (0.4x EBITDA); CEAT debt ₹3,243 cr (1.6x); JK net debt ₹4,945 cr (2.6x). [Screener; company calls]

## Capex cycle
- CRISIL: top six will invest ~₹18,000 cr over FY27–FY28, nearly twice the previous two years. ICRA: capex 7–10% of revenue in FY27. [CRISIL via Upstox, 24-Aug-26; ICRA Mar-26]
- Announced projects: Apollo ₹5,810 cr Andhra Pradesh (+3.7 mn PCR, +1.3 mn TBR a year by FY29) plus Hungary; JK ₹6,110 cr (₹1,130 cr + ₹4,980 cr, +~25% TBR/PCR by FY30); CEAT ₹1,300–1,400 cr FY27 plus ₹1,205 cr two-wheeler plant to FY31; MRF's plans not verified from its own filings. [company calls; Tyrepress Feb-26]
- Utilisation is high (India: Apollo 91%, JK ~95%, CEAT 85–90%), so the capex is demand-led — but it lands in FY28–FY30, when demand growth is forecast to be only mid-single digit. This is the classic set-up for a later price war in truck and car radials.

## Cycle position
- FY24 was the margin peak (cheap rubber; Apollo 18%, MRF 17%). FY25 fell, FY26 recovered to ~14.2% (CRISIL). FY27 is a cost-squeeze year: margins ~11.5–12% (CRISIL), 10–11% for Apollo and JK (CRISIL/CARE). Tyre makers historically earn their best margins when rubber falls and they hold prices — management at all three said they intend to hold prices if costs fall. [CRISIL; CARE; Apollo, CEAT Q1 calls]

## Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecaster named)
- **ICRA (Mar 2026)**: FY27 domestic volume +5–7% (replacement 5–7%, OEM 4–6%); revenue of seven major makers +6–8%; margins to contract in FY27; exports under pressure.
- **CRISIL Ratings (24 Aug 2026, Anuj Sethi)**: FY27 volume +4–5% (aftermarket 4–5%, OEM 4–5%, exports 3–4%) after 7–8% in FY26; operating margin ~11.5–12% in FY27 from 14.2%; recovery to 13–13.5% in FY28; capex ~₹18,000 cr over FY27–FY28.
- **CRISIL Intelligence (Hemal Thakkar, Jul 2026)**: FY27 growth "higher single digits". [The Core, 1-Jul-26]
- **CARE (Aug 2026, on JK)**: industry margins under pressure in Q2 FY27, recovery from Q3 FY27.
- **FY29**: no agency forecast found; my assumption — volumes 5–7% a year (replacement-led), margins 12–14% if rubber normalises, with the risk of capacity-driven price cuts as FY28–FY30 additions arrive.
