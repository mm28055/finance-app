# Apollo Tyres — deep research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Price ₹400, market cap ₹25,410 cr, trailing P/E 11.9, book value ₹263, dividend yield 1.5%, ROCE 13.9% [Screener, 27-Sep-26].

## Business and segments
- Makes truck/bus, passenger car, two-wheeler, farm and off-highway tyres under **Apollo** (India, mass/mid) and **Vredestein** (Europe, premium PCR, all-season, agri). Also owns **Reifencom**, a German online tyre distributor. [AR FY26 segment note; Q1 call]
- Reporting segments are geographic: APMEA (India plus UAE/Thailand/Malaysia/South Africa), Europe, Others (Americas, corporate). FY26 revenue: APMEA ₹20,206 cr (FY25 ₹18,541 cr), Europe ₹8,856 cr (FY25 ₹7,907 cr), Others ₹2,907 cr, before eliminations; consolidated ₹28,471 cr. [AR FY26 note 21; Screener]
- Q1 FY27 revenue split by region: India 69%, Europe 26% (incl. Reifencom), others 5%. [IP Q1FY27 slide 4]
- Channel mix: replacement ~79% of consolidated revenue, OEM ~21% (Q1 FY27 and FY26). Standalone India: replacement 66%, OEM 23%, exports 11%. [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Aug-26]
- Product mix, standalone India Q1 FY27: truck & bus 53%, passenger vehicle 22%, light truck 9%, farm 7%, others 9%. Consolidated: truck & bus 41%, passenger 36%, farm/OHT 11%, light truck 6%. [IP Q1FY27]
- Capacity utilisation Q1 FY27: India 91%, Europe 94%. CFO: "this entire FY '27, we will continue to be very tight on capacity." [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Europe: Enschede (Netherlands) plant stopped production June 2026; ~750,000 tyres a year of its volume shifts mostly to Hungary, low-end 14–15 inch sizes to India, Spacemaster spare tyres to Baroda. High-end agri tyres (Enschede made ~20 tonnes/day) now bought from two offtake partners; ~20–25% of Europe agri business is offtake. Agri was ~12% of Europe revenue, about half of it OEM. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Reifencom: Q1 FY27 revenue €43 mn at ~3% EBITDA; Q4 FY26 "€40" (said "billion", clearly million) at just under 2%. [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- **Q4 FY26 call (15 May 2026) promised:** FY27 capex ₹3,500 cr, ~80% for growth, ~₹3,000 cr in India; Q1 raw material up "mid to high teens"; 6–8% price rise announced for Q1 with "at least a couple more rounds" needed; A&P spend to settle ~2.5% of sales after 4% in Q4; Europe margin benefits from H2 FY27 and "back to a 16%... we can even surpass that"; Enschede cash outflow ~€55 mn in FY27, already provided; India tax rate falls from 34% to 25% from FY27; FY27 capex "largely committed", flexibility only in FY28. [Q4 call]
- **Q1 FY27 delivered:** raw material +17% (at the top of the range); 7–9% price hikes implemented; A&P cut ~50% QoQ; Enschede production stopped in June as promised; consolidated margin 11.7% vs 14.6% in Q4; capex ₹650 cr (₹500 cr India) — below a quarter of ₹3,500 cr but management says Q2–Q3 spend rises; net debt ~₹1,700 cr, net debt/EBITDA 0.4x. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- **New/updated in Q1:** further 8% raw-material rise in Q2; total price rise needed 15–16% vs ~11% in place (9% TBR, 11% other categories); July +1–2%, August ~2% expected; OEM formula prices catch up with one-quarter lag from Q2; Europe needs ~10% hikes, 3–4% taken; "we will be a net borrower" in FY27 and net debt/EBITDA to rise "slightly"; Europe full-year "high teens EBITDA" target after transition; rubber expected to cool from Q3. [Q1 call]
- **Verdict:** capex and restructuring guidance kept; margin guidance effectively pushed to H2 FY27. No revenue or margin number was ever given for FY27. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Outside view:** CRISIL (14 Aug 2026) expects FY27 operating margin 10–11%, revenue growth ~13%, ROCE below 10% in FY27 (13.0% in FY26), net debt/EBITDA (incl. dealer deposits and leases) 1.8–2.0x in FY27, improving in FY28. [CRISIL Aug-26]

## Capex and capacity
- **FY27 capex ₹3,500 cr** (company) vs ~₹3,000 cr (CRISIL), and ~₹2,200 cr in FY28 (CRISIL). FY26 capex: ₹1,400 cr per company slide; CRISIL puts it at ~₹2,400 cr (definition differs; flag). FY25: ₹800 cr. [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Aug-26]
- **Andhra Pradesh expansion ₹5,810 cr** (approved Feb 2026, Q3 FY26 results): +3.7 mn PCR and +1.3 mn TBR tyres a year by FY29, reported as ~52% and ~81% capacity increases; funded by accruals and debt. [Tyrepress Feb-26; AlphaStreet]
- **Hungary**: PCR capacity 17,000 → 21,000 tyres/day, production ramp from H2 FY27. India new capacity "towards the end of this year", ramp through FY28. AP expansion is double Hungary's in PCR (~8,000/day). [Q1 call]
- Balance sheet signs: CWIP ₹1,077 cr at Mar-26 (₹435 cr a year earlier); capital commitments ₹1,774 cr (₹875 cr). [Screener; AR FY26 note 23]
- **Payback (my estimate):** at assumed realisations of ~₹4,000–5,000 per PCR tyre and ~₹18,000–22,000 per TBR tyre, the AP additions could bring roughly ₹4,000–5,000 cr of revenue at full use — asset turns under 1x on ₹5,810 cr, versus ~1.7x on the existing net block (₹16,487 cr net fixed assets vs ₹28,471 cr sales). At 13–15% EBITDA the pre-tax payback is 8+ years unless utilisation is high and rubber eases. This is the key capex risk. [my calc from Screener + Tyrepress]
- Management framing: "If anything, we are a quarter late than early." Demand "surprised" management through July. [Q1 call]

- Operating cash flow FY26 ₹3,667 cr (98% of EBITDA); free cash flow ₹2,312 cr (FY25 ₹1,093 cr). [Screener]
- Working capital: debtor 41 days, inventory 128 days, payables 76 days, cash conversion 93 days (FY24: 79). Inventory is heavy. [Screener]
- Dividend payout 28% FY26; yield 1.5%. [Screener]
- FY27 outlook: annual cash accrual ₹2,200–2,300 cr vs ~₹350 cr debt repayment plus ~€50 mn Enschede settlement plus ₹3,000–3,500 cr capex → turns net borrower. [CRISIL Aug-26; Q1 call]

## Moat evidence
- Share (internal estimates): TBR replacement "upwards of 30%", PCR replacement "21% plus"; regained TBR share lost earlier; MRF and Apollo the two leaders in TBR. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- ~6,500 dealerships in India; 79% replacement revenue gives steady pricing. [CRISIL Aug-26]
- Pricing: took 7–9% in Q1 plus ~11% cumulative, with competitors following "similar magnitude". But Europe is a price follower: "we are more a follower given our size". [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- OEM approvals: BMW, MINI, Genesis, Kia, Mahindra (Q4); multiple EV platforms (Q1). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Cost position: moving Europe volume from high-cost Netherlands (wage inflation 12–13% a year) to Hungary and India; India only retains a fixed transfer-pricing margin on Europe exports. [Q4 call]
- CRISIL: competition "limits the ability to fully pass on any raw material price increases". [CRISIL Aug-26]

## Risks and red flags
- **CCI penalty ₹425.5 cr** (2018 order, 2011–12 truck-tyre price cartel case) — not provided; NCLAT remanded, CCI appeal pending in Supreme Court (last heard Sep 2025). [AR FY26 note 22; CRISIL Aug-26]
- **Contingent liabilities** (Mar-26): sales tax ₹515 cr (₹189 cr a year earlier), income tax ₹221 cr (₹957 cr), excise/customs/GST ₹10 cr, other litigation ₹10 cr, claims ₹16 cr. [AR FY26 note 22]
- **Exceptional items FY26 ₹1,034 cr**, almost all Enschede closure (impairment and ~₹545 cr restructuring); plus ₹26 cr labour-code cost. FY25 exceptional ₹169 cr incl. Trusted Mobility write-off. [AR FY26 B31, C18]
- **Auditor**: unmodified opinion, no qualification. Key audit matters: sales-incentive/warranty accruals ₹822 cr; Reifencom goodwill ₹277 cr plus indefinite intangibles ₹189 cr (Reifencom earns only 2–3% EBITDA — impairment risk); Netherlands asset impairment. Minor: no real-time audit trail for privileged database edits. [AR FY26 auditor's report]
- **Related parties**: FY26 purchases of traded goods from promoter-group firms Classic Industries & Exports and Vilas Polymer ~₹685 cr (FY25 ~₹473 cr); lease rent to PTL Enterprises (promoter-group lessor of the Kalamassery plant) and others; figures read from a garbled table — treat as approximate. [AR FY26 RPT note 20]
- **Pay**: Vice-Chairman & MD Neeraj Kanwar's FY26 pay ₹78.4 cr (₹62.4 cr commission), up 96%, 541 times the median employee; he also drew pay from a UK subsidiary. [AR FY26 CG report]
- **Management exit**: CFO Gaurav Kumar (22 years) resigned as whole-time director effective 6 Aug 2026; stays as CFO for transition; no successor named in the documents read. MD skipped the Q1 call. [Q1 call; News: dess.digital, Aug-26]
- **Ownership**: FII holding fell from 22.1% (Sep-23) to 10.1% (Jun-26); promoters 36.9%, no pledge disclosed. [Screener]
- Macro: US dealers overstocked; West Asia war hit exports; El Niño risk to rural/farm demand. [Q1 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "demand strong, margin pressure coming" to "demand still strong, margin pressure bigger and longer" (Q2 RM +8% on top of +17%).
- New: CFO exit, MD absent; EU anti-dumping on Chinese tyres a tailwind for the Apollo brand in Europe; Reifencom margin up to 3%; A&P halved.
- Capex tone hardened: Q4 said FY27 "largely committed"; Q1 said "we will be a net borrower".
- Market share claim raised: TBR replacement ">30%" (Q1) vs "gained share" (Q4).

## My read
Apollo's India franchise is in its best volume run in years (volume ~12%, utilisation 91%) and the balance sheet is the cleanest it has been in a decade, so it can afford a ₹3,500 cr capex year. The problem is timing: margins have dropped to ~12%, CRISIL sees 10–11% for FY27 and ROCE under 10%, and the ₹5,810 cr Andhra Pradesh project will depress returns until FY29. The stock at ~12 times trailing earnings prices a cyclical margin trough, not a collapse; it needs India margins back to ~14% in H2 FY27 and Europe to show double-digit margins after Enschede. A clear break in replacement demand, a CCI loss, or a weak CFO succession would change the view; a rubber fall with prices held (the classic tyre up-cycle) would make it cheap.
