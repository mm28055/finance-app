## PROFILE (Q4 FY26)
# Rajratan Global

## Executive Summary
Rajratan Global exists to supply the skeletal backbone of the global tire industry: high-quality tire bead wire. Solving the critical problem of supply chain reliability and quality assurance for tire OEMs, the company ensures its clients can maintain just-in-time manufacturing without risking the catastrophic safety failures associated with substandard bead wire. 

Rajratan is a definitive **Category Leader** in India, having successfully clawed its domestic market share back to an apex of 42-43%. Globally, it operates as an aggressive **Challenger**, systematically displacing entrenched competitors and capturing share among North American and European multinational OEMs. By pairing low-cost, high-scale manufacturing in strategic locations (Indore, Chennai, Thailand) with sticky, multi-year OEM approval cycles, Rajratan has transformed a traditionally commoditized steel wire business into a highly defensible, structurally profitable enterprise.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
*   **What they sell:** Primarily tire bead wire (representing the vast majority of volume and development focus), with a secondary revenue stream from non-tire high-carbon steel wire. The company is actively diversifying into high-margin steel cord for conveyor belts.
*   **Who pays them:** Top-tier global tire OEMs (Japanese, European, and US multinationals) and dominant Indian tire manufacturers. 
*   **How they make money:** Revenue is driven by annual volume contracts with premium OEMs, supplemented by spot-market sales to highly price-sensitive segments (such as North Indian manufacturers). Pricing is essentially cost-plus, though raw material price fluctuations usually entail a one-quarter pass-through lag, which heavily impacted Q4 FY26 before normalizing in Q1 FY27. Average realizations have now crossed ₹90,000/ton.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
*   **The Moat:** **Durable but highly process-oriented.** 
*   **Evidence:** The moat relies heavily on high switching costs. Tire bead wire only constitutes 3-4% of a tire's input cost, but failure results in catastrophic tire blowouts. Consequently, OEM approval cycles stretch for years, heavily insulating incumbents. Rajratan has compounded this by building localized scale. The Chennai plant sits amid a cluster of 10 tire factories, enabling the company to offer just-in-time inventory management that distant competitors cannot match without crushing freight margins. Furthermore, the Thailand subsidiary enjoys Board of Investment (BoI) privileges, structurally lowering its tax floor.
*   **The Bear Case:** The product is ultimately a derivative of steel wire rod. Rajratan is a price-taker on raw materials. If domestic competitors weaponize their integrated steel balance sheets, or if Chinese dumping intensifies, Rajratan's pricing parity could fracture. The Q4 FY26 margin collapse validated this vulnerability, proving the company must absorb intra-quarter shocks before passing costs onto OEMs.

## Growth Vectors
*   **The Chennai Scale-Up (Proximity Alpha):** Chennai achieved an 85-90% exit utilization rate on its initial 30,000-ton capacity in FY26. A hyper-efficient INR 25 crore modular capex injection will double this to 60,000 tons by Q2 FY27. Management is aiming for ~34,000-35,000 tons of production from Chennai in FY27, strategically capturing high-volume (albeit slightly lower-margin) orders to defend its 42-43% market share and lock out regional competition.
*   **Western Hemisphere Export Penetration:** Management has successfully converted multi-year approval phases with US and European multinationals into bulk trials and regular supply lines. By capitalizing its US subsidiary to handle FOB logistics and absorb 50% upfront import duties, Rajratan has streamlined the client experience, projecting 30% volume growth in North America and 50% in Europe for FY27.
*   **Steel Cord Adjacency (The Next $100M):** A targeted INR 70 crore capex (with ₹25 crore remaining to be spent) at the Pithampur facility will birth a 10,000-ton steel cord (conveyor belt) product line. Modeled at an average realization of INR 150/kg with industry-standard margins hovering near 20%, this segment offers a ~INR 150 crore high-margin top-line injection once fully ramped.
*   **Management Execution:** Excellent. Leadership walks the talk. They admit when macro headwinds hit them (transparently owning the 400-450 bps Q4 FY26 margin compression due to an INR 10,000/ton steel and energy spike) and pivot capital ruthlessly. When the Korean market proved too fiercely contested, they abandoned it to aggressively harvest Western MNCs. They prefer cheap debt (7-7.5%) over equity dilution to fund working capital. 

## Key Risks & Governance
*   **Forensic Red Flags:** Clean core operations, but working capital metrics require monitoring. Receivables are visibly stretching, directly driven by the new US subsidiary's mechanics. Rajratan absorbs 50% upfront import duties, 45-60 days of ocean freight, plus 30-60 days of customer credit to secure US market share. This structural working capital intensity—and the resulting increase in short-term debt—is the cost of Western growth. 
*   **Business Risks:** 
    *   *Raw Material Volatility:* The severe Q4 FY26 EBITDA miss proves the company remains exposed to intra-quarter steel price shocks before they can enforce pass-throughs. A temporary raw material shortage also stalled immediate April dispatches.
    *   *Geopolitical/Supply Chain:* Shipping disruptions in the Red Sea have extended export transit times and caused congestion at the Singapore and Colombo ports.
    *   *PLI Non-Compliance:* The company failed to meet the aggressive production targets for India's PLI scheme. While they are lobbying for a revision, no subsidies should be baked into forward modeling.
*   **What could derail the thesis?** A sustained period of raw material inflation that customers refuse to absorb, or a synchronized global recession that stalls tire replacement cycles, leaving Rajratan's newly expanded 60k ton Chennai facility structurally underutilized.

## Investment Thesis
*   **The Bull Case:** Rajratan is a masterclass in dominating a boring, critical niche. The Q4 FY26 margin compression was a transient raw material mismatch that is already fully priced through in Q1 FY27, setting up a sharp margin reversion (returning to the 13.5-14% consolidated EBITDA band). With domestic market share back at 43%, a high-margin steel cord line entering trials in Q2 FY27, and global volumes projected to grow 17-18% YoY (targeting ~155,000 tons in FY27), the company is positioned for robust EPS compounding. The use of low-cost debt rather than equity to fund high-ROIC capex heavily favors current shareholders.
*   **The Bear Case:** Capital intensity is silently creeping up. The push into North America and Europe requires carrying vast amounts of inventory on the water, ballooning short-term debt and compressing free cash flow conversion. If global shipping routes remain fractured, extending the 90+ day export cash-conversion cycle, or if tire OEM end-demand softens, the company will be left holding expensive, leveraged inventory in transit while facing heightened domestic pricing aggression.
