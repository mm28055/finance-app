## Business and segments
- Pune-based maker of aluminium die-cast and precision-machined components (formerly Pooja Castings Pvt Ltd, founded 1992; became a public company in 2025). Gravity, low-pressure and high-pressure die casting, 55+ VMC/HMC/CNC machines, 600+ SKUs: cylinder heads, inlet pipes, intake manifolds, EGR housings, clutch and transmission housings, aluminium knuckles, circuit-breaker housings, agricultural and healthcare castings [AR FY26 MD&A; CRISIL Jul-26].
- FY26 revenue mix by end use: automobile 72.58%, EV applications 9.81% (₹28.83 cr), non-automobile 17.61%; by vehicle type, commercial vehicles 50.88% (largest single category), passenger vehicles 14.80%, energy 10.53% [AR FY26 MD&A section 2 and 4].
- Geography: domestic ₹291.37 cr (99.16%), exports ₹2.48 cr (0.84%) to Germany, USA, Italy and Switzerland; imports of materials ₹35.8 cr [AR FY26 MD&A; note 35].
- Customers are not named in the annual report. CRISIL: top five customers 75–78% of revenue, largest 30–35% [CRISIL Jul-26]. Clients are OEMs and Tier-1 suppliers; recently obtained vendor approval for an aerospace customer [Screener profile; IPO summary on ipoji.com].
- Capacity: existing plants in the Pune region; Unit III at Amberthan, Khed (Pune) — 15,000 t/yr melting and 6,600 t/yr casting planned; partial start on 30 Aug 2026 with 3,600 t melting and 1,500 t casting [BSE filing 30 Aug 2026]. Unit IV proposed for magnesium components [AR FY26 Chairman's message]. Utilisation not disclosed.
- Employees: 216 full-time at Mar-26 [IPO summary on ipoji.com].

## Moat evidence
- 30+ years of casting and machining experience; in-house design, simulation and tool room; multiple casting processes under one roof [AR FY26 MD&A].
- Quality awards from customers (2019–2024) [AR FY26 awards page]; customer names not disclosed, so share and switching costs cannot be verified.
- Margin expanded from 10% (FY21) to 18% (FY26) with ROCE above 30% — evidence of a decent cost position [Screener].
- Weakness: aluminium castings are a crowded field (listed peers include Endurance, Rico, Alicon and Menon Alkop — my list, not from the filings), and the business is concentrated in a few customers.

## Risks and red flags
- **Customer concentration**: top five 75–78%, largest 30–35% of revenue [CRISIL Jul-26]; customer identities undisclosed.
- **Related-party purchases**: promoter-linked G K Founders (raw materials ~₹18.5 cr), G K Alloys (~₹6.4 cr), Jayshree Pistons (labour ~₹6.6 cr, materials ~₹3.9 cr) and Dakshendra & Associates (professional fees ~₹1.4 cr; the name matches the CFO's family) — ~₹37 cr, about 12% of revenue (amounts are my reading of a poorly aligned table); shareholders asked to approve further material RPTs with Jayshree Pistons and G K Founders at the 2026 AGM [AR FY26 note 34; AGM notice items 5–6].
- **Disclosure quality**: the published MD&A still contains a drafting instruction — "The final Annual Report should align the segment disclosure in this MD&A with the segment reporting adopted in the audited financial statements" [AR FY26 MD&A section 4]; the Unit III filing has mismatched numbers and words; the Directors' report states the company "had fully utilized the net proceeds of IPO" within weeks of listing, which the rest of the report does not support [AR FY26 Directors' report]; audited and restated profits differ.
- **Bank stock statements**: CARO reports quarterly returns to SBI not in agreement with books for some quarters (e.g., inventory ₹34.56 cr per books in Q1) [AR FY26 CARO ii(b)]. Some borrowing charges were registered late, with fees paid [AR FY26 note 36.7].
- **Execution**: Unit III (15,000 t melting) is several times the existing scale; CRISIL flags project risk [CRISIL Jul-26].
- **Valuation froth**: IPO subscribed 278.9x overall (QIB 206x, NII 464x, retail 248x), listed at ₹470 (+56%), now ₹816 — 2.7x the issue price in eight weeks [IPO summaries on ipoji.com / web search].
- **Ownership**: promoters 60.63% post-IPO (82.63% before); anchors ₹45.14 cr led by Abakkus (31%) and Motilal Oswal Finvest (12%) [investorgain.com; ipoji.com]. Pledges: none disclosed.
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; no emphasis of matter; no key audit matters reported; contingent liabilities tiny (performance guarantees ₹0.77 cr) [AR FY26 auditor's report; note 32].

## My read
Poojaa is a well-run-looking small caster — revenue up 3.4x in five years, 18–19% margins, 30%+ ROCE — that has just raised ₹160 cr to multiply its melting capacity. But the evidence base is thin: no calls, no customer names, one large customer at a third of sales, rising inventory days, and noticeably careless disclosure (a drafting note left in the annual report, a filing whose numbers and words disagree, two versions of FY26 profit). At ~49x audited (or ~53x restated) FY26 earnings the price needs roughly 30%+ earnings growth for years and flawless Unit III execution. The first half-year results (Nov 2026), Unit III ramp and any customer-diversification disclosure would decide whether this is a compounder or an over-subscribed SME listing.
