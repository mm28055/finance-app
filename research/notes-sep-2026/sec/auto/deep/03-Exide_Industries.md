# Exide Industries — deep research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Screener code EXIDEIND (BSE 500086).

## Business and segments
- India's largest lead-acid battery maker: automotive batteries for 2W/3W/4W (replacement and OEM), home inverter/UPS batteries, solar batteries, and industrial batteries (railways, traction, power, telecom, data centres, industrial UPS, submarines). 11 Exide plants plus 3 lead-recycling plants of subsidiary Chloride Metals (CML) and 2 plants of Exide Energy Solutions (EESL). 120,000+ network touchpoints; 75+ OEMs served; exports to 70+ countries. [IP Q1FY27]
- Mix (FY26, management): auto (OEM plus replacement) about 50% of revenue ("53:47" auto vs non-auto); within the rest, home UPS about ₹2,300 cr, solar crossed ₹1,000 cr, exports about 5% (down from 8% two years ago), telecom plus e-rickshaw about 3%. Home inverter share swings 15–25% by season. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- New energy: EESL is building a 6 GWh Phase-1 lithium-ion cell plant in Bengaluru (3 GWh NMC cylindrical for 2-wheelers, 3 GWh LFP prismatic for 3W, telecom, stationary storage, 4W), with land and utilities already sized for 12 GWh. Technology licensed from SVOLT (China). Current pack/module assembly at Prantij (1.5 GWh) with imported cells: ₹100–200 cr revenue in FY26, not profitable. EESL announced commissioning of the Phase-1 plant on 23 Sep 2026. [Q4 call; Q1 call; ICRA Jun-26; BSE announcement 23 Sep 2026 via Screener]
- No segment reporting (single segment: storage batteries). Subsidiary data only yearly (AOC-1). [AR FY26]
- Promoter (Rajan Raheja group) holds 45.99%. Exide also holds shares in HDFC Life worth about ₹5,100 cr at Mar-26. [ICRA Jun-26]

## Key numbers (Screener consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarters (sales / operating profit / OPM / net profit): Jun-25 4,695 / 538 / 11% / 275; Sep-25 4,365 / 391 / 9% / 174; Dec-25 4,201 / 452 / 11% / 195; Mar-26 4,735 / 488 / 10% / 217; Jun-26 5,528 / 621 / 11% / 351. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27 YoY (consolidated): sales +18%, operating profit +15%, net profit +28%. [Screener]
- Standalone Q1 FY27 (company): revenue ₹5,305 cr (+17.6%), EBITDA ₹655 cr (+19.5%, margin 12.4% vs 12.2%; +70 bps QoQ), PBT ₹543 cr, PAT ₹407 cr (+27%). The gap between standalone PAT ₹407 cr and consolidated ₹351 cr is mainly EESL start-up losses. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Annual: FY24 sales 16,770 / OP 1,823 (11%) / PAT 883; FY25 17,238 / 1,805 (10%) / 800; FY26 17,995 / 1,876 (10%) / 860. 3-year sales CAGR only 6%; 5-year profit CAGR 1%; ROE about 6%. [Screener]
- FY26 standalone revenue grew 4.1%, with the Q2 GST washout (distributors stopped buying for about 1.5–2 months); "nearly 92% of the business" grew double-digit while exports, telecom and e-rickshaw fell. [Q4 call]
- Unit economics: Q1 volumes grew double-digit in most businesses — 4W replacement +10%, 4W OEM +21%, 2W OEM +20%, solar +12–14% — plus 4–6% YoY price increases. [Q1 call]
- Subsidiaries FY26: EESL revenue ₹158 cr, loss ₹248 cr; Chloride Metals revenue ₹6,036 cr (mostly recycled lead sold to Exide), small loss of ₹14 cr. [AR FY26 AOC-1]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (6 May 2026): core lead-acid growth "high-single to double-digit" for FY27 and an ~11% (±1%) medium-term CAGR; exports to recover after H1; price increases of ~5–6% taken Jan–Mar and ~3% on 1 Apr, more to come; ₹1,400 cr to be put into EESL in FY27; NMC cylindrical samples "this month", LFP prismatic samples by "June and July"; first cell revenue likely from the LFP line; target yield 90% and utilisation above 85% long term. [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 call (3 Aug 2026): delivered 17.6% standalone growth and 12.4% margin — ahead of the Q4 range; declined FY27 guidance because of the high H2 base (auto OEM boomed from Oct-25); both sample milestones met; first-year cell-plant utilisation 25–30% "we stand by"; FY27 EESL funding ₹1,400 cr (₹100 cr done in July); total cost for 12 GWh may exceed the original ₹7,000 cr because of currency; 50–60% local bill-of-materials in 2–3 years. [Q1 call]
- Track record: core-business promises met or beaten in Q1. Lithium timelines have slipped over time: ICRA notes commercialisation now expected by September 2026 against an earlier H2 FY26, and Phase-1 cost has risen to about ₹5,600 cr from ₹5,200 cr. The Hyundai-Kia co-investment line is delayed ("Of course, there are delays"). [ICRA Jun-26; Q1 call]
- Guidance status: core growth guidance kept (qualitative); lithium schedule kept on the calls but the long-run cost estimate drifted up.

## Capex and capacity
- Core lead-acid capex about ₹500 cr a year, mostly debottlenecking; 4W SLI capacity being added across five 4W plants because the post-GST OEM boom will feed replacement demand in 2.5–3 years. 2W still has headroom. [Q1 call]
- EESL: equity infused ₹4,902 cr by 31 Jul 2026 (₹1,500 cr in FY26, ₹600 cr in Q4 FY26 alone); Phase-1 project cost about ₹5,600 cr, part debt-funded (financial closure done; ~₹2,000 cr of undrawn term loans). Consolidated capex plan ₹1,000–1,500 cr in FY27. Consolidated CWIP ₹4,198 cr at Mar-26. [Q1 call; ICRA Jun-26; Screener]
- Phase 2 (to 12 GWh): mostly LFP; cheaper than Phase 1 because land and utilities are already built. PLI: Exide is studying the reopened 10 GWh PLI; Phase 1 was built without PLI. [Q1 call]
- Payback: management refuses to give margin or return targets until the plant runs at 85% utilisation and 90% yield. [Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Standalone is debt-free. Consolidated gross debt ₹1,575 cr at Mar-26 (including ₹482 cr lease liabilities), against cash and liquid investments of ₹1,082 cr — small net debt of about ₹490 cr, ignoring the ₹5,100 cr HDFC Life stake. Total debt/OPBDIT 0.8x, interest cover 15x. [ICRA Jun-26; Screener]
- Exide has given a ₹3,000 cr corporate guarantee for EESL's borrowings. [AR FY26 Directors' Report]
- Cash flow FY26: CFO ₹2,413 cr (₹1,273 cr FY25) on much better working capital; Screener FCF ₹1,293 cr. [Screener]
- Working capital: inventory days fell to 120 (144 in FY25), payables rose to 110 days, cash conversion cycle 42 days (74). [Screener]
- HDFC Life stake marked down about ₹850 cr in FY26 through OCI (no P&L impact); not sold. [Q4 call]
- Dividend payout about 20%. Rating: ICRA AAA (Stable)/A1+, reaffirmed 11 Jun 2026. [Screener; ICRA Jun-26]

## Moat evidence
- Market leader in both OEM and replacement auto batteries (ICRA); "100% share of business on key new models with large OEMs" (company claim). [ICRA Jun-26; IP Q1FY27]
- Pricing power: passed 5–6% of increases in Q4 and ~3% more on 1 Apr, then 4–6% YoY in Q1, while expanding standalone margin to 12.4%; OEMs agreed to non-lead cost increases with about a quarter's lag. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Cost position: integrated recycling (CML supplies about 80% of lead and lead-alloy needs per the Directors' Report); warranty cost falling as % of sales. [AR FY26; Q4 call]
- Distribution: 120,000+ touchpoints, 18,000+ villages, doorstep "Batmobile" service — the replacement market is where margin sits. [IP Q1FY27]
- Lithium: first mover with a running 6 GWh plant; ICRA calls it an "early mover advantage", but there are no take-or-pay contracts. [ICRA Jun-26]

## Risks and red flags
- Lithium economics unproven: EESL lost ₹248 cr in FY26 on ₹158 cr revenue; no customer volume commitments disclosed; the 2W NMC cells need 2–3 months of OEM homologation; raw materials (and all electrolyte) come from China, and Chinese export controls on battery materials could start in November 2026. [AR FY26 AOC-1; Q1 call; ICRA Jun-26]
- Capital sunk before returns: about ₹6,300 cr committed to EESL by end-FY27 (₹4,902 cr to July plus the rest of ₹1,400 cr), while group ROE is about 6% and ROCE 9%. [Screener; Q1 call]
- Cost overrun and delay: Phase-1 cost up ~8% to ₹5,600 cr; commercialisation moved from H2 FY26 to around Sep 2026. [ICRA Jun-26]
- Technology dependence on SVOLT; management admits China may block further licences, so a pilot line with 100+ engineers is being built. [Q1 call]
- Lead-acid substitution: telecom and e-rickshaw are already shifting to lithium (about 3% of revenue declining). [Q4 call]
- Input costs: sulfur went from ₹15/kg to about ₹75/kg in a year; plastics and rupee weakness hurt; LME lead in rupee terms no longer falling. [Q4 call]
- Contingent liabilities (standalone) ₹66 cr claims, of which excise/service tax/customs demands jumped to ₹57 cr from ₹5 cr; plus a landlord claim (not quantifiable) in the Bombay High Court; guarantees ₹61 cr. Small against net worth of about ₹13,900 cr. [AR FY26 note 36]
- Auditor (B S R & Co.): unmodified; KAMs are warranty provision and dealer loyalty-scheme incentives (both judgement-heavy). No red flags found in related-party dealings beyond normal intra-group lead purchases from CML. [AR FY26 auditor's report]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from commodity alarm (sulfur, plastics, gas) in May to growth confidence in August.
- Numbers: standalone margin 11.7% → 12.4%; growth 9.4% → 17.6%; exports turned from decline to +20%.
- Lithium moved from "validation complete" to samples shipped on both lines; new talk of 4W OEM interest, BESS large-format cells and Chinese raw-material controls.
- New issue raised by the CEO: India needs 2–3 more cell makers for the government to protect local cells; cell import duty still only 5%.

## Stored-data errors
- Q1 FY27 row mixes bases: EBITDA 655 is standalone, revenue 5,528 and PAT 351 are consolidated, so "mgn=11.8%" is neither (standalone 12.4%; consolidated Screener OPM 11.2%).
- Q4 FY26 row shows rev/EBITDA/PAT = 0 (Screener: sales 4,735, OP 488, PAT 217); Q3 FY26 shows rev 4,000 (Screener 4,201) and EBITDA/PAT 0; Q2 FY26 null (Screener 4,365 / 391 / 174).
- Stored analysis/profile call the balance sheet "absolutely debt-free". Only the standalone is; the group has ₹1,575 cr gross debt and a ₹3,000 cr guarantee for EESL.
- Stored profile's segment table (auto replacement 50–55% plus OEM ~20%) contradicts management's "auto is ... about 50%" in total.
- Stored profile claims a "near-monopoly on early domestic cell supply" and that rivals will be "paralyzed"; unsupported — management declined to name rivals and ICRA notes competition from imports and other cell makers.
- Stored analysis omits the Phase-1 cost overrun (₹5,200 cr → ₹5,600 cr) and the earlier commercialisation delay flagged by ICRA.

## My read
The lead-acid business is having its best stretch in years — Q1 standalone growth of 17.6% with margins rising despite a sharp input-cost spike shows real pricing power — but over three years it has grown only about 6% a year and group return on equity is stuck near 6% because ₹5,000+ cr sits in a lithium plant that has not yet earned a rupee. The price needs the lithium bet to turn into a profitable business, not just a working factory. I would turn more positive on disclosed customer orders and a first quarter of EESL revenue with narrowing losses (watch Q3–Q4 FY27); negative on further cost overruns, a Chinese raw-material squeeze after November, or the core margin slipping back below 11%.
