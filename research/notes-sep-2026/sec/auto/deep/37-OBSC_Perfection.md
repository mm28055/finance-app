# OBSC Perfection Ltd (NSE Emerge: OBSCP) — deep research, 27 Sep 2026

SME-listed (NSE Emerge, listed 2024) but now reports quarterly. Standalone company; Screener has no consolidated data. Price ₹930, market cap ₹2,405 cr on Screener (before the September 2026 allotment; about ₹2,540 cr on the enlarged 2.73 cr shares), trailing P/E 76.4, 52-week range ₹270–1,030, stock up 212% in a year [Screener, 27 Sep 2026]. Only one earnings call is on record for the last year (Q4 FY26, 21 May 2026); no Q1 FY27 call or presentation was found, although Q1 results were filed on 13 August 2026 [Screener documents; News: Business Standard, 13 Aug 2026].

## Business and segments
- Build-to-print precision metal parts maker, founded 2017, part of the Delhi-based Omega (Anglian Omega) bright-steel group [IP May-26; AR FY26 notice]. Started as a single-process CNC machining shop; now also does investment casting (Pune, 2022), hot forging (Pune, 2025), cold and hot forging (Faridabad, acquired FY26) and stamping (distressed-asset purchase of 30+ presses of 50–500 tonnes, installation in Q1 FY27) [IP May-26; Q4 call].
- Products: shafts, rack bars, piston/shock-absorber rods, steering and drive shafts, sensor housings; 100+ SKUs at tolerances down to 4 microns [IP May-26; CRISIL Jul-26]. Shock-absorber rods were 8–9% of FY26 revenue (~₹20 cr) [Q4 call].
- FY26 revenue from operations ₹219.5 cr by industry: automotive 85.6% (₹188 cr), marine 6.3%, defence 5.6% (₹12.4 cr, up from ₹5.6 cr), renewables 2.5% [IP May-26]. Commercial vehicles take 40–50% of automotive revenue [Q4 call].
- Geography: domestic 80.4% (₹176.6 cr), exports 19.6% (₹43.0 cr, up 50% from ₹28.7 cr) to 17 countries; Italy ₹13.9 cr, USA ₹11.2 cr, Germany ₹10.7 cr, Slovakia ₹4.6 cr, Israel ₹0.9 cr [IP May-26; AR FY26 MD&A].
- Customers: Tenneco, ZF, Mahle, Tata AutoComp (from 10th-largest a year ago to 2nd-largest in May 2026; supplies Tesla-linked parts through Tata AutoComp) [Q4 call]. Top five customers 50–55% of revenue, largest 15–20% [CRISIL Jul-26].
- New areas (small so far): defence ignition primers in mass production from May 2026; ammunition casings in validation; MK-84 bomb fins cast; first orthopaedic implant castings; >4,000 trial aluminium cold-plate parts for humanoid robots via a Tier-1; AS9100D aerospace certification expected "within two months" of May 2026 [Q4 call]. MoU (non-binding, 36 months) with Aethrone Aerospace on 9 September 2026 to explore a joint precision-machining plant in New Mexico, USA [NSE filing 9 Sep 2026].
- Capacity: forging capacity 12,000 tonnes a year installed in Q4 FY26, utilisation ~30% [IP May-26]. Plants in Pune, Chennai, Faridabad; new land at Supa (8.4 acres acquired, ~2 acres being acquired) and ~1 acre at GIDC Sanand [IP May-26].

## Guidance history
- FY26 (given May 2025): 40% revenue growth and 2% gross-margin improvement; delivered 54% (revenue from operations) and +284 bps gross margin [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A]. **Beaten.**
- FY27 (given 21 May 2026): revenue growth 40–45%; margin up about 1 point ("at least sustain"); exports 30–35% of sales (from ~20%); capex ₹15–20 cr from internal approvals; order book converts at ₹100–200 cr of added revenue a year [Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27 delivery: sales +85% YoY, ahead of the growth guidance; operating margin 17.9%, below FY26's 18.1% and the Q3 peak of 19.3%, so the margin promise is not yet visible [Screener]. Export share for Q1 not disclosed.
- Longer term: Supa plant could do ₹700–800 cr of revenue at peak; own IP products within five years [Q4 call].

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ₹49.75 cr; gross block plus CWIP ₹138 cr [IP May-26]. Screener: fixed assets ₹112 cr (₹70 cr in FY25), investing outflow ₹76 cr in FY26 [Screener].
- Supa land (about 11 acres total per call) cost ₹17–18 cr; stamping assets about ₹9 cr including tooling, ~70% spent; stamping brings about ₹2 cr a month of existing recurring business [Q4 call; IP May-26].
- Sanand: dedicated Tenneco shock-absorber-rod line (including the DaVinci suspension), expected ₹40 cr revenue, about double today's rod sales [Q4 call].
- Phasing: management promises modular, "frugal" build-out to limit depreciation; FY27 capex only ₹15–20 cr — but this sits oddly with ₹86.5 cr of fresh equity raised in September 2026 [Q4 call; NSE filing 11 Sep 2026].
- Funding: ₹43.3 cr preferential issue in FY26; ₹86.5 cr preferential issue allotted 11 Sep 2026 (14.4 lakh shares at ₹601 to Sixteenth Street Asian Gems Fund and Mirasol Finance), about 5.6% dilution; term loans of ₹23.1 cr (to 2031) and ₹10 cr (to 2030) [AR FY26 MD&A; NSE filing 11 Sep 2026; CRISIL Jul-26].

## Cash, debt and working capital

Debt/equity 0.40 (0.26), debt-service coverage 1.50 (8.09), interest cover 8.1–9.7x [AR FY26 MD&A ratios; IP May-26; CRISIL Jul-26].
- Cash flow from operations **negative ₹2 cr** in FY26 (CFO/operating profit 6%); free cash flow −₹79 cr; FY25 FCF −₹24 cr [Screener]. The CFO said it is "tough to say" when free cash flow turns positive [Q4 call].
- Working capital: receivables ₹66.1 cr (+89%), inventory ₹47.2 cr (+77%), payables ₹51.1 cr (+102%) [IP May-26]. Debtor days 110, inventory days 110, payable days 119, cash conversion 101 days (97 in FY25) [Screener]. Gross current assets 223 days; bank-line use averaged 64% and peaked at 95% in February 2026 [CRISIL Jul-26]. Management says inventory was deliberately built (aluminium, oils) ahead of West Asia war price rises [Q4 call].

## Moat evidence
- Multi-process capability (machining + casting + forging + stamping) lets it bid for sub-assemblies; management argues it would be "the cheapest assembly player in India" [Q4 call]. Unproven so far — assemblies are not yet a disclosed revenue line.
- Wallet-share gains with global Tier-1s: served their India arms for 3–5 years before being offered export work for US/Mexico plants; 56% of the ₹1,200 cr order book is export (₹678 cr) [Q4 call; IP May-26].
- Order book ₹1,200+ cr (automotive ₹982 cr, non-auto ₹233 cr) over 5–6 years; management calls automotive nominations "legally binding" but volumes can vary ±20%; only ₹200–300 cr are short-term purchase orders [Q4 call; IP May-26; CRISIL Jul-26]. This is about 5.5x FY26 revenue.
- Pricing: export parts quoted "at least 10%" above domestic; it cut prices 10% temporarily on some US projects during the tariff spike and expects original prices back [Q4 call].
- Weakness: no own IP; build-to-print; customers supply tooling and development costs [Q4 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Cash burn and repeated equity raising**: two preferential issues (₹43.3 cr and ₹86.5 cr) inside a year, borrowings up 2.5x, negative operating cash flow [AR FY26; NSE filing 11 Sep 2026; Screener].
- **Related-party purchases from the promoter group**: ₹20.8 cr bought in FY26 from Omega Bright Steel & Components (holds 37.01% of OBSC) and ₹10.2 cr from Omega Bright Steel Pvt Ltd, plus ₹5.9 cr of sales to it; shareholders asked to approve up to ₹25 cr and ₹20 cr for FY27 [AR FY26 AGM notice items 3–4]. Together ~14% of revenue — bright-bar steel is a key raw material, so pricing fairness matters.
- **Preferential pricing**: September allotment at ₹601 against a market price near ₹930 (price set under SEBI's formula at the relevant date) [NSE filing 11 Sep 2026; Screener].
- Promoter holding diluted from 73.5% to 66.2% in a year [Screener shareholding]. Company Secretary resigned 14 September 2026, replacement named 16 September [NSE filings].
- Auditor: directors' report says the auditors' report "does not contain any qualification or adverse remark", and the governance report calls it unmodified [AR FY26 directors' report]. The NSE-hosted annual report file ends at page 103 and omits the financial statements, so the contingent-liability and related-party notes could not be read [AR FY26].
- Customer concentration (top five 50–55%); CV-cycle exposure (40–50% of auto revenue); tariff risk on the US export ramp [CRISIL Jul-26; Q4 call].
- Execution sprawl: defence, aerospace, medical, humanoids, marine, US plant MoU — all at early stages with revenue not yet disclosed separately.

## My read
The growth is real and ahead of promise — Q1 FY27 sales rose 85% and the order book is about 5.5 times FY26 sales, over half of it exports. But it is being bought with working capital and outside money: operating cash flow was negative in FY26, debt rose to ₹69 cr, and two share issues were needed in a year. Operating margin has not expanded (17–19%), so the story rests on volume. At a trailing P/E near 76, the price needs about 26% a year of profit growth for a decade ((76.4 × 0.1726)^0.1 − 1), which only works if the export order book converts on time and cash flow turns positive. The view would improve on positive operating cash flow and assembly-level orders; it would worsen on a slip in the US ramp or more related-party or equity-funded growth.
