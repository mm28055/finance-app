# Steel Strips Wheels (NSE: SSWL) — deep research, 27 Sep 2026

Transcript check against the BSE filings:

- Q4 FY26 (call 2 Jun 2026): **matches official** (99% word match).

## Business and segments
- India's largest steel-wheel maker and one of the top two alloy-wheel makers. It supplies passenger cars, trucks and buses, tractors, off-road vehicles and 2/3-wheelers. Aluminium steering knuckles are a new line (from FY25). [IP Jul-26; Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 revenue mix:
  - steel wheels ₹953.8 cr (63%);
  - alloy wheels ₹532.9 cr (35%);
  - aluminium knuckles ₹23.0 cr (2%, ~80,000 units, only 2 OEM customers).
  - [IP Jul-26]
- FY26 end-market mix:
  - cars and utility vehicles (steel and alloy) 54%;
  - trucks 28%;
  - tractors 13%;
  - 2/3-wheelers 2%;
  - off-road 1%;
  - knuckles 1%.
  - [Q4 call]
- Management says 70% of revenue is "value added" (alloy, truck, tractor, exports). [Q1 call]
- Exports: ₹127 cr in Q1 FY27 against ₹160 cr in Q1 FY26 (−21% YoY, +37% QoQ). FY26 exports were ₹454 cr, down ~19% because of US tariffs. [IP Jul-26; Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Capacity and utilisation (company presentation):
  - Steel wheels: 20.7m, utilisation 76% in FY26 and 82% in Q1 FY27.
  - Alloy wheels: 5.0m (6.2m by FY27 end), utilisation ~80% in Q1.
  - Knuckles: 0.5m, going to 1.1m.
  - Plants: Dappar (Punjab), Chennai, Jamshedpur, Mehsana and Bhuj (Gujarat), plus a hot-rolling mill at Saraikela.
  - [IP Jul-26]
- The MD's own capacity and utilisation figures differ from the presentation:
  - He said steel capacity is "24 million", rising to 26m; the presentation shows 20.7m.
  - He said "all my business units are running at more than 95% utilization"; the presentation shows steel at 82% and alloy at ~80%.
  - [Q1 call; IP Jul-26]
- Unit prices: a car steel wheel sells for ~₹1,200 and an aluminium wheel for ~₹5,000. [Q4 call]
- Strategic shareholders: Tata Steel 6.9% and Nippon Steel 5.4%. [IP Jul-26]

## Guidance history
- Promises on the Q4 call (2 Jun 2026) for FY27 [Q4 call]:
  - revenue ~₹6,500 cr (+25%);
  - EBITDA ~₹650 cr "with an upward bias";
  - EBITDA per wheel close to ₹300;
  - PAT growth 15–20%;
  - exports ~₹600 cr (from ₹454 cr);
  - debt up ~₹200 cr;
  - capex ~₹500 cr at Bhuj plus ~₹50 cr brownfield;
  - Bhuj trial runs Oct 2026 to Jan 2027.
- Longer-range promises: EBITDA ₹700–750 cr in FY28, and Bhuj adding ₹700–800 cr of revenue at full use with 70–80% utilisation in FY28. [Q4 call]
- What Q1 FY27 delivered [Q1 call; IP Jul-26]:
  - Revenue ₹1,510 cr, about a ₹6,000 cr annual run-rate; it needs a ramp-up to reach ₹6,500 cr.
  - EBITDA per wheel ₹314, which beat the guide. The FY27 target was raised to "more than ₹310", and the MD said "upside bias".
  - PAT +43% YoY, ahead of the 15–20% guide.
  - Exports ₹127 cr, only a ₹508 cr annual pace against the ₹600 cr target. Management kept the target and pointed to June exports up 7% YoY, the first increase in two years.
- Capex was raised in Q1: a new ₹150 cr brownfield steel line (2m tractor and car wheels, which lifts steel capacity from 24m to 26m in the MD's figures) takes FY27 capex to "₹600 cr plus/minus". [Q1 call]
- Manpower: in June the MD said labour shortages cost "~₹80 cr of sales" in April–May, and that the problem was fixed after 20 May. In July he said it was "fully resolved". [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Verdict: margins beat the guide, exports are behind, and capex went up.

## Capex and capacity
- Bhuj (AMW plant bought via NCLT) [Q1 call; Q4 call]:
  - 1.2m alloy wheels plus 0.6m knuckles (the MD also said 1.1m knuckles in total, with 0.5m more in phase 2);
  - ~₹420 cr for Bhuj, plus ~₹80 cr for replacement and other plant capex;
  - trial production in Q4 FY27; at least 80% utilisation targeted by Q1 FY28.
- Bhuj alloy output will mainly serve export aftermarket wheels, with technical support from Arays. [Q1 call]
- Brownfield steel line: ₹150 cr for agricultural and car wheels, due to start by Dec 2026. [Q1 call]
- India Ratings' capex estimate: ~₹430 cr for alloy wheels and ~₹140 cr for knuckles over FY26–FY27. [India Ratings Feb-26 via ScanX]
- Net capex FY26 was ₹196 cr. Capital commitments were ₹155.1 cr at Mar-26 (₹99.1 cr a year earlier). [IP Jul-26; AR FY26 note 40]
- Future ideas: hot stamping; further aluminium and knuckle capacity if exports keep improving. The MD's aim is to sell more wheels abroad than in India within 10 years. [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings (Screener): ₹828 cr at Mar-26, flat on Mar-25 (₹1,048 cr at Mar-24). The company reports gross debt of ~₹826 cr at 8–9% interest. [Screener; Q1 call]
- Factoring and bill discounting: the company uses ₹400–500 cr of factoring limits on top of this debt. This explains why interest cost (~₹123 cr in FY26) is higher than ₹826 cr at 8–9% would imply. I found no disclosure of factoring in the FY26 annual report. [Q1 call; Screener; AR FY26 search]
- India Ratings put net adjusted leverage at 3.2x in 9M FY26, up from 2.8x in FY25 (IND AA-/Stable, affirmed Feb 2026). [India Ratings via ScanX, Feb-26]
- Cash flow (Screener):
  - Cash from operations (CFO): FY24 ₹193 cr, FY25 ₹517 cr, FY26 ₹332 cr; CFO/operating profit 73% in FY26.
  - Free cash flow: FY26 ₹143 cr; FY24 was −₹188 cr.
  - [Screener]
- Working capital (Screener): cash conversion cycle 38 days (debtors 43, inventory 102, payables 108). This is low, helped by factoring and long supplier credit. [Screener]
- Aluminium spike: it added ~₹150 cr of working-capital pressure. Aluminium cost is now passed on monthly (a 30-day lag). [Q1 call]
- Dividend: 150% (₹1.50 a share) for FY26; payout 12%. An analyst asked the company to raise equity to ease the balance sheet. [IP Jul-26; Screener; Q1 call]

## Moat evidence
- Lowest-cost producer: the MD says it is "hands down" the lowest-cost wheel maker outside China ("80–90% true"). [Q1 call]
- Pricing power: after "two decades of very poor pricing", OEMs granted 1–5% price increases, and aluminium is now passed on monthly. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Share: the analyst's figure of 40–50% of steel wheels by segment was not disputed. Company claims:
  - 80% of electric-scooter wheels ("almost a monopoly") and 30% of wheels for petrol two-wheeler customers;
  - sole supplier of aluminium knuckles in India.
  - [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Share of business at car makers, per the presentation: e.g. MG 96% and Kia 100% in steel wheels; Hyundai 74% and Mahindra 64% in alloy wheels. [IP Jul-26]
- Alloy competition: "only 2 serious players". The MD says domestic alloy margins are "crashing" because a rival takes business below cost, and SSWL is steering volume to exports instead. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- Undisclosed factoring: ₹400–500 cr of factoring is off the balance sheet and not described in the annual report, so true debt is ~₹1,250 cr+. India Ratings' 3.2x leverage figure captures some of this. [Q1 call; India Ratings]
- Management's figures are inconsistent:
  - capacity (24m vs 20.7m steel);
  - utilisation (">95%" vs 80–82%);
  - knuckles ("100% utilization" on 0.5m capacity, while Q1 sales of 0.8 lakh units annualise to ~64%);
  - FY26 EBITDA per wheel (₹262 vs ₹272 in the same call).
  - [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Jul-26]
- Related parties: loan to subsidiary AMW ₹133.15 cr, plus ₹8.3 cr of interest receivable. KMP pay was ₹15.7 cr, and the MD's pay is 268 times the median employee's. Promoter-group entities (SAB Industries, Steel Strips Infrastructure, etc.) are listed as related parties. [AR FY26 note 41; Directors' report]
- Auditor: unqualified; the report on the standalone accounts lists no key audit matters. CARO notes a ₹100 cr HDFC term loan taken to buy AMW. [AR FY26]
- Export exposure: US tariffs cut exports by 19% in FY26, and the recovery is only a month old. [Q4 call]
- Capex of ~₹600 cr in one year against FY26 operating cash flow of ₹332 cr means debt will rise by ₹200 cr or more. [Q4 call; Screener]
- Cyclicality: trucks 28% and tractors 13% of sales. The MD's "10–15 year boom" in tractors is not a forecast anyone can rely on. [Q1 call]
- Management turnover: CFO changed in Nov 2025; the Deputy MD was absent from the Q4 call "for family issues". [AR FY26; Q4 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- The manpower crisis (₹80 cr of lost sales) was declared solved.
- EBITDA per wheel jumped from ₹282 to ₹314, and the guide rose from ~₹300 to >₹310.
- A new ₹150 cr steel line was announced, so capex rose from ~₹550 cr to ~₹600 cr.
- The steel-wheel business moved from "declining" to "industry sold out" after the GST cuts.
- Exports: the ₹600 cr target was kept, but Q1 exports were still down YoY.
- The Bhuj knuckle line is now "sold out". A small truck slowdown in June was offset by moving production to export truck wheels.

## My read
The business is improving:
- price increases have lifted profit per wheel ~20% in a year;
- volumes are strong after the GST cuts;
- the Bhuj aluminium plant adds a higher-margin leg from FY28.

But the numbers still show a low-margin, heavily indebted converter: 10–11% operating margin, 4–5% net margin, ROE 11%. On top of that sit ~₹600 cr of capex this year and ₹400–500 cr of factoring that is not in reported debt.

At about 28x trailing earnings (Screener), the price assumes the FY27 guide (₹650 cr EBITDA) and FY28 (₹700–750 cr) are both met.

What would change my view:
- negative: exports stay below ~₹130–150 cr a quarter, or knuckle approvals at Bhuj slip past Q1 FY28;
- positive: formal disclosure of the factoring amount, and cash flow covering the capex.

Management's loose capacity and utilisation claims are a caution sign.
