# Rajratan Global Wire Ltd (RAJRATAN / BSE 517522) — deep research, 27 Sep 2026

Consolidated (India + Rajratan Thailand + Rajratan USA). Price ₹472, market cap ₹2,399 cr, trailing P/E 30.1, 52-week range ₹306–564 [Screener, 27 Sep 2026]. Calls on record: Q2 FY26 (31 Oct 2025) and Q4 FY26 (22 Apr 2026). No Q1 FY27 call transcript was found on BSE or Screener; the Q1 FY27 investor presentation (24 Jul 2026) is used for the latest quarter.

## Business and segments
- Single product in practice: tyre bead wire (the steel hoop that holds a tyre to the rim), 89.9% of FY26 revenue; the rest is other high-carbon wire [ICRA Jun-26]. Bead wire is 3–4% of tyre input cost but safety-critical, so approvals take years [Q2 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Plants: Pithampur/Indore 72,000 TPA (60,000 TPA bead wire); Chennai greenfield 30,000 TPA going to 60,000 TPA; Thailand 60,000 TPA (the only bead-wire maker in Thailand) [IP Q1FY27]. Group capacity use 71% in FY26 (70% FY25); Pithampur and Thailand at ~90%, Chennai exit rate 85–90% of its 30,000 t [AR FY26; Q4 call].
- FY26 revenue by geography: India ₹636 cr, Thailand ₹287 cr, USA ₹116 cr (₹23 cr in FY25), rest of world ₹117 cr; total ₹1,157 cr [AR FY26 note 50.3]. Exports 17% of revenue (14% FY25, 11% FY24) [AR FY26 integrated report]. Q1 FY27: India operations 61% of revenue, Thailand 35% [IP Q1FY27].
- Customers: MRF, Apollo, CEAT, Bridgestone, Continental, Balkrishna in India; Sumitomo and Yokohama in Thailand [CRISIL Jul-26; ICRA Jun-26]. Three customers each above 10% of revenue, together ₹390 cr (34%) [AR FY26 note 50.5].
- Market share in India back to 42–43% (had slipped to 35–37%; peak 43–44%) [Q4 call; ICRA Jun-26].
- New product: steel cord for conveyor belts (a "pilot" to learn wire-rope making), 10,000 TPA at Pithampur; trials in Q2 FY27; peak revenue ~₹150 cr at ~₹150/kg in about two years; industry margins ~20% [Q4 call].

## Key numbers (Screener consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Annual: FY22 sales 893, operating profit 182 (20%); FY23 895 / 162 (18%); FY24 890 / 128 (14%); FY25 935 / 127 (14%); FY26 1,156 / 140 (12%); net profit FY24 72, FY25 59, FY26 70; TTM sales 1,228, NP 80 [Screener]. Profit is still 44% below the FY22 peak of ₹124 cr.
- Quarters (sales / OP / OPM / NP): Q1 FY26 247 / 31 / 12.6% / 14; Q2 294 / 40 / 13.6% / 21; Q3 302 / 40 / 13.2% / 21; Q4 314 / 29 / 9.3% / 15; Q1 FY27 318 / 42 / 13.1% / 23 [Screener].
- Volumes: FY26 ~1.34 lakh t (+18%); Q4 FY26 36,484 t; Q1 FY27 33,300 t (+16% YoY; India 19,710 t, Thailand 13,590 t) [ICRA Jun-26; IP Q1FY27].
- Unit economics (my calculation from company figures): revenue per tonne ~₹86,100 in Q4 FY26 and ~₹95,600 in Q1 FY27; EBITDA per tonne ~₹7,800 in Q4 and ~₹12,500 in Q1 FY27 (₹10,800 in Q1 FY26) [IP Q1FY27]. Management said realisation had "gone beyond 90,000" [Q4 call].
- Q4 FY26 dip: wire-rod prices rose ~₹10,000/t between January and March and could not be passed on at once; raw material went from 57% to 63% of sales, costing 4–4.5 points of margin [Q4 call].
- Rajratan USA (new subsidiary, imports and pays US duty) booked ~₹30 cr of freight/logistics costs in H2 FY26 [Q4 call].
- Returns: ROCE 13% FY26 (40% FY22), ROE 11.5% [Screener].

## Guidance history
- Q2 FY26 call (Oct 2025): FY27 volume growth 15–20%; three-year vision 1.8–2.0 lakh t and ~₹2,000 cr revenue; group export target 35–40k t in FY27 [Q2 call].
- Q4 FY26 call (Apr 2026): FY27 volume 17–18% (~1.55 lakh t, excluding steel cord); Thailand only +10–14% (capacity-bound, to 55–56k t); Chennai ~35k t (from ~17k t); India exports ~15k t (from ~9k t); North America +30%, Europe +50%, SE Asia +10–15%; consolidated EBITDA margin 13.5–14% "safely", back to 13–13.5% in Q1 itself [Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27 delivery: volume +16% (slightly under the 17–18% run-rate); EBITDA margin 13.1% (on the promised 13–13.5%); PAT +70% [IP Q1FY27]. **Margin promise kept; volume a touch light.**
- The PLI subsidy (8% on incremental Chennai sales, ₹40–50 cr over five years if granted) is excluded from guidance because first- and second-year production targets were missed; the company has asked for the targets to be changed [Q4 call, official].

## Capex and capacity
- Steel cord: ~₹70 cr total, ~₹50 cr spent by March 2026, ~₹25 cr left (a shed rebuild delayed it); capitalisation by Q3 FY27 [Q4 call].
- Chennai Phase 2: ~₹25 cr of balancing equipment to reach 60,000 TPA by Q2 FY27; ramp will be gradual, not a one-year payback [Q4 call]. Management says no further capex is planned for FY27 beyond these two [Q4 call]; the Q1 presentation mentions "capacity expansions planned across Pithampur, Thailand and Chennai" and new land secured at Ratchaburi, Thailand [IP Q1FY27].
- CWIP ₹88 cr at March 2026 (about ₹50 cr steel cord, ₹20 cr Chennai) [Q4 call, official; Screener].
- Asset turns: fixed assets ₹619 cr against ₹1,156 cr sales (about 1.9x), well below the ~3.2x of FY22 (my calculation from Screener).

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹324 cr at March 2026 (₹237 cr a year earlier); cash ₹22.5 cr; debt/EBITDA ~2.3x; interest cover ~4.9–5.1x; gearing 0.5x [Screener; ICRA Jun-26; CRISIL Jul-26].
- About ₹50 cr of term loans were repaid in FY26 and another ~₹50 cr fall due in FY27, but working-capital debt will rise with US exports; management prefers 7–7.5% debt to equity [Q4 call].
- Cash flow from operations ₹75 cr FY26 (₹57 cr FY25); free cash flow −₹30 cr (FY25 −₹1 cr, FY24 −₹13 cr) [Screener].
- Working capital: debtor days 81 (71), inventory 54, payables 72, cash cycle 62 days [Screener]. US sales stretch the cycle: 50% US duty paid up front by the US subsidiary, 45–60 days at sea, then 30–60 days' credit [Q4 call].
- Dividend payout ~14% [Screener]. Promoter Sunil Chordia lent and was repaid ₹27 cr during FY26 (₹31 cr in FY25) [AR FY26 note 43].

## Moat evidence
- Approval barrier: every tyre SKU must be requalified to switch supplier; "a bead wire order is... a trust decision made once and honoured over a decade" [IP Q1FY27]. Product acceptance 99.82% in FY26 [AR FY26].
- Share: 42–43% of India; sole local maker in Thailand [ICRA Jun-26].
- Pricing: prices reset quarterly with a one-quarter lag; no compensation for the Q4 loss ("nobody gives compensation") [Q4 call; ICRA Jun-26]. Rajratan prices at "the higher end" versus new entrants [Q4 call, official].
- Cost/location: Chennai sits within 30 minutes to 12 hours of most southern tyre plants and 45–50 km from a port [AR FY26].
- Limits: industry capacity "much more than demand"; margins are unlikely to return to the 18–20% of FY22 [Q4 call; ICRA Jun-26].

## Risks and red flags
- Margin structurally lower: EBITDA margin fell from ~20% (FY22) to 12% (FY26) because of Chinese competition, domestic entrants and Chennai start-up costs [ICRA Jun-26].
- Rising debt and negative free cash flow for three years [Screener].
- Trade policy: US Section 232 tariffs (duty borne through the US subsidiary), Thai export exposure, past Chinese dumping [Q4 call; ICRA Jun-26].
- PLI non-compliance — subsidy at risk [Q4 call].
- Concentration: three customers = 34% of revenue [AR FY26].
- Related parties: small — consultancy fees to LFC Consulting Practice LLP ₹1.66 cr; rent to the chairman's father ₹1.49 cr; CSR through Rajratan Foundation ₹1.31 cr [AR FY26 note 43]. Auditor's report unmodified; key audit matter is revenue recognition only [AR FY26]. Contingent liabilities negligible (a ₹2.26 cr electricity-bill dispute in the MP High Court, mostly reimbursable; ₹0.6 cr tax) [AR FY26 note 44]. No pledge shown [Screener].
- Rating trend improved: CRISIL moved the outlook from Negative to Stable (A+/A1) on 2 Jul 2026; ICRA assigned A+ (Stable)/A1 on 18 Jun 2026 [CRISIL Jul-26; ICRA Jun-26].

## What changed between the two calls
- Q2 FY26 (Oct 2025) was upbeat on volume (record 32,000 t quarter; Thailand at 91% utilisation) with a 1.8–2.0 lakh t / ₹2,000 cr three-year vision [Q2 call].
- Q4 FY26 (Apr 2026) admitted a margin miss from the steel spike and a new ~₹60 cr-a-year US logistics cost line; volume guidance was made more precise (1.55 lakh t) and margins pinned at 13.5–14%, well below old 18–20% levels. The ₹2,000 cr vision was not repeated [Q4 call].
- Since then Q1 FY27 delivered the promised margin recovery (13.1%) and a record ₹318 cr quarter, and both rating agencies turned more positive [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Jul-26].

## My read
The volume engine works — 18% growth in FY26 and 16% in Q1 FY27, with India share regained and US sales built from almost nothing — but it is being bought at lower margins and with more debt, so profit is still well below FY22. The Q1 FY27 quarter showed the margin can come back to 13% once steel costs are passed on. At a trailing P/E of about 30, the price needs roughly 18% a year of profit growth ((30.1 × 0.1726)^0.1 − 1); plausible if Chennai fills up and steel cord works, stretched if margins stall at 12–13% and debt keeps rising. Positive free cash flow in FY27, when the capex tapers, is the test; a second steel shock or US tariff change would hurt.
