## Business and segments
- India's largest maker of car and light-commercial-vehicle exhaust (emission) systems. The company claims ~30% value share. [IP Q1FY27]
- FY26 revenue by business [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]:
  - commercial-vehicle emission systems 44%;
  - car (PV) emission systems 43%;
  - suspension and lightweighting (control arms and links) 9%;
  - supply chain 2%;
  - off-highway, gensets and exports 1%;
  - other 1%.
  - The presentation splits FY26 revenue by end-market as PV 52%, CV 44%, others 4%.
- Lightweighting has ~14% value share in car and light-truck control arms and links (12.5% in FY25). A licensing deal with Donghee Industrial (Korea) adds subframes and torsion beams. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Revenue is inflated by precious-metal catalyst costs that customers direct and pay for. Gross profit is the true growth measure:
  - gross margin 20.2% in Q1 FY27 vs 25.1% a year earlier;
  - catalyst substrate prices are set by customers and passed through with no lag.
  - [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Joint ventures:
  - Purem (formerly Eberspächer), for truck engines above 4 litres. Sharda has no management control and gets only a share of profit.
  - JTPL (profitable).
  - [Q1 call]
- Customers: mainly Korean and Indian car makers (Hyundai, Kia, M&M, Tata) and Indian truck makers. It does not supply Maruti Suzuki (called "the key customer of the industry which we do not serve") or Japanese car makers. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Nine plants: Chennai 2, Pune/Chakan 3, Nashik 2, Sanand 1, Uttarakhand 1. R&D in Chennai and Namyang (Korea); 125+ R&D staff; 24 patents filed, 4 granted. [IP Q1FY27]

## Guidance history
- Management gives **no numerical revenue or margin guidance**: "we do not provide guidance". [Q1 call]
- Promises on the Q4 call, and what Q1 showed:
  - FY27 capex ₹90–110 cr, plus separate spending on new sites (Uttarakhand ~₹20 cr). Not repeated in August; the Uttarakhand plant was said to be on plan.
  - North American engine/genset export orders (~$10.7m a year, $58.5m over the orders' life): first-sample shipments in Q2 and start of production (SOP) in Q3 FY27, after an earlier slip from Q2. In August, SOPs were given as "across Q3 and Q4 FY27", so timing is slightly vaguer.
  - Lightweighting sales about 3x the FY25 level by FY28. Kept.
  - Chakan-3 lightweighting plant: SOP done and ramping (delivered).
  - Temperature-control tube order for an off-highway maker: ramping (delivered).
  - Donghee: products were shown to OEMs in Q1, but "still early to comment on commercial timelines".
  - [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Q1 delivery: gross profit grew 8% against the served market's 8–10%. The CEO/CFO said this was "broadly in line". But margins fell, a one-off premium-freight cost hit (not quantified), and PAT fell. [Q1 call]
- Longer-range target: a lightweighting market worth ₹8,000–9,000 cr in five years, where Sharda aims for a "mid-teen to high-teen" share. Content per vehicle would rise from ₹2,000–8,000 to ₹6,000–18,000 once subframes and torsion beams are added. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]

## Capex and capacity
- Capex guide FY27: ₹90–110 cr (higher than usual, for R&D and new SOPs), plus sites as needed. Gross tangible assets rose from ₹361 cr (FY22) to ₹579 cr (FY26). [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Plants are fully backward-integrated (tube mills, 50+ stamping presses). Management says emission capacity "can grow without capex". [IP Q1FY27]
- Chakan-3 (lightweighting) is modular, adding capacity only against confirmed orders. Uttarakhand (~₹20 cr) moves existing business to sit next to a customer that is relocating. [Q1 call]
- Order pipeline yet to start supply (annual value at peak, from the presentation), totalling ~$34.7m a year (~₹300 cr at current rates) once all have ramped up:
  - NA engine maker $7m (SOP Q3 FY27), $3m (Q3 FY27) and $0.7m (Q4 FY27);
  - PV control arms $8.3m (Q1 FY28) and $3m (Q3 FY27);
  - PV links $5.6m (Q1 FY28);
  - heavy-industry emission adjacency $4.8m (Q4 FY27);
  - North American agricultural OEM $2.3m (Q1 FY28).
  - [IP Q1FY27]
- Orders already in production include control arms at $4m, $4.2m and $5m a year, and off-highway tubes at $3.5m. [IP Q1FY27]

## Moat evidence
- Share: ~30% of car and light-truck exhaust systems (by value), and ~14% of control arms and links, rising. [IP Q1FY27]
- Pass-through: catalysts and steel are back-to-back ("there is no lag in steel prices"). This protects gross profit per unit. [Q1 call]
- Capability for new emission rules:
  - WLTP (a new emissions test) replacement orders from leading car makers in Q1, ahead of BS6.3 from 1 Apr 2027;
  - Euro-7 development work with one customer;
  - tractor Stage V, and diesel particulate filter/catalyst systems for trucks and gensets.
  - [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Returns: ROCE 32–38% and ROE 26–30% over FY22–FY26 on a capital-light base (company figures). [IP Q1FY27]
- Weak points:
  - no business with Maruti or Japanese makers, which the company itself calls a long-term, not immediate, opportunity;
  - EV exposure only through lightweighting.
  - [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Growth lag**: gross profit grew only 8% in both FY26 and Q1 FY27, while car and light-truck production grew 9–17%. The excuses are the unserved OEMs, a fire at a customer's supplier, and premium freight. Analysts pressed on this in both calls. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Margins**: EBITDA margin fell from 14.0% (FY25) to 12.3% (FY26) and 10.2% (Q1 FY27). Part of this is optical, from catalyst pass-through inflating revenue, but absolute EBITDA grew only 5–6%. [IP Q1FY27]
- **EV risk**: 87% of revenue is from exhaust systems, which pure EVs do not need. Lightweighting is only 9%. [Q4 call]
- **Export orders slipping**: the NA engine order moved from Q2 to Q3/Q4 FY27. Early export volumes are small next to a ₹3,400 cr base. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **M&A**: management says it is now "more assertive" on acquisitions (tech from China and Korea, EV/hybrid). This creates a capital-allocation risk for the ~₹1,000 cr of cash. [Q1 call]
- **Governance**:
  - Promoter family holds 64.31% (no change).
  - MD Ajay Relan was paid ₹7.5 cr in FY26 and is proposed for re-appointment as Executive Chairperson. His son Aashim Relan is Group CEO.
  - A promoter associate, Relan Industrial Finance, is disclosed.
  - The auditor's opinion is unqualified. Key audit matters are investment valuation and revenue recognition (price-variation estimates).
  - Tax disputes are small: GST/VAT/excise items of tens of lakhs to a few crores each, plus ₹12.2 cr of disputed income-tax additions.
  - [AR FY26; Screener]
- CRISIL's latest rating in Screener's list is from Jul 2025 (older than 12 months), so I did not use it.

## What changed between the two calls
- The Q4 tone was "CAFE-3 tailwind"; Q1 was more defensive about slow growth, with an unquantified one-off freight cost.
- New in Q1: an entry into premium two-wheeler exhausts (domestic, above a certain engine size), an "assertive" M&A stance, multiple WLTP replacement orders, and a North American agricultural OEM order ($2.3m a year).
- The tractor-muffler opportunity from TREM IIIA (19–37 kW) was sized at only ₹60–100 cr.
- Export SOP dates moved from "Q3" to "Q3 and Q4 FY27".
- The revised CAFE-III draft (16 Jul 2026) cut the super-credits for strong hybrids and flex-fuel vehicles. That is slightly negative for engine-based content, but management said the multi-technology approach is kept.

## My read
Sharda is a cash-rich, high-return business:
- ROE 25%;
- ~₹1,000 cr of cash and investments;
- negative working capital;
- a 33% dividend payout.

But its core exhaust business is growing no faster than its narrow served market, and margins have slipped three years running.

Growth now depends on:
- lightweighting orders scaling up (~$20m a year of new control-arm and link business starting in Q3 FY27 to Q1 FY28);
- export SOPs, which have already slipped once;
- BS6.3/BS7 content gains.

At ~16x trailing earnings (Screener), with about a sixth of the market cap in cash, the price needs only modest growth. The main risks are a poorly priced acquisition and the long-run EV threat.

What would change my view:
- positive: gross-profit growth of 12%+ for two quarters as the new SOPs land;
- negative: further export or lightweighting SOP slips, or an expensive acquisition.
