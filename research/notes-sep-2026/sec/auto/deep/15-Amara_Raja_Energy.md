# Amara Raja Energy & Mobility (NSE: ARE&M) — deep research, 27 Sep 2026

India's second-largest lead-acid battery maker (Amaron brand) after Exide, building a lithium-ion cell and energy-storage business through subsidiary Amara Raja Advanced Cell Technologies (ARACT). Galla family promoters hold 32.86% (via Amara Raja Enterprises, formerly RNGalla Family Pvt Ltd). Price ₹792, market cap ₹14,495 cr, 52-week range ₹670–1,023 [Screener].

## Business and segments
- FY26 revenue ₹13,814 cr: **lead-acid ₹13,005 cr** (+5%), **new energy ₹809 cr** (+61%). Segment profit: lead-acid ₹1,270 cr; new energy **loss ₹135 cr** (FY25 loss ₹46 cr). New-energy assets doubled to ₹1,844 cr [AR FY26 consolidated segment note].
- Q1 FY27 revenue ₹4,215 cr (+24%): lead-acid ~95% (+22%), new energy ₹209 cr (+70%+) [official Q1 transcript].
- Lead-acid detail Q1 FY27: 4W/2W aftermarket volume +15%; OEM 4W +24%, 2W +35%; home energy (tubular/inverter) +60%, tubular plant at 100% with trading = 15% of lead-acid revenue; automotive exports −20% (Middle East); industrial +2% (UPS +10%, telecom lead-acid falling); lithium telecom volumes +50%, telecom share (lead + lithium) >60% [official Q1 transcript].
- Exports ~12% of FY26 revenue; Middle East 3–4% of revenue [Q4 call; CRISIL Mar-26]. 70+ export countries; a US subsidiary (initial US$5 m) is being set up [CRISIL Mar-26].
- Capacity: automotive ~70 m units, industrial ~2.4 bn Ah; lithium pack capacity 1.5 GWh (mobility) + 1.2 GWh (stationary) [IP Q1FY27].
- Distribution: 100,000+ points of sale, 1,000+ Power Zone stores [CRISIL Mar-26].

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly revenue: Jun-25 3,401 | Sep-25 3,467 | Dec-25 3,410 | Mar-26 3,536 | Jun-26 4,215. Operating profit: 364 | 374 | 374 | 386 | 406. Net profit: 165 | 276 | 140 | 314 | 191 [Screener]. (Sep-25 and Mar-26 profits include large other income — Mar-26 includes a tubular-plant fire insurance claim net of a labour-code charge [Q4 call].)
- Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin 9.6% consolidated, 10.1% standalone — lowest in years; ~0.9 points from brand spend (IPL), the Amaron Assist service pilot and "Factory of the Future" work (~₹35 cr), plus poly, sulphuric acid and alloy inflation, warranty re-provisioning and freight; ~0.5 points more from new-energy start-up costs [official Q1 transcript].
- Annual: revenue FY24 11,708 | FY25 12,846 | FY26 13,814; operating profit 1,662 | 1,622 | 1,505; margin 14% | 13% | 11%; net profit 934 | 945 | 896 [Screener]. Profit has fallen three years in a row on the operating line.
- Returns: ROCE 12% (FY26) vs 19–20% in FY23–FY24; ROE 7% [Screener].
- Unit economics: BESS containers sell at about US$100–120/kWh; pack and BESS operating margins ~5–7% (BESS 7–8% at best); Indian cells cost "China plus 15% to 20%" [official Q1 and Q4 transcripts]. Lead and alloys ~70% of material cost, plastics ~10% [Q4 call]. Captive recycling added ~0.5 points of margin in Q4 [Q4 call].

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (26 May 2026): FY27 lead-acid volume growth "mid-to-high single digit"; still "hopeful" of 13–14% EBITDA margin even at high lead prices; price hikes 5–6% taken in Q4, 2–3% more possible; FY27 capex ₹1,500–1,700 cr (lead-acid ~₹400 cr, new energy ₹1,100–1,200 cr); battery-breaking (recycling) plant to stabilise "next quarter"; BESS 5 GWh (10 GWh ultimate) production by end-CY26; Giga-1 (2 GWh NMC 21700 cells) production June 2027; 16 GWh target at Divitipally "unchanged" with mix shifting to ~1/3 ESS [official Q4 transcript].
- Q1 FY27 call (11 Aug 2026): revenue beat (+24%); margin fell to 9.6% and the 13–14% target was not repeated; ~3% price hike in June, 2–3% more in August, B2B pass-through lags; capex set at the top of the range, ₹1,700 cr (new energy ₹1,300 cr), ₹450 cr spent in Q1; lead-acid business can grow revenue 9–10% a year over the medium term ("I am not calling this a guidance"); battery-breaking still in trial production as scrap lead now costs about LME; Giga-1 equipment due Q3 FY27, commercialisation H1 FY28; 16 GWh "milestone… can change", capacity to follow demand, ESS cells may come before EV cells; R&D on cells ₹100–150 cr this year [official Q1 transcript].
- Verdict: **growth beaten; margin target effectively dropped; recycling milestone missed; capex raised; long-term cell capacity target softened**.

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ~₹600 cr in lead-acid (~₹500 cr net of insurance) plus new-energy projects [Q4 call]. CWIP ₹1,658 cr at Mar-26 (₹641 cr at Mar-24) [Screener].
- FY27 ₹1,700 cr: Giga-1 cell plant, 10 GWh BESS integration plant (initial ₹250–300 cr), E-Positive research lab, customer qualification plant (inaugurated Jul-26), recycling [official Q1 transcript].
- Total new-energy plan ~₹9,000–9,500 cr to 16 GWh by FY30; initial risk capital ~₹2,500 cr for five facilities; ~₹1,500 cr already invested in ARACT [official Q1 transcript; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Target economics at 8–10 GWh scale: EBITDA 10–11%, "low double-digit ROCE" [official Q4 transcript].
- Commitments: capital contracts ₹299 cr (standalone) and a committed ₹495.5 cr investment to SIPCOT Tamil Nadu for leased land at Cheyyar (recycling site) [AR FY26 note 31].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹406 cr (Mar-26) vs ₹157 cr (Mar-24); net worth ~₹8,100 cr [Screener]. CRISIL AA+/Stable/A1+ reaffirmed, bank lines raised to ₹665 cr; "negligible debt"; cash generation ₹1,200–1,300 cr a year expected [CRISIL Mar-26].
- FY26 cash from operations ₹1,118 cr; free cash flow **−₹220 cr** (first negative since FY22) [Screener].
- Working capital: inventory 98 days, debtors 33, payables 46; cash cycle 85 days [Screener]. BESS may need ~90–100 days [official Q1 transcript].
- Dividend payout ~22% [Screener]. Management: "at least ₹700–800 cr" a year of post-tax, post-dividend cash to fund new energy before external funding is needed [official Q1 transcript].

## Moat evidence
- Duopoly with Exide in lead-acid; strong Amaron brand and distribution [CRISIL Mar-26].
- Share: telecom batteries >60% combined (lead + lithium) through the lithium switch [official Q1 transcript]; OEM volumes growing 24–35%.
- Pricing: successive price hikes of 5–6% (Q4), ~3% (June), 2–3% (August) — pass-through works in the aftermarket with a lag, not fully in B2B [official Q1 transcript].
- Cost: captive recycling was meant to be a lasting advantage, but with scrap lead now at LME parity it adds little today [official Q1 transcript].
- New energy has no moat yet: Gotion technology licensing is blocked by Chinese export curbs; own R&D ("Plan B"); cost disadvantage 15–20% vs Chinese cells [official Q4 transcript].

## Risks and red flags
- **Related-party dealings (promoter-group firms), FY26 standalone:** plastic components bought from Mangal Industries ₹381 cr (₹347 cr FY25) plus ₹55 cr of assets; plant construction/assets from Amara Raja Infra ₹213 cr and services ₹132 cr; goods from Amara Raja Electronics ₹110 cr. ~₹890 cr in all [AR FY26 note 35]. Goodwill of ₹428 cr arose from buying Mangal's plastics business [CRISIL Mar-26; AR FY26 note 4].
- CARO remark: title deeds of some land not in the company's name — e.g. 18.94 acres (₹77.8 cr) and 15.66 acres of leasehold still registered to Mangal Industries [AR FY26 CARO; note 3]. A subsidiary reported cash losses (₹19.6 cr per CARO xvii) [AR FY26 consolidated CARO annexure].
- Contingent liabilities (standalone) ~₹370 cr vs ~₹250 cr, "other claims" up to ₹100 cr from ₹9 cr; includes a ₹10.5 cr APGPCL power claim with a lien on the company's investment, in arbitration [AR FY26 note 31].
- Auditors Price Waterhouse Chartered Accountants LLP with K.S. Rao & Co: unmodified; KAMs on the warranty provision (₹434 cr) and revenue cut-off [AR FY26 auditor's report].
- Regulatory: APPCB (pollution board) closure orders of Apr-2021 were revoked on 18 Jul 2026 — an overhang removed [official Q1 transcript].
- Execution: first-time cell manufacturing without a technology partner; dependence on Chinese equipment engineers and cathode supply; demand for Indian cells depends on localisation mandates [official Q4 transcript].
- Low promoter holding (32.9%) and falling FII holding (24.6% → 17.3% in three years) [Screener].

## My read
The lead-acid core is still growing well (22% in Q1 on OEM, home-energy and aftermarket strength) but its margin has slid from 14% to under 10% while the company pours ₹1,700 cr a year into a lithium business that loses money and has lost its Chinese technology partner. Cash flow turned negative in FY26 and promoter-group firms remain large suppliers. The price needs only high-single-digit earnings growth, which the core can deliver if margins recover to 11–12%. The view improves if price hikes restore margins above 11% by Q3 and BESS starts on time with orders; it worsens if new-energy losses widen past ~₹200 cr a year or capex rises without customer contracts.
