## Business and segments
- World's largest two-wheeler control-cable supplier (CRISIL). The company calls itself "arguably the largest manufacturer of control cables in the world by volume", making 300m+ cables a year. [CRISIL Jul-26; IP Q1FY27 FAQ]
- Four divisions, renamed from Q1 FY27 [IP Q1FY27]:
  - **GCM**, Global Cables & Mechatronics (was SCD, now includes the acquired SCS). Plants in India (export unit), Mexico, USA, Hungary, Morocco and China. Q1 revenue ₹602.5 cr (+27.6%, about 23% after adjusting for SCS China/Canada being consolidated only from end-May 2025). EBITDA ₹75.8 cr, margin 12.6% (5.8% a year ago).
  - **ICM**, India Cables & Mechatronics (was DCD; also carries the tech-centre and corporate costs). Revenue ₹331.0 cr (+20.8%); EBITDA ₹42.5 cr (+4.2%); margin 12.8% (14.9%).
  - **PLE**, Phoenix Lighting & Electricals: halogen lamps, LED drop-in bulbs, horns. Revenue ₹91.1 cr (+5.4%); EBITDA ₹6.1 cr (−45%); margin 6.7% (12.8%).
  - **SED**, Sensors, Electronics & Displays: digital clusters, electronic throttles, sensors. Revenue ₹45.0 cr (+48%); EBITDA ₹4.2 cr (+100%); margin 9.3% (6.9%).
- Revenue by customer location, FY26: India ₹1,668.5 cr (44%), USA ₹896.2 cr (23%), Morocco ₹38.2 cr, rest of world ₹1,221.9 cr (32%). India was 46% in Q1 FY27. No customer is above 10% of group revenue. [AR FY26 note 42; IP Q1FY27]
- Content per vehicle is moving up: from a ~₹100 cable to a ₹400 combined braking system (CBS) and ₹1,500–2,000 brake systems; from speedometer cables to digital clusters at ₹700–7,000. [Q1 call]
- Capacity: Morocco runs on one shift (plenty of room). India export unit SAL is short of space, so SAL-2 is planned in Chennai. The SED plant is sized for ~₹250 cr of revenue, and demand is higher. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Key numbers
- Quarterly revenue (Screener, ₹ cr): Mar-25 877, Jun-25 863, Sep-25 941, Dec-25 979, Mar-26 1,042, Jun-26 1,070. Operating margin 10%, 9%, 11%, 10%, 12%, 12%. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27: revenue ₹1,070 cr (+24%); operating EBITDA ₹128.7 cr (+57.5%), margin 12.0% vs 9.5%. Net profit ₹52 cr, up only 8% from ₹48 cr, because other income fell to ₹4 cr from ₹39 cr (forex restatement, Morocco subsidy last year) and tax was 32%. Standalone EBITDA ₹60.3 cr, flat (−0.3%), margin 12.8% vs 15.5%. [IP Q1FY27; Screener; Q1 call]
- Annual (Screener, ₹ cr): sales FY24 2,896 → FY25 3,277 → FY26 3,825 (+17%); operating profit 324 → 333 → 397; OPM 11% → 10% → 10%; net profit 167 → 99 → 183. The 5-year profit CAGR is only 5%, and 3-year ROE averages 9%. [Screener]
- FY26 excluding SCS: revenue ₹3,377 cr (+8.7%), EBITDA ₹443 cr (+10.5%). The SCS acquisition added revenue at close to zero margin: its EBITDA went from about −20% in Q1 FY26 to +2% in Q4 FY26. [Q4 call; Q4 press release]
- FY26 standalone: revenue ₹1,840 cr (+7.1%), EBITDA ₹305 cr (+2.4%). India growth was held back by price cuts given to customers. Management says it was ~12% on a like-for-like basis. [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- FY27 guidance (press release 25 May 2026; repeated on the Q1 call as "holds good"):
  - Group revenue up by double digits; consolidated operating EBITDA margin 12–13.5%.
  - GCM: double-digit growth, margin 10–12% (from ~6%).
  - ICM: double-digit growth, stable margin (~15%).
  - PLE: double-digit growth, stable margin (~12%).
  - SED: strong double-digit growth, margin ~10%.
  - [Q4 PR; Q1 call]
- Q1 delivery against that:
  - Group: +24% growth at 12.0% margin (on track).
  - GCM: 12.6%, above its range; the chairman would not raise the range until year-end.
  - ICM: 12.8% (below). Management blames wage increases, especially in the NCR region, that OEMs have not yet paid for; it expects to recover "most of the 100 bps" by Q2/Q3.
  - PLE: 6.7%, well below.
  - SED: 9.3%, roughly on track.
  - [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- SCS turnaround: in FY26 management promised SCS would turn EBITDA-positive by Q4. It did (+2%), so that promise was kept. From Q1 FY27, SCS is no longer reported separately. [Q4 call]
- Tariffs: in May, management expected "full resolution within the next few months". In August, the chairman said most tariff money received will have to be passed back to customers, and the net gain "would not be very significant". A $6m US Section 301 refund claim is not in the guidance. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- STC building: originally due earlier, now due Q3 FY27 (it slipped once). [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- FY27 capex ₹200 cr: India ~₹80 cr, global ~₹50 cr, tech centre ~₹50 cr, IT ~₹15–16 cr. By asset type: land and buildings ~₹80 cr (including a land purchase at AURIC, Maharashtra), plant and machinery ₹100–105 cr, software ₹15 cr. [Q4 call; Q4 PR]
- SED expansion: the unit will move to leased premises for about two years while the old building is demolished and rebuilt as a three-storey electronics plant. The building costs ₹30–40 cr over two years. The move is planned "within 6 months" (said in Aug 2026). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- SAL-2 plant in Chennai, on land the company already owns near the port, for new export business. [Q4 call]
- Joint venture with Chuo Spring (Japan): 50:50, for transmission cables in India, under an MoU signed in FY25. [AR FY26 note]
- CRISIL expects no big debt-funded capex or acquisitions. It sees annual cash accruals of ₹320–410 cr against term-debt repayments of ₹35–70 cr. [CRISIL Jul-26]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Company-reported gross debt ₹775.5 cr at Jun-26 (long-term ₹219.9 cr, short-term ₹555.6 cr). Mutual funds and bonds ₹243.1 cr, so net debt is ~₹532 cr. Screener borrowings (including leases) ₹993 cr at Mar-26, up from ₹817 cr. [IP Q1FY27; Screener]
- Cash flow is weakening:
  - Cash from operations (CFO): FY24 ₹249 cr → FY25 ₹185 cr → FY26 ₹171 cr.
  - CFO as a share of operating profit fell from 101% to 73%.
  - Free cash flow: ₹158 cr → ₹74 cr → ₹54 cr.
  - [Screener]
- Working capital is getting worse: inventory days 108 (FY24) → 123 → 134 (FY26); cash conversion cycle 103 → 114 → 131 days. Debtor days are 68. [Screener]
- CRISIL figures: gearing 0.67x, interest cover 6.6x, liquid investments ₹336 cr at Mar-26, bank-limit use 70%. [CRISIL Jul-26]
- Dividend ₹3.50 a share for FY26 (₹3.00 in FY25), a payout of 26%. [Q4 PR; Screener]
- Ratings: CRISIL AA/Stable (reaffirmed 16 Jul 2026). ICRA and India Ratings also rate the company. [CRISIL Jul-26; Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Scale leader: world's largest two-wheeler control-cable maker; 70–80% supply share at many Indian customers (the analyst's figure, which management did not dispute). [CRISIL Jul-26; Q4 call]
- Global footprint wins business: May 2026 wins worth ~$8.45m a year, ~$55m over the life of the contracts:
  - a North American major's EV cable, $5.25m a year, $37m lifetime, from the Matamoros plant (its largest EV cable contract);
  - a European luxury OEM, $2m a year;
  - a Japanese OEM, $1.2m a year.
  - [News: Trade Brains, 1 Jun 2026; Business Upturn]
- Chinese EV leader (described on the call as the world's largest EV maker): ~25 cable projects won, 5–6 launched. Lone Star China is growing ~20% after two flat years. Target is 20% of that customer's cable business in 2–3 years. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Consolidation benefits survivors: the world's largest halogen-lamp maker in Europe went insolvent, and Phoenix is picking up its orders ("last man standing"). [Q4 call]
- Pricing power is partial: raw materials pass through, but wage increases are "strongly debated" by OEMs. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **GST dispute**: two show-cause notices, for Jul 2017–Mar 2023, on how some products are classified. They demand ₹137.4 cr, about three-quarters of FY26 consolidated net profit (₹183 cr). The Karnataka High Court has granted an interim stay and the company expects no liability. No provision has been made. [AR FY26 note 39]
- Corporate guarantees of ₹553.7 cr ($58.5m) for Suprajit USA's bank loans, plus comfort letters to loss-making Luxlite (Luxembourg) and Suprajit USA. The investment in Luxlite has been written down by ₹79.2 cr. [AR FY26 standalone notes]
- Goodwill ₹158.0 cr (₹111.9 cr a year earlier) on Wescon and Luxlite; testing it for impairment is a key audit matter. [AR FY26 consolidated auditor's report]
- **Much of the group is audited by other firms**: 15 subsidiaries, with ₹2,368.6 cr of revenue (about 62% of group revenue) and ₹1,927.6 cr of assets, are not audited by the group auditor. For these, the consolidated opinion relies on other auditors. [AR FY26 auditor's report, Other Matter]
- Wage inflation in India and slow recovery from OEMs. Phoenix margin halved. Tariff refunds net out to little. [Q1 call]
- Cash conversion is falling (CFO 73% of EBITDA) and inventory is rising, even as debt rose ₹176 cr in FY26. [Screener]
- Disclosure: SCS is no longer reported separately, and the company gives no order book or content-per-vehicle data. An analyst asked for better disclosure of order wins. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Promoter holding is 45.26% and edging up; no pledge is disclosed in the annual report. [Screener; AR FY26]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone went from relief (the SCS turnaround) to growth ("from fighting the tariff war to competing for order wins").
- GCM margin more than doubled to 12.6%, earlier than guided.
- The new problem is India: standalone EBITDA was flat despite 20% revenue growth, hurt by wage inflation.
- Tariff-recovery hopes were toned down.
- SED capacity expansion moved to a "war footing", with the plant to be rebuilt. Three new telematics projects were won.
- Other income collapsed, so profit growth (8%) lagged EBITDA growth (57%).

## My read
The operating turn is real. Group margin is back to 12% and GCM is at 12.6%. Revenue growth of 17–24% comes from new global contracts, not from the market. But reported profit, return on equity (13%) and free cash flow (₹54 cr in FY26) are still weak, and working capital and debt are rising. At about 36x trailing profit (Screener), the stock needs the 12–13.5% margin to hold and profit to compound in the high teens for several years. Recovery of the India wage costs in Q2/Q3 is the first test; if it fails, FY27 margin drifts to the bottom of the range. A clean resolution of the ₹137 cr GST notice and a turn back to positive cash flow would strengthen the case. A second quarter of flat standalone EBITDA, or further borrowing, would weaken it.
