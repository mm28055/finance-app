# Craftsman Automation — deep research (as of 27 Sep 2026)

Screener code CRAFTSMAN (BSE 543276).

## Business and segments
- Three segments: Aluminium products (die-cast and machined parts for 2W/4W, alloy wheels, industrial castings), Powertrain (machined cylinder blocks, heads, camshafts, transmission parts for CVs, tractors, off-highway, gensets; now large stationary-engine parts), Industrial & Engineering (storage/racking/ASRS, material handling, contract manufacturing, special-purpose machines). 28 plants in India, 1 in Germany, 2 under construction (Hosur, Chennai). [IP Q1FY27]
- Q1 FY27 segment revenue / EBIT (₹ cr): Aluminium 1,479 (+38% YoY) / 150 (+39%); Powertrain 623 (+26%) / 114 (+50%); Industrial & Engineering 330 (+53%) / 30 (from 5). Mix: aluminium 61%, powertrain 26%, industrial 13%. [IP Q1FY27]
- FY26 mix (CRISIL): aluminium ~59% (grew ~58%), powertrain 27% (+20%), industrial 14% (+30%); autos >75% of revenue. [CRISIL Jun-26]
- Aluminium is split across three entities: Craftsman standalone (~43% of segment), DR Axion (~31%; cylinder blocks/heads for Hyundai, Kia, M&M; FY26 turnover ₹1,619 cr) and Sunbeam Lightweighting (~26%; Hero, Maruti; FY26 turnover ₹1,397 cr, loss ₹40 cr). Segment is 4W 50%, 2W 46%. DR Axion and Sunbeam are being merged (NCLT Chandigarh; expected by end-FY27). [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 Directors' Report; CRISIL Jun-26]
- Powertrain: 81% Craftsman India, 19% Craftsman Germany (Fronberg foundry bought via asset purchase on 1 Oct 2024; FY26 turnover ₹346 cr, profit ₹16.7 cr). [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- Capacity utilisation: powertrain ~70% (optimum 80%), aluminium >80%, alloy wheels ~3 million/yr exit rate in March against 5.8 million capacity, aiming for ~4 million in FY27; DR Axion 80–85%; Sunbeam being cut from ~70% to 45–50% as loss-making lines are dropped. [Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Key numbers (Screener consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarters (sales / operating profit / OPM / net profit): Jun-25 1,784 / 265 / 15% / 70; Sep-25 2,002 / 302 / 15% / 91; Dec-25 2,057 / 312 / 15% / 107; Mar-26 2,226 / 359 / 16% / 116; Jun-26 2,432 / 384 / 16% / 151. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27 YoY: sales +36%, operating profit +45%, net profit +116%. Company-reported EBITDA (incl. other income) ₹408 cr (+51%, 16.8%). [Screener; IP Q1FY27]
- Annual: FY24 sales 4,452 / OP 879 (20%) / PAT 337; FY25 5,690 / 833 (15%) / 201; FY26 8,069 / 1,238 (15%) / 384. Sales have almost doubled in two years through acquisitions (DR Axion, Sunbeam, Germany) and new aluminium plants, but margin fell from 20–24% to 15–16%. [Screener]
- Interest ₹86 cr and depreciation ₹120 cr a quarter absorb more than half of operating profit; ROCE 14%, ROE ~12%. [Screener]
- Unit economics: alloy-wheel revenue ~₹280 cr in FY26; Bhiwadi plant high single-digit to low double-digit EBIT, Hosur single-digit while ramping. Aluminium segment EBIT ~10%. [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (8 May 2026): FY27 revenue growth "truly double digit, most probably, in the mid-teens" at current aluminium prices; powertrain double-digit; aluminium business to $1 bn in 2–3 years (aluminium revenue "around ₹6,500 cr in FY27", explicitly not guidance); Sunbeam traction "from Q2"; net debt/EBITDA "already at 2.43", below 2 in FY27 and then 1.5; aluminium margin will not improve 1–2 points in FY27 because of new capex; capex decision in September. [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 call (30 Jul 2026): Q1 grew 36% — well ahead of the mid-teens range; Sunbeam restructuring slipped: 90% done "by December", mid-teens Sunbeam EBITDA margin at Q4 exit (from ~4% in FY26); FY27 capex ~₹1,500 cr (₹1,000 cr standalone, ₹430 cr DR Axion, maintenance at Sunbeam); stationary-engine target of $100 m revenue by FY30 reiterated (30% of production in FY28, a further 50% in FY29). [Q1 call]
- Track record: revenue beat; Sunbeam timeline missed (Q2 → Q4); Gurugram land sale still not signed after several quarters ("close to signing a deal"); alloy wheels on track. Guidance status: growth kept (Q1 beat), Sunbeam timing cut back.

## Capex and capacity
- FY27 capex ~₹1,500 cr "conservatively", may be raised at year-end; standalone has averaged >₹1,000 cr a year; maintenance capex alone ₹250–300 cr against depreciation near ₹500 cr. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- New projects: Hosur Unit 3 (HPDC for 2W/4W, phased over 5–6 quarters, built ahead of orders); DR Axion's Sriperumbudur greenfield (50 acres bought for ~₹150 cr through two land-holding companies, Suprash and Srikara, acquired Dec 2025; commissioning targeted December 2026); Nagpur and Ludhiana facilities; Kothavadi foundry plus Arasur machining for large stationary engines (data-centre gensets). New German step-down subsidiary Craftsman Fronberg Precision GmbH incorporated Aug 2026. [Q1 call; Q4 call; CRISIL Jun-26; BSE announcement 28 Aug 2026 via Screener]
- Stationary engines: six large customers; orders from five, sixth expected; the first four fill the $100 m target; pilot machining for two. Phase 2 decision due "by September" 2026. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- A greenfield plant now costs ₹100–250 cr for infrastructure alone; management argues capacity built in 2016 would cost 5–7x more today. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Payback: management says return ratios on the new powertrain business will match current powertrain levels "within a couple of years". No project IRRs given.

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Consolidated debt ₹3,623 cr at Mar-26 (long-term ₹2,723 cr, short-term ₹618 cr, leases ₹283 cr); standalone short-term debt rose to ₹920 cr in May 2026 as West Asia input costs forced inventory build-up. Net debt/EBITDA 2.64x and interest cover 4.2x in FY26 (CRISIL). [CRISIL Jun-26]
- QIP of ₹2,000 cr completed 18 Jun 2026 (the second after ₹1,200 cr in Jun 2024); ₹1,500 cr earmarked for debt reduction. CRISIL expects debt of ₹3,000–3,100 cr at Mar-27 (capex offsets much of the repayment), gearing below 0.7x and net debt/EBITDA ~2x. [CRISIL Jun-26]
- Free cash flow negative for three years: −₹115 cr FY24, −₹706 cr FY25, −₹647 cr FY26; CFO/operating profit only 43–50% in FY25–FY26. Working-capital outflows of ~₹400 cr (FY25) and ~₹600 cr (FY26). [Screener; Q4 call]
- Working capital: inventory 145 days, debtors 50, payables 121, cash conversion cycle 74 days (59 in FY25). Bills discounted ₹157 cr at Mar-26 (₹86 cr prior). [Screener; AR FY26 note 3.8]
- Capital commitments not provided for ₹785 cr (₹491 cr a year earlier). [AR FY26 note 3.8]
- Dividend payout 7%. Rating: CRISIL AA-, outlook raised to Positive on 29 Jun 2026; Sunbeam separately BBB+/Watch Positive. [CRISIL Jun-26; Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Leading independent machiner of CV/tractor engine blocks and heads; long OEM validation cycles make switching slow; import substitution of critical powertrain parts. [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Jun-26]
- Aluminium scale: ~₹5,000+ cr run-rate business with 95% of Craftsman's own aluminium output machined (higher value than raw casting). [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Pricing: new orders "at new prices"; aluminium pass-through enforced — "Where aluminum price pass-through is not there, then we are declining to supply." [Q4 call]
- Stationary-engine wins with global genset makers after a 4-year qualification; new orders now expected to productionise within ~2 years. [Q1 call]
- Industrial segment is a smaller niche (top-two in automated storage per company), now with EBIT jumping to ₹30 cr on the India capex cycle. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]

## Risks and red flags
- Balance sheet stretch and equity dilution: ₹3,200 cr raised in two QIPs in two years plus debt at ₹3,623 cr to fund ~₹4,800–5,000 cr of capex and acquisitions in FY20–FY26; FCF still negative. On the Q1 call management said "there's no ever need to come to the public markets", a month after the QIP. [CRISIL Jun-26; Q1 call]
- Promoter selling: CMD Srinivasan Ravi sold 5.25 lakh shares (2.01%) on 23 Jun 2026 at about ₹9,250 (~₹486 cr), cutting his stake to 38.14%; promoter-group holding fell to 42.41% from 48.70% in the June quarter (QIP dilution plus the sale). No pledges. [News: ScanX, Jun 2026; Screener]
- Management pay: CMD's pay rose 112% in FY26 to ~₹11.9 cr (salary ₹6.05 cr + commission ₹5.86 cr), 169x median employee; whole-time director (his son Ravi Gauthamram) ~₹4.0 cr (+108%). [AR FY26 remuneration disclosures]
- Weak acquisitions: Sunbeam lost ₹40 cr in FY26 (₹53 cr in FY25) with ~4% operating margin; its turnaround has slipped; Gurugram land monetisation still pending. [AR FY26; CRISIL Jun-26; Q1 call]
- Execution load: two new greenfields, an entity merger in NCLT, a German foundry, Sunbeam restructuring and a new stationary-engine line at once. [Q1 call; CRISIL Jun-26]
- Labour: CMD said manpower cost is what causes "sleepless nights", with wages expected to rise ~20% a year. [Q4 call]
- Auditor: unmodified; KAM is accounting for property, plant and equipment (useful lives, impairment). Audit trail in place. Disputed income tax/GST/service tax dues under CARO are small (largest ~₹19 cr income tax). No related-party red flag found beyond family pay. [AR FY26 auditor's report and CARO]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone: Q4 was defensive (labour inflation, working capital, debt questions); Q1 was expansionary (Hosur Unit 3, DR Axion greenfield, "capex cycle has started in India").
- Between the calls the company raised ₹2,000 cr by QIP and the CMD sold 2% — neither was discussed on the Q1 call.
- Numbers: Q1 growth 36% vs mid-teens guidance; Sunbeam timeline pushed from Q2 to Q4; capex firmed at ~₹1,500 cr (it was "not very clear" in May).
- Industrial & Engineering margin spike emerged as a new theme; labour-cost worries disappeared from the Q1 call.

## My read
Craftsman is growing fast (Q1 sales +36%, profit more than doubled from a weak base) and margins have edged back to 16%, helped by powertrain recovery and an industrial upswing. But it has not produced free cash in three years, has leaned on two QIPs and ₹3,600 cr of debt, and its acquired aluminium businesses (especially Sunbeam) still earn little; the founder selling 2% right after the QIP is worth noting. The price assumes the heavy capex turns into higher returns; I would want to see Sunbeam near mid-teens margins by Q4 FY27, positive free cash flow in FY27, and first stationary-engine revenue in FY28 before trusting the story more — a slip on any of these, or another equity raise, would weaken it.
