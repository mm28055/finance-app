# Sundram Fasteners (SUNDRMFAST) — deep research

Research date 27 Sep 2026. Figures in ₹ crore. Screener = consolidated page, read from the raw HTML table rows. Management often quotes **standalone** numbers; they are labelled as such.

## Business and segments
- TVS-group (Suresh Krishna family) maker of high-tensile fasteners, powder-metal (sintered) parts, cold-extruded parts, hot-forged and machined parts, engine pumps (oil/water) and cast-machined assemblies, radiator caps. Sells to CV, PV and tractor OEMs, the aftermarket, and exports (Cummins, GM, Stellantis, Class 8 truck makers, Garrett Motion, ZF). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- FY26 product mix: fasteners 40–42%, pumps and assemblies / cast-machined assemblies 25–27%, cold-extruded and sintered parts ~15%, hot-forged and machined ~12%, rest radiator caps etc. [Q4 call]
- Non-auto (incl. tractors) ~35% of sales; aim ~50% over five years via wind-energy fasteners, aerospace fasteners, railways and industrial. Non-auto margins are "100 to 200 basis points above" auto. [Q4 call; CRISIL Apr-26]
- Geography: domestic 65–70% (OE + aftermarket), exports ~30% of standalone sales, of which Europe is "sub 20%" and North America the bulk. [CRISIL Apr-26; Q1 call]
- Q1 FY27 standalone: domestic ₹1,084.3 cr (+16%), exports ₹466.0 cr (+23%). [IP Q1FY27]
- Subsidiaries: Sundram International (UK, European trucks), Sundram Fasteners China (construction/CV), TVS Upasana (2-wheeler parts, India), TVS Next (IT). Subsidiaries are 13–14% of consolidated revenue but only 8–10% of operating profit. [CRISIL Apr-26; Q1 call]
- Wallet share: single source for "specials" at most OEMs; 30–40% overall presence per OEM on standard fasteners, 50–60% share of business. EV parts are 4–5% of revenue. [Q1 call]

## Key numbers
- Quarterly consolidated sales / operating profit / OPM / PAT (Screener): Jun-25 ₹1,533 / ₹247 / 16% / ₹148 · Sep-25 ₹1,521 / ₹252 / 17% / ₹153 · Dec-25 ₹1,541 / ₹240 / 16% / ₹131 · Mar-26 ₹1,693 / ₹256 / 15% / ₹161 · Jun-26 ₹1,846 / ₹286 / 16% / ₹169. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27: consolidated sales +20.4%, PAT +14% (₹168.7 cr vs ₹147.9 cr). Standalone revenue ₹1,614.8 cr (+20%), PBT ₹203.2 cr, PAT ₹151.0 cr (+9%); standalone EBITDA margin 16.1%. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Volume growth 13% by tonnage in Q1; raw-material price pass-through added only ₹20–25 cr of revenue — so growth is mostly real. [Q1 call]
- Annual consolidated (Screener): sales FY24 ₹5,666 → FY25 ₹5,955 → FY26 ₹6,289 (+5.6%); operating profit ₹889 → ₹945 → ₹999; OPM 16% each year; PAT ₹526 → ₹542 → ₹593. 3-yr sales CAGR 4%, profit CAGR 7%; 10-yr profit CAGR 12%. [Screener]
- FY26 standalone: revenue ₹5,612 cr (+7%), PBT before exceptional items ₹749.6 cr (+12%); exports fell ~8% for the year on US tariffs and weak Class 8 truck demand, then turned positive in Q4. [Q4 call; AR FY26]
- Management's own 3-year verdict: growth "has not taken off in a manner which we would have desired" versus its 12–13% target. [Q4 call]
- ROCE 18% (FY26), ROE 14.9%. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (5 May 2026): FY27 targets — CV 8%, PV 10%, tractors 6–7% (vs industry forecasts of 4–6% and flat), exports +15–20%; "double-digit growth in the coming 2 years"; capex "not less than INR 300 crores" a year; dividend payout ~30%; railway fasteners from ₹2–3 cr/month to ~₹100 cr a year by Q3–Q4; wind fasteners from ₹30–35 cr to ₹50 cr a month. [Q4 call]
- Q1 delivery: revenue +20% (ahead of all targets), exports +23% standalone; management **kept** the segment growth targets but warned of H2 FY26's high base. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- New in Q1: FY27 capex raised to ₹400 cr (~₹250 cr fasteners for wind/space/aerospace, ~₹100 cr cast and machined assemblies); margin guided to recover from 16.1% to "closer to 16.5" for the year. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call; News: Autocar Professional, Aug 2026]
- GM/Stellantis EV and PHEV programme: FY26 revenue under ₹50 cr; FY27 ₹200–250 cr; peak ₹750 cr as originally projected. In Q4 the tone was that GM had cut EV volumes by 50% and Stellantis had postponed; Q1 said "we are on track". [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Aerospace: ₹50 cr (FY25-ish) → ₹100 cr+ target FY27 → ₹500 cr in 2–3 years. New OEMs: Hyundai/Kia fasteners, ₹100 cr+ opportunity; pipeline of new projects over ₹1,000 cr with a similar amount under discussion. [Q1 call]
- CRISIL's independent view: consolidated revenue growth of 6–8% a year after FY26 and margins ~16% — well below management's aspirations. [CRISIL Apr-26]

## Capex and capacity
- Capex ₹400 cr for FY27 (70% growth, 30% replacement); CRISIL models ₹350–400 cr a year over the medium term, including ~₹80 cr for wind-energy capacity. [IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Apr-26]
- Wind fasteners: one phase done lifting run-rate from ₹25 to ₹30 cr a month; a further ~₹100 cr investment to take annualised revenue from ~₹350 cr to ~₹500 cr. [Q1 call]
- Asset turns: sales ₹6,289 cr on net fixed assets ₹2,499 cr ≈ 2.5x; capex fully funded from internal cash (cash accrual ₹600–700 cr a year). [Screener; CRISIL Apr-26]
- Open to buying assets in Europe to shorten 12–18-month project lead times; nothing advanced. [Q1 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Consolidated borrowings ₹625 cr at Mar-26 (₹823 cr a year earlier) against cash ₹80.9 cr; net debt ≈ ₹545 cr, gearing ~0.13x. CRISIL expects gearing below 0.2x and interest cover 30x+. Bank limits ₹1,532 cr, 24% average utilisation. [Screener; AR FY26 consolidated; CRISIL Apr-26]
- Cash from operations ₹837 cr in FY26 (101% of operating profit) vs ₹423 cr in FY25; free cash flow ₹467 cr (FY25 ₹29 cr). [Screener]
- Working capital is the structural weakness: inventory days 175, debtor days 89, cash conversion cycle 173 days (FY23 130). Management expects it to stay "around these levels" as exports return to ~30% of sales. [Screener; Q1 call]
- Dividend payout ~28–30% of profit; dividend yield 0.68%. [Screener; Q4 call]
- Rating: CRISIL A1+ on ₹25 cr short-term debt and ₹100 cr commercial paper, reaffirmed 28 Apr 2026 and again 21 Sep 2026. [CRISIL Apr-26; BSE filing 21-Sep-26]

## Moat evidence
- Largest domestic fastener maker; single-source for special fasteners at most OEMs; a Deming-prize company with multi-decade OEM relationships. [Q1 call; Q4 call; CRISIL Apr-26]
- Aerospace approvals (NADCAP, AS9100) with GE aviation, HAL, ISRO and Skyroot — hard-to-get qualifications that lock in suppliers. [Q1 call]
- BIS quality-control orders on fasteners are shutting out imports and weak small suppliers, which let SFL win Hyundai/Kia and take share. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Contracted raw-material pass-through with domestic OEMs protects margin; aftermarket prices can be raised. Exports are not pass-through but benefit from rupee weakness. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Margin stability is itself evidence: operating margin 15–17% for eight straight years through tariff, EV and commodity shocks. [Screener]

## Risks and red flags
- **Related-party sales to the promoter holding company:** SFL sold goods worth ₹114.6 cr (FY25 ₹110.6 cr) to TVS Sundram Fasteners Pvt Ltd, its 46.79% promoter; ₹25.8 cr was receivable from it at year-end (FY25 ₹50.1 cr). Disclosed as arm's length, but it is a material recurring flow to the family holding company. [AR FY26 Corporate Governance §A, note 37]
- **Promoter pay:** MD Arathi Krishna ₹9.99 cr and Joint MD Arundathi Krishna ₹9.32 cr (≈129x and 121x median pay) — ~3.3% of standalone PAT. [AR FY26 remuneration table]
- **Subsidiary support:** investment in subsidiaries ₹289.9 cr (FY25 ₹261.1 cr), loans to them ₹15.5 cr, guarantees outstanding ₹8.2 cr (FY25 ₹31.3 cr); the UK subsidiary needed a ₹41.3 cr impairment in earlier years, of which ₹28.8 cr was reversed in FY26 as an exceptional gain. The auditor made this impairment/reversal a key audit matter. [AR FY26 notes 6 and 37; auditor's report]
- **Exceptional items flattered FY26 PAT:** net +₹17.8 cr (₹28.8 cr impairment reversal less ₹11.0 cr labour-code gratuity charge). [AR FY26 note 42; Q4 call]
- **Contingent liabilities:** small — ₹28.7 cr (sales tax ₹4.8 cr, indirect taxes ₹18.2 cr, income tax ₹4.7 cr), down from ₹36.7 cr. [AR FY26 contingent-liabilities note, standalone]
- **Auditor:** unqualified; key audit matters are subsidiary-investment impairment and revenue recognition. [AR FY26]
- **Promoter-group housekeeping:** Upasana Pvt Ltd merged into TVS Sundram Fasteners Pvt Ltd (effective 27 Mar 2026); three entities sought reclassification from promoter group to public. No change in the 46.79% stake. [AR FY26 secretarial report; Corporate Governance]
- **Export cyclicality:** US Class 8 demand is being pulled forward by the EPA27 pre-buy; a post-2027 air-pocket is likely. EV programme history is volatile (GM cut EV volumes 50% in FY26). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Working-capital intensity** and heavy North America exposure; indirect costs (LPG, energy, freight) are not contractually passed through. [Q1 call; CRISIL Apr-26]

## What changed between the two calls
- Numbers: from +7% FY26 revenue (standalone) to +20% in Q1 FY27; exports from −8% for FY26 to +23% in Q1. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- US EV: from "downsized their EV projections by 50%" to ₹200–250 cr expected this year from GM/Stellantis. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Margin: Q4 standalone EBITDA 17% ("contribution expanded 150 bps"); Q1 slipped to 16.1% on West Asia-driven indirect cost inflation. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Capex: from "not less than ₹300 cr" to ₹400 cr earmarked. [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- New themes: IoT/automation giving 5–10% productivity and 20–50 bps margin; Hyundai/Kia entry; Class 8 backlog at a 38-month high; data-centre gensets driving Cummins high-horsepower engine demand. [Q1 call]

## My read
The business has re-accelerated sharply — 13% volume growth, exports back to growth and a real EV/PHEV revenue line starting — and the balance sheet is conservative. The quality of growth is decent but not exceptional: margins have sat at ~16% for years, working capital is heavy, and the three-year record (4% sales CAGR) lagged the 12–13% target, while CRISIL models only 6–8% growth. The price needs roughly low-teens earnings growth sustained; watch whether Q2–Q3 hold ~20% growth against the high base, margins reach 16.5%, and wind/aerospace hit ₹500 cr and ₹100 cr run-rates. A US Class 8 slump after the EPA27 pre-buy, or another EV-programme cut, would change the view.
