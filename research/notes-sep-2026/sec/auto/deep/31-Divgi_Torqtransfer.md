# Divgi Torqtransfer Systems (NSE: DIVGIITTS) — deep research, Sep 2026

Figures: Screener standalone (the company has no consolidated accounts on Screener yet; the US subsidiary was set up in FY27). Note the company's own "total income" includes other income (mostly interest on IPO cash); Screener "sales" is revenue from operations. Price ₹1,155, market cap ₹3,531 cr (Screener, 27 Sep 2026).

## Business and segments
- Makes drivetrain systems for vehicles: transfer cases (the gearbox that sends power to all four wheels in 4x4 SUVs and pickups), EV transmissions ("e-gear drive", 10–150 kW designs), manual-transmission parts and synchronisers, and precision components that it exports to global Tier-1s such as BorgWarner and Magna. Its designs are its own (not build-to-print), a legacy of a 21-year joint venture with BorgWarner. [AR FY26; Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 revenue mix (total income ₹141.8 cr): transfer cases ₹76.0 cr (53%, +93% YoY); components & exports ₹34.4 cr (24%, +83%); e-gear drive (EV) ₹5.7 cr (4%, −20% YoY); "other" ₹21.4 cr (NexTrac, ALH, synchro, aftermarket, other operating income; up from ₹6.5 cr). Exports 16% of revenue. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- FY26: total income ₹375 cr (+56%); transfer cases +66%, components +124%, EV +10%; exports ~18% of revenue vs ~6% in FY25 (₹3.4 cr in FY24 → about ₹68 cr FY26). [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Customers: Mahindra is the largest, Tata Motors a close second; both are supplied for the 70,000-unit Indonesia pickup order (Scorpio pickup and Yodha), where Divgi is sole transfer-case supplier. [Q1 call]
- Volumes FY26: ~52,000 transfer cases (low 30,000s in FY25); ~24,000 EV transmissions; EV plant utilisation only 25–30%. Capacity for ~10,000 EV transmissions a month has been installed. A new German-automated transfer-case line at Shirwal will add ~400 units/day (~1.2 lakh a year). Plants: Bhosari (Pune), Shivare, Shirwal (greenfield) and Sirsi (Karnataka; 25 acres). [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26]
- 278 permanent employees; 4 plants; gross block ₹421.6 cr; R&D spend ₹11.8 cr in FY26. [AR FY26 p.82]

## Key numbers
- Quarterly revenue from operations (Screener, ₹ cr): Jun-25 72, Sep-25 83, Dec-25 91, Mar-26 108, Jun-26 137. Operating profit (ex other income): 14, 17, 18, 22, 37; OPM 20%, 20%, 20%, 20%, 27%. Net profit: 9, 11, 12, 15, 25. [Screener]
- Company-basis Q1 FY27: total income ₹141.8 cr (+85% YoY, +25% QoQ); EBITDA ₹41.6 cr incl. other income (29.4% margin vs 24.9% a year ago and 24.5% in Q4); PAT ₹25.2 cr (+183% YoY, +63% QoQ from ₹15.5 cr); gross margin 62.5%. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Annual trend (Screener sales / OPM / net profit): FY24 ₹253 cr / 21% / ₹40 cr; FY25 ₹219 cr / 17% / ₹24 cr; FY26 ₹353 cr / 20% / ₹47 cr; TTM ₹418 cr / 22% / ₹63 cr. FY26 was the recovery after two weak years. [Screener]
- Pre-IPO peak margins were higher: OPM 28–29% in FY18–FY23 on a smaller base; the Q1 FY27 27% is a return towards that, not a new high. [Screener]
- Returns are still thin because of idle IPO cash: ROCE 10% FY26 (6% FY25, 30% FY19); ROE 7.6%. The company's own ROIC excluding cash was 12.7% for FY26 and ~32% for Q1 FY27 annualised. [Screener; IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Unit economics: FY26 transfer-case revenue of roughly ₹180 cr over ~52,000 units implies about ₹35,000 per unit (my estimate from ~47–53% share of ₹375 cr). A new front-wheel-drive AWD application carries "upwards of ₹30,000 per vehicle". [Q1 call; derived]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (27 May 2026) promised: (1) new EV transmission (90–120 kW, for Nexon, Curvv, higher-trim Punch, Harrier) in production "definitely by July", with EV volumes almost doubling; (2) 70,000 Indonesia units on track for FY27; (3) component export programmes worth nearly ₹100 cr a year, monthly run-rate rising from ~₹7 cr to ₹10–12 cr; (4) US office for ~₹3 cr; (5) first automatic-transmission (DCT) revenue "middle to second half of next calendar year" (CY27), big volumes a year later; (6) medium-term export share 20–25%. [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 delivered: Indonesia ~30% dispatched (~21,000 units), but "some of this contract may spill over into one or two months of the next year" (FY28). EV revenue fell to ₹5.7 cr; the Sigma programme has PPAP (production part approval) from Tata but SOP moved to Q2 FY27 because Tata's vehicle testing ran long. Exports ₹23 cr in the quarter (~₹7.7 cr a month, i.e. still below the ₹10–12 cr monthly figure); the presentation now targets FY27 component exports of ~₹80 cr. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Slippage: automatic transmission moved from "H2 CY27 first revenue" (Q4) to "earliest second half of 2028" (Q1). LCV manual transmission (the "beachhead" contract, ₹100–150 cr potential) now "next year"; South Africa pickup transfer cases April 2028–March 2029 (FY29). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Margin guidance kept: management "maintained an EBITDA of about 20%" even in weak FY25 and aims to sustain "20% to 22% and above"; it explicitly told investors not to extrapolate the 29.4% ("one-off opportunity"). [Q1 call]
- Long-term: "potential annual revenues of ₹2,000+ cr" (4WD ~₹300 cr, manual ~₹300 cr, EV ~₹250 cr, automatic/hybrid ~₹1,000 cr, exports ~₹200 cr). This was raised from "₹1,500+ cr" a quarter earlier. The MD says the first job is ₹1,000 cr. These are addressable-potential figures, not dated targets. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call; Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- IPO (March 2023) raised ₹169.7 cr for machinery capex; only ₹77.9 cr was spent by 31 Mar 2026 and ₹72.7 cr remains unspent three years later (parked in bank deposits). The presentation says ~₹96.9 cr deployed including other spend. [AR FY26 note 47; IP Q1FY27]
- Capex: FY23 ~₹68 cr, FY24 ~₹78 cr, FY25 ~₹27 cr, FY26 ₹44.3 cr. Capital commitments rose to ₹50.5 cr at Mar-26 from ₹25.8 cr. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 note 38]
- Project Mayflower: wholly owned US subsidiary (Divgi Transmission Technologies and Systems Inc.) at ICAR, Greenville, South Carolina, near BorgWarner's large transfer-case plant. Phase 1 plant about $5 million (MD: "please do not hold me to this"), greenfield (acquisition rejected on audit-quality grounds), commissioning H2 CY2028 at the earliest. The pull is North American local-content rules (USMCA). [Q1 call]
- Management says India investment will exceed US investment (automatic and manual transmissions). Funding is from cash; no borrowing is planned. [Q1 call]
- Asset turns are low: fixed assets ₹259 cr plus CWIP ₹19 cr against FY26 sales of ₹353 cr (about 1.3x). With EV capacity at 25–30% use, the next ₹100–150 cr of revenue needs little new capex. [Screener; Q4 call]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Debt-free (borrowings ₹2 cr, leases). Cash and bank balances are large; the presentation chart shows cash reserves of roughly ₹295 cr at Mar-26 (my reading of the chart). Other income ₹22 cr in FY26 came mostly from this cash. [Screener; IP Q1FY27]
- Operating cash flow ₹41 cr in FY26 (CFO/operating profit 79%); free cash flow −₹3 cr after capex. FCF was negative in FY23 and FY24 (−₹28 cr, −₹45 cr) as the IPO-funded capex went in. [Screener]
- Working capital is heavy: inventory 167 days, debtors 82 days, payables 160 days (Screener FY26); the company's own net working capital days fell to 68 from 96. Receivable turnover improved to 5.24x from 3.69x. [Screener; AR FY26 p.83]
- Dividend: ₹3.27 per share for FY26 (21% payout); no buyback. [Q4 call; Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Sole transfer-case supplier to both Mahindra and Tata on the Indonesia programme; won against Toyota, Nissan, Mitsubishi, Isuzu, Great Wall and Foton at the OEM level. [Q1 call]
- Nominated by a Japanese OEM ("Japan's oldest… truck company") for a pickup/truck transfer case, SOP FY28–FY29; prototypes finished in 3–4 months. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Export component contracts (₹23 cr a quarter) are "exclusive": Divgi is the only supplier of those parts to the North American plants. [Q1 call]
- Two transfer-case models used by the Indian Army; co-developed Torque-on-Demand with M-Lock for Mahindra. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Gross margin steady at 62–64% for years, which shows pricing power on proprietary designs rather than pass-through. [IP Q1FY27]
- Limits: India's 4WD market is small ("will never exceed 100–120 [thousand]"), and Mahindra kept its three-wheeler EV transmission in-house, which shows OEMs can insource. [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- Customer concentration: Mahindra and Tata dominate; Indonesia volumes are lumpy and management admits other orders were pushed out while it runs. [Q1 call]
- EV business keeps slipping: SOP was "definitely by July" on the Q4 call and is now Q2 with revenue down 20% YoY; the EV plant runs at 25–30%. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Automatic-transmission timeline slipped about a year in one quarter (CY27 → H2 CY28). The ₹1,000 cr AT potential depends on a technology-transfer deal that is still under negotiation. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Idle IPO money: ₹72.7 cr of capex funds unspent after three years depresses ROCE and questions capital planning. [AR FY26 note 47]
- Related parties: "machine and development charges" of ₹4.5 cr (₹3.3 cr in FY25) paid to promoter-linked Divgi Transmission Systems & Technologies Pvt Ltd; rent ₹0.5 cr to promoter holding company; IT services from Quixotic Studios (KMP-influenced). Small but growing; worth watching. MD pay is 31.7x the median employee (+15%). [AR FY26 note 34; AR FY26 p.84]
- Contingent liabilities small: ₹3.9 cr (stamp duty ₹2.4 cr, claims ₹1.0 cr). [AR FY26 note 37]
- Auditor B.K. Khare & Co.: unmodified opinion; key audit matter is revenue recognition (volume discounts). CARO notes "slight delay in few cases" of statutory dues and a disputed GST amount; audit trail operated all year. No credit rating (no debt). [AR FY26 auditor's report]
- Very long, promotional commentary (the MD's own words: "historic crossroads", "afterburner thrust"); "potential" numbers are not commitments.

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from defending the Indonesia order (Q4: media reports of the order being on hold were "frivolous") to "all the cylinders are now beginning to fire together" (Q1). [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- New: Project Mayflower (US plant, ~$5m phase 1), a front-wheel-drive AWD application (content >₹30,000 per vehicle), the Japanese truck OEM, talks with a Korean EV maker, and a push into LCVs, pickups and construction equipment. [Q1 call]
- Numbers: margin jumped 490 bps; but EV slipped again, AT slipped a year, and Indonesia may spill into FY28.

## My read
The business has clearly turned: revenue from operations nearly doubled YoY in Q1 and the margin is back near pre-IPO levels, driven by a real but temporary Indonesia order and a genuine export build-out. The durable part is the ₹90 cr-a-year exclusive export components business, plus new Japanese and FWD-AWD wins; the fragile part is that EV and automatic-transmission revenue keep slipping. FY27 will look spectacular; FY28 needs the Indonesia volume (roughly ₹20–25 cr a quarter) to be replaced by the pickup, LCV manual and EV ramps. Given the price already discounts much of the ₹1,000 cr roadmap, I would want to see EV revenue reach ₹10–12 cr a quarter by Q3 and a signed AT technology agreement before assuming growth above 20% a year beyond FY27.
