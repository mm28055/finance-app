# Automotive Axles Ltd (AUTOAXLES / BSE 505010) — deep research, 27 Sep 2026

Standalone company (no subsidiaries, per CARO clause ix(e) [AR FY26 auditor's report]). All figures from Screener's standalone page unless tagged otherwise. Price ₹1,710, market cap ₹2,584 cr, trailing P/E 14.1, dividend yield 1.87% [Screener, 27 Sep 2026]. No investor presentation has been published recently; an analyst asked for one on the Q4 call and management said it would "look at the possibility" [Q4 call].

## Business and segments
- India's largest independent maker of rear drive axles for medium and heavy commercial vehicles (M&HCV, trucks and buses above 7.5 tonnes), and the second-largest brake maker after Brakes India. Sells drive axles, tag axles, drum and disc brakes; axles for Caterpillar construction equipment. Plants at Mysuru, Pantnagar, Jamshedpur and Hosur [ICRA Feb-26].
- Ownership: a joint venture, 35.52% each held by Meritor Heavy Vehicle Systems LLC (now owned by Cummins Inc.) and Bharat Forge's BF Investments; promoters 71.04%, unchanged for nine quarters [ICRA Feb-26; Screener shareholding].
- Customer concentration is the defining feature: Ashok Leyland has historically given 50–60% of revenue [ICRA Feb-26]. Management says it keeps "50% plus" of Ashok Leyland's axle business on average over the year and is single source on 30–50% of its requirements [Q4 call; Q1 call]. Tata Motors buys mainly brakes, not axles; talks on axles are "continuous" but nothing confirmed [Q4 call].
- Mix: single-segment company (auto components). LCV/ICV axles under 5% of revenue [Q4 call]; defence 5–10% depending on tenders [Q1 call]; exports ₹219 cr in FY26 = 10.05% of turnover [AR FY26 BRSR], 13% in Q1 FY27 versus a normal 8–12% band [Q1 call]. Exports go only to Cummins Drivetrain (CDBS) plants abroad, and are limited to drive-head sub-assemblies and child parts, not full axles [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Business-model change from 1 April 2025: earlier ~80% of sales were routed through Meritor HVS (India) Ltd (MHVSIL, a Meritor–Bharat Forge JV); now AAL sells directly to OEMs and pays MHVSIL a technical/service fee of 4–4.5% of revenue [ICRA Feb-26; Q4 call]. The related-party note shows the effect: sales to MHVSIL fell from about ₹1,669 cr in FY25 to a small amount in FY26, while fees paid to MHVSIL rose to ₹97.5 cr (FY25: ₹10.7 cr) [AR FY26 note 35].
- Capacity: utilisation about 90% in FY26 and "over 90%" in Q4 FY26, falling to a normal 70–80% in April–May [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call]. Management earlier said the existing plants could do about ₹2,700 cr of sales [Q4 call, analyst citing earlier statement].

## Key numbers (Screener, standalone, ₹ cr)
- Annual: FY24 sales 2,229, operating profit 246 (11%), net profit 166; FY25 sales 2,078, OP 220 (11%), NP 156; FY26 sales 2,178 (+4.8%), OP 237 (10.9%), NP 164; trailing twelve months sales 2,205, OP 249, NP 174 [Screener]. Sales are still below the FY23 peak of 2,324 [Screener].
- Quarters (sales / OP / OPM / NP): Q1 FY26 489 / 48 / 9.8% / 36; Q2 FY26 462 / 48 / 10.4% / 36; Q3 FY26 562 / 64 / 11.4% / 39; Q4 FY26 664 / 77 / 11.6% / 54; Q1 FY27 517 / 60 / 11.6% / 46 [Screener]. Q1 FY27 sales +5.7% year on year, −22% quarter on quarter.
- Q3 FY26 carried a one-off ₹12 cr charge (gratuity and leave liability under the new Labour Codes, shown as an exceptional item; Screener nets it into other income of −3) [AR FY26 employee-benefit note; Screener].
- Company-defined "EBITDA" includes other income: Q1 FY27 ₹70.2 cr at 13.6%, Q4 FY26 ₹82.5 cr at 12.4% [Q1 call; Q4 call]. On Screener's operating basis the margin was flat quarter on quarter at 11.6%; nearly all of the 120 bps "improvement" management cited came from other income rising from ₹6 cr to ₹10 cr (treasury gains after a market recovery) [Screener; Q1 call]. Year on year the operating margin did rise about 180 bps (9.8% → 11.6%), helped by mix, a small one-off and the direct-sales model.
- Unit economics: metal cost about 65% of sales in Q1 FY27 (68% in Q4 FY26, 67.5–68% for FY26); employee cost 7.3% of FY26 sales (6.7% in FY25) after a four-year wage settlement [Q1 call; Q4 call]. A tandem axle earns 10–25% more per unit than a single axle, not the 2–3x an analyst suggested [Q4 call].
- Returns: ROCE 22% FY26 (27% FY24, 31% FY23); ROE 16.2%; dividend payout about 29–30% for five years [Screener].

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (20 May 2026): FY27 industry "400 plus" thousand trucks; Q1–Q2 soft, Q4 strong; aspirational revenue of ₹4,000–5,000 cr over 4–5 years (first stated on the Q4 FY25 call) with exports a larger share; Phase 1 and 1A capacity done by December 2026; FY27 capex "similar range" to FY26's ~₹70 cr; next capex phase to be firmed up in 6–12 months; margins expected to improve with fixed-cost absorption [Q4 call, official transcript].
- Q1 FY27 call (6 Aug 2026): management said its internal starting forecast for FY27 had been a 15–20% fall in industry volume; now 5–10% down (≥450,000 against 480,000 in FY26), best case flat. Q2 "reasonable", Q3 rising, Q4 a peak [Q1 call]. New: a PAT-margin band of 7.5–8.5% "for the next few quarters", moving up toward 2030 [Q1 call]. Q1 PAT margin was 8.8% of sales (8.7% on total income as stated), i.e. at the top of the band [Screener; Q1 call].
- Delivery vs promise: Q1 held margin despite a 22% sequential sales fall, as promised. Capex timing kept (December 2026). Note an inconsistency: the Q4 closing remarks said "phase 1 we have already completed", but on the Q1 call "nearly 40%" of the Phase 1/1A investment was done [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Status: industry outlook **raised** (from the internal −15–20% to −5–10%); ₹4,000–5,000 cr aspiration **kept** but not repeated on Q1; margin band **newly given**.

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex was over ₹70 cr; about 60–70% of committed Phase 1 spend done in FY26; still generated ~₹30 cr cash net of investment [Q4 call]. Screener: FY26 investing outflow ₹94 cr, CWIP ₹14 cr, fixed assets ₹225 cr (from ₹200 cr) [Screener].
- Programme size about ₹120 cr (analyst figure, not contradicted) [Q1 call]; ICRA cites ₹120–140 cr over the medium term, mainly modernisation and automation, funded by accruals and possibly some term debt [ICRA Feb-26]. Capital commitments outstanding at March 2026: ₹42.7 cr (₹32.0 cr a year earlier) [AR FY26 note on commitments].
- Result: 25–30% more capacity (mix-dependent), fully by December 2026; it is part replacement with automated lines, part growth, and management would not split the two [Q1 call]. MD&A says the new capacity should be operational by Q3 FY27 [AR FY26 MD&A].
- Payback: not disclosed. With fixed assets of ₹225 cr supporting ₹2,178 cr of sales (about 9.7x), a ₹120 cr programme adding 25–30% capacity would carry high asset turns if volumes come (my estimate from Screener figures).
- Furnace electrification finished "just in time" to cope with LPG shortages during the West Asia war [Q1 call].
- Product pipeline: MS185 single-axle for 4x2 tractor-trailers ramping up ("highest axle number in terms of capacity creation"); new 160 tandem axle in pilot, going into production with "one of the large customers"; a new bus axle is being redesigned for future pass-by-noise norms, so its launch has slipped [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹14 cr (March 2026); investments ₹140 cr; ICRA counted free cash and liquid investments of ₹403 cr at September 2025 and unused bank lines of ₹102 cr [Screener; ICRA Feb-26]. Effectively net cash of ~₹390 cr, or about 15% of market cap (my calculation).
- Cash flow from operations: FY24 ₹260 cr, FY25 ₹128 cr, FY26 ₹173 cr; free cash flow FY26 ₹101 cr; CFO/operating profit 95% in FY26 [Screener].
- Working capital (FY26): debtor days 82 (60 in FY24), inventory days 53, payable days 83, cash conversion cycle 52 days [Screener]. Debtor days rose after the switch to direct OEM billing. Inventories ₹214 cr at March 2026, a key audit matter for obsolescence provisioning [AR FY26 auditor's report].
- Dividend: payout 29% of FY26 profit; final dividend announced in September 2026 [Screener; BSE filing 11 Sep 2026].

## Moat evidence
- Technology licensing and India-specific validation from Meritor/Cummins (MHVSIL tests global products for Indian duty cycles, helps with application engineering and customer strategy); management calls this support ongoing "for the foreseeable future" [Q1 call]. Validation cycles are long and OEMs rarely switch mid-programme.
- Share: 50%+ of Ashok Leyland's axle needs; single source on new products where possible, though every OEM dual-sources [Q4 call; Q1 call]. ICRA: "largest independent manufacturer of rear drive axles" [ICRA Feb-26].
- Pricing: full pass-through of steel, castings and forgings "back-to-back"; conversion-cost inflation (LPG, tooling) absorbed first, then negotiated [Q1 call]. Q1 operating margin held at 11.6% despite that absorption [Screener].
- Competition: American Axle's Indian CV axle business (FY24 revenue ₹1,586 cr) was bought by Bharat Forge — AAL's own co-promoter — for ₹544.5 cr (agreement October 2024) [News: Business Standard, 17 Oct 2024; Autocar Professional]. Management says overlap is "minimal" because AAL is focused on 40-tonne-plus heavy duty [Q1 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Customer concentration**: 50–60% of revenue from Ashok Leyland [ICRA Feb-26]. Q1 FY27 sales grew slower than M&HCV production; management blamed product mix (more buses, where it has no current axle), not share loss [Q1 call].
- **Co-promoter owns a competitor**: Bharat Forge sits on both sides — a 35.52% owner of AAL and now owner of the ex-AAM axle business. Bharat Forge also supplies AAL (outside processing ₹18.5 cr, raw materials) [AR FY26 note 35; News: Business Standard]. Worth watching for conflicts in customer allocation.
- **Related-party fee**: technical/service fee to MHVSIL ₹97.5 cr in FY26, about 4.5% of sales and more than half of net profit [AR FY26 note 35; Screener]. It replaced a margin that MHVSIL previously earned as distributor, so it is not a new cost overall, but the rate is set between related parties and the agreement runs to 2027 (investor remark on Q1 call, not confirmed by management) [Q1 call].
- Finance leadership: CFO Ranganathan S left on 21 November 2025; Raman K has been interim CFO for two quarters [AR FY26 note 35; Q1 call].
- Contingent liabilities tiny: ₹1.5 cr of indirect-tax disputes and ₹0.02 cr income tax [AR FY26 contingent liabilities note]. Auditor's opinion unqualified; the only key audit matter is inventory provisioning [AR FY26 auditor's report]. No pledge shown [Screener].
- Cyclicality: M&HCV volumes; replacement demand from 2017–18 trucks is supporting FY27, and management admits better component reliability will slowly cut aftermarket replenishment [Q1 call].
- EV: management says India is "not fully ready" for e-axles; the AR lists "limited presence in EV-specific segments" as a weakness [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A SWOT].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "peak quarter, capacity maxed out" (Q4) to "moderation handled well" (Q1). Industry outlook upgraded; a PAT-margin band was offered for the first time.
- Exports rose to 13% of sales but management kept 8–12% as the full-year band.
- The low-floor bus regulation worry faded (OEMs manage within existing products), but AAL's own bus axle was pushed back for noise-norm redesign.
- New questions on the Bharat Forge/American Axle overlap; management declined to discuss a "competitor".
- "Highest-ever EBITDA margin" claim rests on including other income; on an operating basis Q1 margin equalled Q4.

## My read
The business is steady rather than transforming: sales in FY26 were still below FY23, but margins have moved up by a point after the direct-sales change, the balance sheet holds about ₹390 cr of cash, and returns stay above 20%. The next leg depends on the tractor-trailer and tandem-axle mix, the ₹120 cr capacity addition from December 2026, and whether Cummins gives more export work (the ₹4,000–5,000 cr aspiration needs roughly 13–18% a year for five years, far above anything delivered). At a trailing P/E of about 14 the price needs only about 9% a year of profit growth ((14.1 × 0.1726)^0.1 − 1), which the cycle plus mix can plausibly give. The view would sour if Ashok Leyland shifted share to Bharat Forge's axle unit, or if the MHVSIL fee is raised when the agreement is renewed; it would improve on a named new OEM or a clear export contract.
