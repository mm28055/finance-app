# Tenneco Clean Air India (NSE: TENNIND) — deep research, 27 Sep 2026

Listed 19 Nov 2025 through a 100% offer for sale (₹3,600 cr; company received no money). Parent: Tenneco Mauritius Holdings (Apollo Global-owned Tenneco group); promoter holding 74.79% (Jun-26). Price ₹515, market cap ₹20,796 cr, 52-week range ₹438–657 [Screener].

## Business and segments
- Two businesses, reported on value-added revenue (VAR = revenue minus pass-through catalyst "substrates" the OEM chooses): **Clean Air & Powertrain (CAPT)** — exhaust after-treatment, mufflers, plus spark plugs (Champion), gaskets and engine bearings; and **Advanced Ride Technologies (ART)** — shock absorbers and struts (Monroe), including the DCx "DaVinci" mechanical adaptive damper and electronic semi-active (CVSA) suspension [AR FY26 MD&A].
- FY26 VAR ₹4,918 cr: CAPT ₹2,430 cr (+5.5%; segment revenue ₹2,916 cr incl. ₹486 cr substrates), ART ₹2,489 cr (+19.7%). ART is now the larger half [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call].
- Q1 FY27 VAR ₹1,382 cr (+18.4%): ART ₹719 cr (+27.9%), CAPT ₹663 cr (+9.6%) [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27].
- Market shares (value, FY26, CRISIL): CV clean air 58% (57% a year earlier), off-highway clean air 68%, PV shock absorbers/struts 55% (52%) [IP Q1FY27].
- Customer concentration: three customers each above 10% of revenue took ₹2,712 cr of ₹5,404 cr FY26 revenue — about 50% [AR FY26 consolidated note 34]. Mahindra, Tata, Daimler India and Toyota are named customers on calls. It is **absent from Maruti** (the "leading Japanese PV OEM") in clean air — a gap that cost growth in FY26 when small cars boomed after the GST cut [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A].
- Geography: India ₹5,106 cr, other countries ₹298 cr (5.5%) in FY26 [AR FY26 note 34]; exports "slightly over 7%" in Q1 FY27 [Q1 call]. About 70% of the export order book is sales to other Tenneco group companies, 30% to third-party OEMs [Q1 call].
- Structure: ART sits in the material subsidiary Tenneco Automotive India (FY26 revenue ₹2,488 cr, PAT ₹244 cr); spark plugs (₹272 cr revenue, ₹30 cr PAT), sealings (₹121 cr, ₹6.7 cr) and bearings (₹234 cr, ₹16.6 cr) sit in three Federal-Mogul subsidiaries; none lose money [AR FY26 Board's report].
- Capacity use: CAPT above 80%, ART above 90% — hence the new plants [Q1 call]. Localisation about 89–90% [Q4 call].

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated, ₹ cr)
- Quarterly revenue: Jun-25 1,286 | Sep-25 1,281 | Dec-25 1,285 | Mar-26 1,552 | Jun-26 1,545. Operating profit: 229 | 217 | 223 | 257 | 247. Net profit: 168 | 151 | 119 | 167 | 165 [Screener].
- Q1 FY27: revenue +20% YoY, operating profit +8%, net profit −1.7%. Margin on revenue fell to 16% from 18%; on VAR 17.9% vs 19.6% (−175 bps) [Screener; IP Q1FY27]. Management says PAT growth matches EBITDA growth once the Q1 FY26 one-off (Motocare sale and other income; other income ₹31 cr then vs ₹9 cr now) is removed [Q1 call; Screener].
- Annual: revenue FY24 5,468 | FY25 4,890 | FY26 5,404; operating profit 613 | 815 | 925; margin 11% | 17% | 17%; net profit 417 | 553 | 604 [Screener]. FY24 is not like-for-like (group restructuring and the Motocare aftermarket move); the step-up in margin from FY24 to FY25 is the main earnings event. On VAR, EBITDA margin went 14.3% (FY24) → 18.8% (FY26) [Q4 call].
- FY26 EBITDA ₹925.5 cr (+13.5%); PAT ₹604 cr (+9.3%) after a ₹27.2 cr one-time labour-code gratuity charge booked as an exceptional item [Q4 call; AR FY26 note on Labour Codes].
- Returns: ROCE 61% (Screener definition); company quotes 94% on its own definition [Screener; Q4 call]. Fixed-asset turnover 9.6x [Q4 call].
- Unit economics: content per vehicle — a CV exhaust is roughly 3–4x a car system; off-highway up to 10–15x; hybrids 1.5–2x a petrol car; CAFE-3 and BS-VII add roughly 1.2–1.5x [Q4 call; Q1 call]. DaVinci costs an OEM "a few percentage points" more than a conventional damper [Q1 call]. Royalty to the group is 2.5% of revenue excluding intercompany sales [Q1 call].

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (transcript dated 3 Jun 2026): lifetime order book ₹12,400 cr at 31-Mar-26, giving "100% visibility" of the internal FY28 revenue target and "double-digit growth" over the medium term; exports 14–20% of the order book vs 5–6% of sales; two plants (North India clean air, West India ART) totalling ₹140 cr capex; margins expected to "remain stable" [Q4 call].
- Q1 call (6 Aug 2026): double-digit top-line growth reiterated; new FY27 capex range ₹350–450 cr, described as "indicative… not really a precise guidance"; order book now to be reported only half-yearly (next at Q2), so no update to ₹12,400 cr [Q1 call].
- Delivery vs promise: growth beat (VAR +18.4% vs served market +16.2%), but the "stable margin" line was missed — VAR margin 17.9% vs 18.8% FY26 and 19.6% a year earlier, blamed on non-indexed inputs (rubber, plastics, LPG/CNG, freight) during the Middle East conflict and listed-company costs; only partial recovery from OEMs so far [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]. Verdict: **growth kept/beaten, margin guidance quietly missed, order-book disclosure reduced**.
- Longer-dated: entry into Maruti clean air via gasoline particulate filters for CAFE-3, revenue from about FY28–29; CAFE-3 + BS-VII addressable market ₹1,300–1,400 cr of extra content over 3–5 years (₹300–400 cr CAFE, ~₹1,000 cr BS-VII) [Q4 call]; exports "middle-ended", critical mass around 2028 [Q4 call].

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex about ₹115 cr [Q4 call]. FY27 plan ₹350–450 cr, of which ₹140 cr for the two announced plants (ART West India ≈ ₹70 cr) and the rest across both units including export-linked capex; fully funded from internal cash [Q1 call].
- Payback: management uses roughly ₹1 of capex for ₹3 of steady-state revenue; plants take 6–12 months to commission, peak around mid-FY28 to FY29 [Q4 call].
- Capital commitments outstanding ₹157 cr at Mar-26 vs ₹10 cr a year earlier — confirms the ramp [AR FY26 consolidated note 32].
- H1 FY27 capex spent to be disclosed only with Q2 [Q1 call].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net cash: net debt-to-equity −0.43 at Mar-26 (−0.17 at Mar-25); borrowings ₹52 cr (mostly leases) [AR FY26 note on capital management; Screener].
- FY26 cash from operations ₹1,429 cr (190% of operating profit); free cash flow ₹1,345 cr [Screener]. Company says cash conversion 58% of EBITDA after capex [Q4 call]; the Screener CFO spike partly reflects working-capital release and intercompany settlements — not a repeatable level.
- Working capital: debtor days 43, inventory 35, payable 111; cash conversion cycle −33 days (Screener) vs −23 days per company [Screener; Q4 call].
- Dividends: ₹1,036.6 cr paid in FY26 (₹409 cr FY25) — a pre-IPO payout, payout ratio 172% — which is why reserves fell from ₹1,206 cr to ₹794 cr [AR FY26 consolidated cash flow; Screener]. No dividend since listing (yield 0%) [Screener]. Management flags M&A as a use of spare cash [Q1 call].

## Moat evidence
- Share gains in a year of cost pressure: CV clean air 57→58%, PV dampers 52→55%, off-highway 68% [IP Q1FY27].
- Technology: DCx DaVinci (patented mechanical shim-stack damper, Indian-developed) won a flagship Mahindra SUV platform, then more applications and four entirely new customers in Q1 FY27; DCx32 extends it to A/B-segment cars; MARD anti-roll damper benchmarked with a domestic OEM [Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A].
- Group IP: access to Tenneco's global patents; Euro-7 proof of concept completed with a European truck OEM [Q4 call].
- Cost position: flexible "Lego-brick" lines that can make PV, CV and genset exhausts on the same line; 9.6x asset turns [Q4 call].
- Pricing: steel is indexed back-to-back (one-quarter lag); other inputs are negotiated — Q1 FY27 shows the limit of pricing power [Q1 call].
- New entries: spark-plug order from India's largest PV OEM (Maruti, inferred from "largest PV OEM") and bearings entry with a Japanese PV OEM — values undisclosed [Q1 call; Q4 call].

## Risks and red flags
- Electrification: CAPT (≈48% of VAR) has no content in full EVs; management puts EV at ~3–3.5 points of the 16% served-market growth [Q1 call].
- Concentration: ~50% of revenue from three customers [AR FY26 note 34].
- Related-party flows: royalty 2.5% of net sales to the group; ~70% of exports go to group companies at transfer prices; the group withdrew its advance-pricing (APA) application on royalty and management-support charges [Q1 call; AR FY26 consolidated note 45]. Transfer-pricing tax matters for FY20, FY21 and FY23 are pending before CIT(A).
- Contingent liabilities ₹131.8 cr (Mar-25: ₹116.5 cr): income tax ₹88.7 cr, Maharashtra stamp duty on the 2019-20 demerger ₹19.7 cr, customs ₹13.7 cr, GST ₹6.8 cr (alleged suppressed turnover/credit) — about 11% of net worth, excluding interest [AR FY26 consolidated note 32; CARO annexure].
- Auditor (Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP): unmodified opinion, no key audit matters, no CARO adverse remarks in any group company; only remark — audit trail not enabled at database level [AR FY26 auditor's report; note 46].
- Disclosure: segment margins not given; order book moved to half-yearly; spark-plug and bearing order values withheld [Q1 call].
- US Section 232 tariffs on exhaust parts and weak Europe could slow exports [Q1 call].
- Overhang: promoter at 74.79% after a 100% OFS; further sell-downs are possible (not announced).
- No credit rating found (debt-free).

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "best ever year… highest ever margin" to defending margins against commodity inflation and listed-company costs [Q4 call; Q1 call].
- Order book: ₹12,400 cr headline in Q4; in Q1 management declined to update it and moved to half-yearly reporting [Q1 call].
- New: FY27 capex range ₹350–450 cr (≈3x FY26), four new ART customers, DCx32 small-car product, spark-plug order, maiden European ATV export order, heat-shield order from Tenneco America [Q1 call].
- Exports rose to ~7% of revenue from 5–6% [Q1 call].

## My read
Tenneco India is a high-return, net-cash leader whose ART half is growing close to 28% on a genuinely differentiated product, while the clean-air half grows slowly until CAFE-3/BS-VII and the Maruti entry arrive around FY28–29. Q1 FY27 showed the weak spot: margins slipped 175 bps on costs it cannot fully pass on, and disclosure became thinner just as the story got harder. The price needs mid-teens earnings growth for several years; that is plausible if ART keeps winning and margins return to ~18.5% on VAR. What would change the view: an H1 order book below ₹12,400 cr, margins stuck under 17.5%, or a large related-party-flavoured acquisition.
