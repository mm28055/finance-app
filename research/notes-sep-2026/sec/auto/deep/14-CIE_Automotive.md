# CIE Automotive India (NSE: CIEINDIA) — deep research, 27 Sep 2026

Indian arm of Spain's CIE Automotive S.A. (promoter 65.70%); Mahindra & Mahindra is a related party ("significant influence over the ultimate holding company"). **Reports on a calendar year** — the database's "Q1 FY27" is Q2 CY26 (Apr–Jun 2026, call 23 Jul 2026) and "Q4 FY26" is Q1 CY26 (Jan–Mar, call 24 Apr 2026). Price ₹391, market cap ₹14,825 cr, 52-week range ₹367–526 [Screener].

## Business and segments
- Multi-technology component maker: forgings (crankshafts, 2W races — Bill Forge), iron castings, aluminium castings, stampings, gears, composites, magnets. India ~65% of revenue, Europe (Spain/Germany/Lithuania forgings, Italy's Metalcastello gears, plus Mexico) ~35% [ICRA Aug-26].
- Q2 CY26: India sales ₹1,655 cr (+13%), EBITDA margin 16.7% (Q2 CY25 17.5%, Q1 CY26 17.6%); Europe ₹888 cr (+7% in rupees, −6% in euros), margin 15.9% (12.5% a year ago) [IP Q2CY26].
- H1 CY26: India ₹3,274 cr (+14%, margin 17.2% vs 18.0%); Europe ₹1,810 cr (+12% in rupees, −3% in euros; margin 15.8% vs 13.1%); consolidated ₹5,084 cr (+13%), EBITDA ₹849 cr (16.7%), PAT ₹485 cr (+18%) [IP Q2CY26].
- Customers: M&M is the largest — sales to M&M ₹1,893 cr in CY2025 (≈20% of consolidated revenue, ~30% of India) [AR CY25 AGM notice, RPT A3]. Other anchors Maruti, Bajaj; growing with Ford, Hyundai (5–6% of India), Royal Enfield, John Deere, Allison; Caterpillar at Metalcastello; GKN and Nexteer in Mexico [Q1CY26 call; Q2CY26 call].
- Exports from India ~13–14% of India sales (direct ~11%, of which ~3% US) [Q1CY26 call].
- Vertical growth Q2 CY26: gears +18%, composites +17%, stampings +16%; aluminium held back by pruning of loss-making 2W parts [Q2CY26 call].
- Metalcastello: ~€13 m quarterly sales, ~20% EBITDA margin, +15% YoY; its US EV programmes largely cancelled (only ~10% of expected volumes) [Q2CY26 call; Q1CY26 call].
- Mexico (moved into "Europe" reporting last year): revenue −20% in Q2 after GKN (new ownership) insourced part of production and the contract was stretched over more years [Q2CY26 call].

## Guidance history
- Q1 CY26 call (24 Apr 2026): "expect it to be a little higher than the market" (after analysts recalled an earlier aim of beating the industry by 3–5 points); new orders ₹350 cr/yr in Q1 (11% EV); export orders to start "during the end of Q2" with the US order's bulk in H2; India capex ₹400–500 cr in CY26; Europe margin ~15% steady [Q1CY26 call].
- Q2 CY26 call (23 Jul 2026): India grew ~13% vs a weighted market of 16.5–16.7% — about 3.5 points **below** market (first time outside ±2%); causes: dropping loss-making aluminium 2W parts, flat exports for a second quarter, Hyundai weakness; US order dispatched but revenue only from Q3; India margin down 80 bps, "a large part" to be recouped over two quarters; guidance reset to growth "close to the weighted average market" next quarter and 12–15% India growth; new orders ₹500 cr/yr in H1 (so ~₹150 cr in Q2); H1 capex only ₹210 cr (consolidated), H2 "significantly higher" [Q2CY26 call; IP Q2CY26].
- Verdict: **missed** — outperformance promise broken, export ramp late by a quarter, margin slipped; Europe margin delivered (15.9%).
- ICRA also flags growth "lower than the pace of growth of the underlying automotive markets through the recent quarters" as a monitorable [ICRA Aug-26].

## Capex and capacity
- CY2025 capex ₹380 cr; H1 CY26 ₹210 cr (maintenance ₹88 cr, growth ₹125 cr per the cash-flow slide) [Q2CY26 call; IP Q2CY26].
- Plan: India ₹400–500 cr in CY26 [Q1CY26 call]; ICRA expects ₹550–600 cr a year group-wide [ICRA Aug-26]. Capital commitments ₹107.5 cr at Dec-25 (₹81 cr a year earlier) [AR CY25 note 33].
- Projects: new mould line at the iron foundry (US order fills current capacity); gear plant expansions at Rajkot and Chakan; robotised stamping line at Chakan by mid-2027; three new forging presses (Chakan, Bengaluru); composites expansion; 2–3 unapproved greenfields; diversification into 4-wheeler aluminium (now ₹50–75 cr a year) and forgings for BS-VII (2028) [Q2CY26 call].
- NCLT approved the merger of CIE Aluminium Casting India into the parent on 24 Sep 2026 [BSE filing 25-Sep-26].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net cash ₹1,416 cr at Jun-26, down from ₹1,881 cr at Dec-25 [IP Q2CY26]. The fall came from a ₹701 cr working-capital outflow in H1, ₹266 cr dividend and ₹125 cr growth capex; operating cash flow was 73% of EBITDA [IP Q2CY26 cash-flow slide]. Net working capital swung from −₹384 cr to +₹291 cr in six months — worth watching.
- Gross debt ₹513 cr (Jun-26) vs ₹1,900 cr at Dec-2020; gearing 0.1x; interest cover 52.7x (CY25) [ICRA Aug-26].
- CY2025 cash from operations ₹1,258 cr, free cash flow ₹885 cr [Screener]. Dividend payout 32% (CY24 and CY25) [Screener].
- Cash pooling: European subsidiaries (led by CIE Galfor) lend to and borrow from the Spanish parent's cash pool — CY2025 subsidiaries borrowed €139.3 m and lent €25.2 m to the parent; shareholders are asked to approve up to €504 m a year of such flows [AR CY25 AGM notice].

## Moat evidence
- Scale and breadth: one of India's largest component makers; a leading European forger for PVs and off-highway [ICRA Aug-26].
- Margins steady in the mid-teens for five years despite Europe's slump; Europe restructuring lifted margin from 12.5% to 15.9% [ICRA Aug-26; IP Q2CY26].
- Supplier consolidation in Europe: customers moving crankshaft volumes to CIE as weaker rivals struggle [Q1CY26 call].
- India-for-Europe sourcing: European OEMs auditing CIE's Indian plants for castings, gears, machining [Q1CY26 call].
- But limited pricing power on non-steel inputs: India margin fell 80 bps in Q2 on gas, energy, consumables [Q2CY26 call]. Share loss evidence: India growth 3.5 points below its weighted market in Q2 [Q2CY26 call].

## Risks and red flags
- Growth under-delivery and disclosure: analysts complained of "surprise" portfolio pruning and the unannounced Mexico contract change; management refuses order-level detail and forward guidance [Q2CY26 call].
- Related parties: 20% of revenue from M&M (shareholder approval sought up to ₹2,300 cr a year); cash-pool flows with the Spanish parent up to €504 m a year; the auditor's revenue KAM specifically notes "significant related party transactions" and arm's-length testing [AR CY25].
- Contingent liabilities ≈₹147 cr at Dec-25: MSEDCL power surcharges ₹52.7 cr, water charges ₹32.5 cr, income tax ₹22.4 cr, stamp duty/cess ₹15.6 cr, excise ₹13.8 cr, new customer claims ₹4.5 cr — about 2% of net worth [AR CY25 consolidated note 33].
- Auditor: unmodified opinion; KAM on revenue recognition incl. related-party sales [AR CY25 auditor's report].
- Electrification: 10–15% of business is ICE-specific (CIE Galfor crankshafts for European PVs) [ICRA Aug-26].
- Europe: IHS sees light-vehicle output flat to −3%; Mexico GKN volume loss; Caterpillar insourcing at Metalcastello [Q1CY26 call; Q2CY26 call].
- Management based in Spain (CEO Ander Arenaza) — raised by an analyst; M&A repeatedly deferred on valuations despite net cash [Q2CY26 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone: April — "confident that we will continue the current trend", outperformance hinted; July — defensive, "we do not play games with customers", "point taken" on accelerating projects [Q1CY26 call; Q2CY26 call].
- Numbers: India growth 15% → 13%; India margin 17.6% → 16.7%; order intake ₹350 cr (Q1) → ~₹150 cr (Q2); Europe margin held ~15.8% [IP Q2CY26].
- New issues: Mexico −20%, aluminium pruning (≈23–25% of India portfolio affected together with exports), H1 working-capital outflow [Q2CY26 call; IP Q2CY26].

## My read
CIE India is a cash-rich, steady-margin business that keeps growing more slowly than its Indian markets; Q2 CY26 made that explicit and moved guidance down to "about market". Europe has been fixed on margins but not on volume, and H1's ₹700 cr working-capital build plus parent cash-pooling deserve scrutiny. The price asks for only single-digit to low-teens earnings growth, which the India order book and the US casting order can deliver. The view improves if Q3 shows India growth at or above market and capex steps up as promised; it worsens if working capital does not unwind or another "surprise" pruning appears.
