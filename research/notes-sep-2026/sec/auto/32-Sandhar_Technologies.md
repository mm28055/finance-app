## PROFILE (Q4 FY26)
# Sandhar Technologies

## Executive Summary
Sandhar Technologies exists to be the outsourced manufacturing backbone for global and domestic automotive OEMs, specializing in locking systems, vision systems (mirrors), complex aluminum die-casting, and sheet metal fabrication. It solves a critical pain point for OEMs: the need for localized, defect-free, and cost-efficient component supply at massive scale. 

Sandhar is transitioning from a phase of restructuring bleeding assets into a high-growth compounder within the Indian two-wheeler (2W) component space—drastically outperforming underlying industry growth by capturing new wallet share. Following an all-time high FY26 with ₹4,852 Cr in revenue and 40% PAT growth, management has pivoted decisively to aggressive scaling, targeting a ₹10,000 Cr revenue run-rate by FY30/31. However, despite generating record top-line results and improving capital returns, the company fundamentally operates in a heavily commoditized, capital-intensive ecosystem where margin expansion is perpetually hostage to raw material volatility, severe labor dynamics, and OEM pricing negotiations.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
*   **What do they sell?** Proprietary automotive locks, electronic/smart mirrors, structural sheet metal, aluminum die-casting parts, off-highway vehicle cabins, a rapidly scaling lineup of EV components (battery chargers and motor control units), and new royalty-based Telematics technology. 
*   **Who pays them?** Domestic and global auto OEMs across the 2W, Passenger Vehicle (PV), and Commercial Vehicle (CV) / Off-Highway segments. 
*   **How do they make money?** Long-term, volume-driven OEM supply contracts. Pricing is dictated by base contracts with built-in commodity pass-through mechanisms and periodic price retriggers.

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
*   **Moat Status: Narrow but Widening**
*   **Evidence:** Sandhar benefits from moderate switching costs. Once integrated into an OEM's platform, replacing Sandhar requires re-tooling and re-validation. The moat is currently widening as Sandhar transitions into complex proprietary products (telematics, smart locks) and consolidates market share via a unique ability to offer comprehensive, zero-to-100 casting solutions across Aluminum, Zinc, and Magnesium. 
*   **The Bear Case:** The moat is consistently breached by raw material volatility and rising labor costs. Sandhar lacks absolute pricing power. While commodity pass-throughs exist, they are lagging (30-90 days in India; up to 180 days overseas). A sudden spike in March/April aluminum prices will directly compress Q1 FY27 margins until the contractual reset triggers in Q2, exposing the company's persistent vulnerability as a price-taker. 

## Growth Vectors
*   **Inflection of Loss-Making Assets:** The next major earnings driver is the turnaround of roughly INR 342 cr in recent growth capex. The integration of the Sundaram-Clayton acquisition, alongside new facilities in Khed City (PV die-casting) and Pune (cabins/fabrication), are slated to transition to profitability once fully capitalized by the end of Q2 FY27. Management anticipates these assets will yield an aggressive 2.5x asset turnover, potentially contributing ~₹750 Cr in FY27 revenue.
*   **The EV & Tech Kicker:** While currently small, the EV business revenue is tracking to double in FY27 to ~₹40 Cr, with management expecting full profitability by FY28 as product development costs normalize. Additionally, new technology transfers in Telematics offer a capital-light, royalty-based growth avenue.
*   **Management Execution:** The tone is highly credible and peak-conviction. Management previously promised an overseas breakeven and delivered it a quarter early. They are guiding for a conservative >15% volume growth for FY27 (~₹5,500 Cr), deliberately excluding potential upward price retriggers. The overarching target is now to double revenues every 3-4 years, shifting focus from margin percentages to absolute return on capital (15-20% post-tax ROCE).

## Key Risks & Governance
*   **Commodity Lag & Margin Squeeze:** With recent spikes in aluminum and energy costs, Q1 FY27 margins in the overseas business are at acute risk. Furthermore, widespread minimum wage hikes (30-50% in key manufacturing states) present a severe new operating cost headwind. Sandhar must absorb the immediate cost shock before recuperating it via crucial "price retriggers" anticipated in Q2 FY27. 
*   **Execution Risk on Relocations:** The Sundaram-Clayton plant physical relocation has been delayed from early May to the end of Q2 FY27. Any further delay will prolong the unit's EBT-level cash burn.
*   **End-Market & Labor Concentration:** Performance is highly tethered to the Indian 2W cycle and specific PV OEMs (softness in Honda Cars' volumes acts as a persistent drag on the legacy four-wheeler segment). Additionally, widespread manpower absenteeism is currently suppressing broader OEM production volumes.
*   **Forensic/Governance View:** Clean. Management remains highly transparent regarding EBT losses at acquired plants and macroeconomic headwinds. Net debt stands at ₹897 Cr; optics around a recent €5M (₹120 Cr) increase in overseas debt were cleanly explained as standard currency translation (Euro appreciating from ₹90 to ₹111) and an Ind-AS reclassification of bill discounting to working capital demand loans (WCDL). Term loans are amortizing on schedule.

## Investment Thesis
*   **The Bull Case:** Sandhar is exiting a heavy investment cycle and entering a profound operational leverage phase. The core India business is firing on all cylinders (ROCE >21%), and the systemic drag from overseas operations has successfully reversed. If management executes its vision to double revenue to ₹10,000 Cr by FY30/31, scales the INR 342 cr new investments to a 2.5x turnover ratio, and achieves its targeted 20% ROCE, PAT could comfortably cross ₹400-450 Cr, meaning the stock is fundamentally mispriced relative to its impending free cash flow generation.
*   **The Bear Case:** The company remains a high-beta play on the cyclical auto sector. The macroeconomic "polycrisis"—European energy instability, severe domestic manpower shortages, extreme minimum wage hikes, and stubborn raw material inflation—makes Sandhar highly fragile. If OEMs push back against frequent price retriggers, the structural lag in pass-through contracts will force Sandhar to absorb severe commodity and labor shocks, permanently depressing earnings and making it a frustrating value trap.
