## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Craftsman Automation

## Executive Summary
Craftsman Automation is a vertically integrated engineering organization, distinct from typical auto-ancillary players due to its diversified exposure across Automotive Powertrain, Aluminium Die-Casting, and Industrial Engineering. The company solves the problem of high-precision manufacturing for critical engine and transmission components—parts where failure is not an option and switching costs for OEMs remain prohibitively high.

While historically a **Category Leader** in precision machining (Powertrain), Craftsman is currently executing a transition to become a dominant player in Aluminium Die-Casting through organic expansion and the integration of acquisitions (Sunbeam, DR Axion). As of Q1 FY27, the company is in a massive expansion phase: absorbing start-up costs from new facilities, finalizing its restructuring efforts, and proactively building infrastructure to capture a booming domestic manufacturing upcycle and multi-year data center power demand. It is a "Process compounder" confidently deploying capital, currently masked by temporary capex-driven margin compression.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
*   **What they sell:**
    *   **Powertrain:** Cylinder blocks, cylinder heads, camshafts, and transmission parts for Commercial Vehicles (CV), Farm Equipment, and Off-Highway vehicles.
    *   **Aluminium Products:** High-pressure die-cast parts, including crankcases and structural components for 2-wheelers and passenger vehicles (PV).
    *   **Industrial & Engineering:** Automated storage and retrieval systems (ASRS), static racking, and contract manufacturing for non-auto segments.
*   **Who pays them:** Tier-1 Global and Indian OEMs (e.g., Daimler, Tata Motors, Mahindra), Warehousing/Logistics operators, and increasingly, power generation OEMs.
*   **Monetization:**
    *   **Long-term Contracts:** Deep integration with OEM product lifecycles (5-7 years).
    *   **Pass-through Mechanisms:** Raw material volatility (especially aluminium) is passed through to customers, though lags can impact short-term working capital and optical margins.

### Segment & Geographic Mix (Based on Q1 FY27 Trajectory)

| Segment | Revenue Contribution | Trend (Q1 FY27) | Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

*   **Geographic Focus:** Predominantly India-centric manufacturing with increasing export focus for Aluminium (Sunbeam) and Stationary Engines (European/US markets).

### Competitive Moat: Durable
Craftsman’s moat is built on **Process Engineering & Switching Costs**, not IP.
*   **High Switching Costs:** In the Powertrain segment, Craftsman supplies critical functional parts. Validation takes years; OEMs rarely switch suppliers mid-lifecycle due to risk of engine failure.
*   **Replacement Cost Barrier:** The moat is widening structurally. Replicating Craftsman's 2016 powertrain capacity today would cost 5x to 7x more due to soaring land, civil, and dollar-denominated machinery costs, creating an insurmountable barrier to entry for smaller domestic peers.
*   **Scale in Niches:** In Industrial Storage, Craftsman has emerged as one of the top two players in India for automated storage, benefiting from the consolidation of the supplier base where smaller regional players cannot compete on project execution capabilities.
*   **The Bear Case:** The moat in Powertrain is threatened by the long-term secular decline of Internal Combustion Engines (ICE), specifically in PVs, though Craftsman’s heavy exposure to CVs and Tractors provides a longer runway.

## Growth Vectors

### 1. Stationary Engines & Data Center Power (The Kotthavadi Project)
Management has identified a massive opportunity in backup power for data centers. With global capacity for large engines constrained, Craftsman is positioning itself as a key supplier for critical heavy-duty parts.
*   **Trajectory:** The initial $100M revenue target for FY30 is now fully secured across four active global clients, with a fifth and sixth in the pipeline.
*   **Execution:** Pilot machining is already underway for two customers. Revenue realization is mapped out clearly: 30% production phase-in during FY28, 80% in FY29, and 100% by FY30. Subsequent contract wins are expected to have accelerated sub-2-year validation timelines now that the company's quality is proven.

### 2. Aluminium Segment Turnaround & Expansion
*   **Sunbeam Restructuring:** The turnaround has been delayed by a few months to handhold exiting customers, with 90% completion now targeted for December. The company is actively exiting low-margin legacy retail businesses (which will mechanically shrink Sunbeam's top-line by 10-20%) to transition into a value-added machining supplier. The target is to exit Q4 FY27 with Sunbeam at a mid-teens EBITDA margin.
*   **Greenfield Expansions:** With existing aluminum capacities maxed out, Craftsman is deploying capital aggressively ahead of the curve. This includes a multi-phase greenfield expansion at Hosur Unit 3 for High-Pressure Die Casting (HPDC) and a major capacity build-out at DR Axion for orders maturing in FY29/FY30.

### 3. Industrial Automation
The Industrial segment is experiencing a structural spike in EBIT margins and demand. As India's broader manufacturing capex wave accelerates, demand for complex lifting equipment and automated storage is driving high operating leverage in a segment where Craftsman faces limited oligopolistic competition.

## Key Risks & Governance

### Financial & Operational Risks
*   **Debt & Capex Levels:** Craftsman is executing a highly elevated consolidated capex plan of ~₹1,500 Cr for FY27 (₹1,000 Cr standalone, ₹430 Cr DR Axion). While funded via internal accruals, this massive scale will continue to consume free cash flow and may create short-term cash mismatches, leaving little room for error if the cycle turns. Net Debt/EBITDA stands at 2.55x.
*   **Optical Margin Compression:** Volatility in aluminium prices creates optical margin pressure, while current development and start-up costs for the stationary engine segment are being heavily expensed, momentarily depressing apparent consolidated margins.
*   **Execution Risk:** The company is simultaneously ramping up multiple greenfield plants (Hosur Unit 3, DR Axion), completing a delayed restructuring of a distressed acquisition (Sunbeam), and scaling a complex new product line (Stationary Engines). This severely stretches management bandwidth.

### Forensic/Governance Watch
*   **Capital Allocation:** The aggressive ~₹1,500 Cr capex in anticipation of demand (e.g., buying land and building ₹100-250 Cr infrastructure per plant before machinery is ordered) indicates a very high risk/reward appetite.
*   **Asset Monetization:** The sale of Sunbeam’s non-core piston assets signaled disciplined cleanup, and management is currently "close to signing" a pending land sale in Gurgaon, which will be a key catalyst for near-term liquidity.

## Investment Thesis

### The Bull Case: "Peak Capex, Approaching Peak Operating Leverage"
Craftsman is firmly in the investment phase of the J-curve, building generational capacity ahead of demand. The current margin dampeners are temporary.
1.  **Margin Expansion:** As Sunbeam successfully sheds its low-margin legacy business to hit mid-teens EBITDA by Q4 FY27, and industrial segment operating leverage holds, consolidated margins will expand significantly.
2.  **Hidden AI Play:** The market underappreciates the "Stationary Engine" segment. This is a direct, secured derivative play on global data center energy needs, providing a non-auto growth vector that hedges ICE risk with standard corporate margins (20%+).
3.  **Cyclical Tailwinds:** India's manufacturing capex cycle structurally elevates the Industrial segment, while the shift to higher tonnage vehicles increases content per vehicle in the Powertrain division.

### The Bear Case: "Debt-Heavy in a Slowing Cycle"
If the anticipated ₹1,500 Cr annual capex outpaces internal cash generation, the company may be forced to take on expensive debt, breaking their stable Net Debt/EBITDA commitment. Furthermore, if Sunbeam's pruning faces deeper pushback, or if global economic slowdowns pause data-center/heavy-engine deployments, Craftsman could be left with severely stranded capacity and extended margin drag.
