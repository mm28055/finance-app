# Cosmic CRF — deep research (for Industrial - Railway note)

Screener code 543928 (BSE SME). Consolidated page used (it only covers FY25–FY26, the years with subsidiaries); standalone page for earlier history. Reports half-yearly. Latest call: H2 FY26 (26 May 2026). Previous: H1 FY26 (6 Nov 2025). No H1 FY27 results yet (due November).

## Business and segments
- Kolkata-based maker of cold-rolled formed (CRF) steel sections and fabricated parts for railway wagons and coaches (flap doors, centre sills, side and end bodies, dispatch-memo and non-DM items), plus infrastructure steel: sheet piles, cofferdam items, crash barriers, octagonal and tubular poles, high masts, with an in-house galvanizing bath. Says 75% of a wagon/coach body (other than interiors) is made under its four roofs; 3,000–3,500 SKUs [Q4 call; IP H2FY26].
- Three entities: Cosmic CRF standalone (Singur, 55,000 t/yr, railway-focused); N.S. Engineering Projects (NSEPPL, Howrah, 65,000 t/yr, infrastructure, bought through insolvency in 2024, 74% owned); Cosmic Springs & Engineers (CSEL, Jangalpur, 13,600 t/yr: 3,600 t springs + 10,000 t fabrication; 11,000 t forging unit under construction) [IP H2FY26; Q4 call].
- FY26 production 1,06,370 t vs 55,941 t in FY25 (~90% up) on 1,33,600 t capacity (~80% use): standalone 42,690 t, NSEPPL 56,380 t, CSEL 7,830 t [Q4 call; IP H2FY26].
- No segment reporting: the annual report says the group is a single segment [AR FY26 MD&A §5, Note 30]. Standalone FY26 revenue ₹334cr against consolidated ₹717cr, so roughly half of group sales now come from the subsidiaries, mostly NSEPPL's infrastructure products [Screener].
- Springs FY26 revenue ₹67cr on about 2,300 t of springs plus fabrication; springs were 30% railway, 70% other buyers in H1 [Q4 call; Q2 call].
- Customers: wagon builders such as Titagarh, Hindustan Engineering and Texmaco (Infomerics), Indian Railways zonal divisions for refurbishment parts (₹120–150cr of such orders held, Nov 2025), and EPC contractors such as L&T for sheet piles [Infomerics Dec-25; Q2 call]. Revenue and order book concentrated in West Bengal [Infomerics Dec-25].
- Selling price has fallen from ~₹1,04,000/t in 2021–22 to ~₹64,000–65,000/t as steel softened, which is why management now guides on tonnes [Q2 call].

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated unless stated)
- FY25 → FY26: revenue ₹402cr → ₹717cr (+78%); operating profit ₹44cr → ₹78cr; margin 11% both years; net profit ₹31cr → ₹51cr (+65%; FY25 included a ₹5.9cr exceptional gain on the NSEPPL takeover) [Screener; Q4 call].
- Half-years: H1 FY25 ₹169cr / 13% / ₹18cr; H2 FY25 ₹232cr / 10% / ₹11cr; H1 FY26 ₹304cr / 12% / ₹24cr; H2 FY26 ₹412cr / 10% / ₹26cr. H2 growth +78% (company: +77%) [Screener; IP H2FY26].
- Standalone history: revenue ₹121cr (FY23), ₹253cr (FY24), ₹302cr (FY25), ₹334cr (FY26); margin 10%, 9%, 12%, 13%; standalone H2 FY26 margin 10.7% vs 15.6% in H1 [Screener standalone; IP H2FY26].
- ROCE 14% FY26 (standalone 9% in FY25–FY26). Cash cycle 112 → 69 days: debtor days 94 → 105, inventory 82 → 65, payable 64 → 100 [Screener].
- Per-tonne economics: conversion cost fell from ~₹6,000/t to ~₹1,600/t (target ₹1,266); NSEPPL gross spread ~₹7,000/t on ~₹53,000/t cost (~13% EBITDA); springs ~₹29,000/set price on ~₹21,000 cost [Q2 call].

## Guidance history
- Nov 2025 (H1 call): FY26 volume 1,00,000–1,10,000 t (delivered 1,06,370 t — met). Springs RDSO licence "within 40–45 days" (slipped to June 2026, prototype approval only). Forging commercial run "April or May" 2026 (slipped: May call says within nine months). Amzen "to complete within this financial year" (slipped). "Will not be diluting till 2028 March." Sustainable EBITDA 13–14%, PAT 9–10% [Q2 call].
- May 2026 (H2 call, latest): FY27 volume 1,22,000–1,30,000 t (+15–22%); revenue "at least 30% higher", and elsewhere "at least 20%, 25% higher" if government spending holds; springs margin to ~10% then 15–18% PAT after RDSO; forging within nine months; 1,75,000 t by FY28 excluding Amzen (standalone 65,000, NSEPPL 85,000, CSEL 25,000); 3,50,000 t and about ₹3,500cr revenue by FY29 with Amzen ("don't hold me against it" was said of a similar figure in Nov) [Q4 call official; IP H2FY26].
- The 13–14% EBITDA claim of November was not met: H2 FY26 was 9.9% consolidated. Management blamed ₹67cr of springs sold at ₹1.5–1.7cr profit to traders, ~₹2–2.5cr of payroll for the future Amzen team and Amzen legal costs the auditor would not let it capitalise, about ₹8–10cr in all [Q4 call].

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ₹133cr (Screener: 18.5% of sales); FY25 ₹47cr. Singur 36,000 → 55,000 t with two new sheds (>₹30cr); NSEPPL 12,500 → 65,000 t with seven bays and a 24,000 t galvanizing bath (plan 85,000 t by FY28); forging unit ~₹40cr, raised from 6,800 t to 11,000 t [Q4 call; Q2 call; IP H2FY26].
- Amzen Transportation (Fatehgarh Sahib, Punjab; 72.5 acres; idle; FY24 turnover ₹0.11cr): letter of intent 30 July 2026 for ₹284cr over 12 months or ₹266.6cr within 90 days, subject to NCLT approval; ₹60cr deposited (₹28.4cr bank guarantee, ₹31.6cr to UCO Bank and Prudent ARC). Capacity 3,600 wagons a year (expandable to 7,200) plus 20,000 t of bridge girders. Management put the total project at ~₹400cr with a ~₹200cr loan and first production "March–May 2027" [News: Whalesbook 30 Jul 2026; ScanX; Q4 call].
- Also ₹20cr paid by a subsidiary to Sagacious Capital "towards participation for acquisition of new units through CIRP" (the call calls it a Plan B site) [AR FY26 Note 5; Q4 call].
- Shareholders asked (EOGM 2 Sep 2026) to raise the borrowing limit from ₹200cr to ₹1,000cr [News: ScanX].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹78cr → ₹133cr (FY26); cash and fixed deposits ₹176cr → ₹111cr; net debt ₹22cr [Screener]. Term loans only ~₹35cr; working-capital lines ~₹80cr [AR FY26 Note 14; Q4 call: "₹36cr" term loan, "₹84cr" working capital].
- ₹100cr of the March 2025 preferential issue sits in fixed deposits; ₹74cr of it spent on capex and subsidiaries [AR FY26 Directors' report §8].
- Operating cash: Screener −₹73cr (FY25) → +₹24cr (FY26); company presentation −₹89.2cr → +₹3.6cr. Free cash flow −₹120cr and −₹109cr (card data) [Screener; IP H2FY26].
- Trade receivables doubled from ₹104cr to ₹205cr; ₹5.5cr is over three years old (inherited with NSEPPL); inventory ₹76cr → ₹108cr [AR FY26 Notes 7–8].
- Funding history: IPO June 2023; preferential issue March 2025 raised ₹159.7cr of shares at ₹1,614 plus warrants (only 18,600 of 3,15,000 converted by March 2026); promoter-group warrant conversions approved Aug–Sep 2026; NSEPPL's remaining 26% to be bought by issuing 7,25,041 new shares at ₹1,330 (~₹96cr, ~7.9% more shares), mostly to promoter-affiliated AVB Endeavors [AR FY26; News: ScanX, Multibagg].
- No dividend. Infomerics BBB+/Stable (upgraded Dec 2025), consolidated view; fund-based limits ~93% used over 12 months to Oct 2025 [Infomerics Dec-25].

## Moat evidence
- Scale in a fragmented niche: says typical CRF peers have ~15,000 t; it cut prices to take share, relying on low overheads [Q2 call]. Claims to have been the only CRF supplier for the BOSM wagon trial design [Q2 call]. Claims market leadership in sheet piles [IP H2FY26] — no third-party share data.
- Fungible plants: 80% of capacity switchable to infrastructure, 75% to railway [Q4 call]. That diversification cushioned the 2025 wagon slowdown ("anticipating a moderation in railway sector momentum, the company pivoted to infrastructure") [IP H2FY26].
- Approvals: springs RDSO prototype approval (7 June 2026) for up to 60,000 hot-coiled helical springs (1,000 wagon sets), but held in the name of Prilika Enterprises, the promoter company still selling the spring unit to CSEL under a Feb 2025 BTA [News: ScanX Jun 2026].
- No pricing power: steel is ~85% of sales; margins 10–13% [Screener].

## Risks and red flags
- **Related parties.** Consolidated key audit matter: "advanced significant amounts to a related party for the procurement of raw materials and capital assets", outstanding at year end [AR FY26 auditor's report]. Prilika Enterprises (a promoter holding ~10%) sold the springs business via BTA (₹9.75cr paid FY25; ₹9.75cr advanced for a further unit; ₹9.75cr still to be executed), rents premises and does job work; trade payable to Prilika ₹10.3cr at March 2025 [AR FY26 Notes 6, 32, 35]. Other related firms: AVB Entech, Raft Motors, Cosmic EV, Asansol Steel Castings.
- **Promoter-group share issue** to buy the NSEPPL minority (₹1,330/share, ~7.9% dilution) despite the Nov 2025 "no dilution till March 2028" statement; May call also spoke of "another ₹200cr of raise" in FY28 [News: ScanX; Q4 call official].
- **Disputes**: ₹10.3cr claim by Cosmic Ferro Alloys under the Jan 2022 BTA in arbitration; ₹49cr corporate guarantee for NSEPPL [AR FY26 Note 32].
- **Controls**: quarterly stock statements to banks differed from books every quarter (up to ₹6cr); audit-trail gaps for fixed assets, inventory and cost records; NSEPPL's software had no audit trail [AR FY26 CARO; consolidated report].
- **Amzen**: an idle, insolvent plant that has not produced for years; ₹284cr price plus repairs; adds debt; wagon building earns 5–12% margins in this sector [News; note section 3].
- Concentration in West Bengal; steel price swings with a lag on escalation clauses; monsoon hits infrastructure offtake; wheel-set shortages stall wagon builders [Infomerics; Q2 call].
- Promoters 55.11% (March 2026) [Screener].

## What changed between the two calls
- November: defensive tone on Amzen ("disheartening" that investors only ask about it), promises of RDSO licence in 40–45 days, forging by April–May, 13–14% EBITDA, no dilution to March 2028. May: Supreme Court win, Amzen the "biggest growth driver", volumes met, but margin fell to 9.9% in H2, springs licence and forging both slipped, and a further raise was floated for FY28. After the call: LoI (July), springs prototype approval (June, in Prilika's name), promoter-group share swap and warrant conversion (Aug–Sep).

## My read
The operating record is real: volumes doubled, capacity nearly doubled and FY26 profit rose 65%, with net debt only ₹22cr. But this is steel bending at 10–11% margins, cash flow after capex has been negative for two years, receivables doubled, and the springs and forging steps that were meant to lift margins have both slipped. Governance is the bigger worry: the auditor flagged related-party advances, the springs unit and its approval sit with a promoter company, and the minority of NSEPPL is being bought with new shares issued mostly to a promoter-affiliated entity months after a no-dilution pledge. At about 25 times earnings the price needs ~16% a year, close to my ~15%, so it is not expensive — but Amzen (₹284cr plus repairs, plus debt) will decide the next three years. I would wait for NCLT approval, a funding plan, and H1 FY27 cash flow.
