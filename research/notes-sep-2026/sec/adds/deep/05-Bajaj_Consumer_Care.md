# Bajaj Consumer Care (BAJAJCON) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Screener code BAJAJCON (consolidated page has figures). Sector: Consumer Durables, lifestyle group (personal care).

## Business and segments
- Sells hair oils and personal care to the Indian mass market. The core is **Bajaj Almond Drops Hair Oil (ADHO)**, a "light hair oil" (a non-sticky oil made on light liquid paraffin with almond and other oils); management put ADHO at "approximately 80%" of revenue in Q1 FY27; CRISIL also says ~80%. [Q1 call; CRISIL Aug-26]
- The rest, which management calls the **growth portfolio** (non-ADHO), was ₹225cr of FY26 sales: mostly Bajaj 100% Pure Coconut Oil and the new Bajaj Gold Enriched Coconut Hair Oil, then **Banjara's** (herbal hair and skin care from Vishal Personal Care, bought in 2025), plus Amla, Nomarks and smaller brands. Management calls it "net contribution positive, with margins in single digit". [Q4 call; IP Q4FY26]
- Banjara's was "less than 5%" of FY26 revenue (Q4 call) and "close to around 5%" in Q1 FY27 (Q1 call); VPCL earned ₹51.2cr of revenue after 16 May 2025 with a ₹0.39cr loss. [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR FY26 Directors' Report]
- Share: CRISIL says over 60% of the light-hair-oil niche; management says the company has "less than 10% market share" of the overall hair-oil category, which it calls a "$2 billion plus" market with 92% penetration. [CRISIL Aug-26; Q4 call]
- Channels: general trade about 70% of FY26 business (half urban, half rural; half of urban is wholesale); organised trade (modern trade, e-commerce, quick commerce, canteens) nearly 30%. International (Nepal, Bangladesh, Middle East and Africa, rest of world) is small; the company does not disclose a reliable figure (the international table in both decks is a garbled overlay). [Q4 call; IP Q4FY26; IP Q1FY27]
- Reach: CRISIL cites more than 45 lakh retail outlets. [CRISIL Aug-26]
- Plants: own units at Guwahati (Assam) and Paonta Sahib (Himachal) plus third-party makers; no capacity figures are given and capacity is not a constraint. [AR FY26 MD&A]
- Subsidiaries: Uptown Properties and Leasing (a "material subsidiary" that constructs and leases commercial space; ₹0.25cr loss in FY26; carries the group's ₹43cr goodwill), Bajaj Bangladesh (₹15.0cr revenue, ₹3.4cr loss), Bajaj Corp International (FZE), and VPCL. [AR FY26 Directors' Report, Note 5]

## Key numbers (Screener, consolidated)
- Revenue: FY22 ₹880cr, FY23 ₹961cr, FY24 ₹984cr, FY25 ₹965cr, FY26 ₹1,165cr; TTM ₹1,240cr. Revenue was flat for four years (₹797cr in FY17) before FY26's 21% jump. [Screener]
- Operating profit and margin: FY22 ₹174cr (20%), FY23 ₹141cr (15%), FY24 ₹155cr (16%), FY25 ₹128cr (13%), FY26 ₹221cr (19%), TTM ₹264cr. The margin was 30–33% in FY17–FY19. [Screener]
- Net profit: FY24 ₹155cr, FY25 ₹125cr, FY26 ₹190cr, TTM ₹223cr. [Screener]
- Quarters (revenue / operating profit / margin / net profit): Q1 FY26 ₹273cr / ₹41cr / 15% / ₹38cr; Q2 ₹265cr / ₹48cr / 18% / ₹42cr; Q3 ₹306cr / ₹56cr / 18% / ₹46cr; Q4 ₹327cr / ₹77cr / 23% / ₹64cr; Q1 FY27 ₹342cr / ₹83cr / 24% / ₹71cr. Year-on-year revenue growth: 11%, 13%, 31%, 31%, 25%. [Screener]
- Company's own Q1 FY27: net sales ₹341.4cr (+28.3%), gross margin 61.8% (63.7% in Q4, 56.7% a year earlier), EBITDA ₹84.4cr (24.7%, +101%), PAT ₹70.7cr (+84.8%). Advertising 14.6% of sales. The company's growth is on net sales (₹266.2cr a year earlier); Screener includes ₹7.2cr of other operating income in the base, hence 25%. [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Company's FY26: revenue ₹1,153.4cr (+21.4%), gross margin 60.1% (+664 bps), EBITDA ₹224.4cr (19.5%, +70.9%), PAT ₹190.2cr (+51.8%). [IP Q4FY26]
- Return on capital: FY22 26%, FY23 21%, FY24 23%, FY25 19%, FY26 30%. [Screener]
- Unit economics: ADHO volume "low teens" on an ml-adjusted basis in Q1 FY27 (Q4: "just about double digit" ml-adjusted, mid-single-digit absolute). Small packs and sachets grew fastest ("strong twenties" in FY26). Much of the margin recovery came from **reducing the ml in packs at the same price** ("MLH reductions") plus mix. [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q4FY26]
- Cost split FY26 (Screener): materials 39% of sales, staff 12%, other costs 27% (advertising ₹168cr, about 14.4%), power and factory 3%. Materials were 45–46% in FY23–FY25. [Screener; AR FY26 Note 30]
- Other income fell to ₹27cr in FY26 from ₹36cr as ₹187cr of cash went on a buyback. [AR FY26 consolidated P&L]

## Guidance history
- The company says it gives no numeric guidance, on both calls. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Margin band**: Q4 call (17 Apr): "anywhere from low 20s to mid 20s is where we would aspire to operate"; felt "confident about our ability to maintain margins in the current approximate range" despite the war, but would need "frontal pricing" in Q1. Q1 call (13 Jul): "happy to operate in the low to mid 20s"; "pricing is not going to drive any further margin expansion. We don't have any pricing actions planned"; gross margin "slightly more under stress in Q2" than Q1, easing in Q3–Q4. Delivered: EBITDA 24.7% in Q1 (company). **Kept, at the top of the band.** [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- In the Q3 call (January, as quoted by an analyst on the Q4 call) management had said margins would "pause" at about 18.5% for a few quarters; Q4 came in at 23–24%. Management said commentary "has to be taken directionally". [Q4 call]
- **Growth**: Q4: core brands at "around a double-digit revenue CAGR ... we'll be happy"; strong momentum "for another two quarters" before lapping high bases. Q1: aspiration of "consistent double-digit to a low teens" growth; "we will be unhappy if we don't deliver double-digit growth". Delivered: +28% in Q1. **Kept.** [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Growth portfolio**: Q4: from ₹225cr to "around ₹500cr" in three years (a "30s kind of CAGR"), organically, with acquisitions as a top-up. Q1: long-term "high 20s" growth for the portfolio; it grew "high single-digit sequentially" in Q1, with coconut prices cut; progress to be reported once a year. Slightly softer wording. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Project Aarohan** (move from indirect to direct distribution): Q4 said about a 4% growth premium in states where it is done, two-thirds of the business covered, the last third (five new states) in FY27. Q1 described the one-time gain as "200 to 300 basis points". The two calls gave different figures. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Advertising**: kept at 15–16% of sales over the medium term; "not an area where we are trying to squeeze". Q1 was 14.6%. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- **International**: Q4 said FY26 was weak (double-digit decline) with new leadership and a turnaround expected over two quarters; Q1 said a "very, very strong rebound", double-digit EBITDA. **Delivered, on a low base.** [IP Q4FY26; Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Capex is tiny: ₹5–6cr a year in FY23–FY25 (0.5–0.6% of sales). FY26 purchase of fixed assets rose to ₹32.8cr; the related-party note shows ₹27.3cr paid in FY26 "against residential flats" to the promoter company Bajaj Resources (₹11.6cr) and to other promoter-linked entities (₹15.7cr), which appears to explain most of the jump. The annual report does not say what the flats are for. [Screener; AR FY26 Note 50, cash-flow statement]
- Capital work in progress has sat at ₹27–28cr since FY22 (Screener), apparently in the property subsidiary Uptown. [Screener; AR FY26]
- Growth spending is on brands (acquisition of VPCL at a ₹108.3cr enterprise value, about ₹120cr in all, bought in two tranches: 49% in March 2025 and 51% on 16 May 2025), an SAP upgrade and distribution. The ₹102.5cr Banjara's brand sits on the balance sheet as an indefinite-life asset tested yearly for impairment. [News: Upstox, Feb 2025; AR FY26 Note 5]
- VPCL's operating business was merged into the parent under an NCLT-approved scheme effective 1 May 2026 (appointed date 15 March 2025); its trademark-licensing business stays in the subsidiary. [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26]
- No capacity constraint; manufacturing is a mix of own and third-party units. [AR FY26 MD&A]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Net cash ₹390cr on Screener (March 2026) after the buyback; CRISIL puts liquid surplus at about ₹409cr. Borrowings ₹16cr (mostly leases). [Screener; CRISIL Aug-26]
- **Buyback**: 64.3 lakh shares (4.69%) at ₹290 in September–October 2025, ₹186.6cr. No dividend recommended for FY26. Promoters' stake rose from 40.95% to 42.97% only because of the buyback. [AR FY26 Directors' Report, Note 15]
- Cash conversion (operating cash ÷ operating profit): FY22 74%, FY23 72%, FY24 75%, FY25 51%, FY26 89%; five years 74%. Free cash ₹164cr in FY26. [Screener]
- Working capital: debtor days rose from 8 (FY22) to 24 (FY26) as organised trade grew; inventory days 52; cash cycle 19 → 42 days. Management says trade stock is lean and there was no channel filling. [Screener; Q4 call]
- Rating: CRISIL A1+ on a ₹100cr commercial-paper programme (reaffirmed 26 Aug 2026); downgrade triggers include operating margin below 14% and large buybacks or dividends. [CRISIL Aug-26]

## Moat evidence
- Over 60% share of light hair oil (CRISIL); ADHO has "volume market share gains" on both last-three-month and annual bases (company). [CRISIL Aug-26; IP Q4FY26]
- Pricing: took ml cuts in Q3–Q4 FY26 and selective price rises in Q1 FY27 while volume still grew low-teens: evidence of pricing power at small price points. Gross margin 53–55% (FY23–FY25) → 60% (FY26) → 61.8% (Q1 FY27). [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Distribution: 45 lakh outlets; Aarohan adds direct reach. [CRISIL Aug-26]
- Weakness: in coconut oil it had to cut its discount to Marico's Parachute and still lost volume at times; management calls the non-ADHO battles "extremely competitive" against "extremely large competitors". [Q1 call; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Management's own view that the category is mature ("It's a mature category, yes") and that D2C brands lower entry barriers. [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A]

## Risks and red flags
- **Single brand**: ADHO about 80% of revenue. [Q1 call; CRISIL]
- **Crude and edible oil**: light liquid paraffin, packaging, mustard and almond oils all rose with the West Asia war; "nearly 100% of our cost base is under inflation". [Q4 call]
- **Margin already at the top of the band**: the recovery came from ml cuts, mix and cost control; management says price will not drive further expansion and Q2 gross margin is under more stress. [Q1 call]
- **Related parties**: royalty of ₹11.0cr paid to the promoter company Bajaj Resources in FY26 (₹9.5cr in FY25), about 1% of sales; rent ₹1.1cr to promoter entities; professional fees of ₹1.5cr to a director; CSR of ₹3.5cr through the family's Kamalnayan Jamnalal Bajaj Foundation; ₹27.3cr paid to promoter entities "against residential flats" with no explanation. The promoter group includes Bajaj Hindusthan Sugar. [AR FY26 Note 50]
- Auditor (Chopra Vimal & Co.): unqualified; one key audit matter (estimates of trade discounts and rebates); the SAP audit trail ran all year and was preserved: a clean report. [AR FY26 auditor's report]
- Contingent liabilities small: ₹9.9cr of indirect-tax claims. [AR FY26 Note 22]
- Institutional selling: Nippon Life cut from 8.93% to 5.89% in FY26 and HDFC Mutual Fund filed a stake-cut disclosure on 22 Sep 2026. [AR FY26 Note 15; BSE filing list on Screener]
- MD&A sloppiness: it quotes a consultant's figure of USD 152 million for India's hair-oil market, against management's "$2 billion plus" on the call. [AR FY26 MD&A; Q4 call]
- Management: new MD Naveen Pandey since 1 July 2025 (previous MD Jaideep Nandi). A short record. [AR FY26 Note 50]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "extremely volatile ... will need us to take pricing" (April) to "the worst is behind us" on supply, with high-cost inventory still to flow through Q2 (July).
- Volume improved: ADHO ml-adjusted volume from "just about double digit" to "low teens".
- Rural caught up with urban; international turned from decline to strong growth.
- Growth-portfolio language softened from a "30s CAGR" to ₹500cr to "high 20s" long-term and annual disclosure only.
- Aarohan's growth premium was put at about 4% in April and 2–3 points in July.
- Both calls: no acquisitions to disclose.

## My read
The turnaround is real. After four flat years, revenue grew 21% in FY26 and 25–28% in Q1 FY27, the margin went from 13% to 24%, and return on capital to 30%, with the brand gaining volume share. But the easy part is done. The margin is at the top of the company's own band after ml cuts and cost savings; FY27's first half faces high-cost stock, and growth will slow to the double digits management aspires to once it laps H2 FY26. From today's ₹223cr of trailing profit, I expect about 13% a year over three to five years (strong FY27 on a weak H1 FY26 base, then about 10–11%). The price needs about 17%, so it is Full, not cheap. Governance is mostly clean (a clean audit trail and a small contingent list), but the promoter royalty and an unexplained ₹27cr paid to promoter entities for residential flats are the kind of leakage to watch. It would become an own below about 22x trailing earnings, or if the growth portfolio reaches ₹350cr+ with margins held.
