# Shiprocket (NSE: SHIPROCKET) — deep research, 30 Sep 2026

Listed 19 Aug 2026 (issue price ₹97, first trade ₹131). Q1 FY27 was its first quarter as a listed company and the 8 Sep 2026 call was its **first** shareholder call, so there is no Q4 call. The "two calls" comparison below is therefore the Q1 call against the IPO prospectus (RHP, 5 Aug 2026) and the FY26 annual report. No credit rating exists: the offer was equity only and borrowings are bank overdrafts [RHP, credit rating section].

**Transcript check:** the stored Q1 FY27 transcript is the exchange-filed official transcript. A word-level comparison with the PDF on the company site showed only page-header differences. Verdict: "matches official" [Q1 call].

## Business and segments
- Asset-light "operating system" for small online sellers who sell outside Amazon and Flipkart (own website, Instagram and WhatsApp, quick-commerce, exports). It charges per shipment or per checkout, not a subscription. FY26: GMV ₹32,777cr, 20.2cr unique transactions, 2.15 lakh active merchants. Year to Jun-26: GMV ₹34,662cr, 21.6cr transactions, 2.24 lakh merchants [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 p.49].
- **Core** (domestic shipping over 42 couriers, plus add-ons such as early COD and Delivery Boost): Q1 FY27 revenue ₹411.7cr (+22%), contribution margin 21.2%, adjusted EBITDA ₹52.7cr (12.8%). FY26: 73% of revenue (₹1,485cr), adjusted EBITDA margin 12.6% (12.0% in FY25); profitable every year since FY22 [IP Q1FY27; AR FY26 p.50-57].
- **Emerging** (30% of Q1 revenue vs 24% a year ago): revenue ₹180.4cr (+70%); adjusted EBITDA –₹43.8cr (–24.3%). FY26 split: Omnichannel (cargo, fulfilment, quick-commerce slots) ₹303cr (+76%); Cross-border ₹139cr (+14%); Ads and marketing tech (Fastrr checkout, ads) ₹84cr (+213%) [AR FY26 p.51]. In Q1: Omnichannel +92%, Martech +193%, Cross-border –2.3% [IP Q1FY27 p.17].
- Geography: mostly India, heavy in Tier 2/3 cities. Cross-border reaches 146 countries (5,884 merchants in FY26) [AR FY26 p.61].
- Capacity: none in the plant sense. Own leased fulfilment centres: 13 in 10 cities, 7.63 lakh sq ft (Mar-26), plus 5 customer-owned centres it runs and 11 third-party warehouses. Utilisation is not disclosed [RHP risk factor 10].

## Key numbers
- Screener consolidated revenue: FY24 ₹1,316cr, FY25 ₹1,632cr, FY26 ₹2,024cr (+24%). Screener operating profit (after share-based pay): –₹293cr, –₹60cr, –₹67cr (margin –22%, –3.7%, –3.3%). Net loss: –₹595cr in FY24 (includes a goodwill write-down), –₹74cr, –₹79cr [Screener; AR p.50].
- Quarters (Q1 FY26 / Q4 FY26 / Q1 FY27): revenue ₹442 / 554 / 592cr; Screener operating profit –₹18 / –13 / –11cr; net loss –₹18 / –16 / –14cr. Company "adjusted EBITDA" (adds back ESOP cost, deducts lease rent, excludes other income): ₹1.0 / 7.6 / 8.9cr, margin 0.2% / 1.4% / 1.5%. Contribution margin 18.2% / 18.8% / 19.4% [Screener; IP Q1FY27 p.13-15]. Screener holds only these three quarters.
- **Unit economics per transaction (my arithmetic from the call):** the CFO gave adjusted EBITDA of ₹1.45 per transaction (₹0.22 a year ago), which implies about 6.1cr transactions in Q1. That works out to revenue of about ₹96, contribution of about ₹19 and central costs of about ₹17 per transaction. FY26 revenue per transaction was about ₹100 (₹20,241mn over 202mn), flat on FY25 [Q1 call; AR p.50].
- Where the margin gain came from: contribution margin rose 124bp, while costs between contribution and adjusted EBITDA stayed at 17.9% of revenue (18.0% a year ago). So gains came from mix and pricing, not yet from overhead leverage [IP Q1FY27 p.19].
- **Path to profit:** Core's adjusted EBITDA rose ₹11.4cr on the year (₹41.3cr to ₹52.7cr) but Emerging's loss widened in rupees (–₹40.3cr to –₹43.8cr; –₹43.0cr in Q4). Total adjusted EBITDA rose only ₹7.9cr. The company needs Emerging's rupee loss to stop growing. Below adjusted EBITDA sits ESOP cost of ₹28.5cr a quarter (₹112cr in FY26), which is why the reported result is still a loss [IP Q1FY27 p.19, 27; AR p.53].
- Other KPIs: net revenue retention 107.8% (FY26). Core CAC (cost to win a merchant) ₹3,560 vs ₹3,136 a year ago and ₹2,781 in Q4. Power merchants (100+ transactions a month) 10,170 vs 10,282, but revenue per power merchant up 25% to ₹18.7 lakh. 8.8% of Core merchants use Emerging products (7.3%). Merchants served via Emerging 48,406 (+57%) [IP Q1FY27 p.18, 26; AR p.51].

## Guidance history
- No formal numeric guidance was given; the stored analysis saying "No guidance" is correct [Q1 call].
- Statements on the call: contribution margin expected to "maintain around that range" (about 19%). No timeframe for Emerging to break even. CAC not expected to rise steadily. Q3 is seasonally the weakest quarter because direct-channel sellers shift stock and spend to marketplaces in the festive season [Q1 call].
- Growth ambition: e-commerce to "more than double to $180–200 billion by 2030"; about 60mn retail MSMEs are the pool [Press release 7 Sep 2026; AR p.45]. Redseer (July 2026): online share of retail 8% now, 14–15% by CY30 [AR p.61].
- The prospectus promised no profit date. It said most IPO growth money would go to Emerging [RHP Objects of the Offer].

## Capex and capacity
- Capex is small: ₹18.2cr of fixed assets bought in FY26 (Screener fixed assets of ₹1,029cr are mostly goodwill and lease assets); contracted capital commitments are only ₹1.86cr [AR p.56; note 33].
- IPO fresh issue ₹885.5cr; the Offer for Sale (₹732cr) went to selling holders; total ₹1,617cr. Planned use of the fresh money: marketing ₹205.8cr (FY27 ₹48.3cr, FY28 ₹75.6cr, FY29 ₹81.9cr); technology infrastructure ₹159.8cr (₹40.3cr, ₹60.1cr, ₹59.4cr); repay bank borrowings ₹210cr in FY27; balance to unidentified acquisitions and general purposes (together capped at 35% of gross proceeds, each 25%). About 68% of the ₹365.6cr growth spend is for Emerging. CARE Ratings monitors use of proceeds [RHP p.133-136].
- A consulting firm is engaged to improve cross-border and Martech profits and shares profits on milestones [RHP risk factors].

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Mar-26: cash ₹185cr; bank deposits longer than one year (₹443cr non-current financial assets) moved out of current assets. Borrowings of ₹242cr are all bank overdraft against fixed deposits (₹265cr under lien), so the company is effectively net cash. Operating cash flow ₹52.6cr in FY26 (–₹216cr in FY24); free cash flow ₹35cr [Screener; AR p.56; AR note 12].
- Receivables ₹236cr (+61% vs revenue +24%); debtor days 43 vs 33 [Screener; AR p.56]. COD money and payment-gateway balances sit here.
- My estimate: after the ₹885cr fresh money (less the company's share of expenses, which I did not find itemised) net cash is roughly ₹1,100–1,200cr, about 13% of the ₹8,700cr market cap. The ₹210cr repayment lowers cash and debt equally.
- No dividend.

## Moat evidence
- Data and reach: 730mn+ transactions since 2016, 15.5cr consumers served, 250+ partners, 93% of checkout addresses auto-filled, consumer repeat rate 59% [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].
- Routing across 42 couriers for best rate and delivery time. Management says this layer becomes more valuable as brands grow (its answer on whether big brands leave for couriers directly) [Q1 call].
- Weak points: Core pricing per shipment is falling (Core transactions +31%, Core revenue +22%, so revenue per Core transaction is down about 7%) as courier rates compress and large merchants grow. The power-merchant count is flat. Management accepts that couriers are also chasing D2C sellers [Q1 call].
- Peers named by Redseer: Unicommerce, Shopify, Global-e; couriers such as Delhivery are both suppliers and rivals [RHP industry chapter].

## Risks and red flags
- Emerging burn: –₹169cr adjusted EBITDA in FY26 (–31.4%) against Core +₹187cr [AR p.57].
- Supplier concentration: top 5 couriers carry 84.5% of shipments; top 3 vendors are 41.5% of total expenses; no exclusive contracts [RHP p.47].
- ESOP cost ₹112cr in FY26; key managers exercised options worth ₹50.4cr in FY26 (₹12.4cr in FY25) [AR note 34]. 3.8cr shares (5.2% of the post-issue count) remain in the employee trust [AGM notice].
- Goodwill ₹915cr, 37% of assets, unchanged; the FY24 loss included a large write-down. Management sees no impairment signal now (discount rate 16.4–18.25%, growth 5%) [AR goodwill note].
- Audit trail: the accounting software log did not cover deleted-transaction attributes, administrator changes or database-level changes (auditor S.R. Batliboi; opinion unqualified) [AR note 44]. Provident-fund liability under the 2019 Supreme Court ruling is not provided for [AR note 46]. Contingent liabilities are nil apart from a tiny tax item.
- A criminal complaint (Blue Line Logistics, Nov 2022) against the company, three directors and the CFO is pending; no summons received at the RHP date [RHP litigation].
- Co-founder Vishesh Khurana stopped being an employee on 31 May 2026 to join Tribe Capital [News: startuptalky, BW Disrupt, reported].
- Cross-border fell 2.3% in Q1 on global volatility; a Q3 dip is seasonal [Q1 call].

## My read
Shiprocket is a real, growing platform: revenue +34%, Core steadily profitable, adjusted EBITDA barely positive. But the profit is thin. Reported operating profit is still negative because of ESOP cost, and Emerging's rupee loss is not falling. At about ₹8,700cr (roughly 4x trailing sales, about 3.5x after my cash estimate) the market is paying for Emerging to turn profitable within a couple of years; Screener shows no P/E because there are no profits. The IPO cash gives a long runway. The stock (₹120) is 24% above the issue price and 8% below its first trade. I would want Emerging's rupee loss shrinking, power-merchant numbers growing and receivable days back under 40 before calling it a compounding business. Supply risk from a few couriers and heavy ESOP dilution are the counter-weights.
