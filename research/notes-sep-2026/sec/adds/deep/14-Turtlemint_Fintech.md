# Turtlemint Fintech Solutions — deep research (30 Sep 2026)

Ticker TURTLEMINT (NSE) / 544799 (BSE). Listed 29 Jun 2026 at an IPO price of ₹152 (opened ₹134.90). Screener price ₹76.4, market cap about ₹2,256cr, roughly 50% below the IPO price and at its all-time low after the IRDAI commission-cap proposal of 23 Sep 2026. [Screener; Chittorgarh; Inc42]

## Business and segments
- Turtlemint is an insurance broker that sells through about 691,000 registered "digital partners" (mostly part-time advisers, many of them PoSPs, the light-touch agent licence for simple policies). They sit in 19,000+ PIN codes, and about 76% of premium comes from towns beyond the top 30. Insurers pay the commission; the customer pays nothing extra. [IP Q1FY27 p.4-6; RHP]
- The regulated broking licence sits in the subsidiary Turtlemint Insurance Broking (TIB), which gave 96-97% of revenue. TIB was bought from co-founder Dhirendra Mahyavanshi on 8 May 2024, so FY25 is shown "pro forma" (as if owned all year). [RHP p.28; Q4 call]
- Revenue mix: general insurance (motor, health) was 93.3% of revenue in the nine months to Dec 2025 and life 4.8%; the RHP calls it "primarily motor". FY25 pro forma was 88.2% general and 9.3% life. The company does not split revenue between motor, health and life, and refused to when asked on the Q4 call. [RHP p.25; Q4 call]
- Turtlefin (white-label software for banks, NBFCs, fintechs) brings about 20% of premium but only a low fee. Technical and support services revenue was just ₹10.7cr of ₹1,098cr in FY26 (about 1%); mutual funds and loans add a further small slice (non-insurance 3% of revenue per the Q4 call). [Q4 call; AR FY26 note 40]
- Scale (Q1 FY27): 90,791 partners sold a policy in the last 90 days, 3.1 crore policies to date, 46 insurers, 83 branches, 2,300+ staff, 4.62% of premium from the top 100 partners. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.5; AR FY26 p.121]

## Key numbers
- FY24 to FY26 (Screener, consolidated): revenue ₹79cr, ₹663cr, ₹1,098cr. The jump is the TIB acquisition, not organic growth; pro forma revenue was ₹564cr, ₹700cr, ₹1,098cr. Operating profit -₹211cr, -₹188cr, -₹130cr. Net loss -₹193cr, -₹194cr, -₹184cr (FY26 includes a ₹55cr non-cash exceptional item). [Screener; IP Q1FY27 p.12]
- Screener's FY24 revenue collapse (₹420cr in FY23 to ₹79cr) is the last regulatory shock: after April 2023 rules, insurers moved from "marketing fees" to commissions, and revenue moved into TIB. [Screener; RHP p.27]
- Quarters (Screener, ₹cr): Q1 FY26 revenue 210, operating profit -46; Q3 FY26 278, -39; Q4 FY26 357, -4 (net profit +3 only because of an ₹8cr tax credit; pre-tax -5); Q1 FY27 294, -33, net loss -38. Q2 FY26 is missing on Screener; by subtraction it was about ₹253cr. [Screener; Q1 results filing]
- Company measures: Q1 FY27 platform premium ₹1,205cr (+49.8%), revenue ₹294cr (+40%), service EBITDA ₹39cr (+89%, 13.3% margin), corporate overheads ₹66cr (+5%, 22% of revenue vs 30%), adjusted EBITDA -₹26cr (-9% vs -20%), ESOP cost ₹7cr. Q4 FY26: service EBITDA ₹60cr (16.8%), overheads 16% of revenue, adjusted EBITDA about zero (first ever). [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.11; Q4 call]
- Unit economics (my arithmetic from company premium and revenue): revenue divided by platform premium was 23.8% in FY25 pro forma, 28.4% in FY26, 26.1% in Q1 FY26, 28.9% in Q4 FY26 and 24.4% in Q1 FY27. Q1 revenue fell 18% on Q4 while premium fell only 2.5%, yet management said take rates were "more or less similar". Enterprise premium (about 20% of the total) earns a small fee, so the take rate on PoSP-sold policies is much higher than the blended figure. [IP Q1FY27; RHP KPI table; Q1 call]
- Payout: costs of recruiting, paying and managing partners were 77.5% of total expenses in the nine months to Dec 2025, about 92% of revenue. Service EBITDA is what is left after those costs; renewal service EBITDA is 2.5-3 times the new-business margin because there is almost no selling cost. [RHP p.545; Q1 call]
- Renewals: ₹225cr in FY26 (+51%, 20% of revenue), ₹65cr in Q1 FY27 (+66%, 22% of revenue vs 19%). About half of renewal premium is health and life. [Q4 call; Q1 call; AR p.120]
- Productivity: revenue per active partner rose about 9% in Q1 (revenue +40% on active partners +28.6% using the deck's 70,587 base; the call said +30-33%, and its own base of 72,244 gives +25.7%). Frontline staff productivity is up 20%+ a year. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27 p.12]

## Guidance history
- Q4 call (17 Jul 2026), first call after listing: board chose not to give formal guidance, but the CEO said (a) FY27 losses down by more than 40%, (b) full year profitable on a consolidated basis, (c) every quarter bigger than the last, Q4 the largest, (d) in five years adjusted EBITDA of 18-20% of revenue, service EBITDA 24-25%, corporate overheads "sub 5-6%", (e) growth of 40%+ "historically". [Q4 call]
- Q1 call (14 Aug 2026), the latest: CFO restated 40% growth and said the team "will try really hard" for full-year adjusted EBITDA breakeven, with profit "in the second half". CEO gave the service EBITDA path as about 16%, then 18%, then 23-24%, and said overheads should fall below 7-8% of revenue in four to five years. [Q1 call]
- Delivery in Q1: revenue +40% (on guide); adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed 37% (the CEO had said "more than 40%" for the year); overheads 22% vs 30%. Q2 is guided to beat Q1. [Q1 call]
- Softening to note: "entire year profitable" in July became "try really hard" in August, and the five-year overhead target moved from sub 5-6% to 7-8%, which points to about 16-17% end-state margin rather than 18-20%. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- My check: FY26 overheads were ₹247cr and Q1 ran at ₹66cr (+5%). At +40% revenue (about ₹1,540cr) and overheads of about ₹265cr, breakeven needs a service margin near 17%. Trend (FY26 12.9%, Q1 up 3.3 points on last year) points nearer 16%, so a small full-year loss of ₹20-30cr looks more likely than breakeven. This is my estimate, not a company figure. [Screener; Q1 call]
- Dividend: only after "one or two years" of sustained profit. ESOP cost (₹25cr in FY26) to fall sharply by FY28; no exceptional items expected in FY27. [Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- Asset-light. The CFO said there are few fixed assets beyond office IT and no cost is capitalised; contracts on capital account are nil; Screener fixed assets are ₹34cr. Scale is people and software: 2,300+ staff and partners. [Q4 call; AR FY26 note 49; Screener]
- IPO money is not for plants. Of ₹661cr fresh issue: tech and product salaries ₹193cr (FY27-29: ₹56cr, ₹64cr, ₹74cr), marketing ₹39cr, cloud servers ₹26cr, office leases ₹43cr, working capital for TIB ₹129cr, unidentified acquisitions about ₹151cr, and general purposes for the rest. So about two-thirds pays running costs and the broker's cash needs. [RHP objects; Chittorgarh]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- FY26 operating cash flow was -₹182cr (Screener; matches the annual report cash flow), against a pre-exceptional loss of about ₹129cr. Management claimed "99%" cash conversion; I could not reconcile that to the cash flow. [Screener; AR FY26 p.216; Q4 call]
- March 2026: cash and bank ₹125cr (₹68cr cash plus ₹57cr other bank balances), down from ₹183cr; receivables ₹159cr (53 days; 88 in FY25); borrowings mostly leases (₹18cr). Equity ₹305cr. [AR FY26 p.214; Screener]
- After March: ₹50cr of 13.75% secured debentures drawn in April (Trifecta venture debt; a further quarterly 0.60% coupon takes the cost above 16%; eight months without principal repayment; repayable within 36 months; first charge on group assets). Not part of the IPO objects, so it stays despite the IPO cash. [AR FY26 note 52; Q1 results note 9]
- After the IPO: June cash is not disclosed. My sum of March cash, ₹661cr fresh issue and the debentures is about ₹830cr before offer costs and Q1 outflow, roughly a third of the current market cap. [my arithmetic]
- Working-capital claims: insurers bill every 15 days or monthly; partners are mostly paid after collection. [Q4 call]

## Moat evidence
- Management says Turtlemint holds the highest share of PoSPs and that the PoSP market is "two three player". The RHP (Redseer) says PoSP-led premium grew 23-28% a year in FY20-25 and should reach 25-29% of motor and 9-10% of retail health by FY30, while the industry grows 10-12%. [Q1 call; RHP]
- Granularity: top 100 partners are 4.6% of premium; top 3 insurers 22.6% (FY26), so no partner or insurer can dictate terms. 17 insurers each gave over ₹100cr of premium in FY26 (12 in FY25). [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- Partner earnings rise with tenure (the FY20 cohort earns 3.8 times its first-year income); attrition is 28-30% in the first 24 months, then 2-3% a year, about 65% left after five or six years. [AR FY26 p.119; Q1 call]
- But the moat does not include pricing power: commission rates are set by insurers, who have cut them before (a general insurer took a motor rate from 32.5% to 30% in 2024). And partners can multi-home. [RHP p.27]

## Risks and red flags
- Regulation is the main risk. On 23 Sep 2026 IRDAI proposed caps on distributor pay: reports say health first-year 15% for distribution entities (20% for individual agents), motor third-party nil and own-damage 5% for new vehicles, term life 25%, and all rewards, marketing and technology payments counted inside the cap; PoSP pay would come out of the entity's capped commission; insurer expense limits tighten to 25% then 20% (general) over 2 and 5 years. It is a proposal only: comments close 25 Oct 2026, reported start FY2027-28. Turtlemint fell 20% on 24 Sep and 20% on 25 Sep. Jefferies estimates a 10% cut in new-business commission rates lowers earnings by 10-12%. Reports differ on old-vehicle motor caps, which matter most for Turtlemint's rollover business; I have not read the paper itself. [Coverfox; Medianama; Mondaq; Finnovate; Inc42]
- Management's defence (July and August, before the paper): after the September 2025 GST change on health and life, commission cuts were passed to partners and volume grew, so it expects to absorb caps too. That shifts pain to partner income, and partners can switch. No management comment on the September paper was found. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- GST case: DGGI alleged invoices raised on insurers "without actual supply of services" for FY2017-23; penalties of ₹51.2cr confirmed, under appeal, disclosed but not provided for (about 17% of March equity). Income-tax disallowances of ₹6.2cr are also contested. The RHP lists these tax matters at about ₹57cr in total. [AR FY26 note 48; RHP p.52]
- Motor and general insurance dominate revenue (88-93%); Maharashtra and Gujarat give 31-33% of premium. [RHP p.25, p.62]
- Promoters and VCs sold on the IPO: promoters cut from 43.2m to 38.9m shares (about 10% of their holding, roughly ₹66cr), and every major VC also sold. Promoters now hold 13.2%. [RHP; Shareholding 30 Jun 2026]
- Lock-ins: 83.4% of shares were locked at 30 Jun 2026. Anchor lock-ins (₹397cr) ended by about 22 Sep; the six-month lock on other pre-IPO holders ends about 24 Dec 2026; promoters' minimum contribution (topped up by Nexus and Peak XV shares) is locked 18 months, to about Dec 2027. Nexus (19.8% with its second entity) and Peak XV (16.5%) are the overhang. [RHP p.144; Shareholding 30 Jun 2026]
- Dilution: 6.9m ESOP options (2.3% of the fully diluted count), all preference shares converted in May 2026, and an unidentified-acquisition object that could be paid partly in shares. [Shareholding 30 Jun 2026; RHP]
- Accounting: the auditor's opinion is unqualified; the only key audit matter is IT controls, and database-level audit trail began only on 7 Sep 2024. Adjusted EBITDA excludes ESOP and other income. FY26 has a ₹52.5cr non-cash charge from a change in the preference-share conversion ratio. Six junior staff traded in a closed window (warnings only). [AR FY26 p.204-206; Q1 results note 6; Scanx]
- No credit-rating rationale exists for the company; I found none.

- New disclosures in August: Q1 premium mix (health +45%, term +60%), renewal share 22%, AI in renewals (500 basis points better renewal rate), claims support (₹26cr settled in Q1 vs ₹85cr in Q4), partner attrition curve. [Q1 call]

## My read
Volume and operating leverage are real: revenue up 40%, premium up 50%, overheads flat in rupees, service margin rising with renewals. The business is still loss-making after ESOP (Screener operating loss ₹33cr in Q1) and burned ₹182cr of cash last year, but the IPO gives it a cushion of several years at the current burn. The issue is price setting, not execution: 100% of revenue depends on commissions that insurers and now the regulator set, and the September proposal targets exactly the health, term and motor lines Turtlemint grows in. At ₹76 the market has already halved the IPO value, so the stock is a bet that final rules are far milder than the draft or that partners absorb the cut. I would change my view on the final IRDAI rules (after comments close 25 Oct), the Q2 take rate, and whether the full-year breakeven is kept.
