# Optical fibre and telecom networks: sector research (for the Cables & Wires note, group 4)

Researched 20 Oct 2026. "Call via Arihant" means a results-call figure taken from an Arihant Capital broker note; I did not read the call itself.

## 1. How the business works

- **Four steps, each with a different profit.** (1) A glass **preform** is made: a thick rod of ultra-pure silica, built up from silicon tetrachloride, with germanium added to the core. (2) The preform is heated and drawn into hair-thin **fibre**, sold by the fibre-kilometre (fkm). (3) Fibres are bundled into **cable**. A 1 km cable with 288 fibres counts as 288 fkm. (4) **Connectivity** covers connectors, pre-terminated assemblies and cassettes used inside data centres. Below these sit network-building and maintenance contracts such as BharatNet.
- **Preform is the costly, hard step.** HFCL says preform is **65–70% of the cost of fibre**. Its own ₹580 cr preform plant is meant to cut raw-material cost by **10–12%** [HFCL Q1 FY27 call via Arihant, 24 Jul 2026]. The CARE rating note says the plant will make **310 tonnes a year** [CARE, 9 Jul 2026]. Adding preform capacity takes **18–24 months**, and a new entrant needs **20+ months** to reach mass production [News: 36Kr, 14 Sep 2026]. CRU names preform as the main bottleneck today [CRU, 26 Feb 2026].
- **Who makes which step in India.** **STL is fully integrated:** it makes preform from silica, draws its own fibre and makes cable, which few companies worldwide do [ICRA on STL, 6 Jul 2026]. **HFCL is only partly integrated.** Its fibre capacity doubled to 28 mn fkm in FY26, enough for all its own cable, but it buys preform until its plant is ready [CARE, 9 Jul 2026].
- **Inputs are tight.** Germanium cost **$10,000–12,750/kg in Jul-26**, against **$1,289–1,400/kg in Jun-23**, before China's export controls [MMTA/Fastmarkets, 21 Aug 2026]. STL is adding germanium and helium suppliers and can pass on part of the cost [STL Q1 FY27 call via Arihant, 27 Jul 2026].

## 2. Global market: size, China, the price crash and the 2026 shortage

- **Size (CRU, Nov 2025, as shown in STL's deck).** Global optical cable demand was **537 mn fkm (2023)**, **528 (2024)** and **549 (2025)**, up about 4.0% in 2025 against an earlier forecast of 1.7%. CRU forecasts **577 (2026), 607 (2027), 640 (2028), 670 (2029) and 700 mn fkm (2030)**. North America and Europe grow from **191 mn fkm (2025) to 319 (2030)**, about 10.7% a year. North America alone grows about **13.7%** a year [STL Q3 FY26 deck citing CRU, Jan 2026].
- **Higher forecasts since then.** One research institute (Jul 2026) expects fibre demand **above 670 mn fkm in 2026** (+10%), with data-centre fibre **+69%** [YOFC interim, 21 Aug 2026]. A Chinese industry estimate says **760 mn fkm (2026)** and **889 mn (2027)**, including drone fibre, with supply growing only **11%** a year. That leaves a shortfall of **35 mn fkm (2026)** and **82 mn (2027)** [News: 36Kr, 14 Sep 2026]. The bases differ, so treat 550–760 mn fkm as the range.
- **China** holds about **60%** of global capacity [36Kr]. In 2025 its low prices won tenders in the Middle East, Latin America, Europe and the US [CRU, 16 Dec 2025].
- **The 2023–25 crash.** CARE says oversupply and weak prices lasted through most of 2024 and 2025 [CARE on HFCL, 9 Jul 2026]. US cable makers held too much stock, so orders were deferred. STL's operating margin fell to **10.4% in FY25**, from about **20.5%** in FY17–FY20 [ICRA, 6 Jul 2026]. After the 2018 peak, China's spot price fell from **RMB 78.8 to 30/fkm** [36Kr].
- **The 2025–26 surge.** Chinese G.652.D bare fibre (the standard grade) rose **80%+ between Nov-25 and Jan-26**. China's prices passed Europe's and India's for the first time in over seven years [CRU, 26 Feb 2026]. China's spot price went from **RMB 15.5/fkm (May-25) to 81.9 (May-26)** [PhotonCap, 17 Jul 2026] and about **100 by Jul-26**. Data-centre fibre (G.657.A2) went from **RMB 32 to 240**. The industry expects **RMB 180–200**, and Fujikura raised data-centre interconnect cable prices **30%** [36Kr]. US fibre prices were up **as much as 50%** in H1 2026 [CoBank, 8 Jul 2026].
- **Export prices.** HFCL says cable sells for **$18–28/fkm**, depending on type and contract. Spot prices are **5–15%** above contract prices, and contracts reset yearly [HFCL Q1 FY27 call via Arihant].
- **Leaders' results.** Corning's optical sales rose **32% to $2.07 bn in Q2 2026**, with data-centre sales **+65%** and segment net income **+77% to $438 mn** [Corning 8-K, 28 Jul 2026]. YOFC's H1 2026 profit rose **889% to RMB 2,924.5 mn** on revenue **+53.6%** [YOFC interim]. Fujikura's operating profit to Mar-26 rose **39.2% to ¥188.7 bn** [FilingReader, 14 May 2026].
- **Hollow-core fibre** (lower latency) saw more development work in 2025 than in any earlier year. Microsoft partners with Corning and Heraeus, and Prysmian with Relativity, but deployment is still limited [CRU, 16 Dec 2025]. STL and HFCL both have it in development [calls via Arihant].

## 3. Demand drivers for FY27–FY29

- **AI data centres are the main driver.** An Nvidia Blackwell rack uses **576 fibres** (72 GPUs × 8), **18x a legacy CPU rack** [Corning via Light Reading, 8 Jul 2024]. Hyperscalers are expected to spend **>$700 bn** in 2026, and AI could be **~30% of fibre demand by 2027** [CoBank, 8 Jul 2026]. CRU expects data-centre fibre to grow **20.74% a year in 2025–30** and pass **half of all demand by 2030** [YOFC interim citing CRU]. Corning has deals with Meta (up to **$6 bn**), Amazon and Nvidia. It will add **50%+** to its US fibre capacity [Corning 8-K; CoBank].
- **Indian makers are winning this business.** STL: data centres were **21%** of Q1 FY27 revenue, and the FY27 goal was raised to **50%** (from 30%). It has a **$1.1 bn multi-year hyperscaler contract** for FY27–FY29 [STL call via Arihant], with deliveries likely from **Q3 FY27** [ICRA]. HFCL: data-centre connectivity revenue is guided at **₹700–800 cr in FY27** [HFCL call via Arihant]. It also has a **₹10,159 cr multi-year overseas cable supply deal**, expected to use over half its cable capacity [CARE, 9 Jul 2026].
- **Telecom FTTH is slow.** CRU expects telecom fibre demand to grow only **2.47% a year in 2025–30** [YOFC interim citing CRU]. Europe (EU39) fibre passes **295 mn homes (79%)**, rising **23 mn** a year but slowing, with **353 mn** forecast by 2031 [FTTH Council Europe via optics.org, 17 Mar 2026].
- **US BEAD ($42.45 bn).** All **56** state plans were approved by **25 Aug 2026** [Light Reading]. Fibre won **63%** of planned locations and satellite **23%** [Broadband Breakfast, 18 Mar 2026]. CRU warns building may slip into **2027** [CRU, 16 Dec 2025], and builders must compete for scarce fibre that meets Buy America rules [CoBank].
- **India BharatNet (Phase III).** Outlay is about **₹1.39 lakh cr** [ThePrint, 10 Aug 2026]. It has **16 packages**, and private firms build the middle-mile network and run it for **10 years** [tele.net, 8 Aug 2024]. The last four packages went to TCIL and Pratap Technocrats as lowest bidders, and BSNL aims to finish by **end-2028** [tele.net, 8 Jan 2026]. Known awards:
  - **HFCL, Punjab:** **₹2,501.30 cr** (Jan-25), of which **₹1,244.61 cr** is build cost [search summaries; filing not opened].
  - **HFCL, Uttar Pradesh (via RVNL):** **₹2,167.65 cr** (Jan-25) plus **₹2,666.09 cr** (Jun-26) [Wire & Cable India, 19 Jun 2026].
  - **STL, J&K and Ladakh:** **₹2,631 cr** with Dilip Buildcon [Indian Infrastructure, 31 Mar 2025].
- **BharatNet's record is poor.** By Feb-26, only **78,899 of ~2.64 lakh** village councils had working connection points, after **₹40,635 cr** spent. There are **9.5 lakh** active home connections, against a goal of **1.5 crore** [ThePrint]. HFCL takes **174 days** on average to collect its debtors, and about **57%** of them are strong payers such as BSNL [CARE].
- **Railways and defence.** By Jul-26, Kavach (the railways' anti-collision system) had **11,547 km** of fibre laid and **21,794 route-km** under work [Indian Infrastructure, 7 Aug 2026]. HFCL targets **₹500 cr** of defence revenue in FY27 and **₹3,000 cr by FY29** [HFCL call via Arihant].
- **India's own demand is small:** **4.7 mn fkm in Q1 2026**, against **136.7 mn** globally [Birla Cable directors' report FY26]. Indian makers mainly export.

## 4. Policy and trade

- **India's anti-dumping duty** covers standard single-mode fibre from **China, South Korea and Indonesia**. It was imposed on **3 Aug 2023** for **five years**, so it runs to about Aug 2028 (my working) [Taxscan; Notification 07/2023-Customs (ADD)]. The duty is **$122–857.23 per 1,000 fkm**. The petitioners were Birla Furukawa, STL and Corning India, who make **90%** of Indian single-mode fibre [Wire & Cable India, 5 Aug 2023].
- **The EU charges both anti-dumping and anti-subsidy duties on Indian cable** [EU Reg 2024/3014; Reg 2025/1135, 10 Jun 2025; corrected by Reg 2026/698, 23 Mar 2026]:

  | Company | Anti-dumping | Anti-subsidy |
  |---|---|---|
  | STL | 8.8% | 3.7% |
  | HFCL | none listed | 8.1% |
  | Birla Cable / Universal Cables / Vindhya | 2.9% | 5.4% |
  | All others | 4.5% | 8.1% |

- **China** extended its anti-dumping duty on Indian single-mode fibre (**7.4–30.6%**) for five more years from **14 Aug 2026** [Xinhua via CTILS, 13 Aug 2026].
- **US: no anti-dumping or anti-subsidy case on Indian fibre or cable was found.** I found no Commerce Department or ITC case. The trade cases against Indian cable are in the EU and China.
- **US tariffs.** A **50%** tariff on Indian goods began on **27 Aug 2025** [Communications Today, 28 Aug 2025]. STL says the tariff reset cut its reported EBITDA margin by **~760 bps in Q3 FY26**, to 10.3% [STL Q3 FY26 deck]. In Feb 2026 the US announced a cut from **25% to 18%** and dropped the extra 25% [Communications Today, 3 Feb 2026]. A temporary **10%** levy followed. Since **24 Jul 2026**, Indian goods pay normal duty plus a **10% Section 301 duty**, which has no end date [The Week, 24 Jul 2026]. HFCL says Chinese fibre faces about **35%** duty in the US, which limits Chinese competition there [HFCL call via Arihant].
- **India's telecom rules.** Since 2021, operators may use only trusted products from trusted sources, vetted by the National Security Council Secretariat. The goal is for all network devices to be trusted by about **2027** [Communications Today, 28 Oct 2023, older than 12 months]. Telecom production-linked incentive (PLI) scheme: outlay **₹12,195 cr**, cumulative sales of about **₹1.1 lakh cr**, exports over **₹23,500 cr**. Only **₹1,597.93 cr** had been paid out by 31 Mar 2026 [Communications Today, 11 Aug 2026].

## 5. Competitive structure

- **Global leaders:** Corning, Prysmian, YOFC, Fujikura, Sumitomo, Furukawa, Amphenol (bought CommScope's cable business for **$10.5 bn** in 2025 [CRU, 16 Dec 2025]), and China's Hengtong, FiberHome and ZTT.
- **STL** is India's largest fibre and cable maker [ICRA]. Its share of the world cable market outside China is **8%** (FY25 and 9M FY26) [STL Q3 FY26 deck] and **9%** in Q1 FY27 [STL call via Arihant]. I found no current capacity figure in fkm from STL itself. It runs at about **70%** utilisation. A **₹3,000 cr** capex plan should add **~50%** capacity by end-FY29 [Wire & Cable India, 3 Sep 2026]. It is also building a **$100 mn** connectivity plant in the US [STL call via Arihant].
- **HFCL's capacity is growing fast:**
  - Fibre: **28 mn fkm (FY26)**, about **33.9 mn by Dec-26** [CARE], and **43.1 mn** when all projects finish.
  - Cable: about **43 mn fkm by Dec-26** [CARE], and **62 mn** when all projects finish.
  - Preform: from **~300 to 600 tonnes**.
  - Total capex is about **₹1,800 cr**. Fibre and cable lines finish by **Jul-28** and preform by **Oct-28** [Multibagg on HFCL board approval, 14 Sep 2026].
- **Birla Furukawa** (a Universal Cables–Furukawa JV) proposes about **3x** fibre capacity plus a preform plant. The cost is about **₹4,800 cr**, with a target of **Dec 2028** [JM Financial, 10 Aug 2026], and terms are still to be agreed [Developing Telecoms, 5 Aug 2026]. Birla Cable and Vindhya Telelinks, from the same group, make cable.
- **Others:** Finolex's preform plant (**100 tonnes**, due Mar-26) adds **2 mn fkm** [Wire & Cable India, 10 Feb 2026]. Corning India makes fibre locally. I found no recent capacity data for Aksh or Paramount, and no Indian market-share data.

## 6. Where the profit sits, cycle position and risks

- **Profit is highest in preform and fibre.** YOFC's preform, fibre and cable segment had a **63.11%** gross margin in H1 2026, against **48.97%** for components and modules [YOFC interim]. STL is integrated: gross margin **49.6%**, EBITDA margin **20.2%** in Q1 FY27, with **23%** guided for FY27. HFCL is not yet integrated: gross margin **38.8%**, EBITDA margin **21.6%** [Arihant notes, Jul 2026].
- **Connectivity adds margin.** STL's connectivity revenue was **16%** of its cable revenue in Q1 FY27, with a target of **25% by Q4 FY27** [STL call via Arihant].
- **EPC and turnkey work is the weakest layer.** HFCL's turnkey segment made a loss of about **₹88 cr at EBIT** in Q1 FY27 [Arihant, 24 Jul 2026]. CARE puts HFCL's working-capital cycle at **191 days** [CARE].
- **Cycle position: near the peak of an upswing.** Prices are at multi-year highs. CRU says the open question is how long the tightness lasts [CRU, 26 Feb 2026]. CoBank thinks supply constraints could last well into 2027 or beyond [CoBank, 8 Jul 2026]. Preform takes 18–24 months to add, so new supply should arrive in **2028**. HFCL (Jul–Oct 2028), Birla Furukawa (Dec 2028), STL (end-FY29) and Furukawa (H2 FY2028) all add capacity in that window.
- **Main risks:**
  - **Chinese supply returning.** China holds 60% of capacity. When its home prices cool, its exports rise. The 2018–19 drop was **~60%**.
  - **Few big customers.** STL's single **$1.1 bn** contract and HFCL's single **₹10,159 cr** deal are both large against their order books.
  - **Trade rules.** EU duties apply, and US tariffs have changed four times in a year.
  - **Germanium supply** depends on China.
  - **BharatNet** execution and payments, given the record above.
  - **BEAD** building delays.

## 7. India's telecom equipment market

- **Size:** **$25 bn in FY25**, rising to **$50 bn by FY32**, with about **60%** of imports already replaced locally [KPMG via Telangana Today, 9 Oct 2026].
- **Buyers:** Jio, Airtel, Vi and BSNL, plus BharatNet, railways and defence. BSNL has proposed **₹77,000 cr** over five years for **2 lakh** 4G/5G sites, still awaiting Cabinet approval [Sahi, 11 Aug 2026].
- **Indian makers:** The TCS-Tejas-C-DOT group builds BSNL's network. Tejas got a **₹1,537 cr** order letter for **18,685 sites** in Aug-26 [Angel One, 28 Aug 2026], but lost **₹202 cr in Q1 FY27** [Sahi].
- **HFCL's equipment business:** radios, routers and Wi-Fi, at **₹500–600 cr a year** and **12–15%** EBITDA margin [HFCL call via Arihant], less profitable than cable. Jio's share of HFCL revenue fell from **~63% (FY18) to 13–14% (FY26)** [CARE].
