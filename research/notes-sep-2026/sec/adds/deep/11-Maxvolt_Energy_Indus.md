# Maxvolt Energy Industries (NSE Emerge: MAXVOLT) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Sources read: H2 FY26 call (13 May 2026, official NSE transcript) and H1 FY26 call (14 Nov 2025, official NSE transcript), H2 FY26 investor presentation (12 May 2026), FY26 annual report and AGM notice (NSE, filed 26 Sep 2026; MD&A, auditor's report and CARO annexure, AOC-2, notes on inventory, receivables, borrowings, related parties, contingent liabilities, segment, deferred tax), CRISIL rating rationale (12 Feb 2026), NSE press releases of 23 Sep 2026 (Aligarh Bhoomi Pujan) and 8 Sep 2026 (loan to subsidiary), Screener standalone and consolidated pages (raw HTML). Maxvolt reports half-yearly; there is no Q1 FY27 result. H1 FY27 results are due after 1 Oct 2026 (trading window notice).

## Business and segments
- Assembles lithium-ion battery packs (buys imported cells, adds its own battery-management electronics, casing and testing) under the "MaxVolt Energy" brand, plus chargers and inverters. It does not make cells. [IP H2FY26; Press release 23 Sep 2026]
- FY26 revenue by product (company, ₹cr): e-scooter packs 216.9 (73%), e-rickshaw packs 22.0 (7%), energy storage (ESS) 21.4 (7%), chargers 20.2 (7%), others 16.2 (5%), e-cycles 0.15. FY25: e-scooter 87.6, ESS 3.4, e-rickshaw 0.4. [IP H2FY26 slides 34–35; AR FY26 MD&A]
- Channel: mostly retail through dealers. In H1 FY26 OEMs were about 35–40% of the e-scooter line, the rest retail. 875+ dealers/distributors (620 a year earlier), 31+ OEM clients, 16 states, 12 warehouses, 1,300+ pin codes. [H1 call; Q4 call]
- Capacity: 6,000–6,500 packs a month to Dec 2025; 14,000 a month from Jan–Feb 2026 after a new 55,000 sq ft Ghaziabad (Duhai) plant; stated as 600 MWh (presentation: 600–750 MWh). FY26 utilisation about 75% (under half in H1, full in H2); "more than 100%" now. [Q4 call; IP H2FY26]
- Segment note: the company reports a single segment ("single business and geographical segment"). [AR FY26 note 27]
- Subsidiary Maxvolt ReEarth Pvt Ltd (75% owned, incorporated 13 Jan 2026) for battery recycling; ₹37.5 lakh invested. The presentation calls it "wholly-owned"; the annual report says 75%. [AR FY26 AOC-1, note 3; IP H2FY26 slide 27]

## Key numbers (Screener standalone, company release for exact figures)
- Revenue: FY22 ₹6cr, FY23 ₹14cr, FY24 ₹48cr, FY25 ₹107cr, FY26 ₹297cr (+176%). Half-years: H2 FY24 ₹28cr, H1 FY25 ₹40cr, H2 FY25 ₹67cr, H1 FY26 ₹130cr (+223%), H2 FY26 ₹167cr (+148%). [Screener; IP H2FY26 slide 37]
- Operating margin: FY24 14%, FY25 13%, FY26 12.0%; half-years 21% (H2 FY24), 13%, 13%, 14.2% (H1 FY26), 10.3% (H2 FY26). Gross margin fell from 19.6% (H2 FY25) and 19.5% (H1 FY26) to 17.9% in H2 FY26. [Screener; IP H2FY26 slide 37]
- Profit after tax: FY25 ₹10.1cr, FY26 ₹24.4cr (standalone). H2 FY26 PAT ₹11.5cr was 11% below H1's ₹12.9cr as finance cost rose from ₹0.8cr to ₹2.6cr. [IP H2FY26 slide 37]
- Return on capital (Screener): 19% FY23, 66% FY24, 32% FY25, 27% FY26.
- Unit economics: retail e-2W/3W packs earn 20–23% gross margin, OEM 15–18%; ESS retail 25–26%, ESS projects and OEM 18–20%. Revenue per MWh ₹1.4–1.5cr to retail, ₹1.15–1.2cr to OEMs. Pack prices went from ₹13–14 per watt-hour at launch to about ₹9, and are "going slightly upside" now. [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- H1 call (Nov 2025): the FY26 target had been ₹170–180cr; FY26 delivered ₹297cr, a large beat. Margin guided at 13–14%; H2 came in at 10.3%, a miss. [H1 call; IP H2FY26]
- H1 call: 12,500 packs a month by January 2026 (delivered 14,000); phase 2 to 25,000 by August 2026 at ₹20–25cr of machines. By May this had become a 35,000-pack plant (14,000 + 21,000), about 2.2–2.5 GWh, operational "October–November", production December 2026–January 2027, total ₹74–76cr, revenue potential ₹1,000–1,100cr. [H1 call; Q4 call]
- Recycling plant at Aligarh: in Nov 2025 construction was to begin by March 2026 with operations in Q4 FY27; in May 2026 it slipped to August 2026 because map approvals were pending; FY27 recycling revenue not committed. [H1 call; Q4 call; IP H2FY26 slide 41]
- FY27 revenue: May call "at least 50% to 70%" growth with "sustainable" margins; the FY26 annual report (Board report dated 3 Sep 2026) says 40–50%. The guide was cut in the newer document. [Q4 call; AR FY26 p.15]
- 23 Sep 2026: new ₹700cr, 10 GWh pack campus at Aligarh (two 5 GWh phases: 24–30 months, then 18 more months), claimed turnover potential "over ₹6,800cr" at full use; funded by "internal accruals, debt and planned capital raises". The release says 2.5 GWh is "operational today" at Duhai, which the May call described as 600 MWh installed with the 1.5 GWh automatic line due December–January. The release does not mention recycling, and the site (about 23,576 sq m, Khyamai industrial estate) matches the plot the annual report describes as allotted for the recycling plant (23,524 sq m). [Press release 23 Sep 2026; AR FY26 p.25; Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ₹16cr in cash-flow terms (Screener); CWIP ₹13cr at March for the Duhai plant; SIDBI term loan of ₹7.5cr for it. [Screener; AR CARO ix(c)]
- Committed: Duhai phase 1+2 about ₹75cr (₹28–29cr infrastructure; ₹31cr for the 1.5 GWh automatic line). Recycling phase 1 ₹74–75cr of a ₹282cr three-phase plan, with 25–30% state capital subsidy and hoped-for central grants of 20–25%. [Q4 call]
- New: ₹700cr for Aligarh 10 GWh. Against FY26 equity of ₹108cr and operating profit of ₹36cr, total announced capex (about ₹850cr) is roughly 8 times net worth. Shareholders are asked on 30 Sep 2026 to lift the borrowing limit from ₹200cr to ₹500cr and the loans/investments limit to ₹500cr. [Press release; AR AGM notice items 5–7]
- Asset turns: management implies ₹75cr of pack capex for ₹1,000–1,100cr of revenue (about 14 times); the ₹700cr Aligarh plan implies roughly 10 times at full use. Pack assembly is light on plant; the cash goes into stock and receivables. [Q4 call; Press release]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Operating cash negative every year: FY22 −₹2cr, FY23 −₹0cr, FY24 −₹8cr, FY25 −₹45cr, FY26 −₹29cr; five-year total −₹84cr against ₹58cr of operating profit. Free cash FY26 −₹45cr (Screener cash-flow basis). [Screener]
- The H1 FY26 operating cash was positive (investor on H1 call); H2 reversed it. [H1 call]
- Inventory tripled: ₹20cr (Mar 2025) to ₹62cr (Mar 2026), of which raw material ₹51cr; advances to suppliers ₹27cr (₹8cr). Receivables ₹39cr (₹31cr), 98% under six months. Management now holds 4–5 months of imported stock (3 months before). [AR FY26 notes 12, 13, 15; Q4 call]
- Borrowings ₹85cr (₹10cr a year earlier), cash ₹26cr, deposits and funds ₹22cr (a ₹3cr deposit pledged to SIDBI). Net debt ₹59cr excluding deposits, about ₹37cr including them. Debt/equity 0.79 (0.04 in FY25). Working-capital borrowing at 8.15–8.9%. [Screener; AR note 11, ratio note 35, CARO; Q4 call]
- No dividend. Warrants: 9,61,822 at ₹440 to Mukesh Gupta (CMO, co-founder, promoter group; 5,61,822) and Suresh Jha (4,00,000) on 27 Feb 2026; 25% (₹10.6cr) paid, balance ₹31.7cr due within 18 months; full conversion adds about 8.8% to the share count. [AR FY26 notes 2; Board report]
- Promoters 39.14%, unchanged since March 2025; FIIs 0.44%, DIIs 0.95%. [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Service network: dealer diagnosis through smart BMS, repairs in 6–7 days, swap for cell faults, buyback of old packs for second-life use; three-year warranty against one year for lead-acid. [H1 call; IP H2FY26 slide 31]
- Approvals: AIS 156 safety certification; L5 three-wheeler approval from ICAT (May 2026). These are entry tickets, not barriers. [Q4 call; IP]
- Competitors named by management: Eastman, Prontac, iPower, Tata and others; imports from China (CRISIL). No market-share figure; "targeting 5% share" by FY32. [H1 call; CRISIL Feb 2026]
- Pricing: H2 FY26 gross margin fell 1.7 points as the rupee weakened and cells got dearer; management chose supply security over price. Weak pricing power in a crowded pack-assembly market. [Q4 call; IP]

## Risks and red flags
- **Related-party supplier:** the AOC-2 lists purchases of ₹72.44cr in FY26 from M/s Volts AMP, described as a "relative of promoter/director" — about 30% of FY26 material cost (₹241.5cr) and 24% of revenue. Also sales of ₹5.89cr to and purchases of ₹1.11cr from M/s Ultra Energy (relative), and a ₹23.9 lakh advance to Mukesh Gupta. All stated to be at arm's length. [AR FY26 AOC-2 and note 28/29]
- **Disclosure quality:** the related-party note is garbled (names and categories misaligned); the MD&A contains draft wording left in ("The final Annual Report should include management's specific assessment of liquidity…", "The Company should disclose its approved foreign-exchange risk-management/hedging policy…"); the recycling capacity is given as 5,200, 7,600 and 7,800 tonnes in different places; dealer counts of 875, 945 and 950 and 16, 17 or 22 states appear in documents months apart; OEM partners "31+" in May became "over 130" in September; the chairman's letter states FY25 PAT as "1,011.68 million" (it was ₹1,011.68 lakh); note 32 says "no events after the reporting period". [AR FY26 pp.13–15, 25, 74–75; Press release]
- **Standalone versus consolidated profit:** both show profit before tax of ₹32.8cr, but consolidated profit after tax is ₹28.1cr against ₹24.4cr standalone because of a ₹3.6cr deferred-tax credit booked only in the consolidated accounts; the subsidiary had only ten weeks of small operations. Screener's consolidated P/E (16.9) therefore looks lower than the standalone one (19.5). [AR FY26 consolidated P&L and note 5]
- **Auditor:** ATK & Associates, Ghaziabad; unmodified opinion, no key audit matters, audit trail working all year, quarterly stock statements to banks agree with books, no contingent liabilities. [AR auditor's report and CARO]
- **Rating triggers:** CRISIL BBB-/Stable, A3 (Feb 2026) on ₹65cr of limits; it expected gearing of 0.3–0.5 times at March 2026 (actual 0.79) and named operating margin below 12% as a downgrade trigger (H2 FY26: 10.3%). 75–80% of revenue from the automotive sector. [CRISIL Feb 2026]
- **Execution and funding:** ₹850cr of announced projects against ₹108cr of equity; promoters at 39% have said equity raises come "after H1"; management pay rises proposed at the AGM. [Q4 call; AR AGM items 3–4]
- **Import and currency dependence:** cells come mainly from China; stock of 4–5 months at a time of volatile cell prices risks write-downs if prices fall. [Q4 call; AR MD&A]
- **Half-yearly reporting:** investors asked on both calls for quarterly updates; management "will consider". The stock has previously been moved to trade-to-trade/periodic call auction, per an investor on the call. [H1 call; Q4 call]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tone moved from "phenomenal" H1 growth and 13–14% margins to explaining an H2 profit dip (war, currency, cell supply, more OEM sales).
- Capacity plan grew from 25,000 packs a month (Nov) to 35,000–40,000 (May), then the 10 GWh Aligarh campus (Sep).
- Recycling slipped from a March 2026 start to August 2026, and the Sept release for the same site describes a pack plant.
- Funding moved from "debt only, no dilution" (Nov) to "equity after H1" (May) to "planned capital raises" (Sep).
- Inventory policy moved from 6–8 months of price hedging (Nov) to 4–5 months of physical stock (May).

## My read
The pack business is real and fast-growing: revenue went from ₹48cr to ₹297cr in two years at a 12–14% operating margin and a 27% return on capital. But it has never produced operating cash, margins fell to 10.3% in H2 as costs rose, and management has in four months layered a ₹75cr plant, a ₹282cr recycling plan and now a ₹700cr 10 GWh campus on a ₹108cr balance sheet, which means heavy borrowing and new shares. The price asks for only about 13% a year of earnings growth; the business can deliver more than that on revenue, but per-share earnings will be diluted and the quality of disclosure (a related-party supplier for 30% of purchases, draft text left in the annual report, standalone and consolidated profits that disagree) is below what I would accept. I would change my view on two clean half-years of positive operating cash, a clear funding plan with limited dilution, and the Volts AMP purchases falling or explained.
