# Media and Entertainment — sector research (Phase A2), 28 Sep 2026

## Market size and growth
- Indian M&E grew 9% to ₹2.78 trillion in 2025; FICCI-EY forecasts ₹3.3 trillion by 2028 (over 7% a year); only ~2.8% growth expected in 2026. Digital media crossed ₹1 trillion (+31%); digital advertising ₹947bn (+26%, 63% of ads); digital subscriptions ₹163bn (+60%); live events +44%; films a record ₹205bn (theatrical +16%); linear TV ads −10%; video games −17% after the Aug-2025 money-gaming ban. [FICCI-EY Mar-26 via EY press release; Tips AR FY26 MD&A]
- Music: ₹59bn in 2025 (+10%): digital licensing 58%, other licensing (performance, publishing, sync) 27% (+23%), other income (live, brand, artist management) 15%. FICCI-EY forecasts 9% a year to ₹75bn by 2028. Audio subscription revenue +48% to ₹10bn (₹10.3bn) in 2025; paid music subscriptions 14.4m (+37%); FICCI-EY expects 28–30m paid subscribers by 2028. [FICCI-EY via Tips AR FY26; EY press release]
- Global recorded music: US$31.7bn in 2025 (+6.4%), streaming 69.6%; India earns ~1% of global music revenue with 18% of the population; Indian labels earn 4–10 paise a stream vs 50–90 paise globally. [IFPI via Tips AR/IP; Saregama Q1 call]
- Paid streaming penetration: India ~3% of internet users vs 67% Sweden, 57% US, ~18–19% Brazil/China (Goldman Sachs estimate cited by Saregama). [Saregama Q4/Q1 calls]
- Animation, VFX and post-production: ₹105bn in 2025, +1.6–2% after a sharp 2024 fall; FICCI-EY forecasts 10% a year to ₹138bn by 2028; VFX alone ~₹48bn. Technicolor's shutdown affected up to 2,000 Bengaluru professionals; Jellyfish halted global operations. [FICCI-EY via AnimationXpress]
- Video OTT: 216m paid video subscriptions in 143m households; connected TVs 40m. [EY press release]

## Demand drivers
- Music labels: paid subscription (the only fast-growing digital pool; free-tier ad revenue on audio apps is flat per Saregama), YouTube ad share, short-form licences (still flat annual fees, not ad share), public performance (Tips: industry ~₹500cr, "at least ₹3,000cr in three years" on compliance), brand sync, live events and artist management. [Saregama Q4 call; Tips Q4 call; Tips IP]
- Free platforms shutting (Wynk Nov 2024, Resso, Hungama; Gaana went paid) depressed FY25–FY26 label growth; Saregama says that headwind is over. [Saregama Q4 call]
- VFX: Hollywood recovery after the 2023 strikes (2026 global box office forecast ~US$35bn; LA feature shoot days +52% in Q1), tax credits of 30–50% (France 40%, Ireland 40%, Qatar 40–50%), and offshoring to India; streaming budgets (Netflix, Amazon) set demand. [Basilic Q1 call; conference]

## Policy
- Copyright: labels own sound recordings and (for most film music) underlying works in perpetuity; public performance collected via societies (IPRS, PPL). A Calcutta High Court ruling in a Vodafone/IPRS matter was raised on Saregama's call; management said "no implication on us". [Saregama Q4 call]
- AI: DPIIT's Dec-2025 working paper proposes a mandatory blanket licence for AI training on lawfully accessed works, with statutory royalties collected by a single government-designated body (CRCAT) — no opt-out; royalties due on commercialisation, rates set by a government committee. Labels prefer licensed deals (UMG/WMG AI deals) and want platforms to pay nothing for "AI slop". [DPIIT via ALG India; Tips IP; Saregama Q4 call]
- VFX: Indian studios gain from UK/France rules that allow part of subsidised work to be spent in India; PM's 15 Aug 2026 speech named VFX/animation/gaming as soft-power priorities. [Basilic Q1 call]

## Cost drivers
- Labels: new-content cost (film music bidding; Saregama spent ~₹316cr FY25 and ~₹339cr FY26; Tips' FY27 budget ₹90–100cr), marketing, royalties on Hindi film music, and (for Saregama) amortisation of rights over ~10 years. Tips expenses all content on release. [CARE Jul-26; Tips Q1 call; Saregama IP]
- VFX: people (64% of Basilic's FY26 sales), GPUs/storage, FX rates; an India artist costs 1/7–1/8 of London. [Screener; Basilic Q1 call]

## Competitive structure and where profit sits
- Music is concentrated: T-Series (unlisted) is No. 1 by revenue, Saregama No. 2, then Sony, Zee Music, Tips, Universal, Warner; Saregama claims 25–30% of new film music. Catalogues earn 60–75%+ margins once paid for; the fight is over new film albums. [Saregama Q4 call; Screener]
- VFX is fragmented and global (DNEG, Framestore, MPC/Technicolor legacy, Rodeo FX, ILM have Indian operations); prices set by studio budgets; margins 15–25% for integrated studios, 40%+ for India-only service work. [Basilic Q1 call; Screener]
- Profit pool (FY26 net profit): Tips ₹217cr on ₹376cr revenue; Saregama ₹206cr on ₹985cr; Basilic ₹51cr on ₹408cr. [Screener]

## Cycle position
- Music: early in the paid-subscription cycle; content-cost cycle peaking (Saregama's ₹1,000cr plan ends FY27; Tips raising spend). Short-form monetisation is the swing factor.
- VFX: recovering from the 2023 strikes and 2024–25 repricing; 2025 was flat per FICCI-EY; AI cuts roto/prep hours 40–65%, deflating the lowest-value work that India does most of. [FICCI-EY via AnimationXpress; Basilic conference]

## Outlook FY27–FY29 (forecasters named)
- FICCI-EY: M&E +2.8% in 2026, ~7% a year to ₹3.3 trillion by 2028; music 9% a year to ₹75bn by 2028; animation/VFX/post-production 10% a year to ₹138bn by 2028; paid music subscribers 28–30m by 2028. [FICCI-EY Mar-26]
- CARE (Saregama, Jul 2026): scale to grow with content acquisition, margins "healthy". [CARE]
- My view: labels can grow 12–18% a year only if subscriptions compound and content costs stop rising faster than revenue; VFX growth depends on a few streamer budgets and on studios accepting India-led delivery as AI changes the work.
