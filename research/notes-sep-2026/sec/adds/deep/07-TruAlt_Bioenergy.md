# TruAlt Bioenergy (NSE: TRUALT) — deep research, 28 Sep 2026

Screener code used: TRUALT (consolidated page has figures). Listed 3 Oct 2025 (IPO at ₹496; price ₹448 on 28 Sep 2026).

## Business and segments
- Makes fuel ethanol from five distilleries in Bagalkot/Belagavi, Karnataka: 2,000 KLPD installed (Mudhol 700, Jamkhandi 500, Khanapur 400, Kerakalmatti 200, Badami 200); 1,300 KLPD (65%) converted to dual feed (sugar syrup/molasses and maize/broken rice) between Nov 2025 and Feb 2026. Capacity about 5.5–6 crore litres a month; 55–60 crore litres a year on 300–330 days. [IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Buyers: public-sector oil companies (Indian Oil, BPCL, HPCL) under the annual ethanol tender and a long-term offtake agreement; private marketers (Nayara, Jio-bp) and potable-alcohol makers (ENA). [Q4 call; CRISIL Aug-26]
- By-products: DDGS animal feed from grain (₹45cr in Q1 FY27), CO2, extra neutral alcohol (₹37cr in Q1). [Q1 call]
- Compressed biogas (CBG): one 10.2 tonnes-a-day plant (Leafiniti, 51% TruAlt / 49% GAIL) running at about 78% utilisation; gas about 58–60% of CBG revenue, organic manure about 40%. [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Fuel retail: 7 franchise outlets (~₹105cr a year revenue at ~5% EBITDA), 4 more under construction, 76 sites shortlisted but paused. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Planned sustainable aviation fuel (SAF): 100 million litres a year ethanol-to-jet plant in Srikakulam, Andhra Pradesh, with Honeywell UOP technology; ~₹2,250cr project (₹2,000cr on the call); ₹150cr PM JI-VAN grant sanctioned; revenue targeted FY29. [AR FY26 p.142; Q1 call; IP Q1FY27]
- Segment split FY26 (consolidated, AR note 40): ethanol and other products ₹1,705cr, CBG ₹22.5cr. Q1 FY27 segment total income: ethanol ₹626cr (EBITDA ₹126.6cr, 20.7%), CBG ₹11.3cr (EBITDA ₹6.2cr, 55.6% vs 66.7% a year earlier), retail ₹4.4cr (PAT ₹0.05cr). [AR FY26 p.265; IP Q1FY27]
- All revenue is from India. [AR FY26 note 40]

## Key numbers (Screener consolidated)
- Quarterly revenue / operating profit / margin / net profit: Q1 FY26 ₹304cr / ₹42cr / 14% / ₹5cr; Q2 ₹115cr / −₹5cr / −4% / −₹38cr (three plants shut for dual-feed work); Q3 ₹713cr / ₹134cr / 19% / ₹69cr; Q4 ₹596cr / ₹129cr / 22% / ₹69cr; Q1 FY27 ₹627cr / ₹133cr / 21% / ₹59cr. [Screener]
- Years: revenue FY23 ₹762cr, FY24 ₹1,223cr, FY25 ₹1,908cr, FY26 ₹1,728cr, TTM ₹2,050cr. Operating profit ₹105cr, ₹188cr, ₹309cr, ₹290cr, TTM ₹392cr. Net profit ₹35cr, ₹32cr, ₹147cr, ₹97cr, TTM ₹159cr. Interest ₹160cr in FY26 (TTM ₹166cr); depreciation ₹86cr. [Screener]
- FY26 fell 9% because the ESY 2025-26 tender allotted only 26 crore litres of a 72 crore bid (34%), private buyers stopped lifting at ₹60, and three plants were shut about three months. FY26 volume about 24–26 crore litres (both figures given on the Q4 call). [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27: 8.29 crore litres sold at 60.57% utilisation; 4.37 crore litres sugar-based (₹277cr), 3.92 crore litres grain-based (₹314cr). Average realisation ₹67–68 a litre; OMC prices ₹71 (maize), ₹65 (syrup), ₹64 (damaged grain), ₹60 (B-heavy), ₹58 (FCI rice). [Q1 call; IP Q1FY27; Q4 call]
- Unit economics: grain yield ~450 litres a tonne against ~317 for sugar feed; grain-based ethanol about 6–7 points more profitable. On maize bought at ₹17–22 a kg contribution was ₹15–16 a litre; at the current ₹25.5 a kg it drops to ₹6–7. Q1 margins came from maize stocked in Oct–Feb. [Q1 call]
- CBG: ~₹4.5cr revenue per tonne a day of capacity; billing ~₹85 a kg of gas; EBITDA >55–60%. [Q1 call; Q4 call]
- Returns: ROCE 11% FY24, 14% FY25, 10% FY26; ROE 8% last year. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- Q4 FY26 call (22 May 2026): FY27 target 55 crore litres = 26 (OMC tender) + 8 (private) + 6 (ENA) + 15 (court-ordered carry-forward from ESY25, worth ~₹1,062cr); minimum 40 crore litres. The 15 crore litres were expected "within H1" FY27, after a Supreme Court ruling due in June. CBG to reach ~162 TPD with nine new plants by Q4 FY27; GAIL-JV EPC tenders in June and construction from July. PM JI-VAN approval "soon". Retail to ~87 outlets "by next year". No further major capex in ethanol. [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27 call (29 Jul 2026): volume guidance now 44 crore litres from orders in hand, **excluding** the 15 crore litres, whose timing it could not give ("any time now"). Q2 sales ~11 crore litres. Realisation ₹67–68. Sumitomo plants: three of four "by Q3", revenue from Q4; GAIL six plants construction from August, revenue from Q1 FY28. SAF EPC award "in the next 2 or 3 months", revenue FY29. Pledge on promoter shares to be released "this quarter". A de-leveraging plan promised "in the next few days". Retail roll-out paused because of the Middle East war. [Q1 call]
- Presentation dates: Mudhol CBG commissioning August 2026, Kedarnath September 2026, Badami December 2026; Bhima Patas (Daund) awaiting sub-lease. Phase I ramp 40% in FY27, 85% in FY28, ~90% from FY29. [IP Q1FY27]
- Verdict on delivery: **Mixed.** Revenue +106% year on year and a 21% margin beat anything in FY26, and the ₹150cr SAF grant came through; but volume guidance was cut from 55 to 44 crore litres, the court quantity slipped out of H1, the Sumitomo plant count for FY27 fell from four to three (one site has a land dispute), and by 24 September the pledge had not been released — a new promoter pledge was added instead (see Risks). [Q4 call; Q1 call; BSE filing 24-Sep-26]

## Capex and capacity
- Ethanol: dual-feed conversion done (₹150.7cr of IPO money allocated to Unit 4's 300 KLPD; ₹140.1cr used by March). Company says no further ethanol capex. [AR FY26 p.143; Q1 call]
- CBG: ~₹760cr gross for about 132 TPD more (Sumitomo JV 4 × 20 TPD ~₹330–340cr; GAIL JV 6 plants, 72 TPD, ~₹425cr), funded 70:30 debt:equity, TruAlt 51%. NABARD term loan ₹180cr at 8.55% for the Sumitomo JV. CRISIL puts total CBG capex at ₹950–1,000cr over FY27–FY28 with ₹650–700cr of term debt, and capacity reaching ~190 TPD by FY29. At ₹4.5cr revenue per TPD, 132 TPD at full use is ~₹590cr of revenue (TruAlt's share 51%). [Q1 call; Q4 call; IP Q1FY27; CRISIL Aug-26]
- Board added ₹21.42cr equity into Leafiniti and ₹8.27cr into TruAlt Sumi Gas on 22 Sep 2026. [BSE filing 22-Sep-26]
- SAF: ~₹2,000–2,250cr, financing not finalised; the AR calls the AP MoU "non-binding" and subject to feasibility studies. Management expects a ~24–25% margin at ₹180–190 a litre and asset turn of ~1x. [AR FY26 p.142; Q1 call]
- Capital commitments ₹164cr at March 2026 (₹222cr a year earlier). [AR FY26 note 56]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings ₹1,652cr, cash ₹90cr at March 2026 → net debt ₹1,562cr, about 4x TTM operating profit. Equity ₹1,521cr after the ₹750cr IPO. [Screener]
- Operating cash **−₹296cr** in FY26: inventory rose ₹318cr (unsold ethanol and stocked grain; ~₹460–500cr of ethanol inventory at March per the Q4 call) and payables fell ₹199cr. Capex ₹511cr, so free cash flow −₹807cr, covered by the IPO and ₹221cr of new short-term borrowing. [AR FY26 MD&A p.85; Screener; Q4 call]
- Four-year operating cash ÷ operating profit about 35%; FY24 21%, FY25 110%. Inventory days 173 and cash cycle 170 days in FY26 (−14 in FY25). [Screener]
- Standalone interest cover 1.61x in FY26 (2.09x FY25); company-reported consolidated DSCR 1.36 and debt/equity 0.59 for Q1. Finance cost ~₹180cr a year. [AR FY26 p.85; IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]
- CRISIL: bank limits 82% used over 12 months to July 2026; cash accrual ₹270–360cr a year against repayments of ₹180–260cr; sale proceeds go through an escrow waterfall. [CRISIL Aug-26]
- No dividend since listing. [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Largest single ethanol capacity in India (2,000 KLPD; ~3.6% of national capacity at FY25 per the company's IPO documents as summarised by Screener). [Screener about; IP Q1FY27]
- Feedstock: group sugar mills (Nirani Sugars) supply molasses and press mud; FY26 purchases from Nirani Sugars ₹879cr (₹1,081cr in FY25). Group share of sourcing "less than 55%" and falling as grain use rises. Biomass boilers lower fuel cost. [AR FY26 note 39; Q4 call; Q1 call]
- Dual feed lets it choose the cheapest feed and run 300+ days instead of a 100-day crushing season. [Q1 call]
- But pricing is set by the government and allocation by the oil companies: it got 34% of its bid in ESY 2025-26, and India's installed capacity (~1,995 crore litres) far exceeds OMC procurement (~1,200 crore litres). [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A pp.64–65]
- CBG: JV partners GAIL (offtake) and Sumitomo (city-gas licensee in its area); sites need 3x the feedstock within 20–30 km. [Q1 call; Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Promoter pledge.** About 2.23 crore shares (26% of the company, 36.8% of the promoters' 70.55%) were pledged in November 2025 to SBI and IREDA as collateral for the company's term loans. On 29 Jul the CFO said release was requested and expected "this quarter". On 22 Sep promoter company Nirani Holdings pledged a further 14 lakh shares (1.63%) to Jio Credit to secure a loan to Vijaykumar and Vishal Nirani used "for financing the acquisition of shares" of TruAlt; Nirani Holdings then bought 1.52 lakh shares for ₹6.69cr on 25 Sep (stake 2.11%). [AR FY26 note 54; Q1 call; BSE filings 24-Sep-26, 25-Sep-26; Screener]
- **Exchange fines.** NSE and BSE each fined ₹5.37 lakh (incl. GST) under Regulation 17(1) — the board had no woman independent director for all 91 days of Q1 FY27 — plus ₹11,800 each under Regulation 29 for late notice of a board meeting in July. A woman independent director was appointed on 22 Sep 2026 for one year; fines paid. [BSE filing 23-Sep-26]
- **Rating.** CRISIL A-/Negative since Oct 2025, reaffirmed 13 Aug 2026; the stipulated three-month debt-service reserve account has not been created (bank guarantee sought instead). Downgrade trigger: margin below 12%. Acuité classed it "non-cooperative" in June 2025 for not furnishing information. [CRISIL Aug-26]
- **Related parties.** ~₹879cr of FY26 raw materials from promoter-owned Nirani Sugars; ₹175cr owed to it at March. TruAlt bought 51% of TruAlt Gas from "its shareholder" at ₹24 a share (₹8.42cr) in Oct 2025; the subsidiary then issued rights shares to Nirani Holdings, and in April 2026 **Sumitomo bought its 49% from Nirani Holdings**, not from TruAlt, at ₹30.90 a share (1.14 crore shares, ~₹35cr). TruAlt guarantees the subsidiary's NABARD loan and pledged 15.11% of its stake. [AR FY26 notes 36, 39, 59; standalone note 8]
- **Auditor.** Unmodified opinion with an emphasis of matter: componentisation of the three dual-feed plants is incomplete, so the fixed-asset register is not updated. Key audit matters: inventory valuation and revenue recognition. A whistle-blower complaint was received and withdrawn. A ₹10.5cr GST reversal from FY26 was corrected through reserves in Q1 FY27 (an "oversight" by tax consultants). No contingent liabilities reported. [AR FY26 pp.215, 277; CARO xi(c); Q1 call]
- **Policy.** Volumes depend on the annual OMC tender and a court case; private OMCs reneged on ₹60 orders (5 of 8 crore litres still unsold at ₹60.50). Ethanol prices unchanged; management does not expect a rise. [Q4 call; Q1 call]
- **Feedstock.** Maize at ₹25.5 a kg cuts contribution to ₹6–7 a litre once cheap stock runs out. [Q1 call]
- **SAF scale.** A ₹2,000cr+ project against ₹1,521cr of equity and ₹1,562cr of net debt; financing not yet disclosed. [AR FY26; Screener]

## My read
The ethanol platform is now built and Q1 showed what 60% utilisation on cheap maize can earn (₹133cr of quarterly operating profit). But the business does not control its two main levers: the volume the oil companies allot and the price of maize. FY26 turned ₹290cr of operating profit into −₹296cr of operating cash, net debt is ~₹1,560cr, CRISIL has it on Negative, and a ₹950–1,000cr CBG programme plus a ₹2,000cr+ SAF plant sit on top. The governance signals this quarter point the wrong way: no woman director for a whole quarter, a pledge that was to be released but was added to, and a subsidiary stake sold to Sumitomo by the promoter rather than the company. At the current price it needs about 16% a year; I expect about 12% (from TTM profit of ₹159cr: volumes of ~44 crore litres help, maize and interest hurt, CBG adds from FY28). I would change my view on a clean ESY 2026-27 allocation above 40 crore litres, operating cash turning positive, the pledge released, and SAF funding that does not strain the balance sheet.
