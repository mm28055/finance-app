## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Clean Max Env. Energ

## Executive Summary
Clean Max Enviro Energy Solutions (CleanMax) exists to solve a dual mandate for heavy power consumers in India: structurally lowering energy costs while aggressively decarbonizing supply chains. The company bypasses inefficient state distribution companies (DISCOMs) to deliver bilateral green power directly to blue-chip industrials and global hyperscalers, saving clients upward of 25% on utility bills. 

CleanMax is the definitive **Category Leader** in the Indian Commercial & Industrial (C&I) renewable energy space. Unlike commodity independent power producers (IPPs) bidding for low-margin government tenders, CleanMax commands a massive 35% domestic market share in the lucrative hyperscaler segment (serving Apple, Meta, Google, Amazon). By shifting the constraint from demand generation to supply execution, the company has insulated itself from pricing wars, locking in high-quality, long-term cash flows that are uniquely positioned to capture the ensuing AI and data center supercycle.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** CleanMax sells renewable electricity (solar, wind, and wind-solar hybrids) and turnkey EPC/O&M services. It does not sell equipment; it sells energy certainty and decarbonization.
- **Who pays them?** High-credit B2B off-takers. Over 80% of their volume is contracted with AA/AAA-rated entities or multinational subsidiaries, including tier-one data center operators (NTT, STT, Equinix) and heavy industrial manufacturers (cement, auto components, chemicals).
- **How do they make money?** The business model is deeply recurring. The core engine is long-term Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) averaging 23 years. In this OpEx model, CleanMax finances, builds, and owns the assets, selling the generated power at contracted tariffs (currently stabilizing around an attractive INR 4.00/unit).

*Commentary:* RE Power Sales is the undisputed profit engine, benefiting massively from operating leverage as capacity scales (SG&A remains flat while megawatts double). Geographically, the company has intentionally decentralized its asset base. Historically concentrated in Karnataka and Gujarat, the footprint is now highly fragmented across 10+ states (including Maharashtra and Rajasthan), neutralizing single-state regulatory or weather (e.g., El Niño wind impacts) risks. 

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
The moat is **Durable** and built on a combination of execution scale, entrenched client relationships, and high switching costs. 
- **Evidence:** CleanMax’s repeat business rate is staggering—nearly 80% of all newly contracted volumes come from existing customers. Their 23-year PPAs create near-permanent customer lock-in. Furthermore, the company has successfully lowered its borrowing costs by 100 bps (to 8.4%) over the last year, securing an AA- credit upgrade. This creates a formidable cost-of-capital advantage over smaller challengers.
- **The Bear Case:** The moat relies heavily on state-level "open access" grid policies. If state DISCOMs successfully lobby to hike cross-subsidy surcharges or wheeling charges to protect their own monopolies, CleanMax’s 25% cost-saving proposition to clients could mathematically compress, weakening their pricing power.

## Growth Vectors
- **Where is the next $100M coming from?** The AI and Data Center infrastructure buildout. Data & AI clients now constitute 42% of CleanMax's contracted capacity, up from just 14% two years ago (a 10x explosion in MW terms). Management effectively capitalizes on a brutal industry math: 1 GW of data center IT load requires approximately 6 GW of round-the-clock renewable generation and storage (a ~INR 40,000 crore capex pool). CleanMax has 2.5 GW contracted and under construction to feed this specific pipeline.
- **Management Execution:** Management is aggressively walking the talk. The company has shifted from being demand-constrained to execution-constrained, and they are rising to the challenge. Trailing 12-month capacity additions accelerated violently from 450 MW a year ago to 1,740 MW by Q1 FY27. Management has confidently projected a minimum reported EBITDA of INR 3,000 crores by FY28 (a 2.3x jump from FY26), underpinned by a visible path to 4.6 GW of operational OpEx capacity by April 2027.

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Forensic Red Flags:** Governance appears notably clean. The company’s ability to clear the stringent ESG and compliance due diligence of global hyperscalers serves as a rigorous external audit. Receivables are impressively tight at ~25 days, refuting any concerns of aggressive revenue recognition or uncollectible public-sector debt.
- **Business Risks:** 
  - *Grid Curtailment:* Central Transmission Utility (CTU) projects, particularly in Rajasthan, have faced grid backdowns. While diversified, this specific exposure threatens roughly 2.5% to 6.5% of their run-rate EBITDA if curtailments persist at 40-50% levels.
  - *Revenue Stabilization Lag:* Newly commissioned assets historically suffer a 3 to 6-month drag on revenue optimization due to technical stabilization and regulatory approvals. 
- **What could derail the thesis?** Execution bottlenecks. With 2.5 GW under construction, any macro supply chain shocks (solar module pricing, storage availability) or local delays in securing transmission and evacuation infrastructure could cause a miss on their 1.5 GW annual addition guidance, immediately derailing the FY28 INR 3,000 crore EBITDA narrative.

## Investment Thesis
- **The Bull Case:** CleanMax is the most pristine, privately executed derivative on the Indian data center and manufacturing supercycle. You are buying a business delivering a 46% CAGR in conventional industrial volumes and a 10x two-year growth in hyperscaler volumes. With tariffs holding steady at ~INR 4.00/unit and debt costs falling, the company is experiencing compounding margin expansion. The upcoming maiden corporate bond issuance will further optimize their capital structure, providing ample liquidity to fund their 2.5 GW contracted pipeline and achieve their INR 3,000 crore FY28 EBITDA target.
- **The Bear Case:** The valuation likely prices in seamless execution of the 1.5 GW annual build rate. If state-level grid infrastructure fails to support this pace of evacuation, or if regulatory headwinds (like aggressive grid curtailments in Rajasthan) spread to other states, the operational leverage will reverse. Investors must carefully monitor state DISCOM politics, which remain the structural Achilles' heel of any Indian independent power producer.
