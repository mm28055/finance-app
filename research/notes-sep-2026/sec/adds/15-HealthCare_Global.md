# HealthCare Global Enterprises (HCG) — deep research (as of 18 Oct 2026)

Screener code HCG (consolidated). Price ₹588, market cap ₹8,782cr, 52-week high ₹800, P/E 182x on Screener [Screener, 18 Oct 2026]. ISIN INE075I01017. Controlled by KKR since 30 May 2025 (bought from CVC at ₹445 a share); rights issue at ₹512 in March 2026; new options granted at ₹495 in September 2026 [AR FY26 note 15.3; AR FY26 note 4 of Q1 results; NSE 9 Sep 2026]. Q2 FY27 results are due 5 Nov, with the call on 6 Nov [NSE 13 Oct 2026].

## Business and segments
- India's largest cancer-only hospital chain: 26 hospitals (25 in India plus Nairobi), 19 cities, 10 states, 38 linear accelerators (LINACs, the radiotherapy machines), 20 PET-CTs, 11 surgical robots, 105 operating theatres, "450+" oncologists earning revenue [IP Q1FY27 slides 18–20]. It covers medical, surgical and radiation oncology, nuclear medicine, bone-marrow transplant, CAR-T and genomics. Four of the hospitals are multi-speciality, which is why non-oncology is ~11% of revenue [IP Q1FY27; CARE Jan-26].
- One reported segment, "Medical and Healthcare Services" [AR FY26 note 36]. Revenue outside India (Kenya) was ₹74.0cr of ₹2,538cr in FY26, or 2.9% [AR FY26 note 36].
- **By cluster (FY26, ₹cr):** West 1,133 (45%, +14.3%), South 995 (39%, +13.6%), East 283 (11%, +10.9%), Kenya 74 (+70.9%), Milann fertility 60 (+4.5%) [AR FY26 MD&A]. **Q1 FY27:** South ₹276.5cr (+16%, of which North Bangalore ₹6.7cr), West ₹301.0cr (+9%), East ₹79.9cr (+22%), Kenya ₹20.8cr (+9%), Milann ₹16.9cr [IP Q1FY27 slides 5, 8–10].
- **By treatment (FY26 → Q1 FY27):** medical oncology (chemo, immunotherapy) 39% → 39%; surgical 19% → 21%; radiation 14% → 14%; outpatient and other services 16% → 15%; non-oncology 12% → 11% (fertility excluded) [IP Q1FY27 slide 7]. The Q4 deck gave FY26 differently: medical 38%, surgical 21%, non-oncology 11% [IP Q4FY26 slide 5]. The two decks do not agree on FY26.
- **Payers (India, excluding fertility and Kenya):** cash 40% (FY26) → 41% (Q1 FY27); insurers (TPA) and corporates 25% → 25%; medical travel 2% → 3%; government schemes 33% → 31% [IP Q1FY27 slide 7]. "Non-institutional" (cash, insurance and foreign patients) rose from 67% to 69% in a year [Q1 call]. A government share of about a third is high for the sector: Shalby is at 32% and Yatharth at about 40%, while Jupiter is under 1% [Hospitals note].
- **International patients:** medical value travel is 2–3% of the payer mix and fell in Q4 FY26 because of the Middle East conflict. A new head of international sales was hired [Q4 call; IP Q1FY27]. Kenya (Nairobi, plus a molecular-imaging joint venture) grew 71% in FY26 but only 9% in Q1 FY27 [IP Q4FY26 slide 13; IP Q1FY27 slide 5].
- **Mature vs new hospitals (FY26):** 4 hospitals earn over ₹10cr a month: 44% of revenue, a 26% hospital-level margin and 27% ROCE. 14 earn ₹5–10cr a month: 50% of revenue, 18% margin, 9% ROCE. 6 earn under ₹5cr: 6% of revenue, 5% margin, −22% ROCE [IP Q4FY26 slide 18; AR FY26 MD&A]. In Q1 FY27 the counts moved to 7 / 11 / 7 (North Bangalore joined the bottom group) [Q1 call]. The Bangalore flagship (KR Road) earns 30%+ ROCE [Q4 call]. The South cluster's margin is "25% plus" [Q4 call].
- **Beds (Q1 FY27):** 3,304 bed capacity, 2,855 operational, 2,440 "census" (revenue) beds including chemo day-beds. South 1,483/1,299, West 1,440/1,190, East 359/344 [IP Q1FY27 slide 15]. CARE counted only 2,189 operational beds at Sep-25 against HCG's 2,605 at Mar-26 (the company says ~60 beds were "reclassified"), so HCG's bed numbers are generous [CARE Jan-26; IP Q4FY26 slide 3].
- **Occupancy and ARPOB:** HCG no longer reports either. It reports a blended "centre utilisation" of 58% for FY26 (South 68%, West 50%, East 57%) and ARPP, revenue per in-patient "volume", where a volume includes each chemo cycle and radiation course. ARPP was ₹84,261 in FY26 (+3%) and ₹85,943 in Q1 FY27 (+2%) [IP Q4FY26 slide 17; IP Q1FY27 slides 4, 25]. CARE's figures: **ARPOB ₹44,041 (FY25), ~₹45,188 (H1 FY26); occupancy 65.7% (FY25), 68.7% (H1 FY26)** [CARE Jan-26]. On ARPOB, HCG ranks with Shalby (₹44,711) and GPT (₹42,350), far below Jupiter (₹73,500), Medanta (₹70,244) and Artemis (₹85,690) [Hospitals note].
- **Doctor model:** full-time, organ-specific oncology teams under a network-wide tumour board (36,000+ cases discussed), with 88% doctor retention in FY26 [IP Q1FY27 slides 19, 21]. Doctors are paid largely as consultants rather than employees: medical consultancy charges were ₹549cr in FY26 (21.6% of revenue) and ₹157cr in Q1 FY27 (22.7%), against employee cost of ~15% [AR FY26 MD&A; IP Q1FY27 slide 14]. About 23 oncologists were added in Q4 FY26 and about 20 in the following 4–5 months [Q4 call; Q1 call]. The MR-Linac at North Bangalore, and some other radiation machines, are on pay-per-use contracts rather than owned [Q4 call; AR FY26 MD&A].
- **Milann (fertility):** sold on 29 Jun 2026 to Inviga Healthcare Fund I for ₹37.6cr of equity (enterprise value ₹63.2cr), ₹28.2cr upfront and ₹9.4cr within 18 months. FY26 revenue was ₹60.45cr [NSE 19 May 2026; NSE 29 Jun 2026; Q1 results note 3]. **The buyer is controlled by Dr B.S. Ajaikumar, HCG's founder, promoter and chairman** [IP Q4FY26 slide 6; NSE 19 May 2026].

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ₹292cr (FY25 ₹209cr): growth ₹170cr (North Bangalore, Ahmedabad CoE, Kenya), maintenance ₹119cr [Screener; IP Q4FY26 slide 15]. Q1 FY27 capex ~₹75cr: ₹35cr growth, ₹40cr maintenance [Q1 call].
- Plan: bed capex ~₹150cr in FY27, ~₹300cr over FY28–29 and ~₹100cr in FY30, excluding maintenance of ~₹100cr a year [IP Q1FY27 slide 12; Q1 call]. That is roughly ₹250cr a year all-in, close to FY26's level. CARE had been told ₹550–600cr over three years, ₹270–300cr of it maintenance [CARE Jan-26].
- Acquisitions: Vizag (MG Cancer Hospital, 196 beds) 51% for ₹206cr (Oct 2024) and another 34% for ₹154.5cr (13 Apr 2026); HCG now owns 85%, and the sellers hold a put option on the last 15% [AR FY26 note 45.1; Q1 results note 5]. Up to 10% more of Cancer Care Kenya was bought, with an SBLC guarantee for its loans [NSE 12 Nov 2025]. A 26% stake in a Solapur solar plant (₹0.38cr) gives captive power [NSE 4 Aug 2026]. M&A "definitely" remains on the agenda [Q1 call].
- Capacity headroom: ~400 built but unused beds can open over 3–4 years [AR FY26 MD&A]; existing hospitals "can manage utilization level up to 75%, 80%" [Q1 call].
- Leases are a second, hidden capex: lease liabilities rose from ₹602cr (FY24) to ₹835cr (FY25) as premises for North Bangalore, Whitefield and Cuttack were leased. The undiscounted lease commitment is ₹1,453cr, of which ₹150cr falls due within a year [Screener; CARE Jan-26; AR FY26 note 6].

ICRA's table (garbled in extraction) appears to show total debt to operating profit of about 3.7x for FY26 (4.7x FY25). CARE's net debt to PBILDT was 3.98x in FY25 [ICRA Jun-26; CARE Jan-26].
- Rights issue: 82.95 lakh shares at ₹512 = ₹424.7cr (net ₹419.9cr), allotted 27 Mar 2026. Use: ₹170cr of debt repaid, ₹150cr toward the Vizag stake, the rest for general purposes. Fully used by June [Q1 results note 4; Q1 call]. In practice, about a third of the money bought out Vizag's minority shareholders.

An ICRA board member (Pradip Kanakia) is an independent director of HCG [ICRA Jun-26; IP Q1FY27 slide 23].

## Moat evidence
- Scale and focus: the only listed pure-oncology chain, a 35-year brand and "top-3 in 16 of 19" markets by its own estimate. It claims to have been first in India with several technologies (CyberKnife, Tomotherapy-H, digital PET; it now has an MR-Linac in Bangalore) [IP Q1FY27 slides 18, 20; Q4 call].
- Clinical network: weekly national tumour boards, 30+ new clinical trials in FY26, 1,000+ papers, 88% doctor retention [IP Q1FY27 slides 19, 21]. Cancer treatment runs over months (chemo cycles, radiation courses), so patients rarely switch mid-treatment.
- Pricing power is weak. ARPP grew only 2–3%, below medical inflation. Government schemes still pay for a third of patients. CGHS price caps cost ~1.5% of revenue in Q1 [IP Q1FY27; Q1 call]. Margins (18%) are below multi-speciality peers (ICRA's 13-chain average 24.1%) despite oncology's reputation as a premium speciality [Hospitals note].
- Competition is rising: Apollo, Manipal, Narayana and others are investing in oncology. An analyst pressed management on this; its answer was "clinical differentiation" and legacy [Q4 call].

## Risks and red flags
- **Related-party sale of Milann to the chairman's fund.** Milann went to Inviga Healthcare Fund I, which Dr Ajaikumar sponsors and whose manager he promotes. The price was ~1x sales (EV ₹63cr on ₹60cr revenue), after a ₹31.9cr goodwill write-down. Management called it a "competitive sale process" on the call without naming the buyer's link; the deck and the filing disclosed it [NSE 19 May 2026; IP Q4FY26 slide 6; Q4 call].
- **Payouts around the change of control.** HCG paid ₹58.1cr in cash to buy back 16.2 lakh vested options, including ₹17.1cr to the former CEO Meghraj Gore and ₹2.0cr to the former CFO (amounts above grant-date fair value). This was charged to equity, so it never touched "adjusted EBITDA" [AR FY26 MD&A; note 43; Directors' report annexure]. Separately, CVC's parent paid ₹48.3cr of bonuses directly to HCG employees on 30 May 2025, approved by shareholders [AR FY26 note 44(ii)]. The founder, after becoming non-executive, moved to a consultancy contract: in FY26 he received ₹2.57cr of medical consultancy charges and ₹2.0cr of professional fees (matching the one-time "additional consultancy fee" approved in Dec 2025), on top of ₹1.0cr of salary for April–May [AR FY26 notes 43, 44].
- **New ESOP dilution:** the ESOS 2026 pool allows up to 74.2 lakh options (~5% of equity). 64.47 lakh were granted on 9 Sep 2026 at ₹495, below the market price. Exercise starts after 6 years **or on a change of control**, which suits a KKR exit. The CEO alone may get up to 18.55 lakh options (1.24%) [Q1 results note 7; NSE 9 Sep 2026].
- **Regulator action:** CDSCO suspended the Bangalore flagship's ethics committee from approving new clinical trials for 24 months (order of 12 Jun 2026). It cited late or missing reporting of serious adverse events, missing conflict-of-interest declarations, unsigned 2022 minutes and weak participant-safety oversight [NSE 17 Jun 2026]. The money involved is small, but it reflects on the "clinical excellence" pitch.
- **Contingent liabilities ₹103cr** (₹69cr a year earlier), of which ₹76.1cr is customs duty under EPCG licences (₹52.2cr a year earlier). The ₹8.3cr demand for wrong classification carries a 100% penalty and personal penalties on employees. There is also a ₹10.4cr GST classification demand on FDG (the PET tracer), plus old VAT, sales-tax and excise disputes [AR FY26 note 33]. Q1 FY27 already carried a ₹4.6cr EPCG provision [IP Q1FY27].
- **Auditor (B S R & Co., KPMG network):** unmodified opinion. The key audit matter is impairment of goodwill (₹388cr) and non-financial assets. The audit-trail (edit-log) feature was not enabled for privileged and database-level changes at the parent and four subsidiaries, and not at all at four other group companies. CARO notes two properties (Bengaluru, Vijayawada; ₹49.5cr gross) whose title deeds are still in the names of merged entities [AR FY26 auditor's report; CARO i(c)]. 11 subsidiaries and 7 step-down subsidiaries (₹875cr of assets) were audited by other firms [AR FY26].
- **Turnover in finance roles:** the CFO resigned on 2 Sep 2025, the CEO doubled as interim CFO from 1 Dec 2025, and a new CFO joined on 25 May 2026. A new internal auditor was appointed in Dec 2025 and again in May 2026 [NSE 2 Sep 2025; NSE 1 Dec 2025; NSE 19 May 2026; AR FY26 note 43].
- **KKR control and exit:** Hector Asia (KKR) owns 54.27% and the founder 9.30%, giving promoters 64.21% [AR FY26 note 15.4; Screener Sep-26]. There is **no pledge**, but Hector's HSBC acquisition loan carries a non-disposal undertaking on its 7.17cr shares, which counts as an encumbrance [NSE 5 Oct 2026]. KKR's open offer at ₹504.41 drew just 318 shares, because the market price was higher [Post-offer ad, 12 Aug 2025]. A private-equity owner will sell within ~4–6 years. The new option scheme's change-of-control clause is built for that.
- **Low profit cushion:** PBT was only ₹27cr in FY26 on ₹2,538cr of revenue. Interest plus depreciation of ₹421cr leaves little room. One more slow-filling greenfield, or a CGHS-type price cap (the Supreme Court case and the parliamentary committee's August 2026 proposal), would wipe out the profit [Screener; Hospitals note].
- **Disclosure shifts:** HCG dropped occupancy and ARPOB in favour of "centre utilisation" and ARPP. Bed counts were restated (~60 beds "reclassified"). Specialty mix for FY26 differs between the Q4 and Q1 decks. Cluster utilisation is now "annual disclosure only" [IP Q4FY26; IP Q1FY27; Q1 call].

## What changed between the two calls
- Q4 tone: a reset year done (rights issue, Milann sale, new team). Growth slowed to 11% "by choice". Talk of 15% growth, +100 bp margin, 1,000 beds and 20% ROCE in 4–5 years [Q4 call].
- Q1 tone: execution. 16 of 25 hospitals had record revenue, adjusted margin +121 bp, a new CFO on the call, and a sharper margin path: 21–22% in two years, 24–25% in 4–5 years [Q1 call].
- New in Q1: the North Bangalore ramp figures; the CGHS price-cap impact (~1.5% of revenue); marketing up 20%+ to 2.9% of sales (to settle at 2.5–2.6%); maintenance capex ~₹100cr a year spelled out; Whitefield pushed to end-FY28; the Gujarat slowdown blamed on cutting low-margin scheme immunotherapy [Q1 call].
- Not repeated in Q1: the 20% ROCE target, the 2.5x leverage ceiling, the day-care plan and the Bhavnagar/Cuttack timing.

## My read
HCG is a good franchise with a weak income statement. It is the national cancer brand, its mature hospitals earn 26% margins and 27% ROCE, and KKR's managers are clearly pushing out low-paying business and making the network more productive: adjusted margin up 120 bp, finance cost falling, Milann gone. But the group still earns an 8% ROCE. A third of its patients come through government schemes. Its revenue per bed is among the lowest in the sector. Leases worth ₹825cr hide about ₹130cr a year of rent below the EBITDA line, so free cash flow after rent was negative in FY26. And the "adjusted" numbers repeatedly leave out real costs: new-hospital losses, the ₹58cr option buyout charged to equity, and the EPCG provision. Governance after the takeover is mixed. KKR brings discipline, but Milann went to the chairman's own fund, the old CEO took ₹17cr on the way out, and options were just granted below market with a change-of-control trigger. On my numbers (13–14% revenue growth, margin rising to 20–22%, interest flat, depreciation up), owners' profit could rise from about ₹65cr (adjusted TTM) to ₹170–210cr by FY29, an EPS of ₹11–14. Even at 45x that is about ₹500–630 a share against ₹588 today. Over 10 years that path is worth about 22% a year of earnings growth, while the price needs about 37–41%. The business is worth tracking, but the price already assumes the margin recovery. **Wait.** Watch the Q2 FY27 reported (not adjusted) margin, North Bangalore's losses, the ESOP charge, CGHS exposure and any Supreme Court move on price caps.
