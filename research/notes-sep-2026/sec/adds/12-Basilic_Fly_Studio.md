## PROFILE (Q1 FY27)
# Basilic Fly Studio

## Executive Summary
Basilic Fly Studio (BASILIC) exists to solve the fundamental economic bottleneck of modern entertainment: the linear relationship between visual effects (VFX) volume and production costs. By combining a low-cost, India-led delivery model with proprietary AI workflows and the creative pedigree of its Emmy/BAFTA-winning UK subsidiary ("One of Us"), BASILIC provides Tier-1 Hollywood studios and streaming giants with premium VFX at a 30-40% structural cost advantage. 

BASILIC is a **Challenger** rapidly transitioning into a Tier-1 vendor. Rather than competing purely on commodity offshore labor, the company has weaponized high-end infrastructure, global platform integration, and geographic tax arbitrage (such as France's 40% production rebates) to disrupt legacy Western VFX houses, positioning itself to capture highly complex, blockbuster-scale mandates.

## The Business Model

### Revenue Drivers
- **What do they sell?** End-to-end VFX services encompassing pre-visualization, 3D asset creation, animation, effects simulation, lighting, rendering, final compositing, and advanced 4K de-aging. 
- **Who pays them?** Global film production houses and Tier-1 streaming networks. Key clients include Netflix, Amazon, Disney, Sony, Warner Brothers, and HBO. Revenue is heavily concentrated at the top, with the top 5 clients (largely streaming networks) driving 60% of total revenue.
- **How do they make money?** Project-based contracts. Historically, ticket sizes averaged £1M–£2M, but recent aggressive investments in Western business development leadership have expanded their bidding capacity to £5M–£7M per mandate, capturing marquee global franchises.

### Segment & Geographic Mix
While BASILIC operates as a single VFX service segment, its geographic revenue base is heavily concentrated in Western markets, driven by the global footprint of its target clientele. 

| Geography | Revenue Mix (FY27 Update) | Commentary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Europe (incl. UK & France)** | 60% | Driven heavily by the UK-based "One of Us" acquisition and a 125-member French team aggressively leveraging 40% regional tax rebates. |
| **Americas** | 21% | A primary growth vector; advancing via North American BD hires and a revived M&A pipeline targeting gaming and immersive experiences. |
| **India & ROW** | 19% | Fast-growing domestic OTT demand; onboarding specific client-side leaders to capture high-end Bollywood and Tollywood mandates. |

*Note: Management confirmed in Q1 FY27 that Europe and North America collectively contribute >80% of total revenue.*

### Competitive Moat (or Lack Thereof)
- **Is the moat Durable or Fleeting?** **Widening and transitioning to Durable**. 
- **Evidence:** The core advantage is geographic labor arbitrage (offshoring UK/European roles to Chennai, Pune, and Bengaluru), which alone is easily replicable. However, BASILIC is building durability through three avenues: 
  1. **High switching/qualification costs:** Securing TPN Gold certification and maintaining strict Apple/Disney security frameworks creates a rigorous barrier to entry for handling sensitive Tier-1 IP.
  2. **Pipeline IP & Operational AI:** Beyond generative tools like ComfyUI, BASILIC has integrated non-generative machine learning directly into its production pipeline (ShotGrid). This enables multi-site capacity tracking and predictive global planning that structurally reduces per-shot costs.
  3. **Capital Intensity:** The transition to AI-assisted workflows demands continuous, heavy investments in high-end distributed GPU hardware. This unavoidable capex acts as a natural capital barrier against smaller, undercapitalized studios.
- **The Bear Case:** The moat is heavily exposed to client concentration, with the top 5 clients commanding 60% of revenue. Vendor rationalization or streaming budget cuts could instantly evaporate pricing power. 

## Growth Vectors
- **Where is the next $100M coming from?** 
  - **Moving Upmarket & Tax Arbitrage:** BASILIC is aggressively bidding on larger franchises. The bid pipeline sits at a record ₹456 crores, with roughly 50% in advanced stages of conversion. Management is actively combining offshore execution with strategic European presence to capture high-margin global tax rebates.
  - **Margin Expansion via Offshoring:** Expanding domestic capacity to absorb high-cost Western workloads. The Bengaluru remote-hub has rapidly scaled to 35 high-end CG artists, with management revising its target to an aggressive 100 employees by FY27 end. 
  - **Inorganic Expansion:** The previously stalled North American M&A is now progressing rapidly, which will open access to the domestic US market and highly adjacent, high-margin revenue streams in gaming, commercials, and immersive experiences.
- **Management Execution:** Management is visibly walking the talk. They consciously absorbed a consolidated EBITDA margin hit in FY26 (dropping to 20.9%) to front-load expensive leadership hires and accommodate Q4 project rescheduling. The underlying thesis is validated by the standalone India business, where PAT margins expanded 155 bps to 24.2% and EBITDA margins hit 43.1%. Management projects a 200-250 bps consolidated margin expansion in FY27 as offshoring scale and tech-driven leverage kick in.

## Key Risks & Governance
- **Forensic Red Flags:** Working capital management remains a friction point. While overall Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) sits near a seasonal 96 days, the legacy bucket of aged receivables (>6 months) sitting at ~₹44 crores requires continuous monitoring. Management concedes this legacy debt will take until Q3 FY27 (September-December) to fully clear. 
- **Business Risks:** 
  - **Extreme Client Concentration:** 60% dependency on the top 5 clients.
  - **Structural Capex Drag:** Management's admission that heavy, continuous IT and GPU capex is now "inevitable" to support AI pipelines risks creating a permanent drag on free cash flow.
  - **Integration Risk:** Successfully merging the culture and tech stack of legacy Western studios with a high-growth Indian offshore engine remains historically fraught with friction.
  - **Industry Cyclicality:** The VFX space is notoriously sensitive to project slippages, Hollywood strike actions, and macro-driven content budget slashes.
- **What could derail the thesis?** A failure to normalize the aged receivable cycle leading to outright write-offs, or a shift in streaming strategy where platforms mandate domestic (US/UK) post-production for tax credit purposes without allowing hybrid Indian execution.

## Investment Thesis
- **The Bull Case:** BASILIC is a highly profitable, scalable proxy to global content consumption. By pairing top-tier creative credibility (Academy Award nominations for *Mission Impossible* and *The Electric State*) with an aggressive Indian offshore engine, the company generates robust standalone EBITDA margins (>43%). With a highly visible ₹232 crore order book, a ₹456 crore bid pipeline, and newly integrated AI pipeline infrastructure, the FY26 consolidated margin compression was a clear head-fake. Operating leverage will violently kick in during FY27 (driving 200-250 bps in margin expansion) as larger mandates flow through the expanding Bengaluru hub and the North American M&A opens up the $200B global animation and gaming market. 
- **The Bear Case:** The VFX industry is a notoriously tough neighborhood characterized by intense price competition and total reliance on the capex cycles of a few Hollywood/Tech giants. Furthermore, BASILIC's continuous need for expensive AI servers and software pipelines threatens to permanently depress ROCE. Paired with a sluggish recovery of legacy >180-day receivables, clients clearly hold the balance of power, and any further tightening of global content budgets will squeeze BASILIC's cash flows before its structural cost efficiencies can fully materialize.
