# Bikaji Foods International (BIKAJI, ISIN INE00E101023)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Consolidated figures (Screener) unless marked.

## Business and segments
- Third-largest Indian ethnic-snacks company; largest maker of Bikaneri bhujia; second-largest maker of handmade papad. Founded 1986 (formerly Shivdeep Industries); the Bikaji brand dates from 1993. [Screener] [ICRA Aug-25]
- Q1 FY27 category mix: ethnic snacks (bhujia, namkeen) 75.7% of sales (+11.4% YoY), packaged sweets ~6% (+4.4%), western snacks ~9% (+21.3%), papad ~4.5% (-6.5%, handmade and hit by monsoon), others (frozen, gift packs, cookies). [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- Markets: core states Rajasthan, Bihar, Assam = 72.4% of Q1 sales (+10.8%); focus states (UP, Delhi, Punjab, Haryana, Karnataka, Chhattisgarh) 14.3% (+18.9%; UP +37%); other states 9% (+5.6%); exports 4.3% (-2.2% because of US tariffs and freight). ICRA notes ~70% of revenue has come from the top three states for five years. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call] [ICRA Aug-25]
- Pack mix: family packs 58.6% (+11.2%), impulse (₹5/₹10) packs 41.4% (+10.5%). The company says it leads the family-pack segment. [IP Q1FY27]
- Distribution: 370,893 outlets directly served (+17,000 in Q1), 1.446 million outlets in total reach; target ~5 lakh direct outlets in about three years. E-commerce and quick commerce ~3% of sales, with quick commerce growing 100%+ in Q1. [IP Q1FY27] [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Capacity (30 Jun 2026): 325,320 t a year — bhujia 57,600, namkeen 141,540, sweets 62,280, western snacks 39,300, papad 11,400, others 13,200 — at ~48% utilisation. Six plants in Bikaner plus Guwahati, Tumkur and others, and contract units (Kanpur, Muzaffarpur, Patna, Raipur, Kolkata). [IP Q1FY27] [AR FY26]
- Other businesses: retail via The Hazelnut Factory (THF, premium sweets and gifting; 48.99% held via Bikaji Foods Retail at Mar-26; retail revenue ₹38.4 cr in Q1 FY27, +71.8%, 28 stores); Bikaji Foods USA; Ariba Foods (55%, frozen foods for export); Petunt Food Processors (contract plant); Bhujialalji (49% associate); Bikaji Bakes (70% after a 30% stake was transferred to a bakery expert in Aug 2026); a Nepal JV. [AR FY26] [IP Q1FY27] [BSE filing, 26 Aug 2026]

## Key numbers
- Quarterly revenue (₹ cr): Jun-25 653 → Sep-25 830 → Dec-25 790 → Mar-26 721 → Jun-26 734. Operating profit 96 → 128 → 98 → 88 → 99. OPM 15% → 15% → 12% → 12% → 13%. Net profit 59 → 78 → 62 → 56 → 59. [Screener]
- Q1 FY27: revenue ₹734.3 cr (+12.5%), volume +7.7% to 35,778 t; gross margin 35.7% (+70 bps); EBITDA ₹99 cr (+2.8%), margin 13.5% (-130 bps YoY, +130 bps QoQ); PAT ₹59.5 cr (+1.6%). About ₹12.5 cr of the quarter's other operating income is PLI subsidy. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- Annual revenue (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 1,611 → 1,966 → 2,329 → 2,617 → 2,994. OPM 9% → 11% → 17% → 13% → 14%. Net profit 76 → 136 → 263 → 194 → 254. [Screener]
- FY26: volume +9.5%; gross margin 35.1%; EBITDA margin 13.7% (+120 bps). Standalone core growth was only ~11% in FY26 against the 15–17% investors expected. Ad spend was 2.0% of sales (1.6% in FY25), with H2 ad spend at ~3.2–3.3% of sales against ~1% in H1. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Realisation: Q1 FY27 revenue per tonne ~₹205/kg (₹734 cr / 35,778 t, derived), against ~₹197/kg a year earlier. [IP Q1FY27]
- ROCE 20% (FY26), ROE 17.5%. Valuation: market cap ₹14,380 cr, P/E 55; stock -23% over one year. [Screener]

## Guidance history
- **Revenue growth:** Q4 said a 15% core-state pace "would not be sustainable" — about 13% ± for core and 20%+ for focus states. Q1 said "16% plus minus" consolidated growth each quarter, "mid-teens towards high-teens" for FY27, core 13–15%, UP 30%+. Q1 delivered 12.5% (about 20% in the last 45 days, per management). **Kept; Q1 missed, blamed on one-offs.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **EBITDA margin:** Q4 aimed to keep gross margin flat with EBITDA improving on operating leverage. Q1 set FY27 at 13–13.5% including PLI, a 15% long-term goal "in next 3 years", and at least 50 bps a year of improvement. **Quantified in Q1; the 15% target was pushed out.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **PLI cliff:** FY27 is the last year of the ~₹50 cr PLI (~150 bps of EBITDA). Management expects to recover 50–75 bps through pricing and gross margin, and full margin in 1.5–2 years. **New disclosure in Q1.** [Q1 call]
- **Pricing:** Q4 said the ~3% April price rise was "suffice" for input inflation. By Q1 a second price rise had been taken and "no further increase till Diwali". **Cost pressure larger than Q4 implied.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Ad spend:** ~2% of sales in both calls. **Kept.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Sweets:** Q4 targeted sweets growth of 11–12.5%; Q1 targeted festive (Q2+Q3) sweets at 12–13% and gift packs at 17–18%. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Adjacent units:** THF to grow 50–60% a year for 3–4 years (35+ stores this year, 50 in 2.5–3 years) with 15–16% EBITDA; US business 3x in two years; exports from 3–4% to ~5.5% of sales in 3–4 years. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]

## Capex and capacity
- FY27 capex "less than or close to ₹100 cr": a new sweets factory in Bikaner and an automated warehouse (ASRS) in Bikaner (~1 lakh sq ft, holding 6–8 days of finished goods against <2 days today); trials started May 2026, go-live around Q2. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Capital commitments ₹124 cr at Mar-26. [AR FY26]
- Nepal JV: each partner invests ~₹15 cr; plant between Birgunj and Kathmandu due in 8–9 months (from Aug 2026). [Q1 call]
- Bhujia is to be made at a second plant outside Bikaner after the Q1 labour disruption. [Q1 call]
- With utilisation at ~48%, there is no need for large greenfield capacity; management targets higher utilisation by FY29. [AR FY26]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 160 → 170 → 166 → 231 → 299. ICRA: gearing 0.2x, interest cover 20.8x (FY25), ₹192 cr of free cash and FDs (Mar-25). [Screener] [ICRA Aug-25]
- Operating cash flow (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 57 → 171 → 245 → 193 → 304; FCF -50 → 86 → 118 → 72 → 127 (acquisitions and investments absorb much of the cash). [Screener]
- Working capital is light: debtor days 13, inventory days 25, payable days 14, cash conversion cycle 24 days (FY26). [Screener]
- Dividend payout ~12% (₹1.25 final for FY26); no buyback. [Screener] [BSE AGM results, 20 Aug 2026]

## Moat evidence
- Brand and category leadership in bhujia; a pan-India "Bhujia ho toh Bikaji" campaign; a new Amitabh Bachchan campaign; Pankaj Tripathi for UP. [IP Q1FY27] [Q4 call]
- Cost position: Bikaner location gives access to moth dal and cow milk; low working-capital needs (24-day cycle). [IP Q1FY27] [Screener]
- Pricing power is partial. It took ~3% in April plus a second rise, and cut grammage in impulse packs, yet gross margin was still up 70 bps YoY. ICRA says cost increases cannot always be passed on "immediately and fully" because of competition. [Q4 call] [ICRA Aug-25]
- Share is small outside home states: western snacks share ~1.1% nationally, and UP alone has ~200 listed brands. [Q4 call]
- The GST cut on namkeen from 12% to 5% (Sep 2025) revived category growth from H2 FY26 and should favour organised players over 2–4 quarters. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]

## Input costs
- Key raw materials: edible oil (palm and others), pulses (moth dal, chana dal, moong), flour, milk (sweets), and packaging (laminates, corrugated boxes). [IP Q1FY27] [ICRA Aug-25]
- Q4 FY26: edible oil up 12–14%, packaging up 25–30% at the peak (average laminate cost from ₹190–200 to ₹260–270, then easing to ₹220–225) after the Gulf conflict began in Feb–Mar 2026; pulses benign. [Q4 call]
- Q1 FY27: edible oil still rising; moth and chana dal prices started rising because of weak rain; coal costs added ~30–40 bps to manufacturing cost. The company's edible-oil index stands at 1.63 against its base period, and corrugated-box costs are up ~19%. [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- Pass-through lag: about one quarter. Price rises must be timed around festive gifting, so no further rise until after Diwali 2026. [Q1 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Founder's death:** founder-chairman Shiv Ratan Agarwal died on 23 Apr 2026; Deepak Agarwal is now Chairman and MD. Plants were shut for 2.5–3 days. [Q4 call] [News: Business Standard, 23 Apr 2026] [AR FY26]
- **Single-site dependence:** core bhujia is made only in Bikaner, and migrant labour from Bengal left for elections (Apr–May 2026), cutting production for ~4–4.5 days. [Q1 call]
- **Loss-making subsidiaries (FY26, ₹ cr):** Petunt Food Processors -5.4 (negative net worth), Bikaji Foods USA -4.7, Ariba Foods -2.7, Bikaji Foods Retail -2.1, Bikaji Bakes -0.6. The auditor lists impairment of investments/loans to subsidiaries and fair valuation of investments as key audit matters. [AR FY26]
- **PLI expiry** after FY27 removes ~₹50 cr (~150 bps of margin). [Q1 call]
- **Concentration:** ~70% of revenue from Rajasthan, Bihar and Assam; Delhi underperforming. [ICRA Aug-25] [Q4 call]
- **Promoter selling:** promoter stake fell from 74.92% (Jun-25) to 73.88% (Jun-26); FIIs cut from 7.33% to 4.61% while DIIs rose to 17.51%. [Screener]
- **Contingent items are small:** income-tax matters ₹0.62 cr, a ₹0.36 cr stamp-duty demand, ~₹10.8 cr of deferred duties on bakery machinery under the MOOWR scheme. The auditor raised no qualification or emphasis of matter. Some property title deeds are still in the old Shivdeep Industries name. [AR FY26]
- Related-party: the group's history of merging promoter entities (Hanuman Agrofood, Vindhyawasini Sales) into the listed company; Bikaji Mega Food Park was divested in May 2025. [AR FY26]

## What changed between the two calls
- Q4 was upbeat (Q4 growth ~18%, "no slowdown"); Q1 growth slowed to 12.5% after the founder's death and labour exodus, though June–July were described as record months.
- Margin guidance became explicit (13–13.5% FY27; 15% in three years), and the PLI cliff was acknowledged.
- A second price rise was taken; input inflation broadened from oil and packaging to pulses and coal.
- Exports turned negative (-2.2%) as freight rose 2–3x; in Q4 exports had crossed ₹100 cr and grew 52% for FY26.
- The strategy shifted to decentralising bhujia production and to a Bikaji Bakes partnership (30% stake to a bakery expert).

## My read
The underlying franchise is steady rather than accelerating. Volumes grow high single digits, focus states and quick commerce are doing well, and working capital is excellent, but margins are capped by input inflation, heavier advertising and the PLI expiry, and subsidiaries still lose money. At ~55x earnings after a 23% fall in the stock, the market wants proof of the "mid-to-high teens" growth now promised. The view would improve if Q2–Q3 festive quarters show 15%+ growth with EBITDA at or above 13.5%. It would worsen if growth stays near 12% and PLI loss compresses margin toward 12% in FY28.
