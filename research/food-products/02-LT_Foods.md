# LT Foods (LTFOODS, ISIN INE818H01020)

Research date: 25 Sep 2026. Latest quarter: Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026). Consolidated figures (Screener) unless marked.

## Business and segments
- Global branded basmati and specialty-rice company. Brands: Daawat (India, #2 basmati brand), Royal (#1 basmati brand in North America, 60%+ share of US basmati imports), Golden Star (#1 jasmine-rice brand in the US; now 100% owned), 817 Elephant, Heritage, Ecolife (organic), Mazza (Middle East). Sells in 85+ countries. [IP Q1FY27] [Q1 call]
- Q1 FY27 segments: basmati & specialty rice ₹2,845 cr (90% of revenue, +34% YoY, volume +11%, EBITDA margin 13%); organic foods & ingredients ₹254 cr (8%, -13%, EBITDA 4% vs 10%); ready-to-heat/ready-to-cook (RTH/RTC) ₹53 cr (2%, +13%, EBITDA -9%). [IP Q1FY27]
- Geography Q1 FY27: India 29% of revenue (+19%), North America 50% (+49% reported, +27% normalised for the Golden Star consolidation), Europe 12% (-12%, after cutting non-strategic B2B business), Middle East & rest of world 9% (+44%). [IP Q1FY27]
- Volumes Q1 FY27: India 109,000 t; international 117,000 t. [Q1 call]
- India: Nielsen market share 23.1% (MAT Jun-26, vs 23.7% for FY26); 6.44 million households (Kantar, up from 4.56 million in MAT'23); ~172,000 outlets directly reached (flat); 40%+ share on e-commerce platforms; regional (non-basmati) rice ~₹170 cr and growing at a high double-digit rate. [IP Q1FY27] [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Organic: over 30 years exporting; 110,000 ha of certified farmland; ~12% of India's organic food exports. The business moved from selling to wholesalers to its own consumer-brand (CPG) model in Europe (new plant plus a sales organisation), and holds 30% of the European brand Leev. [IP Q1FY27] [Q4 call]
- Capacity: 8 lakh tonnes of paddy-to-rice milling in India, similar packing capacity; plants in India, the US (new RTH plant due to start Q2 FY27, plus a Houston warehouse), the UK/Europe and Africa. [Q4 call] [ICRA/CRISIL Feb-26]

- Annual revenue (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 5,427 → 6,936 → 7,772 → 8,681 → 10,946. OPM 11% → 10% → 12% → 11% → 11%. Net profit 309 → 423 → 598 → 612 → 625. 5-year sales CAGR 19%, profit CAGR 18%. [Screener]
- FY26: normalised revenue growth 19% (excluding US tariff pass-through), basmati volume +19%, US volume +38% (7–8% excluding Golden Star), EBITDA margin 11.8% ex-tariff. [Q4 call]

## Guidance history
- **Long-term growth:** 10–12% organic growth "on a long-term basis" (Q4). Q1 split: basmati 10–12%, RTH 15–20%, organic 10–12%. Q1 delivered 19% normalised. **Kept; beating.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **EBITDA margin FY27:** "we are confident that we will be in the range of 12%" (Q4). Q1 was 11.5% (up from 9.2% in Q4 on the Screener basis). **Kept; slightly below in Q1.** [Q4 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Gross margin:** ~33–33.5% for FY27 (Q4). Q1: 32.6%. **Roughly on track.** [Q4 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Organic turnaround:** Q4 described the segment as "under stress". Q1 guidance: EBITDA margin from 4% now to 7–8% by FY27-end (~₹70–80 cr EBITDA), back to double-digit growth and margin by FY28. **New and specific; watch.** [Q1 call]
- **RTH:** Q4 said the US RTH plant was "late" and production would start by July. Q1 said it is expected to run in Q2 FY27. Target: US RTH to ~$30 million (~₹300 cr) in two years; segment breakeven at ~₹400 cr revenue in 2–3 years (target pushed back about a year because of capacity). **Delayed.** [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Geographic ambitions:** UK revenue ~GBP 45 million in FY26, target GBP 100 million by 2030 (Q4). Q1: Europe declined 12% after exiting B2B. India revenue to more than double by FY30 with expanding margins (Q1). Middle East to grow ~15%. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- **Capex:** FY26 ~₹330–350 cr; FY27 "in the same range"; long-run ~₹250 cr a year (about equal to depreciation). [Q4 call]

## Capex and capacity
- FY26 capex ~₹330–360 cr, mainly US land plus a new RTH facility and a Houston warehouse; the rest went to UK packaging and Indian warehouses and land. [Q4 call]
- FY27 capex similar (~₹330 cr), with more Indian capacity (a new Raichur, Karnataka plant for regional rice) and European packaging. Management's rule of thumb is ~₹3 cr of capex per 1,000 t of added rice capacity (said as "INR3 crores per ton"); annual volume growth is 50,000–60,000 t. [Q4 call]
- Funded from internal accruals (FCF ₹554 cr in FY26). A new Australian company was opened with minimal equity; no capex plan yet. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- The company will buy the remaining 49% of the Kameda LT Foods JV for ₹1.12 cr, making it wholly owned by 31 Dec 2026. [BSE filing, 16 Sep 2026]

## Cash, debt and working capital
- Borrowings (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 1,320 → 1,221 → 917 → 1,261 → 1,610. Net debt/EBITDA 0.48x and net debt/equity 0.15x in Q1 FY27 (0.59x / 0.18x a year earlier). [Screener] [IP Q1FY27]
- Operating cash flow (₹ cr) FY22→FY26: 517 → 258 → 757 → 462 → 910; FCF 366 → 115 → 556 → 224 → 554. [Screener]
- Working capital is structurally heavy because basmati is aged for 12–24 months. Inventory days 256 in FY26 (Screener). Company figures for Q1 FY27: inventory 187 days (221 a year ago), receivables 26 days, working capital 170 days (195). [Screener] [IP Q1FY27]
- Inventory on 30 Jun 2026: 346,000 t of rice at ~₹56/kg and 164,000 t of paddy at ~₹38/kg — enough for about a year of branded demand. [Q1 call]
- Payable days ~100 are "going to remain", helped by supplier-financing arrangements that raise interest cost (Q1 interest ₹40 cr vs ₹28 cr a year earlier). [Q4 call] [Q1 call] [Screener]
- Dividend payout 17% (FY25, FY26). [Screener]

## Moat evidence
- Scale in sourcing and ageing: 346,000 t of rice inventory. Only 15% of the trade is bought outside the season, and the company buys 60–70% of its needs in season. [Q1 call] [Q4 call]
- Brand leadership: Royal has 60%+ of US basmati imports; Golden Star is #1 in US jasmine; Daawat is #2 in India, #1 in Maharashtra, Gujarat and MP, and holds 60–65% of the biryani-kit category on e-commerce platforms. [IP Q1FY27] [Q4 call]
- Pricing power: passed on a 20–25% rise in paddy prices and US tariffs with volumes still up 11%; basmati EBITDA stays at 13% in a 13–14% band ("plus/minus 1%"). [Q1 call]
- Rating: CRISIL upgraded LT Foods to AA/Stable from AA-/Positive (25 Feb 2026), citing market position, diversification and a strong financial profile. [CRISIL Feb-26]

## Input costs
- Paddy is 65–70% of cost of sales. [CRISIL Feb-26]
- FY26 crop: domestic paddy prices rose 20–25% in the season (25–30% from the opening base by Q4); off-season international deals were 15–20% above the season price. [Q4 call]
- FY27 crop: an El Niño year, but 80–85% of basmati area is canal- or groundwater-irrigated. Management expects the crop to be no bigger than last year and prices to stay "firm". The picture will be clearer by mid/end August. [Q1 call]
- Pass-through: historically full, with a lag; the company carries a year of inventory, so new-crop costs hit margins with a 2–4 quarter delay. [Q1 call]
- Freight: Middle East container rates rose from ~$200 to $2,200–4,000 during the Gulf conflict, which could not be passed on there; Europe was also hit; logistics ~4.7% of revenue. [Q4 call] [Q1 call]
- Currency: 50–60% of exposure hedged; a weaker rupee lifts reported revenue. [Q4 call]

## Risks and red flags
- **Auditor emphasis of matter (FY26):** the ₹134.1 cr insurance-claim asset in subsidiary Daawat Foods (Bhopal fire). The insurer repudiated the claim; the district court ruled for LT Foods (Dec 2023); the insurer's appeal is pending in the MP High Court. Management said ₹136 cr is outstanding and ₹50 cr has been received against a bank guarantee, with no income booked. CRISIL notes a ₹265 cr receipt backed 100% by bank guarantee. Figures differ by source; the outcome is binary. [AR FY26] [Q4 call] [CRISIL Feb-26]
- **US trade policy:** most revenue comes from North America. Tariffs moved from 50% to 10%; a court-ordered duty refund is "partly" received but not booked while talks with customers continue. A countervailing duty on Ecopure's organic soybean meal exports to the US was cut from 340% to 75%; an appeal has been filed. [Q1 call] [IP Q1FY27]
- **Related-party dealings:** large transactions with group entities (e.g., purchases from Raghunath Agro Industries, packaging from Sara Flexi Pack LLP). Standalone guarantees of ₹617 cr were given for related parties' working-capital loans (₹253 cr drawn). The change in shipping terms with the US subsidiary lowered reported gross margin. [AR FY26] [Q1 call]
- **Loss-making units:** organic segment (4% EBITDA), RTH (-9% EBITDA), the UK and Europe "in investment phase", Bonne Nature (Uganda) with negative reserves and a loss in FY26, and new Middle East and Arabia entities with small losses. [IP Q1FY27] [AR FY26]
- **India share:** Nielsen share has slipped (23.7% FY26 → 23.1% MAT Jun-26), which management attributes to a Nielsen methodology change. A third player (Fortune/Kohinoor) is gaining. [Q1 call]
- **Working-capital intensity and supplier-financing** raise interest costs; free cash flow is lumpy. [Screener] [Q1 call]
- Promoter holding flat at 51.00%; no pledge on Screener; FII holding down from 10.15% to 8.21% over the year, while DIIs rose to 10.84%. [Screener]

## What changed between the two calls
- Tariff moved from 50% (Q4) to 10% (Q1), so reported gross margin recovered quarter on quarter. Freight shock in the Middle East and Europe was flagged more strongly in Q1.
- A new, explicit organic-turnaround path (7–8% margin by FY27-end, double digit by FY28) replaced Q4's vaguer "under stress / remodelled".
- The El Niño question became central in Q1. Management moved from "farmers will grow more" (Q4) to "we don't see the crop to be more than last year… prices will remain firm" (Q1).
- The India ambition was upgraded to "more than doubling" revenue by FY30.
- The RTH plant start slipped from "by July" to "this quarter" (Q2).

## My read
The core basmati franchise is improving: volume +11%, a steady 13% EBITDA margin, strong US leadership, and working capital cut by 25 days. Group margin, however, is held back by the organic restructuring, RTH losses and UK investment, so EPS is growing more slowly (+9%) than revenue (+26%). At ~23x earnings with net debt/EBITDA below 0.5x and a fresh AA rating, the stock is priced for steady rather than heroic growth. The view improves if organic margin reaches 7–8% by March 2027 and the US RTH plant ramps up. It worsens if the El Niño crop drives paddy costs up faster than prices can follow, or if the insurance-claim appeal goes against the company.
